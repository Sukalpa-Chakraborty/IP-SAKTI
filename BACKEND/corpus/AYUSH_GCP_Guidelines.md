## 1. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Act** | Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 (23 of 1940) and Rules made thereunder |
| **Adverse Event (AE)** | Any untoward medical occurrence during treatment with a pharmaceutical product in a patient or human volunteer that does not necessarily have a relationship with the treatment |
| **Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)** | For approved ASU medicines: noxious and unintended response at normal doses. For new unregistered ASU medicines: noxious and unintended response at any dose. In clinical trials, also includes occurrence seemingly caused by overdosing, abuse/dependence, and interactions |
| **Audit of a Trial** | Systematic verification of the study by persons not directly involved, to determine consistency with protocol, ensure no contradictions with source documents, and check compliance with SOPs |
| **Ayurveda Siddha Unani Drugs** | Medicines intended for internal or external use for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease in humans or animals, manufactured exclusively per authoritative books in First Schedule |
| **Patent or Proprietary Medicine** | Formulations containing only ingredients from authoritative books, excluding parenteral medicines and formulations specified in clause (a) |
| **Other Traditional Medicine** | Chinese, Japanese, Korean Medicines, etc.; Schedule Y parameters to be followed |
| **Blinding/Masking** | Method of control experimentation where one or more parties are not informed of treatment. Single blind: subject unaware; Double blind: subject and/or investigator, monitor, data analyst unaware |
| **Case Record Form (CRF)** | Document designed per protocol to record data on each trial subject; may be printed or electronic |
| **Clinical Trial** | Systematic study of ASU Medicines on human subjects to discover or verify clinical, pharmacological, and/or adverse effects, determining safety and/or efficacy |
| **Phases of Clinical Trial** | Phase I (Human Pharmacology), Phase II (Therapeutic Exploratory), Phase III (Therapeutic Confirmatory), Phase IV (Post Marketing) |
| **Comparator Product** | Pharmaceutical product/ASU Medicine (including placebo) used as reference |
| **Confidentiality** | Maintenance of privacy of study subjects including identity and medical information |
| **Co-Investigator** | Person legally qualified to be an investigator, to whom the Investigator delegates part of responsibilities |
| **Clinical Research Organisation (CRO)** | Organisation to which sponsor may transfer/delegate tasks, duties, obligations regarding a Clinical Study |
| **Contract** | Written, dated, signed document describing agreement between parties (Investigator, Sponsor, Institution) |
| **Documentation** | All records that describe or record methods, conduct, results of study, and actions taken |
| **Rescue Medicine** | Supplementary treatment to relieve symptoms caused by investigated disease |
| **Essential Documents** | Documents that permit evaluation of conduct of study and quality of data generated |
| **Ethics Committee** | Independent review board comprising medical/scientific and non-medical/non-scientific members, responsible for verifying protection of rights, safety, well-being of human subjects |
| **Final Report** | Complete description of study after completion, including methods, results, statistical analyses, and critical appraisal |
| **Good Clinical Practice (GCP)** | International ethical and scientific quality standard for designing, conducting, recording, and reporting trials involving human subjects |
| **Impartial Witness** | Independent witness not influenced by those involved in trial, who assists at informed consent process |
| **Informed Consent** | Voluntary written consent of subject's willingness to participate in a study |
| **Inspection** | Official review by regulatory authority of documents, facilities, records related to study |
| **Institution** | Public or private medical facility where clinical study is conducted |
| **Investigator** | Person responsible for conduct of study at trial site, responsible for rights, health, welfare of subjects |
| **Investigational Labelling** | Labelling developed specifically for products involved in study |
| **Investigational Product** | ASU Medicine (including Comparator) being tested or used as reference |
| **Investigator's Brochure** | Collection of clinical and non-clinical data on Investigational Product(s) relevant to study |
| **Monitor** | Person appointed by Sponsor/CRO for monitoring and reporting progress of trial, verification of data |
| **Multi-Centric Study** | Clinical trial conducted according to one protocol at different investigational sites, by more than one investigator |
| **Non-Clinical Study** | Biomedical studies not performed on human subjects |
| **Non-Therapeutic Study** | Study with no anticipated direct clinical benefit to subjects |
| **Pharmaceutical Product** | Substance or combination with therapeutic, prophylactic, or diagnostic purpose |
| **Principal Investigator** | Investigator responsible to coordinate between different investigators at one site or multiple sites |
| **Protocol** | Document stating background, objectives, rationale, design, methodology, statistical considerations, and conditions for study |
| **Protocol Amendment** | Changes or formal clarifications appended to protocol, agreed and signed by original signatories |
| **Quality Assurance (QA)** | Systems and processes to ensure trial performed and data generated in compliance with GCP |
| **Quality Control (QC)** | Operational techniques and activities to verify requirements for quality fulfilled |
| **Randomisation** | Process of assigning study subjects to treatment or control group, giving all subjects same chance |
| **Raw Data** | All records or certified copies of original clinical and laboratory findings |
| **Serious Adverse Event (SAE) / Serious Adverse Drug Reaction (SADR)** | Any untoward medical occurrence at any dose resulting in death, life-threatening, requires hospitalization, persistent disability, or congenital anomaly |
| **Schedule** | Proposed Schedule Z to Drugs & Cosmetics Rules |
| **Source Data** | Original documents necessary for evaluation of Clinical Trial |
| **Sponsor** | Individual/company/institution responsible for initiation, management, and/or financing of study |
| **Study Product** | Any ASU Medicines/therapies or Comparator used in clinical study |
| **Sub-Investigator** | See Co-Investigator |
| **Subject Files / Patient Files** | File containing demographic and medical information about study subject |
| **Study Subject** | Individual participating in clinical trial as recipient of Investigational ASU Medicine |
| **Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)** | Detailed written instructions to achieve uniformity of performance in management of clinical studies |
| **Subject Identification Code** | Unique identification number assigned by Investigator to each subject to protect identity |
| **Study Management** | Steering, supervising, data management, verification, statistical processing, preparation of study report |
| **Traditional Medicine** | Sum total of knowledge, skills, practices based on theories, beliefs, experiences indigenous to different cultures, used for health maintenance, prevention, diagnosis, improvement, or treatment |
| **Validation** | Process of proving that procedure, process, equipment, material, activity, or system leads to expected results |
| **Data Safety Management Board** | Board charged with monitoring accumulating data from clinical trial to detect and report early evidence of benefit or harm |
| **Interim Analysis** | Assessment of data during patient enrolment or follow-up for assessing center performance, data quality, or treatment effects |
| **Vulnerable Participant** | Individual who lacks ability to fully consent to participate in a study |

## 2. Pre-requisites for the Study

### 2.1 Investigational ASU Medicines

- Physical, chemical, pharmaceutical properties and formulation must be documented.
- Instructions for storage and handling of dosage form should be documented.

### 2.2 Pre-clinical Supporting Data

- Available pre-clinical and clinical information should be adequate and convincing to support proposed study.

### 2.3 Protocol

A well-designed study relies on a thoroughly considered, well-structured, and complete protocol.

#### 2.3.1 Relevant Components of Protocol

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **General Information** | Protocol title, identifying number, date; amendments; sponsor and monitor details; authorised signatories; investigator details; institution details; disease review; drug review |
| **Objectives and Justification** | Aims, objectives, phase; investigational product description; non-clinical/clinical findings; known/potential risks and benefits; route, dosage, treatment periods; compliance statement; inclusion/exclusion criteria; references |
| **Ethical Considerations** | General ethical considerations; how subjects will be informed and consent obtained; possible reasons for not seeking consent |
| **Study Design** | Primary and secondary endpoints; type of study (randomised, blinded, etc.); schematic diagram; permitted/prohibited medications; treatments, dosage, route, dosage form; packaging and labelling; duration and sequence; initiation date; time-schedule justification; discontinuation criteria; accountability procedures; randomisation codes and breaking procedures; monitoring compliance; Anupan, Desh, Kala, etc. |
| **Inclusion, Exclusion & Withdrawal** | Subject inclusion criteria; exclusion criteria; withdrawal criteria and procedures; statistical justification for number of subjects; Prakriti evaluation questionnaire if applicable |
| **Handling of Product(s)** | Safe handling and storage measures; labelling system (code numbering); label content: "For Clinical Studies only", name/code, investigator details, institution, subject identification code |
| **Assessment of Efficacy** | Effect parameters; measurement and recording; time and periodicity; special analyses/tests |
| **Assessment of Safety** | Safety parameters; methods and periodicity; ADR/AE reporting; follow-up type and duration; study-code establishment and breaking |
| **Statistics** | Statistical methods; timing of interim analysis; number of subjects; break-up by site; significance level; missing/unused/unauthentic data procedures; deviations reporting; subject selection for final analyses |
| **Data Handling and Management** | Statement permitting monitoring, audits, EC review, regulatory inspection; CRF copy; procedures for records; patient lists and records |
| **Quality Control and Assurance** | Plan for controlling and monitoring study; specifications for deviations; allocation of duties and responsibilities |
| **Finance and Insurance** | Financial aspects; sources of support; expenditure distribution; financial arrangements; insurance for subjects; liability of parties |
| **Publication Policy** | If not in separate agreement, described in protocol |
| **Evaluation** | Account for response evaluation; methods of computation and calculation of effects |

#### 2.3.2 Supplementaries and Appendices

- Information to Study Subjects and mode of providing it
- Instructions to staff
- Descriptions of special procedures

### 2.4 Ethical & Safety Considerations

#### 2.4.1 Ethical Principles

All research involving human subjects should be conducted in accordance with ethical principles and respect:

- **Justice**
- **Respect for persons**
- **Beneficence** (maximize benefits, minimize harms)
- **Non-maleficence** (do no harm)

As defined by ICMR Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical Research on Human Subjects (2006) and other laws.

**Principles to be followed:**

a. **Essentiality** – Research absolutely essential after considering alternatives
b. **Voluntariness, informed consent and community agreement** – Subjects fully apprised; right to abstain
c. **Non-exploitation** – Subjects remunerated; fully apprised of dangers
d. **Privacy and confidentiality** – Identity and records kept confidential
e. **Precaution and risk minimisation** – Due care at all stages
f. **Professional competence** – Conducted by competent, qualified persons
g. **Accountability and transparency** – Fair, honest, impartial, transparent
h. **Maximisation of public interest and distributive justice** – Benefits all humankind, especially least advantaged
i. **Institutional arrangements** – Procedures complied with; reports preserved
j. **Public domain** – Results made known through scientific publications
k. **Totality of responsibility** – Responsibility on all connected with research
l. **Compliance** – Letter and spirit of guidelines observed

#### 2.4.2 Ethics Committee

**Basic Responsibilities:**

- Ensure competent review of all ethical aspects
- Protect dignity, rights, well-being of potential participants
- Ensure universal ethical values expressed in local community values
- Assist in development and education of research community

**Composition:**

- Multidisciplinary and multi-sectorial
- 5-7 members minimum; maximum 12-15
- Chairperson preferably from outside Institution
- Member Secretary from same Institution
- Mix of medical/non-medical, scientific/non-scientific, lay public
- One expert member of ASU

**Terms of Reference:**

- Members aware of role and responsibilities
- Changes in regulatory requirements communicated
- Terms of Appointment including duration, removal, replacement, resignation
- Operating procedures available

**Review Procedures:**

- Review every research proposal within reasonable time
- Scientific evaluation completed before ethical review
- Evaluate possible risks, expected benefits, adequacy of documentation
- Formal meetings; not decisions through circulation

**Submission of Application:**

- Clear research objectives and rationale
- CV of Investigators
- Subject recruitment procedures
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Methodology, dosages, routes, duration, invasive procedures
- Plans to withdraw/withhold standard therapies
- Statistical analysis plans
- Informed consent procedures with sample forms in English and vernacular
- Safety of intervention and drug
- Medical therapy plans for risk/injury/toxicity
- Compensation and reimbursement
- Data storage and maintenance
- Publication plans
- Ethical issues and steps
- Regulatory clearances
- Agreement to comply with GCP
- Funding agency/sponsor details
- Compensation for research-related injury, risk-benefit analysis, QC of investigational product

**Decision Making Process:**

- Broad consensus after quorum
- Member Secretary communicates decision in writing
- Member with conflict of interest withdraws
- Negative decision supported by reasons
- May reverse positive decision if benefit/risk ratio adversely affected
- Discontinuation ordered if goals achieved midway or unequivocal results
- Premature termination notification with reasons and summary
- Matters requiring IEC attention: protocol amendments, SAEs, new information
- Applicant/investigator may be invited; patient groups may be invited
- Subject experts may offer views but not take part in decision making
- Meetings minuted, approved and signed by Chairperson

**Interim Review:**

- Special circumstances and mechanism recorded
- For re-examination, minor research, urgent national interest

**Record Keeping:**

- Constitution and composition
- CVs of all members
- SOPs
- National/international guidelines
- Copies of protocol, data collection formats, CRFs, investigational brochures
- Correspondence
- Agenda of meetings
- Minutes with signature of Chairperson
- Decisions communicated
- Premature termination notifications
- Final report including microfilms, CDs, video recordings
- Maintain for at least 5 years after completion/termination

**Special Considerations:**

- Research involving children, pregnant/lactating women, vulnerable subjects, diminished autonomy
- Commercialisation of research and international collaboration

#### 2.4.3 Informed Consent Process

**Informed Consent of Subject:**

- Obtain EC approval for written informed consent form and information provided
- No language causing waiver of legal rights or releasing from liability
- Information in language and complexity understandable to subject
- Ample opportunity to enquire and have questions answered
- No coercion or undue influence
- Careful consideration for hierarchical groups (students, employees, etc.)
- Vulnerable populations: incurable diseases, nursing homes, detention, unemployed, impoverished, emergency rooms, homeless, nomads, refugees, ethnic/racial minorities

**Signatures:**

- Subject or legal representative/guardian or impartial witness
- Investigator
- Witness attests information accurately explained and understood, consent freely given

**Essential Information for Prospective Research Subjects:**

i. Aims and methods of research
ii. Expected duration of participation
iii. Benefits reasonably expected
iv. Alternative procedures/treatments
v. Foreseeable risks or discomfort
vi. Extent of confidentiality and consequences of breach
vii. Free treatment for research-related injury
viii. Compensation for disability or death
ix. Freedom to participate and withdraw without penalty
x. Identity of research teams and contact persons
xi. Foreseeable extent of information on current and future uses of biological material and data
xii. Risk of discovery of biologically sensitive information
xiii. Publication including photographs and pedigree charts
xiv. Information on standard of care (including modern medicines)

**Informed Consent in Non-Therapeutic Study:**

- Consent must always be given by subject
- May be conducted with consent of legal representative/guardian if:
  1. Objective cannot be met by subjects who can personally give consent
  2. Foreseeable risks are low
  3. Ethics Committee's written approval expressly sought

#### 2.4.4 Essential Information on Confidentiality

- Investigator must safeguard confidentiality of research data
- Data of individual subjects disclosed only in court of law under orders of presiding judge, or to drug registration authority or health authority
- Limitations in maintaining confidentiality should be anticipated and assessed

#### 2.4.5 Compensation for Participation

- Subjects may be paid for inconvenience and time; reimbursed for expenses
- May receive free medical services
- Payments not so large or services so extensive as to induce participation
- All payments, reimbursement, medical services approved by IEC
- Care taken:
  - Guardian/legally acceptable representative: no remuneration except refund of out-of-pocket expenses
  - Withdrawn for medical reasons: benefit for full participation
  - Withdrawn for other reasons: paid proportionately

#### 2.4.6 Selection of Special Groups as Research Subjects

**Pregnant or Nursing Women:**

- No research unless minimal risk to fetus/nursing infant and object is to obtain new knowledge about foetus, pregnancy, lactation
- Justification: should not be deprived arbitrarily of opportunity to benefit
- Women should not be encouraged to discontinue nursing
- Research related to termination of pregnancy: as per MTP Act, GOI, 1971
- Research related to pre-natal diagnostic techniques: limited to detecting foetal abnormalities or genetic disorders as per PNDT Act, GOI, 1994; not for sex determination

**Children:**

- Children not involved in research that could be carried out equally well with adults
- Purpose is to obtain knowledge relevant to health needs of children
- Clinical evaluation of new drug in children after Phase III in adults; earlier only if therapeutic value in primary disease of children
- Parent or legal guardian gives proxy consent
- Assent of child obtained to extent of capabilities
- Research conducted in settings with adequate medical and psychological support
- Interventions intended for direct benefit justified in relation to risks and benefits
- Child's refusal respected unless no medically acceptable alternative
- Interventions intended for therapeutic benefit at least as advantageous as alternatives
- Risk presented by interventions not intended to benefit child is low compared to importance of knowledge

**Vulnerable Groups:**

- Individuals/communities selected so burdens and benefits equally distributed
- Research on genetics should not lead to racial inequalities
- Economically/socially disadvantaged not used to benefit better off
- Rights and welfare of mentally challenged and mentally differently able persons protected
- Adequate justification for involvement of prisoners, students, subordinates, employees, service personnel

#### 2.4.7 Compensation for Accidental Injury

- Subjects suffering physical injury entitled to financial or other assistance
- In case of death, dependents entitled to material compensation

**Obligation of Sponsor to Pay:**

- Sponsor (pharmaceutical company, government, institution) should agree before research begins to provide compensation for serious physical or mental injury or insurance coverage

## 3. Responsibilities

### 3.1 Sponsor

#### 3.1.1 Investigator and Institution Selection

- Responsible for selecting Investigator(s)/Institutions considering appropriateness and availability
- Assure Investigator's qualifications and availability for entire study duration
- For multi-centric studies, organisation and selection of coordinating committee/investigators

#### 3.1.2 Contract

- Formal legal agreement with Investigator(s)/Institution(s) on:
  - Conduct study in compliance with GCP, regulatory requirements, protocol
  - Procedures for data recording and reporting
  - Permit monitoring, auditing, inspection
  - Retain essential documents until sponsor informs no longer needed or minimum 5 years
- Define relationship: financial support, fees, honorarium, payments in kind

#### 3.1.3 SOP

- Establish detailed Standard Operating Procedures
- Sponsor and Investigator sign copy of Protocol and SOPs

#### 3.1.4 Allocation of Duties and Responsibilities

- Define and allocate all study-related duties and responsibilities
- Overall responsibility lies with sponsor

#### 3.1.5 Study Management, Data Handling and Record Keeping

- Secure agreement on allocation of responsibilities:
  - Access to sites, source data/documents, reports for inspection, monitoring, auditing
  - Data processing
  - Breaking of code
  - Statistical analysis
  - Preparation of Study Report
  - Submission to Ethics Committee, Regulatory Authorities
  - Reporting ADRs, AEs to Ethics Committee
  - QA and QC systems with written SOPs
- Safe and secure custody of all study-related documents for 3 years after completion or submission to regulatory authority (whichever later)
- May establish Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC)

#### 3.1.6 Compensation for Participation

- Per guidelines in 2.4.5

#### 3.1.7 Confirmation of Review by Ethics Committee

- Obtain from Investigator(s)/Institutions:
  - Particulars of EC members (names, addresses, qualifications, experience)
  - Undertaking that EC organised and operates per GCP and laws
  - Documented approval/favourable opinion before initiation
  - Copy of recommendations if approval conditional
  - EC documents relating to re-evaluations/re-approvals, withdrawals or suspensions

#### 3.1.8 Information on Investigational Products

- Provide Investigator's Brochure with available information on product standardization and other relevant data
- Bring any relevant new information to attention of Investigator(s) and EC

#### 3.1.9 Supply, Storage and Handling of Pharmaceutical Products

- Responsible for supplying Investigational Products, Comparators, Placebo
- Products manufactured per GMPs; suitably packaged; investigational labelling
- Determine acceptable storage conditions; communicate in writing
- Document procedures and responsibilities for receipt, handling, storage, dispensing, retrieval, return
- Maintain records of quantities with batch numbers
- Maintain sufficient samples from each batch

#### 3.1.10 Safety Information

- Responsible for ongoing safety evaluation
- Promptly notify all concerned of findings that could adversely affect safety, impact conduct, alter EC approval

#### 3.1.11 Adverse Drug Reaction Reporting

- Provide ADR/AE reporting forms
- Expedite reporting to all concerned (including EC and regulatory authorities) of all serious and/or unexpected ADRs

#### 3.1.12 Study Reports

- Ensure preparation and approval of comprehensive final clinical study report
- Submit safety updates and periodic reports as prescribed

#### 3.1.13 Monitoring

- Ensure studies adequately monitored
- Appoint adequately trained monitors or CRO

#### 3.1.14 Audit

- Perform audit as part of QA system
- Independent and separate from routine monitoring/QC
- Evaluate study conduct and compliance with protocol, SOPs, GCPs, regulatory requirements
- Auditors independent of parties involved; qualifications documented
- Document observations; archive; available to Regulatory Authorities
- Prompt action if non-compliance; terminate defaulting party's participation if serious/persistent; notify regulatory authority

#### 3.1.15 Multicentre Studies

- Special administrative arrangements
- Tasks: commencement, overall performance, data supervision, monitoring ADRs/AEs, policy matters
- Functions, responsibilities, mandate of special committee(s)/person(s) described in protocol
- Coordinating committee/coordinator for control of practical performance and progress
- Studies ideally begin and end simultaneously at all institutions
- Facilitate communication between investigators
- Training to follow same protocol and systems
- Written acceptance of protocol and annexes from each investigator and institution
- CRFs designed to record required data at all sites; supplemental CRFs for additional data

#### 3.1.16 Premature Termination or Suspension of a Study

- Notify investigator(s), institution(s), ethics committee, regulatory authorities
- Document reason(s) for termination/suspension

### 3.2 The Monitor

- Principal communication link between sponsor and investigator

#### 3.2.1 Qualifications

- Medical, pharmaceutical, and/or scientific qualifications
- Adequate ASU-related clinical trial experience/training
- Fully aware of all aspects of product and protocol

#### 3.2.2 Responsibilities

- Oversee progress of study; ensure compliance with protocol, GCPs, ethical and regulatory requirements
- Verify investigator qualifications, expertise, resources
- Confirm institutional facilities adequate
- Verify:
  - Investigational products sufficiently available and stored properly
  - Supplied only to eligible subjects at specified dose/time
  - Subjects provided necessary instructions
  - Receipt, use, return, disposal controlled and documented
  - Investigator receives current IB and supplies
  - Investigator follows protocol
  - Investigator maintains essential documents
  - All parties informed and follow GCP and SOPs
  - No delegation to unauthorised individuals
- Promptly inform sponsor and EC of unwarranted deviations or transgressions
- Follow written SOPs
- Written record of visits, phone calls, correspondence
- Assess institution(s) prior to study
- Observe and report subject recruitment rate
- Visit investigator before, during, after study
- Ensure staff informed and comply
- Assist investigator in reporting data and results
- Ensure CRFs correctly filled, legible, complete, dated
- Verify:
  - Data required by protocol reported accurately and consistent with source documents
  - Dose/therapy modifications documented
  - AEs, concomitant medications, inter-current illnesses promptly reported
  - Missed visits, tests, examinations reported
  - Withdrawals and drop-outs reported and explained
- Clarify deviations, errors, omissions; take actions to prevent recurrence
- Ensure investigator certifies accuracy of CRF
- Submit written report to sponsor after each site visit and correspondence
- Confirm procedures for storage, handling, dispensing, return of investigational product followed and documented

### 3.3 Investigator

#### 3.3.1 Qualifications

- Qualified by education, training, experience
- Qualifications per CCIM/MCI/DCI
- Principal Investigator/Chief Investigator from respective ASU system
- Provide CV and other documents
- Understand time and resource demands
- Ensure other studies do not divert essential subjects or facilities
- Thoroughly familiar with safety, efficacy, appropriate use of investigational product
- Aware of and comply with GCPs, SOPs, regulatory requirements

#### 3.3.2 Medical Care of the Study Subjects

- Qualified Medical Practitioner (Investigator or Co-Investigator) responsible for all study-related medical decisions
- Ensure adequate medical care for AEs including clinically significant laboratory values
- Inform subject when medical care needed for inter-current illness
- Inform subject's other attending physician(s) about participation if subject agrees
- Ensure medical care and follow-up after study completion or drop-out
- Make reasonable effort to ascertain reason(s) for withdrawal while respecting rights

#### 3.3.3 Monitoring and Auditing of Records

- Allow monitoring and auditing by sponsor, EC, CRO, authorised representatives, regulatory authority
- Maintain list of qualified persons delegated study-related duties
- Ensure all persons involved adequately informed about protocol, SOPs, investigational product(s), duties

#### 3.3.4 Communication with Ethics Committee

- Before initiating study, ensure proposed study reviewed and accepted in writing by relevant EC
- Promptly report to EC, monitor, sponsor:
  1. Deviations from or changes of protocol to eliminate immediate hazards
  2. Changes increasing risk to subjects and/or affecting conduct
  3. All ADRs and AEs that are serious and/or unexpected
  4. New information that may adversely affect safety or conduct
  5. For reported deaths, additional information (autopsy reports, terminal medical reports)

#### 3.3.5 Compliance with the Protocol

- Agree and sign protocol and/or legally acceptable document with sponsor
- Confirm in writing read and understood protocol, GCPs, SOPs, will work as stipulated
- May implement deviation to eliminate immediate hazard(s) without prior EC approval
- Submit implemented deviation, reasons, proposed amendments to EC, sponsor, regulatory authority
- Document and explain any deviation
- Follow randomisation procedure; ensure code broken only per protocol
- Document and explain any premature un-blinding

#### 3.3.6 Investigational Product(s)

- Primary responsibility for accountability at study site(s)
- Maintain records of delivery, inventory, use by each subject, return/disposal
- Records include dates, quantities, batch/serial numbers, expiry dates, unique code numbers
- Ensure products stored under specified conditions, used only per approved protocol
- May assign duties to subordinate under supervision
- Explain correct use to each subject; check at intervals; document periodic checks

#### 3.3.7 Selection and Recruitment of Study Subjects

- Ensure unbiased selection of adequate number of suitable subjects per protocol
- Secure cooperation of other physicians if needed
- Assess probability of adequate recruitment rate
- Keep confidential list of names of all subjects allocated to each study
- Maintain Subjects' screening log and enrolment log
- Give adequate information to subjects per GCP
- Obligations regarding informed consent:
  1. Communicate all necessary information
  2. Exclude unjustified deception, undue influence, intimidation
  3. Seek consent only after adequately informed
  4. Obtain signed form as evidence of informed consent
  5. Renew informed consent if material changes or new information
  6. Not use intimidation; assure decision won't affect relationship or benefits
- Supply subjects with information about participation and contact persons

#### 3.3.8 Records/Reports

- Ensure accuracy, completeness, legibility, timeliness of data in CRFs
- Data derived from source documents consistent with source documents or discrepancies explained
- Any change or correction to CRF dated, signed, explained; not obscure original entry
- Sponsor provides guidelines for corrections; written procedures
- Investigator retains records of changes and corrections

**Progress Reports:**

- Submit written summaries of study status at periodicity specified in protocol to EC and sponsor
- Identify subjects by unique code numbers

**Termination and Final Report:**

- If premature termination/suspension, promptly inform subjects, EC, Regulatory Authorities
- Ensure appropriate therapy and follow-up
- Maintain documents per essential documents list
- Study closed when Investigator (or Monitor/CRO) reviewed files and confirmed all necessary documents in appropriate files
- Completion informed to institution, sponsor, EC
- Investigator signs and forwards data (CRFs, results, interpretations, analyses, reports) to sponsor and EC
- Collaborative investigators and those responsible for analyses sign relevant portions
- Submit signed and dated final report to institution, EC, sponsor
- In multi-centre study, signature of coordinating investigator may suffice if agreed in protocol
- If investigator is sponsor, assumes responsibilities of both

### 3.4 Data Safety Management

- Data Safety Management Board may be established
- Carefully monitor data and side effects during study
- Prompt reporting of adverse events
- Data reviewed at regular intervals
- Research team reports immediately to Investigator(s) and Data Monitoring Board regarding life-threatening conditions
- Protocols written and approved for treatment of study-related AEs
- Immediate transport facility to nearby hospital for medical referral

## 4. Record Keeping and Data Handling

### 4.1 Documentation

- All steps in data management documented for retrospective assessment
- Documentation SOPs include checklists, forms with details of actions, dates, individuals responsible

### 4.2 Corrections

- Corrections in CRFs or other documents made without obscuring original entry
- Correct data inserted with reason if not obvious
- Corrections carry date and initials of Investigator or authorised person

### 4.3 Electronic Data Processing

- Only authorised persons enter or modify data
- Recorded trail of changes and deletions
- Security system to prevent unauthorised access
- Alterations documented; system validated
- Data changes documented; no deletion once entered
- List of authorised persons maintained
- Adequate backup maintained

### 4.4 Validation of Electronic Data Processing Systems

- If trial data entered directly into computer, adequate safeguard for validation including signed and dated printout and backup records
- Computerised systems (hardware and software) validated; detailed description produced and kept up-to-date

### 4.5 Language

- All written documents, information, material in language clearly understood by all concerned

### 4.6 Responsibilities of the Investigator

- Ensure observations and findings recorded correctly and completely in CRFs, signed by responsible person(s)
- Laboratory values with normal reference ranges recorded on CRF or enclosed
- Values outside clinically accepted reference range or differing importantly from previous values evaluated and commented upon
- Data other than requested by protocol clearly marked as additional findings and significance described
- Units of measurement stated; transformation of units indicated and documented
- Medical records indicate individual participating in clinical trial

### 4.7 Responsibilities of the Sponsor and the Monitor

- Sponsor ensures electronic data processing system conforms to documented requirements for completeness, accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance (validation)
- Maintain SOPs for using systems
- Monitor takes adequate measures to ensure no data overlooked
- If computer system automatically assigns missing values, documented clearly
- Sponsor safeguards blinding during data entry and processing
- Use explicit Subject identification code
- Ownership of data and transfer documented and intimated to concerned party(ies)

## 5. Quality Assurance

- Sponsor responsible for implementing QA system
- Ensure study performed in GLP-compliant laboratories
- Data generated, recorded, reported in compliance with Protocol, GCP, other applicable requirements
- Documented SOPs prerequisite for QA
- All observations and findings verifiable
- Verification processes specified and justified
- Statistically controlled sampling may be acceptable
- Quality control applied to each stage of data handling
- Sponsor's audits conducted by persons independent of those responsible for study
- Investigational sites, facilities, all data and documentation available for inspection and audit by Sponsor's auditor and Regulatory Authority(ies)

## 6. Statistics

### 6.1 Role of a Biostatistician

- Appropriately qualified and experienced statistician involved in planning stage and throughout study
- Make statistical model to help Sponsor, CRO, and/or Investigator in writing Protocol
- Number of subjects determined in relation to statistical model

### 6.2 Study Design

- Scientific integrity and credibility depend on design
- In comparative studies, Protocol should describe:
  1. "A priori" rationale for target difference, power to detect
  2. Measures to avoid bias, particularly randomisation
  3. In observational studies, proper care for unbiased reporting

#### 6.2.1 Randomisation and Blinding

- Key idea: compare groups differing only in treatment
- Randomisation deals with possible bias at treatment allocation
- Ensures allocation independent of characteristics
- Statistical methods based on random samples from populations
- Protocol must state method used for Randomisation
- Use maximum degree of blindness possible
- Where sealed code assigned, kept at site and with sponsor
- Protocol must state conditions for breaking code and by whom
- System allows access to only one subject's treatment at a time
- Coding system includes mechanism for rapid identification in medical emergency but prevents undetectable breaks

### 6.3 Statistical Analysis

- Types of Statistical Analyses clearly identified; form basis of statistical model
- Any subsequent deviations described and justified in Final Report
- Need and extent of interim analysis specified in Protocol
- Results presented to facilitate interpretation of clinical importance (estimates of magnitude, confidence intervals)
- Missing, unused, spurious data accounted for; omissions documented

## 7. Special Concerns

### 7.1 Clinical Trials of Contraceptives

- All procedures for clinical trials applicable
- Subjects clearly informed about alternatives
- In women with implant, proper follow-up for removal whether trial over or subject withdrawn
- Children borne due to contraceptive failure followed up for abnormalities if woman does not opt for MTP

### 7.2 Clinical Trials with Panchakarma / Para-surgical Procedures / Medical Devices in ASU Medicine / Other Traditional Medicine

- Panchakarma including Snehana, Swedana, Dhara, Pizhichil and para-surgical procedures like Ksharasutra, Leach therapy, Agni karma, Hajamat, Hammam, Nutul Dalk, Riazat, Prachanna, Rakta mokshana, Tarpana, Vidalaka, Verm etc.
- Proper documentation of end point, procedure, standardization of drugs, parameters of evaluation, statistical considerations
- Informed consent procedures as in drug trials
- Patient information sheet contains information on procedure if patient wishes to opt out
- ASU Medical Devices: Proven quality and safety, intended for internal/external use or diagnosis, treatment, mitigation, prevention of disease, as specified by Central Government through Gazette notification

### Appendix II: General Ethical Issues (ICMR Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical Research on Human Participants, 2006)

**I. Informed Consent Process**

- Informed Consent of Participants: For all biomedical research involving human participants, investigator must obtain informed consent of prospective participant or legal guardian
- Adequate information about research given in simple, easily understandable unambiguous language in Informed Consent Form with Participant/Patient Information Sheet
- Components:
  1. Nature and purpose of study stating it as research
  2. Duration of participation with number of participants
  3. Procedures to be followed
  4. Investigations, if any
  5. Foreseeable risks and discomforts
  6. Benefits to participant, community or medical profession
  7. Policy on compensation
  8. Availability of medical treatment for injuries or risk management
  9. Alternative treatments if available
  10. Steps for ensuring confidentiality
  11. No loss of benefits on withdrawal
  12. Benefit sharing in event of commercialisation
  13. Contact details of PI or local PI/Co-PI
  14. Contact details of Chairman of IEC for appeal
  15. Voluntary participation
  16. If test for genetics and HIV, counseling for consent as per national guidelines
  17. Storage period of biological sample and related data with choice offered to participant
- Copy of participant/patient information sheet given to participant
- Informed consent brief in content highlighting free will, withdrawal without loss of benefits, confidentiality, investigations/interventions only after consent
- When written consent not possible, verbal consent after documentation by unrelated witness; ombudsman; audio-visual methods with prior consent and EC approval
- For drug trials, if volunteer gives thumb impression, another thumb impression by relative/legal custodian not accepted; unrelated witness signs
- Fresh or re-consent in conditions: new information, regaining consciousness, long-term follow-up/study extension, change in treatment modality/procedures/site visits, before publication if possibility of disclosure of identity
- Waiver of consent: if research involves not more than minimal risk, or participant and researcher do not come into contact, or emergency situations; if protections for privacy and confidentiality, and do not violate rights
- Instances: impractical to conduct research due to confidentiality; research on publicly available information; research on anonymised biological samples; emergency situations
- Obligations of investigators regarding informed consent
- Essential information for prospective research participants

**II. Compensation for Participation**

- Participants may be paid for inconvenience and time, reimbursed for expenses, receive free medical services
- Payments not so large or services so extensive as to induce participation
- All payments, reimbursement, medical services approved by IEC
- Care for guardian, withdrawal for medical reasons, withdrawal for other reasons

**III. Conflict of Interest**

- Set of conditions where professional judgment concerning primary interest unduly influenced by secondary interest
- Academic institutions in alliance with industries require strong review
- Significant financial interest means anything of monetary value
- Investigators declare conflicts of interest in application to IEC
- Institutions and IECs need self-regulatory processes
- Prospective participants informed of sponsorship
- Undue inducement through compensation prohibited
- Undue compensation includes assistance to related persons, insurance for unrelated conditions, free transportation, free hot meals, freedom for prisoners, free medication not generally available, academic credits, disproportionate compensation
- IEC examines on case-by-case basis

**IV. Selection of Special Groups as Research Participants**

i. Pregnant or nursing women
ii. Children
iii. Vulnerable groups

**V. Essential Information on Confidentiality for Prospective Research Participants**

- Safeguarding confidentiality
- Data disclosed only in court of law, threat to life, severe adverse reaction to drug registration authority, risk to public health

**VI. Compensation for Accidental Injury**

- Subjects suffering physical injury entitled to financial or other assistance
- In case of death, dependents entitled to material compensation
- Obligation of sponsor to pay
- Arbitration committee or appellate authority
