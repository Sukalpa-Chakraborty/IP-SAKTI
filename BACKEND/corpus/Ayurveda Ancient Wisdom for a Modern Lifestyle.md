## Chunk AY-AW-001 — What Is Ayurveda?

**Source pages:** 3–4  
**Topics:** definition, holistic approach, individualized wellness, balance, diet and lifestyle

**Summary:**  
Ayurveda is a 5,000-year-old science of life that treats the whole individual, not just symptoms. It emphasizes living in harmony with nature’s cycles and rhythms. Health is a holistic balance of physical, emotional, and spiritual makeup. The famous saying: “With proper diet and lifestyle, there is no need for medicine; without proper diet and lifestyle, there is no use for medicine.”

**Key points:**
- Ayurveda means “science of life.”
- Treats the whole individual, not just symptoms.
- Individualized approach based on constitutional makeup.
- Health shifts with seasons and time of day.
- Balance encompasses physical, emotional, spiritual, and social.
- Diet and lifestyle are primary.

**Notable quotes:**
> “With proper diet and lifestyle, there is no need for medicine; without proper diet and lifestyle, there is no use for medicine.”

> “Ayurveda treats the whole individual—not just symptoms of an illness.”

## Chunk AY-AW-002 — The Five Mahabhutas (Elements)

**Source pages:** 4–5  
**Topics:** Space, Air, Fire, Water, Earth, senses, qualities, balance

**Summary:**  
All matter, including the human body, is composed of five elements (mahabhutas): Space (akash), Air (vayu), Fire (tejas), Water (aap), and Earth (prithvi). Each element has specific qualities and is associated with a sense organ. When the body is out of balance, opposite qualities are used to restore equilibrium. The elements combine to create personal mind/body constitutions.

**Key points:**
- Space: empty, formless, weightless; sense: hearing; organ: ear.
- Air: dry, cold, light, clear, moving; sense: touch; organ: skin.
- Fire: hot, changeable, dry, illuminating; sense: sight; organ: eyes.
- Water: flowing, wet, heavy, cold, cohesive; sense: taste; organ: tongue.
- Earth: heavy, cold, dense, stable, solid; sense: smell; organ: nose.
- Panchamahabhutas are fundamental to Ayurvedic diagnosis and treatment.

**Notable quotes:**
> “When the body is out of balance, mahabhutas with opposite qualities are used to restore equilibrium.”

## Chunk AY-AW-003 — Getting to Know the Three Doshas

**Source pages:** 6–7  
**Topics:** Vata, Pitta, Kapha, elements, biological energies, constitution

**Summary:**  
The three doshas—Vata, Pitta, and Kapha—are biological energies that govern physiology, psychology, and subtle energy. They derive from the five mahabhutas: Vata = Air + Space; Pitta = Fire + Water; Kapha = Water + Earth. We are born with a unique proportion of doshas; typically one predominant and one secondary. When birth constitution (prakriti) and current state (vikriti) align, health is optimal.

**Key points:**
- Vata: Air + Space; governs movement and communication.
- Pitta: Fire + Water; governs metabolism and transformation.
- Kapha: Water + Earth; governs structure and cohesion.
- Everyone has all three doshas; one or two usually predominate.
- Tridoshic (equal amounts) is rare.
- Divergence between prakriti and vikriti indicates imbalance.

**Notable quotes:**
> “The doshas are essentially our guides for choosing foods, activities, and lifestyle habits that create and maintain balance.”

## Chunk AY-AW-007 — Finding Your Prakriti & Vikriti Constitutions

**Source pages:** 14–15  
**Topics:** prakriti, vikriti, seven prakritis, balance, imbalance, uncovering true constitution

**Summary:**  
Prakriti is one’s lifelong constitution determined at conception. Vikriti is current dosha imbalance. Seven prakritis: Vata, Pitta, Kapha, Vata-Pitta, Pitta-Kapha, Vata-Kapha, Vata-Pitta-Kapha. When vikriti matches prakriti, health is optimal. Imbalance can mask true prakriti; detoxification (clearing ama) can reveal it.

**Key points:**
- Prakriti = birth constitution; Vikriti = current state.
- Seven types: three single, three dual, one tridoshic.
- Most people are single or dual.
- Health = alignment of prakriti and vikriti.
- Imbalance causes “not feeling like myself.”
- Treatment: remove cause or introduce opposite quality.
- Long-term imbalance can mask true prakriti.

**Notable quotes:**
> “When the vikriti doesn't match the prakriti, a person often feels like something's ‘off’.”

Digestion metabolizes food into seven dhatus (tissues) and three malas (wastes). Dhatus: rasa (plasma), rakta (blood), mamsa (muscles), meda (fat), asthi (bone/cartilage), majja (marrow), shukra (reproductive tissue). Malas: urine, feces, sweat. Proper digestion replenishes dhatus; impaired digestion leaves ama (toxic residue).

**Seven Dhatus:**

| Dhatu | Function |
|---|---|
| Rasa (plasma) | Carries nutrients from digested food |
| Rakta (blood) | Oxygenates body, essential to life |
| Mamsa (muscles) | Covers bones, allows movement |
| Meda (fat) | Insulation, lubrication |
| Asthi (bone/cartilage) | Gives form |
| Majja (marrow) | Fills bone, adds life |
| Shukra (reproductive tissue) | Creates life |

**Three Malas:**

| Mala | Function |
|---|---|
| Urine | Removes water, salt, mineral wastes; balances fluids |

## Chunk AY-AW-009 — Digestion: Agni, Ama, and Ojas

**Source pages:** 18–23  
**Topics:** agni, catabolism, anabolism, ama, ojas, digestion tips

**Summary:**  
Agni is the digestive fire that transforms food into tissues and energy. It has catabolic and anabolic aspects. Weak agni leads to ama (undigested residue) which clogs tissues and causes disease. Ojas is the end product of perfect digestion: radiant health, strong immunity, and balanced mind/body/spirit. Tips to improve agni and signs of ojas are provided.

**Key points:**
- Agni = digestive fire; embodied in Pitta.
- Catabolism = breakdown; Anabolism = build-up.
- Weak agni causes ama.
- Ama symptoms: stagnation, fatigue, coated tongue, aches, heaviness, poor mental clarity, indigestion.
- Ojas = perfect digestion; signs: rested awakening, glowing skin, pink tongue, light body, centered, strong digestion, energized, clear mind, pleasant smell, rarely sick.
- 7 tips to improve agni (e.g., warm lemon water, avoid cold drinks, lunch largest meal, light dinner, warm cooked foods, digestion-aiding spices, daily exercise).
- 3 steps to address ama: Prevent, Reduce, Treat (panchakarma).

**Notable quotes:**
> “Life span, health, immunity, strength, energy, positivity, creativity, emotional balance, muscle formation, a lustrous complexion with good skin tone, homeostasis, and vital breath—all these depend on our ‘body-fire’ or agni.” — Charaka Samhita

> “You have to be careful about what you eat, or your food will begin to eat you!”

## Chunk AY-AW-012 — Daily and Seasonal Routines: The Science of Chronobiology

**Source pages:** 31–36  
**Topics:** circadian rhythm, dosha times, dinacharya, seasonal balance, daily routine tips

**Summary:**  
Living in harmony with nature’s rhythms (circadian, seasonal, lunar, tidal, celestial) is crucial. Each dosha dominates specific times: Vata 2–6 am/pm; Pitta 10 am–2 pm and 10 pm–2 am; Kapha 6–10 am/pm. A dinacharya (daily routine) is prescribed. Seasonal changes require dietary and lifestyle adjustments. 10 tips for an ideal daily routine and 10 tips for seasonal balance are provided.

**Dosha Times:**
- Vata: 2–6 am, 2–6 pm
- Pitta: 10 am–2 pm, 10 pm–2 am
- Kapha: 6–10 am, 6–10 pm

**10 Tips for an Ideal Daily Routine (Dinacharya):**
1. Wake up early (sunrise or before).
2. Practice daily hygiene (tongue scraping, oil pulling, nose drops, warm lemon water, bowel movement, self-massage, shower).
3. Exercise or do yoga in the morning.
4. Eat breakfast between 7:00–7:30 am (light).
5. Go to work/school with awareness.
6. Eat lunch between 11 am–1 pm (largest meal).
7. End the day with a walk in nature.
8. Eat dinner between 6–7 pm (light to moderate).
9. Before-bed routine (spiritual reading, warm milk, meditation).
10. Go to sleep at the same time (~10:30 pm) each night.

**10 Tips for Seasonal Balance:**
1. Eat fresh, alive, vibrant foods.
2. Eat small portions, chew well.
3. Drink plenty of water (1 oz per 2 lbs body weight).
4. Meditate daily for 20 minutes.
5. 30 minutes brisk physical exercise daily.
6. Go to bed early, wake with the sun.
7. Minimize compulsive habits (smoking, alcohol, drugs).
8. Do something good daily.
9. Treat your body like a temple.
10. Do cleanses twice a year (spring and fall).
