## Chunk AY-CVD-001 — Editorial: Bioactives and Traditional Herbal Medicine

**Source pages:** 7–10  
**Authors:** Joen-Rong Sheu, Pitchairaj Geraldine, Mao-Hsiung Yen  
**Topics:** cardiovascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, herbal medicine, special issue overview

**Summary:**  
Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are the major cause of morbidity and mortality in developed countries, with developing countries rapidly catching up. The underlying pathology is atheromatous vascular disease, leading to coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease, heart failure, and cardiac arrhythmias. There is growing awareness of the role of dietary factors and herbal medicines in CVD prevention and treatment. This special issue presents research on bioactives and traditional herbal medicine for CVD/cerebrovascular diseases, covering neuroprotective effects of Chinese herbal medicine, antiatherosclerotic effects of Guanxinkang decoction, anti-inflammatory effects of Sini Tang, antiplatelet activity of Morus alba, and Ayurveda for cardiac autonomic dysfunction in ischemic stroke.

**Key points:**
- CVDs are leading cause of morbidity/mortality worldwide.
- Underlying pathology: atheromatous vascular disease.
- Growing awareness of dietary factors and herbal medicines in CVD prevention.
- Special issue covers neuroprotection, antiatherosclerosis, anti-inflammation, antiplatelet activity, and Ayurveda.
- Chinese herbal formulas studied: Sini Tang, Guanxinkang, QSYQ, Qingkailing, Moschus + Borneolum.
- Natural compounds studied: hinokitiol, andrographolide, evodiamine, geniposide, Morus alba extract, Piper betle extract, eugenol.

**Notable quotes:**
> “There is growing awareness of the role of dietary factors and herbal medicines in the prevention of CVD and the possibility of their use in treatment.”

> “In spite of the major advances of neuroprotective therapeutic approaches for treating ischemic stroke over the last decade, stroke is still a serious problem for which effective drug therapy is not yet available.”

## Chunk AY-CVD-002 — Ayurveda Treatment for Cardiac Autonomic Dysfunction in Ischemic Stroke

**Source pages:** 11–18  
**Authors:** Sriramjini Sitaram Jaideep, Dindagur Nagaraja, Pramod Kumar Pal, D. Sudhakara, Sathyaprabha N. Talakad  
**Topics:** Ayurveda, ischemic stroke, pakshaghata, heart rate variability, baroreflex sensitivity, panchakarma

**Summary:**  
This study evaluated the effect of Ayurveda therapies on cardiac autonomic dysfunction in ischemic stroke. Fifty patients with ischemic stroke (MCA territory) were randomized to receive physiotherapy (Group I) or Ayurveda treatment (Group II) for 14 days. Ayurveda treatment included Abhyanga (massage) with Niramisha mahamasha taila, Bhaspa svedana (steam therapy), Matra basti with Balaswagandhadi taila, and internal medications (Ashtavarga kashaya, Ksheerabala 101). Results: Group II showed statistically significant improvement in cardiac autonomic parameters — SDNN, total power, LF power significantly enhanced; baroreflex sensitivity increased. This is the first study reporting positive modulation of cardiac autonomic activity after adjuvant Ayurveda treatment in ischemic stroke.

**Key points:**
- Stroke leads to autonomic nervous system dysfunction.
- HRV, BPV, and BRS are markers of cardiac dysregulation.
- Ayurveda describes stroke as pakshaghata (hemiplegia) due to vata dosha aberration.
- Treatment: Abhyanga (massage), Svedana (sudation), Basti (enema), internal medications.
- Group II showed significant improvement in SDNN, TP, LF power, and BRS.
- First study to report positive modulation of cardiac autonomic activity after Ayurveda treatment in ischemic stroke.

**Medicines used:**
- Ashtavarga kashaya: Bala, Sahachara, Eranda, Shunti, Rasna, Suradruma, Sinduvara, Lasuna
- Ksheerabala 101: Balamoola, Tila taila, Goksheera, Balamoola kalka
- Balaswagandhadi thailam: Ashwagandha, Bala, Tila taila, Lodhra, etc.
- Niramisha mahamasha taila: Masha, Bilva, Agnimantha, Shyonaka, etc.

**Notable quotes:**
> “The current study is the first to report a positive modulation of cardiac autonomic activity after adjuvant Ayurveda treatment in ischemic stroke.”

> “Most of the herbs used as internal medication in the current study have been studied for their antioxidant and neuroprotective activity.”

## Chunk AY-CVD-003 — Guanxinkang Decoction: Antiatherosclerotic Effect via Inhibiting Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress

**Source pages:** 19–26  
**Authors:** Hao Wang, Zhi-Min Zheng, Bu-Lang Gao  
**Topics:** Guanxinkang, atherosclerosis, endoplasmic reticulum stress, HUVEC, homocysteine

**Summary:**  
Guanxinkang (GXK) decoction is a compound traditional Chinese herbal medicine composed of Radix Astragali, Trichosanthes, Radix Salviae Miltiorrhizae, Allium macrostemon, Pinellia tuberifera, and Radix Puerariae. This study investigated its antiatherosclerotic effect on apoptosis, mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP), and endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) pretreated with homocysteinemia (HCY). Results: GXK can antagonize HCY-induced injury of HUVEC; the effect increases with concentration and treatment duration; possible mechanism is through inhibiting ERS caused by HCY.

**Key points:**
- Atherosclerosis is a chronic inflammatory condition.
- Hyperhomocysteinemia is an independent risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.
- GXK decoction has antiatherosclerotic effect.
- GXK decreased apoptosis rate and GRP78 expression, increased MMP.
- Mechanism: inhibiting endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS).
- GRP78 is a biomarker of ERS.

**Notable quotes:**
> “GXK can antagonize the injury of HUVEC caused by HCY and the antagonism effect increases with the concentration and treatment duration of GXK.”

> “GXK exerts its effects by inhibiting ERS and subsequent signal transmission of apoptosis.”

## Chunk AY-CVD-008 — Chinese Herbal Medicine for Aspirin Resistance: Systematic Review

**Source pages:** 68–83  
**Authors:** Ai-ju Liu, Hui-qin Li, Ji-huang Li, Yuan-yuan Wang, Dong Chen, Yan Wang, Guo-qing Zheng  
**Topics:** aspirin resistance, Chinese herbal medicine, systematic review, Tongxinluo, Danshen, randomized controlled trials

**Summary:**  
This systematic review assessed efficacy and safety of Chinese herbal medicine (CHM) for aspirin resistance (AR). Sixteen RCTs with 1011 subjects were identified. Tongxinluo capsule and Danshen-based prescriptions were most frequently used. Despite apparent reported positive findings, it is premature to determine efficacy and safety of CHM for AR due to poor methodological quality and insufficient safety data. CHMs appeared well tolerated. Large sample-size, well-designed rigorous RCTs are needed.

**Key points:**
- AR is prevalent (mean 25% in secondary prevention).
- Current interventions for AR are insufficient.
- CHM has multitarget intervention effects on AR pathophysiology.
- Tongxinluo capsule and Danshen-based prescriptions most frequent.
- Danshen root, milkvetch root, Leech, Rosewood most frequent single herbs.
- CHMs well tolerated in all included studies.
- Poor methodological quality of included trials.
- Need for large, rigorous RCTs.

**Mechanism of CHM for AR:**
- Inhibiting TXA2 synthesis
- Inhibiting COX-1 expression
- Blocking TXA2 receptor
- Inhibiting platelet aggregation

**Notable quotes:**
> “Despite the apparent reported positive findings, it is premature to determine the efficacy and safety of CHM for the treatment of AR due to poor methodological quality and insufficient safety data.”

> “CHM as a promising candidate is worthy of improvement and development for further clinical AR trials.”

**Summary:**  
Morus alba L. leaves (MAE) have been used in folk medicine for beriberi, edema, diabetes, hypertension, and atherosclerosis. This study investigated whether MAE affects platelet aggregation and thrombosis. Results: MAE dose-dependently inhibited collagen-induced platelet aggregation, attenuated serotonin secretion and TXA2 formation, and in vivo significantly attenuated thrombus formation in rat arterio-venous shunt model. - MAE reduced fibrinogen binding to integrin αIIbβ3.
- MAE decreased thrombus weight in AV-shunt model by 52.3%, 28.3%, 19.1% at 400, 200, 100 mg/kg.
- MAE could be a candidate for antiplatelet and antithrombotic agent.

**Notable quotes:**
> “MAE inhibit platelet activation, TXB2 formation, serotonin secretion, aggregation, and thrombus formation.”

## Chunk AY-CVD-017 — Hinokitiol Offers Neuroprotection from Thromboembolic Stroke

**Source pages:** 170–177  
**Authors:** Thanasekaran Jayakumar, Wen-Hsien Hsu, Ting-Lin Yen, Jun-Yun Luo, Yu-Cheng Kuo, Tsong-Harn Fong, Joen-Rong Sheu  
**Topics:** hinokitiol, β-thujaplicin, thromboembolic stroke, MCAO, neuroprotection, HIF-1α, iNOS, TNF-α, caspase-3

**Summary:**  
Hinokitiol (β-thujaplicin), a tropolone-related compound from heartwood of cupressaceous plants, was investigated for neuroprotective effect against MCAO-induced thromboembolic stroke in rats. Results: Hinokitiol (0.2 and 0.5 mg/kg) dose-dependently attenuated cerebral ischemia, improved neurobehavioral deficits, reduced infarct size, and inhibited expressions of HIF-1α, iNOS, TNF-α, and active caspase-3. Hinokitiol possesses potent neuroprotective activity via inhibition of inflammatory responses and apoptosis.

**Key points:**
- Stroke is third leading cause of death worldwide.
- rt-PA has narrow therapeutic window and risks.
- Hinokitiol is iron-chelating compound with anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antifungal, antioxidant, antitumor activities.
- MCAO-induced thromboembolic stroke model in rats.
- Hinokitiol improved neurological deficit scores.
- Hinokitiol reduced infarct volume dose-dependently.
- Hinokitiol inhibited HIF-1α and iNOS expression.
- Hinokitiol inhibited TNF-α and active caspase-3 expression.
- Mechanism: inhibition of inflammatory responses and apoptosis.

**Notable quotes:**
> “Hinokitiol possesses potent neuroprotective activity. This activity is mediated, at least in part, by inhibition of inflammatory responses (i.e., HIF-1α, iNOS expression) and apoptosis (i.e., TNF-α, active caspase-3).”

> “Hinokitiol offers a stupendous therapeutic window for the prevention of ischemic brain injury.”
