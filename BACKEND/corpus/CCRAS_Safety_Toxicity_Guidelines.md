## Prologue

R&D in the field of AYUSH system in different areas such as drug development including quality assurance, pre-clinical safety evaluation and clinical research are being conducted at different levels such as Research Council under AYUSH, Academic institutions (both AYUSH and non AYUSH institutes such as Medical Colleges, Universities etc.), other Research organization such as ICMR, CSIR etc. Further, research support is also being extended through grant under EMR vide Ministry of AYUSH, DST, DBT, ICMR etc. in the area of traditional medicine.

Lot of research is being conducted at different levels as above in the field of AYUSH adopting different guidelines, methods and protocols and ending up research outcomes with low or poor translational value. Only few of them have led to clinical trial and marketing level. This may be attributed to lack of awareness regarding AYUSH strategies for R&D and provisions of Drug & Cosmetic Act related to AYUSH.

In spite of availability of several guidelines such as GCP guidelines for ASU drugs, ICMR guidelines for bio medical research for human participants, GCP guidelines published by CDSCO Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, WHO guidelines for traditional medical research etc., there is no single comprehensive guideline to conduct research in AYUSH sector is available.

This might be one among the major reasons that has led to R&D in AYUSH sector with diverse approaches with low translational value.

In view of this, it becomes imperative to develop directives on research practices for various components of AYUSH research sectors for uniform adoption across all stake holders such as research councils, academic institutes, funding agencies engaged in AYUSH research.

CCRAS developed three comprehensive and concise Guidelines focusing on drug development (Standardization and quality assurance), safety/toxicity and clinical evaluation for ready reference of stakeholders. These Guidelines encompassed research practices generally to be adopted and followed by researchers in the field of AYUSH system such as research organizations, academic institutions and researchers seeking grant from EMR/IMR schemes of different funding agencies for research on AYUSH system, and would help the researchers while designing and formulating the proposals and also planning academic/industrial research in the field of AYUSH systems. The users may refer to other two documents for having an overall idea concerning drug development and R & D in this field.

## Background

Quests for healthy and long life are perhaps as old as human existence and efforts are unremitting to address the challenges and triumph over the bottlenecks across this journey. Ayurveda - the science of life, evolved as a comprehensive system of healthcare systematically through scientific experimentations of high order backed by sound and reproducible evidence base and stood the test of the time. Several strategies and road maps are being drawn to carry forward merits of this science so as to meet the current day health needs and mainstream its core strengths alongside through research & development in this country and across the globe. The fundamental aspects of holistic systems needs adequate positioning, while designing clinical trials to examine the safety and efficacy of Ayurveda. Furthermore, the other challenges and issues related to quality, safety, dosage forms/delivery system, diverse concepts and complex approaches in trial design, diagnosis and therapy, outcomes of clinical efficacy and drug interactions also pose certain limitations in research. A systems approach may be adopted to validate the therapies with integration of principles of Ayurveda and bio-medicine without losing the vital fundamentals of both systems. Such an approach with well designed research plans could possibly facilitate to generate tangible evidence.

The roots of experimentation for ensuring safety of food and drugs have been in vogue since ancient times. There are evidences that Ayurveda is well versed with the preclinical testing i.e. Vishanna/Virudhanna Pareeksha conducted on animals. Sushruta mentioned the animal experiments like testing the food/drug by giving it to the animal like birds (pigeon, peacock), animals (rabbit, monkey) in order to establish the safety and toxicity. Charak Samhita elaborates the importance of safety of a medicine by defining that medicine is safe and effective, if it alleviates the disease and does not produce any concomitant bad effect. In Ayurveda, plants, animal products and minerals based medicines are used for healthcare and treatment of various disease conditions.

It was known to ancient Ayurvedic scholars that metals, minerals and some plants are toxic and harmful to the body and therefore, it was advocated to process them properly so as to render them therapeutically safe. Ayurveda pharmaceuticals strongly recommend various other safety aspects, which are known for their contemporary relevance, like Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), Good Field Collection Practices (GFCP) of medicinal plants and Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) for preparation of quality assured drugs. Factors like place, soil, season and time for collection of natural drugs also play a significant role in the quality, strength and purity of drugs.

With tremendous expansion in the use of natural products, globally, safety and efficacy as well as quality control of ASU drugs have become pivotal concern for both health authorities and public in general.

Nonclinical evaluation of drug safety usually consists of standard animal toxicology studies. Animal toxicity studies need to be carried out to assess the potential undesirable pharmacodynamic effects of drugs on physiological functions in relation to exposure within the therapeutic range.

The development of a drug is a stepwise process involving an evaluation of both animal and human efficacy and safety information. The goals of the nonclinical safety evaluation generally include characterization of toxic effects with respect to target organs, dose dependence, relationship to exposure, and, when appropriate, potential reversibility. This information is used to estimate an initial safe starting dose and dose range for the human trials and to identify parameters for clinical monitoring for potential adverse effects.

Before conducting any toxicological testing in animals, the study should be approved by the Institute Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) or the protocol should satisfy the guidelines of the local governing body. In India, the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) guidelines should be followed for the maintenance of experimental animals.

However, Ayurvedic drugs are prescribed considering various aspects viz. Anupana, Dose, Dosage, Age etc. which may play pivotal role in their therapeutic potential. Further, it is pertinent to say that some drugs are used after processing such as Shodhna, Marana. Therefore, safety/toxicity studies of such drugs may only be conducted adopting similar Ayurvedic procedures. Hence, there is a need for fundamentally different approach for toxicological studies that need to be adopted for ASU drugs.

Owing to the importance of safe use of ASU drugs, Ministry of AYUSH has issued guidelines for licensing of these drugs vide Gazette notification Rule 158B, dated 10th August, 2010 wherein, the safety requirements for different categories of ASU drugs are described.

Despite existence of several guidelines such as General Methodologies & Research evaluation Traditional Medicines, OECD guidelines, Schedule Y of Drug and Cosmetic Act etc., GCP Guidelines for ASU Medicines, there is a need to evolve a comprehensive guideline to address system specific issues for conducting safety/toxicity studies of ASU drugs. The present document would certainly serve as a ready reference for researchers engaged in Research and Drug development in ASU systems.

## 1. General Research Guidelines and Methodologies for Drug Development at a Glance

The following flowchart outlines the general research guidelines and methodologies for drug development:

**PREPARATORY PHASE (1)**
- Prevalence survey and formulation of drug/combination for specific targeted indication/activity (1)
- (Appropriate basis of literary survey, previous clinical data of ingredients / any other data of claims, classical evidences)
- Formulation of SOPs and Standardization, stability studies, Quality assurance (4)
- (Considering the classical methods and current available physical/chemical, Biological parameters, microbial loads, heavymetal estimation, pesticide residues, etc. for standardization and safety)
- Design of study and formulation of Clinical protocols (7)
- (As per current guidelines and adopting Classical methodology)
- Finalization with task force of experts
- Bulk preparation of quality assured drug for clinical trial, packing, labeling etc. as per requirement

**DRUG DEVELOPMENT PHASES (2-8)**
- Collection of raw drugs (2) (considering current good agricultural practices, good field collection practices and Ayurvedic textual methods)
- Botanical identification/Pharmacognostic/Chemical studies of ingredients (3) (based on available guidelines and classical methodology)
- Non clinical safety studies (5) (acute/subacute/chronic studies as per the clinical use of the drug) (with appropriate animal ethical clearances as per available guidelines)
- Animal Studies for biological activity/efficacy (specific/mechanism of action) (6)
- Execution of Clinical Trial (8)
  - Approval of IEC/IRB and CTRI registration
  - Trial conduct
  - Trial monitoring
  - Trial coordination
  - Data analysis
  - Publication

*Note: IPR Protection and issues of filing of patent to be addressed at suitable stage.*

## 2. Single-Dose Acute Toxicity Study

### 2.1 Single-Dose Toxicity Studies (Acute Toxicity)

Acute toxicity studies aim to determine toxic manifestations of the test substance that occur when animals are exposed to one or more doses within a 24-hour period.

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Animal species** | Two rodent species: mice and rats |
| **Sex** | Both males and females (nulliparous and non-pregnant) |
| **Number of animals** | Each group should consist of at least six animals per sex |
| **Dose levels and route of administration** | Limit of 2 gm/kg or at least 10 times of the intended clinical therapeutic dose whichever is less, using the same route as recommended for human |
| **Frequency of administration** | Test substance should be administered in one or more doses during a 24-hour period |
| **Study observations** | Toxic signs and severity, onset, progression and reversibility of signs and mortality for 14 days after administration. Additional observations if animals continue to display signs of toxicity. Observations should include changes in skin and fur, eyes and mucous membranes, respiratory, circulatory, autonomic and central nervous systems, somatomotor activity and behaviour pattern. Attention to tremors, convulsions, salivation, diarrhoea, lethargy, sleep and coma. Animals found moribund or in severe distress should be humanely killed. Time of death recorded. Autopsy of any animal that dies. Histopathological examination if necessary. |

### 2.1.7 Results

- Body weight/body weight changes
- Tabulation of response data and dose level for each animal (nature, severity, duration of effects, mortality)
- Individual weights of animals at day of dosing, weekly intervals thereafter, and at time of death or sacrifice
- Time course of onset of signs of toxicity and whether reversible
- Necropsy findings and histopathological findings
- Maximum tolerated dose may be calculated
- Statistical treatment of results

## 3. Repeated-Dose Oral Toxicity Study

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Animal species** | Two mammalian species, one non-rodent |
| **Sex** | Males and females |
| **Number of animals** | Rodents: at least 10 males and 10 females per group. Non-rodents: at least 3 males and 3 females per group |
| **Route of administration** | Same as recommended for human |
| **Dose levels** | Three dose levels. High dose should produce observable toxicity (based on MTD from acute study); low dose should not cause observable toxicity. Vehicle control group included |
| **Period of exposure** | As per intended therapeutic duration (see table below) |
| **Recovery phase** | 50% of animals in each group allowed to live for at least 15 days after cessation of administration |

### Period of Exposure

| S. No. | Duration of proposed human administration | Period of exposure in toxicity study |
|--------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1. | Single dose | 2 weeks |
| 2. | More than 2 weeks - less than 4 weeks | 4 weeks |
| 3. | More than 4 weeks - less than 12 weeks | 12 weeks |
| 4. | More than 12 weeks - less than 24 weeks | 24 weeks |
| 5. | More than 24 weeks | Same as expected period of use of trial drug |

### Observations

**3.8.1 General signs:** Home Cage Activity (Behavior, Convulsions, Biting, Locomotor activity, Tremors, Eye Prominence, Hair Coat, Lacrimation, Salivation, Respiration Character & Rate, Fecal excretion, Urine output) at least twice a week.

**3.8.2 Body weight:** Weekly before start, then weekly for first three months, then every four weeks if duration >3 months.

**3.8.3 Food intake:** Weekly before start, then as per protocol.

**3.8.4 Clinical Chemistry:**

| Category | Parameters |
|----------|------------|
| **Urine qualitative** | Appearance, Colour, Glucose, Bilirubin, Ketone, Specific Gravity, Blood, pH, Protein, Urobilinogen, Nitrites & Leucocytes |
| **General parameters** | Plasma Glucose, Serum Albumin/Globulin, Total Proteins |
| **Liver function tests** | Serum-Alkaline Phosphatase, Bilirubin, SGOT, SGPT, AST |
| **Renal function tests** | Blood Urea, Serum Creatinine |
| **Lipid profile** | Serum-Cholesterol, HDL, Triglyceride |
| **Electrolytes** | Serum-Na+, K+, Ca++ |

**3.8.5 Haematological examination:**

| Parameter | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Clinical Haematology** | Hemoglobin (Hb/HGB), Total Red Blood Cell (RBC) count, Hematocrit (HCT), Reticulocyte count, Total White Blood Cell (WBC) count, Differential WBC count, Platelet Count (PLT), Terminal bone marrow examination, ESR (Non-Rodents only), General Blood picture |
| **Coagulation Parameters (Non-rodents only)** | Bleeding Time, Coagulation Time, Prothrombin Time, Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time |

**3.8.6 Other Function tests:** ECG, visual, auditory tests if appropriate. Ophthalmological examination for rodents (fixed number) and non-rodents (all animals).

**3.8.7 Necropsy and histopathological examination:**

| Organ/Tissue | Notes |
|--------------|-------|
| Brain* (Cerebrum, Cerebellum, Midbrain), Spinal Cord, Eye, Middle Ear, Thyroid, Parathyroid, Spleen*, Thymus, Adrenal*, Pancreas, Trachea, Lung*, Heart*, Aorta, Oesophagus, Stomach, Duodenum, Jejunum, Terminal ileum, Colon, Rectum, Liver*, Kidney*, Urinary bladder, Epididymis, Testis*, Ovary, Uterus*, Skin, Mammary gland, Mesenteric lymph node, Skeletal muscle | * Organs marked with asterisk should be weighed. () Organs in parenthesis should be examined if indicated. |

### 3.9 Estimation of Metals

Heavy metal estimation in blood and tissue homogenate if ASU drug contains any metal/mineral.

**3.9.1 Arsenic Estimation:** Method of Stahr (1977). Digestion with concentrated nitric acid, measurement at 193.7 nm using graphite furnace AAS.

**3.9.1 Trace metal (Zinc and Copper etc.) Estimation:** Method of Parker et al. (1967). Digestion with concentrated nitric acid, measurement at 213.9 nm (Zn) and 324.8 nm (Cu) using AAS.

**3.9.2 Gold and Mercury Estimation:** As per AOAC (1998) guidelines. For Au: digestion with HNO3:HClO4 (6:1). For Hg: digestion with conc. H2SO4, titration with KMnO4, analysis using AAS with Vapour Generation Assembly.

**3.9.3 Lead Estimation:** Method of Dhanlakshmi et al. (2013). Wet ashing with HNO3 and H2O2, microwave digestion, analysis by ICP-OES at 220.353 nm.

## 4. Reproduction and Developmental Toxicity Studies

These studies are required for all ASU drugs defined under section 3(h) containing crude drugs/Aqueous and Hydro-alcoholic extract(s) (without textual rationale or not as per text)/any other solvent based extract(s), ingredients of Schedule E (1), proposed to be studied or used in women of child bearing age.

Species: mice or rats, second species rabbit if required.

Segments:
- **Segment I (Female Fertility Study)**
- **Segment II (Teratogenicity Study)**
- **Segment III (Perinatal Study)**

### 4.1 Segment I (Female Fertility Study)

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Animals** | One species, both sexes (rats or mice) |
| **Number** | At least 20 males and 20 females per group |
| **Route** | Same as intended clinical route |
| **Dose levels** | Three dose levels (therapeutic, average, high) + negative control, positive control, comparative control (desirable) |
| **Period of exposure** | Males: at least 40 days of age, dosed daily for 60 days or more before mating, continued until successful copulation. Females: sexually mature, dosed daily for at least 14 days before mating, during mating and after successful copulation until beginning of organogenesis |
| **Observations** | Mortality, general signs, body weight, food intake. Mating confirmation. Autopsy at term: number of corpora lutea, successful pregnancies, fetal mortality. Gross examination of organs and tissues for dams. Males and non-pregnant females autopsied at appropriate time. |

### 4.2 Segment II (Teratogenicity Study)

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Animals** | Females of at least one rodent species and a non-rodent (rabbits) |
| **Number** | At least 30 for rats/mice, at least 12 for rabbits per group |
| **Route** | Same as expected clinically |
| **Dose levels** | Three dose levels (therapeutic, average, high) + negative control, positive control, comparative control (desirable) |
| **Observations** | Mortality, general signs, body weight, food intake for dams. Autopsy at term for 2/3 of dams (rodents) or all dams (non-rodents): successful pregnancy, fetal mortality, number of corpora lutea, implantation sites, resorptions, fetal number, gender, body length, weight, gross/visceral/skeletal abnormalities. Remaining 1/3 of dams allowed to deliver: litter size, mortality, sex, external changes, body weights, growth and development, reproductive performance. |

### 4.3 Segment III (Perinatal Study)

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Animals** | At least one species of female animals (rats or mice) |
| **Number** | At least 20 animals per group |
| **Route** | Expected clinical route |
| **Dose levels** | Three dose levels (therapeutic, average, high) + negative control, positive control, comparative control (desirable) |
| **Observations** | Mortality, general signs, body weight, food intake for dams. All dams allowed to deliver and nurse offspring. Litter size, mortality, sex, external changes, body weights. Growth and development, reproductive performance of offspring. Autopsy and gross observations on organs and tissues of treated dams. |

### 4.3.6 Analysis of Results

- Present results in tables and figures with discussion.
- For statistical analysis before weaning, litter as unit of analysis.
- Discuss no-effect dose level for reproduction and development.
- Compare reproductive and developmental toxicity with similar drugs.

## 5. Special Toxicity Tests

For test substances intended for chronic illnesses or long-term use (six months to one year), genotoxicity and carcinogenicity studies may be necessary.

### 5.1 Genotoxicity

Genotoxicity tests detect compounds inducing genetic damage directly or indirectly. Required before Phase III trials.

**Tests:**
1. Gene mutation in bacteria (Ames test)
2. In-vitro cytogenetic evaluation of chromosomal damage with mammalian cells or in vitro mouse lymphoma tk assay
3. In-vivo test for chromosomal damage using rodent hematopoietic cells

**5.1.1 Test Methods:**
- **Ames' Test:** S. typhimurium strains (TA98, TA100, TA102, TA1535, TA97) or E. coli WP2 uvrA. In-vitro exposure with and without metabolic activation (S9 mix) at minimum 5 log dose levels. Positive control: 9-aminoacridine, 2-nitroflurone, sodium azide, mitomycin C. 2.5-fold increase in revertants considered positive.
- **In-vitro Cytogenetic Assay:** CHO cells or human lymphocyte culture. Minimum 3 log doses. Positive control: Cyclophosphamide (with activation), Mitomycin C (without activation). Increased chromosomal aberrations in metaphase.
- **In-vivo Micronucleus Assay:** One rodent species (mouse). Route same as intended for humans. Five animals per sex per dose group. At least three dose levels plus solvent and positive control. Dosing day 1 and 2, sacrifice 6 hours after last injection. Bone marrow from femora, Giemsa-MayGruenwald staining. Increased micronuclei in polychromatic erythrocytes (minimum 1000).
- **In-vivo Cytogenetic Assay:** One rodent species (rat). Five animals/sex/dose group. At least three dose levels plus solvent and positive control. Dosing day 1, colchicine at 22 hours, sacrifice 2 hours later. Bone marrow from femora, Giemsa staining. Increased aberrations in metaphase chromosomes (minimum 100).

### 5.2 Carcinogenicity Test

Studies in a rodent species (preferably rat). Mouse with scientific justification. Includes Preliminary and Full-scale study.

**5.2.1 Preliminary Test:** Determines dose levels for full-scale study.
- Animals: At least two species, both sexes, no more than six weeks old.
- Number: Rodents: 10 males and 10 females per group. Non-rodents: 3 males and 3 females per group.
- Route: Same as intended for human.
- Dose levels: Three dose levels with vehicle control.
- Period: 90 days (longer if delayed/cumulative effect).
- Observations: Daily general signs, weekly body weight and food intake.
- Necropsy & Histopathology: Organs and tissues of dead animals autopsied.
- Results: Highest dose that inhibits body weight gain <10% compared to control, no death due to toxic effects, no remarkable changes.

**5.2.2 Full-Scale Study:**
- Animals: At least two species, both sexes, no more than six weeks old.
- Number: 50 males and 50 females per group.
- Route: Same as intended for human.
- Dose levels: Three dose levels with untreated and vehicle control.
- Period: 24 months for rats, 18 months for mice.
- Observations: Daily general signs, weekly body weight and food intake. Blood biochemistry and haematology at initial stage and before autopsy. Necropsy and histopathology.
- Results: Positive for carcinogenicity if: development of tumours not seen in control; greater frequency of tumours; greater variety of organs involved; earlier development of tumours.

### 5.3 Local Toxicity Test for Topical Preparations

Required for:
1. ASU drugs under section 3(h) containing crude drugs or Aqueous/Hydro-alcoholic extracts without textual rationale or not as per text.
2. ASU drugs containing other solvent based extracts.
3. ASU drugs containing ingredients of Schedule E(1).

**5.3.1 Preparation of Test Formulations:**
- Solid: wet with water or suitable solvent for uniform application.
- Semi-solid: tested undiluted.
- Liquid: tested undiluted. Aerosol may be diluted.

**5.3.2 Dermal Toxicity Study:** Rabbit and rat. Daily topical application on shaved skin (≥10% body surface area). Porous gauze dressing. At least 3 concentrations several fold higher than clinical dosage. Period 7-90 days depending on clinical duration. Recovery group if skin irritation visible. Observe local signs (erythema, oedema, eschar).

**5.3.3 Photo-allergy or Dermal Photo-toxicity:** Armstrong/Harber Test in guinea pig. Pretest in 8 animals with 4 concentrations (patch for 2 hours ± 15 min) with and without UV exposure (10 J/cm²). Main test: 10 test animals, 5 controls. Induction: 0.3 ml/patch for 2 hours ± 15 min followed by 10 J/cm² UV. Repeated on days 0, 2, 4, 7, 9, 11. Challenge between day 20-24. Grade erythema and oedema at 24 and 48 hours. Positive control: musk ambrette or psoralin.

**5.3.4 Vaginal Toxicity Test:** Rabbit or dog. Topical application (pessary, cream, ointment). 6-10 animals per dose group. Higher concentrations or several daily applications. Minimum 7 days (up to 30 days). Observe swelling, closure of introitus, histopathology of vaginal wall.

**5.3.5 Rectal Tolerance Test:** Rabbits or dogs. 6-10 animals per dose group. Formulation applied rectally once or several times daily. Minimum 7 days (up to 30 days). Observe clinical signs, pain, blood/mucus in faeces, condition of anal region/sphincter, gross and histological examination of rectal mucosa.

**5.3.6 Other Local Toxicity Tests:** Inhalation toxicity, Ocular toxicity, etc., if intended.

**5.3.7 Allergenicity/Hypersensitivity:** Guinea Pig Maximization Test (GPMT) or Local Lymph Node Assay (LLNA) in mouse.
- **GPMT:** Two steps: determination of maximum non-irritant and minimum irritant doses, then main test. Intradermal induction (day 1) and topical challenge (day 21). Re-challenge if no response. Erythema and oedema as evaluation criteria.
- **LLNA:** Mice of same sex. Three graded doses (highest = maximum non-irritant dose) plus vehicle control. Minimum 6 mice per group. Apply on ear skin for three consecutive days. On day 5, dissect draining auricular lymph nodes 5 hours after i.v. 3H-thymidine or BrdU. Increase in incorporation as criterion.

## 8. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Dose** | The amount of test substance administered. Expressed as weight of test substance per unit body weight of test animal per day (e.g. mg/kg body weight/day) or as a constant dietary concentration. |
| **Dosage** | General term comprising of dose, its frequency and duration of dosing. |
| **Evident toxicity** | General term describing clear signs of toxicity following administration of test substance. |
| **NOAEL** | No-observed-adverse-effect level. Highest dose level where no adverse treatment-related findings are observed. |
| **Developmental toxicology** | Study of adverse effects on the developing organism from exposure prior to conception, during prenatal development, or postnatally to time of sexual maturation. Manifestations: death, structural abnormality, altered growth, functional deficiency. |
| **Adverse effect** | Any treatment-related alteration from baseline that diminishes an organism's ability to survive, reproduce or adapt to the environment. |
| **Implantation (nidation)** | Attachment of the blastocyst to the epithelial lining of the uterus. |
| **Acute oral toxicity** | Adverse effects occurring following oral administration of a single dose or multiple doses within 24 hours. |
| **LD50 (median lethal dose)** | Statistically derived single dose of a substance expected to cause death in 50% of animals. Expressed as weight of test substance per unit weight of test animal (mg/kg). |

### 9.2 Guidelines on the Regulation of Scientific Experiments on Animals

**Statutory Provisions:** Persons engaged in scientific experiments on animals must comply with Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960, and Breeding of and Experiments on Animals (Control and Supervision) Rules, 1998, as amended. Enforced by CPCSEA.

**Ethical Principles Adopted by CPCSEA:**

1. Experiments on animals may be carried out for advancement of physiological knowledge, saving/prolonging human life, alleviating suffering, significant gains in well-being of people, or combating disease.
2. Animals lowest on phylogenetic scale with least sentience should be used. Minimum number of animals for statistically valid results at 95% confidence. Alternatives to animal testing should be considered.
3. Avoidance/minimization of pain and suffering. Procedures causing more than momentary pain should be performed with sedation, analgesia or anesthesia.
4. Moral responsibility for welfare after experiments. Euthanasia permissible only in specified situations.
5. Living conditions appropriate for species. Veterinary care as necessary.

**Procedures for Approval of Scientific Experiments:**

- **Definition of experiment:** Any programme or project involving use of animals for acquisition of knowledge.
- **Experimental animals subject to regulation:** Anything higher than invertebrates.
- **Functions of CPCSEA:** Registration of establishments, appointment of nominees in IAEC, approval of animal house facilities, permission for experiments, import recommendations, action against violations.
- **Functions of IAEC:** Constituted per CPCSEA. Composition includes biological scientist, two scientists from different disciplines, veterinarian, scientist in charge of animal facility, outside scientist, non-scientific socially aware member, CPCSEA nominee. IAEC approves experiments up to rodent level; above that, forward to CPCSEA.
- **Registration of establishments:** Mandatory for breeding/trade and experiments.
- **Approval of animal house facilities:** Required.
- **Use of animals:** Lowest phylogenetic scale, minimum number.
- **Procurement:** From registered breeders or alternative legal sources with permission.
- **Welfare during experiments:** Personnel responsible.
- **Aftercare and rehabilitation:** Investigator responsible; costs part of research.
- **Euthanasia:** Permissible only in defined situations.
- **Suspension/revocation of registration:** CPCSEA may suspend/revoke for violations.

### 9.3 CPCSEA Guidelines for Laboratory Animal Facility - 2005

**Goal:** Promote humane care of animals used in research.

**Veterinary Care:** Adequate veterinary care provided by veterinarian or trained person. Daily observation. Policies for ancillary aspects.

**Animal Procurement:** Lawful acquisition. Health surveillance. Quarantine and stabilization.

**Quarantine, Stabilization and Separation:** Separation of newly received animals. Duration: small lab animals 1 week to 1 month; large animals up to 6 weeks. Physical separation by species.

**Surveillance, Diagnosis, Treatment and Control of Disease:** Daily observation. Postmortem examination. Isolation of contagious disease.

**Animal Care and Technical Personnel:** Trained staff. Personal hygiene. Protective clothing. No eating/drinking/smoking in animal rooms.

**Animal Experimentation Involving Hazardous Agents:** Policies and Institutional Bio-safety Committee. Review by IAEC and Bio-safety Committee.

**Multiple Surgical Procedures:** Not to be practiced unless approved by IAEC.

**Duration of Experiments:** No animal for more than 3 years unless justified.

**Physical Restraint:** Brief restraint for examination. Prolonged restraint avoided. Guidelines for restraint equipment.

**Physical Facilities:** Location separated from personnel areas. Animal rooms 50-60% of total area. Functional areas: receiving, quarantine, isolation, housing, surgery, necropsy, radiography, diet preparation, storage, administration, showers, washing/sterilization, equipment repair, waste storage.

**Building Materials:** Durable, moisture-proof, fire-resistant, seamless. Corridors wide. Utilities accessible.

**Animal Room Doors:** Rust, vermin, dust proof. Observation window. Rodent barriers.

**Exterior Windows:** Not recommended for small animal facilities.

**Floors:** Monolithic or epoxy, smooth, moisture-proof, non-absorbent, skid-proof, resistant.

**Drains:** Not essential for rodent rooms. If used, sloped, filled with water or mesh.

**Walls & Ceilings:** Free of cracks, unsealed penetrations. Surface materials withstand scrubbing.

**Storage Areas:** Separate for feed, bedding, cages. Refrigerated storage for dead animals.

**Facilities for Sanitizing Equipment:** Area for sanitizing cages.

**Experimental Area:** Separate for procedures. Aseptic surgery areas.

**Environment:** Temperature 18-29°C, humidity 30-70%. Ventilation 12-15 air cycles/hour. Power and lighting: fluorescent, <400 lux for rodents. Noise control <85 dB.

**Animal Husbandry:** Caging/housing system designed for well-being. Space adequate. Materials: polypropylene, polycarbonate, stainless steel. Shelters for outdoor housing. Social environment: group housing for communal animals. Activity provisions. Food: palatable, non-contaminated, nutritionally adequate. Bedding: absorbent, free from toxic chemicals. Water: continuous access, potable, uncontaminated.

**Sanitation and Cleanliness:** Cleaning with detergents and disinfectants. Cages sanitized before use. Water bottles washed and sanitized.

**Waste Disposal:** Regular removal. Incineration preferred. Waste containers lined.

**Pest Control:** Programs to prevent/control pests.

**Emergency, Weekend and Holiday Care:** Daily care by qualified personnel.

**Record Keeping:** Animal house plans, staff records, health records, SOPs, breeding records, IAEC minutes, experiment records, mortality records, clinical records, training records, water/feed analysis, health monitoring, rehabilitation records.

**Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** Name of author, title, date of approval, reference, location, objectives, instruments, reagents, normal values, hazard identification.

**Personnel and Training:** Selection of staff. Protective clothing. Medical check-up. Training.

**Transport of Laboratory Animals:** Mode, containers, density, food/water, protection from infections/injuries/stress.

**Anaesthesia and Euthanasia:** Anaesthesia for painful procedures. Neuromuscular blocking agents not without general anaesthesia. Euthanasia methods: physical, inhalation, drug administration.

**Laboratory Animal Ethics:** Deep ethical consideration.

**Transgenic Animals:** Housing in clean room environment or isolators. Disposal after euthanasia. Breeding and genetics: procurement from registered breeders.
