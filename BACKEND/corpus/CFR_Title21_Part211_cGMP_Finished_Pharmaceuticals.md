## Prologue / Background

This document contains the FDA regulations for **Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) for Finished Pharmaceuticals** as set forth in Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), Part 211. These regulations establish minimum requirements for the preparation of drug products for administration to humans or animals. The document includes:

- General provisions and definitions (Subpart A)
- Organization and personnel (Subpart B)
- Buildings and facilities (Subpart C)
- Equipment (Subpart D)
- Control of components and drug product containers and closures (Subpart E)
- Production and process controls (Subpart F)
- Packaging and labeling control (Subpart G)
- Holding and distribution (Subpart H)
- Laboratory controls (Subpart I)
- Records and reports (Subpart J)
- Returned and salvaged drug products (Subpart K)

The PDF also includes definitions from Part 210 that are referenced in Part 211, and a brief listing of Part 212 (cGMP for Positron Emission Tomography Drugs).

## Part 211 – Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals

### Subpart A – General Provisions

#### § 211.1 Scope

| Paragraph | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | The regulations in this part contain the minimum current good manufacturing practice for preparation of drug products (excluding positron emission tomography drugs) for administration to humans or animals. |
| **(b)** | The cGMP regulations in this chapter as they pertain to drug products; in parts 600 through 680 as they pertain to drugs that are also biological products; and in part 1271 as they are applicable to drugs that are also HCT/Ps; supplement and do not supersede the regulations in this part unless explicitly provided otherwise. In the event of a conflict, the regulation specifically applicable to the drug product in question shall supersede the more general. |
| **(c)** | Pending consideration of a proposed exemption, the requirements in this part shall not be enforced for OTC drug products if the products and all their ingredients are ordinarily marketed and consumed as human foods, and which may also fall within the legal definition of drugs by virtue of intended use. Until further notice, regulations under parts 110 and 117, and where applicable, parts 113 through 129, shall be applied. |

**Citation:** 43 FR 45077, Sept. 29, 1978, as amended at 62 FR 66522, Dec. 19, 1997; 69 FR 29828, May 25, 2004; 74 FR 65431, Dec. 10, 2009; 80 FR 56168, Sept. 17, 2015. **Effective Date Note:** At 89 FR 51769, June 18, 2024, §211.1 was amended, effective Dec. 18, 2025.

#### § 211.3 Definitions

The definitions set forth in § 210.3 of this chapter apply in this part.

### Subpart B – Organization and Personnel

#### § 211.22 Responsibilities of Quality Control Unit

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | There shall be a quality control unit with responsibility and authority to approve or reject all components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, packaging material, labeling, and drug products, and authority to review production records to assure no errors have occurred or, if errors have occurred, that they have been fully investigated. The quality control unit shall be responsible for approving or rejecting drug products manufactured, processed, packed, or held under contract by another company. |
| **(b)** | Adequate laboratory facilities for testing and approval (or rejection) shall be available to the quality control unit. |
| **(c)** | The quality control unit shall have responsibility for approving or rejecting all procedures or specifications impacting on identity, strength, quality, and purity of the drug product. |
| **(d)** | Responsibilities and procedures applicable to the quality control unit shall be in writing and followed. |

#### § 211.25 Personnel Qualifications

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Each person engaged in manufacture, processing, packing, or holding shall have education, training, and experience to perform assigned functions. Training shall be in the particular operations performed and in cGMP (including regulations and written procedures) as they relate to the employee's functions. Training in cGMP shall be conducted by qualified individuals on a continuing basis and with sufficient frequency. |
| **(b)** | Each person responsible for supervising shall have education, training, and experience to perform assigned functions to provide assurance that the drug product has the safety, identity, strength, quality, and purity it purports to possess. |
| **(c)** | There shall be an adequate number of qualified personnel to perform and supervise manufacture, processing, packing, or holding of each drug product. |

#### § 211.28 Personnel Responsibilities

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Personnel shall wear clean clothing appropriate for duties. Protective apparel shall be worn as necessary to protect drug products from contamination. |
| **(b)** | Personnel shall practice good sanitation and health habits. |
| **(c)** | Only authorized personnel shall enter limited-access areas. |
| **(d)** | Any person with apparent illness or open lesions that may adversely affect safety or quality shall be excluded from direct contact until condition corrected or determined not to jeopardize safety/quality. All personnel shall report health conditions that may have an adverse effect. |

#### § 211.34 Consultants

Consultants advising on manufacture, processing, packing, or holding shall have sufficient education, training, and experience. Records shall be maintained stating name, address, qualifications, and type of service provided.

### Subpart C – Buildings and Facilities

#### § 211.42 Design and Construction Features

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Buildings shall be of suitable size, construction, and location to facilitate cleaning, maintenance, and proper operations. |
| **(b)** | Adequate space for orderly placement of equipment and materials to prevent mixups and contamination. Flow of components, containers, closures, labeling, in-process materials, and drug products shall be designed to prevent contamination. |
| **(c)** | Operations shall be performed within specifically defined areas of adequate size. Separate or defined areas or control systems are necessary for: (1) Receipt, identification, storage, withholding of components, containers, closures, labeling pending sampling/testing; (2) Holding rejected materials; (3) Storage of released materials; (4) Storage of in-process materials; (5) Manufacturing and processing; (6) Packaging and labeling; (7) Quarantine storage before release; (8) Storage after release; (9) Control and laboratory operations; (10) Aseptic processing (with specified requirements). |
| **(d)** | Operations relating to penicillin shall be performed in facilities separate from those used for other drug products for human use. |

#### § 211.44 Lighting

Adequate lighting shall be provided in all areas.

#### § 211.46 Ventilation, Air Filtration, Air Heating and Cooling

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Adequate ventilation shall be provided. |
| **(b)** | Equipment for adequate control over air pressure, micro-organisms, dust, humidity, and temperature shall be provided when appropriate. |
| **(c)** | Air filtration systems, including prefilters and particulate matter air filters, shall be used when appropriate. If air is recirculated, measures shall be taken to control dust. Adequate exhaust systems required where air contamination occurs. |
| **(d)** | Air-handling systems for penicillin shall be completely separate. |

#### § 211.48 Plumbing

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Potable water shall be supplied under continuous positive pressure in a plumbing system free of defects that could contribute contamination. Potable water shall meet EPA Primary Drinking Water Regulations (40 CFR part 141). |
| **(b)** | Drains shall be of adequate size and, where connected directly to a sewer, shall be provided with an air break or other mechanical device to prevent back-siphonage. |

#### § 211.50 Sewage and Refuse

Sewage, trash, and other refuse shall be disposed of in a safe and sanitary manner.

#### § 211.52 Washing and Toilet Facilities

Adequate washing facilities shall be provided, including hot and cold water, soap or detergent, air driers or single-service towels, and clean toilet facilities easily accessible to working areas.

#### § 211.56 Sanitation

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Buildings shall be maintained in a clean and sanitary condition, free of infestation by rodents, birds, insects, and other vermin (other than laboratory animals). Trash and organic waste shall be held and disposed of timely and sanitarily. |
| **(b)** | Written procedures assigning responsibility for sanitation and describing cleaning schedules, methods, equipment, and materials shall be established and followed. |
| **(c)** | Written procedures for use of suitable rodenticides, insecticides, fungicides, fumigating agents, and cleaning/sanitizing agents shall be designed to prevent contamination and followed. Rodenticides, insecticides, and fungicides shall be registered and used in accordance with FIFRA. |
| **(d)** | Sanitation procedures shall apply to work performed by contractors or temporary employees as well as full-time employees. |

#### § 211.58 Maintenance

Any building used in manufacture, processing, packing, or holding shall be maintained in a good state of repair.

### Subpart D – Equipment

#### § 211.63 Equipment Design, Size, and Location

Equipment shall be of appropriate design, adequate size, and suitably located to facilitate operations for intended use and for cleaning and maintenance.

#### § 211.65 Equipment Construction

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Equipment shall be constructed so that surfaces that contact components, in-process materials, or drug products shall not be reactive, additive, or absorptive so as to alter safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity beyond established requirements. |
| **(b)** | Substances required for operation (lubricants, coolants) shall not come into contact with components, containers, closures, in-process materials, or drug products so as to alter safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity beyond established requirements. |

#### § 211.67 Equipment Cleaning and Maintenance

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Equipment and utensils shall be cleaned, maintained, and sanitized and/or sterilized at appropriate intervals to prevent malfunctions or contamination that would alter safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity beyond established requirements. |
| **(b)** | Written procedures shall be established and followed for cleaning and maintenance, including: (1) Assignment of responsibility; (2) Maintenance and cleaning schedules; (3) Description of methods, equipment, materials, and disassembly/reassembly; (4) Removal or obliteration of previous batch identification; (5) Protection of clean equipment from contamination; (6) Inspection for cleanliness immediately before use. |
| **(c)** | Records shall be kept as specified in §§ 211.180 and 211.182. |

#### § 211.68 Automatic, Mechanical, and Electronic Equipment

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Automatic, mechanical, or electronic equipment may be used. It shall be routinely calibrated, inspected, or checked according to a written program. Written records shall be maintained. |
| **(b)** | Appropriate controls shall be exercised over computer or related systems to assure changes only by authorized personnel. Input/output shall be checked for accuracy. Backup file of data shall be maintained. Hard copy or alternative systems shall be maintained. |
| **(c)** | Automated equipment can satisfy requirements for performance by one person and checking by another if used in conformity with this section and one person checks that the equipment properly performed the operation. |

#### § 211.72 Filters

Filters for liquid filtration used in manufacture of injectable drug products intended for human use shall not release fibers. Fiber-releasing filters may be used when necessary, but an additional nonfiber-releasing filter with a maximum nominal pore size rating of 0.2 micron (0.45 micron if manufacturing conditions dictate) shall subsequently be used. Use of asbestos-containing filter is prohibited.

### Subpart E – Control of Components and Drug Product Containers and Closures

#### § 211.80 General Requirements

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures describing receipt, identification, storage, handling, sampling, testing, and approval or rejection of components and drug product containers and closures shall be followed. |
| **(b)** | Components and containers/closures shall be handled and stored to prevent contamination. |
| **(c)** | Bagged or boxed components shall be stored off the floor and suitably spaced. |
| **(d)** | Each container or grouping shall be identified with a distinctive code for each lot in each shipment. This code shall be used in recording disposition. Each lot shall be appropriately identified as to status (quarantined, approved, or rejected). |

#### § 211.82 Receipt and Storage of Untested Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Upon receipt and before acceptance, each container or grouping shall be examined visually for appropriate labeling, container damage, broken seals, and contamination. |
| **(b)** | Components, containers, and closures shall be stored under quarantine until tested or examined and released. |

#### § 211.84 Testing and Approval or Rejection of Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Each lot shall be withheld from use until sampled, tested, or examined, and released by the quality control unit. |
| **(b)** | Representative samples of each shipment of each lot shall be collected. Number of containers and amount of material based on appropriate criteria. |
| **(c)** | Sampling procedures: (1) Clean containers; (2) Open, sample, reseal to prevent contamination; (3) Sterile equipment and aseptic techniques when necessary; (4) If sampling from top, middle, bottom, subdivisions not composited; (5) Sample containers identified; (6) Containers marked to show samples removed. |
| **(d)** | Samples shall be examined and tested: (1) At least one identity test; (2) Conformity with written specifications for purity, strength, quality; (3) Containers and closures tested for conformity; (4) Microscopic examination when appropriate; (5) Examine for filth, insect infestation, or other extraneous adulterant; (6) Microbiological tests before use if potential for objectionable contamination. |
| **(e)** | Lots meeting specifications may be approved and released; those not meeting shall be rejected. |

#### § 211.86 Use of Approved Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures

Approved components shall be rotated so that oldest approved stock is used first. Deviation permitted if temporary and appropriate.

#### § 211.87 Retesting of Approved Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures

Components, containers, and closures shall be retested or reexamined as necessary, e.g., after storage for long periods or after exposure to air, heat, or other conditions that might adversely affect them.

#### § 211.89 Rejected Components, Drug Product Containers, and Closures

Rejected materials shall be identified and controlled under a quarantine system designed to prevent their use.

#### § 211.94 Drug Product Containers and Closures

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Containers and closures shall not be reactive, additive, or absorptive so as to alter safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity beyond established requirements. |
| **(b)** | Container closure systems shall provide adequate protection against foreseeable external factors. |
| **(c)** | Containers and closures shall be clean and, where indicated, sterilized and processed to remove pyrogenic properties. Such depyrogenation processes shall be validated. |
| **(d)** | Standards or specifications, methods of testing, and methods of cleaning, sterilizing, and processing shall be written and followed. |
| **(e)** | Medical gas containers and closures must meet specific requirements for gas-specific use outlet connections and labeling/coloring. |

### Subpart F – Production and Process Controls

#### § 211.100 Written Procedures; Deviations

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures for production and process control shall be designed to assure drug products have identity, strength, quality, and purity. Such procedures shall be drafted, reviewed, and approved by appropriate organizational units and reviewed and approved by the quality control unit. |
| **(b)** | Written procedures shall be followed and documented at time of performance. Any deviation shall be recorded and justified. |

#### § 211.101 Charge-in of Components

Written production and control procedures shall include:

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Batch shall be formulated to provide not less than 100% of labeled or established amount of active ingredient. |
| **(b)** | Components shall be weighed, measured, or subdivided appropriately. If transferred to another container, new container identified with component name, receiving/control number, weight/measure, and batch information. |
| **(c)** | Weighing, measuring, or subdividing shall be adequately supervised. Each container dispensed shall be examined by a second person to assure: (1) Released by QC; (2) Weight/measure correct; (3) Containers properly identified. If automated equipment under §211.68, only one person needed. |
| **(d)** | Each component shall be added to batch by one person and verified by a second person, or if automated equipment, verified by one person. |

#### § 211.103 Calculation of Yield

Actual yields and percentages of theoretical yield shall be determined at conclusion of each appropriate phase. Calculations shall be performed by one person and independently verified by a second person, or if automated equipment, independently verified by one person.

#### § 211.105 Equipment Identification

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | All compounding and storage containers, processing lines, and major equipment shall be properly identified at all times. |
| **(b)** | Major equipment shall be identified by a distinctive identification number or code recorded in batch production record. |

#### § 211.110 Sampling and Testing of In-Process Materials and Drug Products

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures shall describe in-process controls, tests, or examinations on appropriate samples. Such controls shall include, where appropriate: (1) Tablet or capsule weight variation; (2) Disintegration time; (3) Adequacy of mixing; (4) Dissolution time and rate; (5) Clarity, completeness, or pH of solutions; (6) Bioburden testing. |
| **(b)** | Valid in-process specifications shall be consistent with final specifications and derived from previous acceptable process average and variability. |
| **(c)** | In-process materials shall be tested for identity, strength, quality, and purity and approved or rejected by QC. |
| **(d)** | Rejected in-process materials shall be identified and controlled under quarantine. |

#### § 211.111 Time Limitations on Production

When appropriate, time limits for completion of each phase of production shall be established. Deviation may be acceptable if it does not compromise quality; such deviation shall be justified and documented.

#### § 211.113 Control of Microbiological Contamination

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Appropriate written procedures to prevent objectionable microorganisms in non-sterile drug products shall be established and followed. |
| **(b)** | Appropriate written procedures to prevent microbiological contamination of sterile drug products shall be established and followed, including validation of all aseptic and sterilization processes. |

#### § 211.115 Reprocessing

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures shall prescribe a system for reprocessing batches that do not conform to standards and steps to ensure reprocessed batches conform. |
| **(b)** | Reprocessing shall not be performed without review and approval of the quality control unit. |

### Subpart G – Packaging and Labeling Control

#### § 211.122 Materials Examination and Usage Criteria

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures for receipt, identification, storage, handling, sampling, examination, and/or testing of labeling and packaging materials shall be followed. Materials shall be representatively sampled and examined/tested upon receipt. |
| **(b)** | Materials meeting specifications may be approved and released; those not meeting shall be rejected. |
| **(c)** | Records shall be maintained for each shipment indicating receipt, examination/testing, and acceptance/rejection. |
| **(d)** | Labels and labeling materials for each different drug product, strength, dosage form, or quantity shall be stored separately with suitable identification. Access limited. |
| **(e)** | Obsolete and outdated labels, labeling, and packaging materials shall be destroyed. |
| **(f)** | Use of gang-printed labeling for different drug products or strengths is prohibited unless adequately differentiated by size, shape, or color. |
| **(g)** | If cut labeling is used, special control procedures required: (1) Dedication of lines; (2) Electronic/electromechanical 100% examination; (3) Visual inspection by one person and independent verification by second; (4) Automated technique preventing incorrect labeling. |
| **(h)** | Printing devices shall be monitored to assure imprinting conforms to batch production record. |

#### § 211.125 Labeling Issuance

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Strict control shall be exercised over labeling issued. |
| **(b)** | Labeling materials shall be carefully examined for identity and conformity. |
| **(c)** | Procedures to reconcile quantities issued, used, and returned; discrepancies outside narrow preset limits shall be investigated per §211.192. Labeling reconciliation waived for cut/roll labeling if 100% examination per §211.122(g)(2), and for 360° wraparound labels on portable cryogenic medical gas containers. |
| **(d)** | Excess labeling bearing lot/control numbers shall be destroyed. |
| **(e)** | Returned labeling shall be maintained and stored to prevent mixups. |
| **(f)** | Written procedures describing control procedures for issuance shall be followed. |

#### § 211.130 Packaging and Labeling Operations

Written procedures shall assure correct labels, labeling, and packaging materials. Features include:

- (a) Prevention of mixups and cross-contamination by physical or spatial separation.
- (b) Identification and handling of filled containers set aside for future labeling.
- (c) Identification of drug product with lot/control number.
- (d) Examination of packaging and labeling materials before use.
- (e) Inspection of packaging and labeling facilities immediately before use.

#### § 211.132 Tamper-Evident Packaging Requirements for Over-the-Counter (OTC) Human Drug Products

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | General: FDA has authority to establish uniform national requirement for tamper-evident packaging of OTC drug products. Non-compliant products are adulterated/misbranded. |
| **(b)** | Requirements: (1) Each manufacturer/packer shall package OTC drug product in tamper-evident package if accessible to public. Tamper-evident package has indicator/barrier to entry; distinctive by design or identifying characteristic. (2) Two-piece hard gelatin capsules must be sealed using acceptable tamper-evident technology. |
| **(c)** | Labeling: (1) Each retail package must bear statement identifying all tamper-evident features, prominently placed, unaffected if breached. (2) If identifying characteristic used, must be referred to in labeling statement. |
| **(d)** | Requests for exemptions: May be submitted as citizen petition under §10.30. |
| **(e)** | OTC drug products subject to approved NDAs: Holders must notify FDA of changes per §314.70. |
| **(f)** | Poison Prevention Packaging Act of 1970: This section does not affect special packaging requirements. |

#### § 211.134 Drug Product Inspection

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Packaged and labeled products shall be examined during finishing operations to assure correct label. |
| **(b)** | Representative sample of units shall be collected at completion and visually examined for correct labeling. |
| **(c)** | Results shall be recorded in batch production or control records. |

#### § 211.137 Expiration Dating

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Drug product shall bear an expiration date determined by appropriate stability testing per §211.166. |
| **(b)** | Expiration dates shall be related to storage conditions stated on labeling. |
| **(c)** | If reconstituted at dispensing, labeling shall bear expiration information for both reconstituted and unreconstituted. |
| **(d)** | Expiration dates shall appear on labeling per §201.17. |
| **(e)** | Homeopathic drug products exempt. |
| **(f)** | Allergenic extracts labeled "No U.S. Standard of Potency" exempt. |
| **(g)** | New drug products for investigational use exempt if they meet appropriate standards; labeling shall bear expiration info for reconstituted product. |
| **(h)** | Pending consideration of proposed exemption, requirements not enforced for human OTC drug products if labeling lacks dosage limitations and they are stable for at least 3 years. |

### Subpart H – Holding and Distribution

#### § 211.142 Warehousing Procedures

Written procedures describing warehousing shall be established and followed, including:

- (a) Quarantine of drug products before release by QC.
- (b) Storage under appropriate conditions of temperature, humidity, and light.

#### § 211.150 Distribution Procedures

Written procedures shall describe distribution, including:

- (a) Procedure whereby oldest approved stock distributed first. Deviation permitted if temporary and appropriate.
- (b) System by which distribution of each lot can be readily determined to facilitate recall.

### Subpart I – Laboratory Controls

#### § 211.160 General Requirements

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Establishment of specifications, standards, sampling plans, test procedures, or other laboratory control mechanisms shall be drafted by appropriate unit and reviewed/approved by QC. Requirements shall be followed and documented. Deviations recorded and justified. |
| **(b)** | Laboratory controls shall include scientifically sound and appropriate specifications, standards, sampling plans, and test procedures. Include: (1) Conformity to written specifications for acceptance of components, containers, closures, labeling; (2) Conformance to written specifications for in-process materials; (3) Conformance to written descriptions of sampling procedures and specifications for drug products; (4) Calibration of instruments, apparatus, gauges, recording devices at suitable intervals. |

#### § 211.165 Testing and Release for Distribution

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | For each batch, appropriate laboratory determination of satisfactory conformance to final specifications, including identity and strength of each active ingredient, prior to release. Sterility/pyrogen testing may be completed as soon as possible for short-lived radiopharmaceuticals. |
| **(b)** | Appropriate laboratory testing for each batch required to be free of objectionable microorganisms. |
| **(c)** | Sampling and testing plans described in written procedures. |
| **(d)** | Acceptance criteria adequate to assure batches meet specifications and statistical quality control criteria. |
| **(e)** | Accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and reproducibility of test methods established and documented. |
| **(f)** | Drug products failing to meet standards shall be rejected. Reprocessing may be performed; reprocessed material must meet standards. |

#### § 211.166 Stability Testing

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written testing program designed to assess stability characteristics. Results used to determine storage conditions and expiration dates. Include: (1) Sample size and test intervals; (2) Storage conditions; (3) Reliable, meaningful, specific test methods; (4) Testing in same container-closure system; (5) Testing for reconstitution. |
| **(b)** | Adequate number of batches tested. Accelerated studies may support tentative expiration dates if full shelf life studies are being conducted. |
| **(c)** | For homeopathic drug products: written assessment of stability based on testing/examination for compatibility and marketing experience. |
| **(d)** | Allergenic extracts labeled "No U.S. Standard of Potency" exempt. |

#### § 211.167 Special Testing Requirements

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | For each batch of drug product purporting to be sterile and/or pyrogen-free, appropriate laboratory testing. |
| **(b)** | For each batch of ophthalmic ointment, testing for foreign particles and harsh or abrasive substances. |
| **(c)** | For each batch of controlled-release dosage form, testing for rate of release of each active ingredient. |

#### § 211.170 Reserve Samples

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Reserve sample of each active ingredient retained. Retention times: (1) 1 year after expiration date of last lot; (2) For radioactive drug products: 3 months if expiration ≤30 days, 6 months if >30 days; (3) For OTC drug products exempt from expiration dating: 3 years after distribution. |
| **(b)** | Reserve sample of each lot or batch of drug product retained. Stored under conditions consistent with labeling. Examined visually at least annually. Retention times: (1) 1 year after expiration date; (2) For radioactive: 3 months if ≤30 days, 6 months if >30 days; (3) For OTC exempt: 3 years after distribution. |

#### § 211.173 Laboratory Animals

Animals used in testing shall be maintained and controlled to assure suitability. Identified, with adequate records.

#### § 211.176 Penicillin Contamination

If reasonable possibility of cross-contamination with penicillin, non-penicillin drug product shall be tested. Shall not be marketed if detectable levels found.

### Subpart J – Records and Reports

#### § 211.180 General Requirements

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Production, control, or distribution records associated with a batch shall be retained for at least 1 year after expiration date, or 3 years after distribution for OTC products exempt from expiration dating. |
| **(b)** | Records for components, containers, closures, labeling retained for at least 1 year after expiration date, or 3 years after distribution of last lot. |
| **(c)** | Records readily available for authorized inspection during retention period. |
| **(d)** | Records may be retained as original or true copies. |
| **(e)** | Written records maintained so data can be used for evaluating, at least annually, quality standards of each drug product. |
| **(f)** | Procedures to assure responsible officials notified in writing of investigations, recalls, inspectional observations, or regulatory actions. |

#### § 211.182 Equipment Cleaning and Use Log

Written record of major equipment cleaning, maintenance, and use included in individual equipment logs. If dedicated equipment, logs not required if batches follow numerical order; records part of batch record. Persons performing and double-checking shall date and sign or initial log. Entries in chronological order.

#### § 211.184 Component, Drug Product Container, Closure, and Labeling Records

Records shall include:

- (a) Identity and quantity of each shipment, supplier name, lot numbers, receiving code, date of receipt.
- (b) Results of any test or examination and conclusions.
- (c) Individual inventory record and reconciliation of use.
- (d) Documentation of examination and review of labels and labeling.
- (e) Disposition of rejected materials.

#### § 211.186 Master Production and Control Records

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Prepared, dated, signed by one person and independently checked, dated, signed by second person. Written procedure shall be followed. |
| **(b)** | Include: (1) Name and strength, dosage form; (2) Name and weight/measure of each active ingredient; (3) Complete list of components; (4) Accurate statement of weight/measure; (5) Statement of calculated excess; (6) Theoretical weight/measure; (7) Theoretical yield, max/min percentages; (8) Description of containers, closures, packaging materials, specimen labels; (9) Complete manufacturing and control instructions. |

#### § 211.188 Batch Production and Control Records

Prepared for each batch. Include:

- (a) Accurate reproduction of master record, checked, dated, signed.
- (b) Documentation of each significant step: (1) Dates; (2) Identity of equipment and lines; (3) Specific identification of each batch of component or in-process material; (4) Weights and measures; (5) In-process and laboratory control results; (6) Inspection of packaging and labeling area; (7) Actual yield and percentage of theoretical yield; (8) Complete labeling control records; (9) Description of containers and closures; (10) Any sampling performed; (11) Identification of persons performing/supervising/checking; (12) Any investigation per §211.192; (13) Results of examinations per §211.134.

#### § 211.192 Production Record Review

All production and control records shall be reviewed and approved by QC before batch release. Any unexplained discrepancy or failure to meet specifications shall be thoroughly investigated, whether or not batch distributed. Investigation shall extend to other batches. Written record with conclusions and follow-up.

#### § 211.194 Laboratory Records

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Include complete data: (1) Description of sample, source, quantity, lot number, dates; (2) Statement of each method used, location of data establishing accuracy/reliability; (3) Weight or measure of sample; (4) Complete record of all data, graphs, charts, spectra; (5) Record of all calculations; (6) Statement of results and comparison with standards; (7) Initials/signature of person performing test and date; (8) Initials/signature of second person reviewing. |
| **(b)** | Records of any modification of established method, reason, and data verifying modification produces accurate/reliable results. |
| **(c)** | Records of testing and standardization of laboratory reference standards, reagents, standard solutions. |
| **(d)** | Records of periodic calibration of instruments, apparatus, gauges, recording devices. |
| **(e)** | Records of all stability testing per §211.166. |

#### § 211.196 Distribution Records

Contain name and strength, dosage form, name and address of consignee, date and quantity shipped, lot or control number. For compressed medical gas products, lot/control numbers not required.

#### § 211.198 Complaint Files

| Paragraph | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **(a)** | Written procedures for handling all written and oral complaints. Include review by QC of complaints involving possible failure to meet specifications, determination of need for investigation per §211.192. Review to determine if serious and unexpected adverse drug experience requiring reporting to FDA per §§310.305 and 514.80. |
| **(b)** | Written record of each complaint maintained. Retained until at least 1 year after expiration date or 1 year after complaint received, whichever longer. For OTC exempt, 3 years after distribution. Record includes name and strength, lot number, complainant, nature, reply. If investigation, findings and follow-up. If no investigation, reason and responsible person. |

### Subpart K – Returned and Salvaged Drug Products

#### § 211.204 Returned Drug Products

Returned drug products shall be identified and held. If conditions cast doubt on safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity, shall be destroyed unless examination/testing proves meets standards. May be reprocessed if subsequent product meets standards. Records maintained. If reason implicates associated batches, investigation per §211.192. Procedures in writing.

#### § 211.208 Drug Product Salvaging

Drug products subjected to improper storage conditions shall not be salvaged and returned to marketplace. Whenever question exists, investigation shall be conducted to determine if safety, identity, strength, quality, or purity has been adversely affected.

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Act** | Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act |
| **Acceptance criteria** | Numerical limits, ranges, or other criteria for tests used to accept or reject a unit, lot, or batch |
| **Active ingredient** | Component intended to furnish pharmacological activity or other direct effect |
| **Actual yield** | Quantity actually produced |
| **Batch** | Specific quantity intended to have uniform character and quality |
| **Component** | Any ingredient used in manufacture of a drug product |
| **Drug product** | Finished dosage form containing active drug ingredient |
| **Inactive ingredient** | Any component other than active ingredient |
| **In-process material** | Material produced for and used in preparation of drug product |
| **Lot** | Batch or specific identified portion of a batch |
| **Lot number** | Distinctive combination to identify complete history |
| **Manufacture** | Includes packaging, labeling, testing, and quality control |
| **Master production record** | Document with complete instructions for production |
| **Quality control unit** | Person or organizational element responsible for QC |
| **Representative sample** | Sample drawn based on rational criteria |
| **Strength** | Concentration or potency of drug substance |
| **Theoretical yield** | Quantity expected based on components used |
| **Tamper-evident package** | Package with indicator/barrier to entry that provides visible evidence of tampering |
