# The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945

## Document Overview

This file consolidates:

1. **The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** (23 of 1940) – As amended up to 31st December, 2016
2. **The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945** – As amended up to 31st December, 2016

The Act regulates the import, manufacture, distribution, and sale of drugs and cosmetics in India. The Rules provide the detailed framework for implementation.

---

# Part 1: The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940

## Chapter I: Introduction

### Section 1: Short Title, Extent and Commencement
- This Act may be called the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
- It extends to the whole of India.
- It came into force at once; Chapter III (Import) and Chapter IV (Manufacture, Sale, Distribution) take effect from dates appointed by notification.

### Section 2: Application of Other Laws Not Barred
- Provisions of this Act are in addition to and not in derogation of the Dangerous Drugs Act, 1930, and any other law in force.

### Section 3: Definitions
- **Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug**: Medicines intended for internal or external use for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation, or prevention of disease in human beings or animals, manufactured exclusively in accordance with formulae in authoritative books specified in the First Schedule.
- **Board**: Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board (Section 33C) or Drugs Technical Advisory Board (Section 5).
- **Cosmetic**: Any article intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled, sprayed on, introduced into, or otherwise applied to the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering appearance.
- **Drug**: Includes medicines for internal or external use, substances for diagnosis/treatment/prevention of disease, substances affecting structure or function of human body, components of drugs including empty gelatin capsules, and devices notified by Central Government.
- **Government Analyst**: Appointed under Section 20 or 33F.
- **Inspector**: Appointed under Section 21 or 33G.
- **Manufacture**: Any process for making, altering, ornamenting, finishing, packing, labelling, breaking up, or treating any drug or cosmetic with a view to sale or distribution; excludes compounding or dispensing in ordinary retail business.
- **Patent or proprietary medicine**: Formulations containing ingredients from authoritative books, or drugs ready for administration not included in Indian Pharmacopoeia.
- **Prescribed**: Prescribed by rules under this Act.

### Section 3A: Construction of References (Jammu and Kashmir)
- References to laws not in force or functionaries not in existence in J&K shall be construed as references to corresponding laws/functionaries in that State.

### Section 4: Presumption as to Poisonous Substances
- Substances specified as poisonous by rules are deemed poisonous for purposes of Chapter III, IV, or IVA.

## Chapter II: Drugs Technical Advisory Board, Central Drugs Laboratory, Drugs Consultative Committee

### Section 5: Drugs Technical Advisory Board
- Constituted by Central Government to advise on technical matters.
- Members include Director General of Health Services (Chairman), Drugs Controller India, Directors of Central Drugs Laboratory, Central Research Institute Kasauli, Indian Veterinary Research Institute, Presidents of Medical and Pharmacy Councils, etc.
- Term: 3 years; eligible for re-nomination/re-election.
- May constitute sub-committees.

### Section 6: Central Drugs Laboratory
- Established by Central Government under a Director.
- Functions: Testing/analysis of samples, prescribing functions, procedure for submission of samples, forms of reports, fees.
- Functions may be carried out at Central Research Institute, Kasauli, or other prescribed laboratories.

### Section 7: Drugs Consultative Committee
- Advisory committee to advise Central Government, State Governments, and DTAB on uniformity in administration.
- Consists of two Central Government representatives and one representative of each State Government.
- Meets when required.

### Section 7A: Sections 5 and 7 Not to Apply to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Drugs
- Nothing in Sections 5 and 7 applies to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drugs.

## Chapter III: Import of Drugs and Cosmetics

### Section 8: Standards of Quality
- **Standard quality** for drugs: Complies with standards in Second Schedule.
- For cosmetics: Complies with prescribed standards.
- Central Government may amend Second Schedule after consultation with Board and 3 months' notice.

### Section 9: Misbranded Drugs
A drug is misbranded if:
- Coloured, coated, powdered, or polished to conceal damage or make it appear of better therapeutic value.
- Not labelled in prescribed manner.
- Label/container bears false or misleading statement, design, or device.

### Section 9A: Adulterated Drugs
A drug is adulterated if:
- Consists of filthy, putrid, or decomposed substance.
- Prepared, packed, or stored under insanitary conditions.
- Container composed of poisonous or deleterious substance.
- Contains colour other than prescribed.
- Contains harmful or toxic substance.
- Any substance mixed to reduce quality or strength.

### Section 9B: Spurious Drugs
A drug is spurious if:
- Imported under a name belonging to another drug.
- Imitation of or substitute for another drug likely to deceive.
- Label bears fictitious manufacturer name.
- Substituted wholly or partly by another drug.
- Purports to be product of manufacturer of whom it is not truly a product.

### Section 9C: Misbranded Cosmetics
A cosmetic is misbranded if:
- Contains non-prescribed colour.
- Not labelled in prescribed manner.
- Label/container bears false or misleading statement.

### Section 9D: Spurious Cosmetics
A cosmetic is spurious if:
- Imported under name belonging to another cosmetic.
- Imitation of or substitute for another cosmetic likely to deceive.
- Label bears fictitious manufacturer name.
- Purports to be product of manufacturer of whom it is not truly a product.

### Section 10: Prohibition of Import of Certain Drugs or Cosmetics
No person shall import:
- Any drug or cosmetic not of standard quality.
- Any misbranded, adulterated, or spurious drug or cosmetic.
- Any drug or cosmetic requiring licence, otherwise than under licence.
- Any patent or proprietary medicine without true formula/list of active ingredients.
- Any drug claiming to cure/mitigate prescribed diseases.
- Any cosmetic containing unsafe/harmful ingredient.
- Any drug or cosmetic prohibited by rules.

**Exceptions**: Small quantities for examination, test, analysis, or personal use; Central Government may permit import of non-standard quality drugs with conditions.

### Section 10A: Power to Prohibit Import in Public Interest
- Central Government may prohibit import of any drug or cosmetic if it involves risk to human beings/animals, lacks therapeutic value, or contains ingredients without therapeutic justification.

### Section 11: Application of Sea Customs Law
- Sea customs law applies to drugs/cosmetics whose import is prohibited.
- Customs officers have powers to detain suspected packages and forward samples to Central Drugs Laboratory.

### Section 12: Power of Central Government to Make Rules
- Rules for import: specify drugs requiring licence, methods of test/analysis, standards, labelling, packing, storage, etc.

### Section 13: Offences
- **Import of adulterated/spurious drug or spurious cosmetic**: Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to ₹5,000.
- **Other prohibited imports**: Imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine up to ₹500, or both.
- **Contravention of Section 10A notification**: Imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to ₹5,000, or both.
- **Subsequent offences**: Enhanced penalties.
- Punishment in addition to penalties under customs law.

### Section 14: Confiscation
- Consignment liable to confiscation where offence under Section 13 committed.

### Section 15: Jurisdiction
- No court inferior to Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate First Class shall try offence under Section 13.

## Chapter IV: Manufacture, Sale and Distribution of Drugs and Cosmetics

### Section 16: Standards of Quality
- Same as Section 8 for manufacture/sale/distribution.

### Section 17: Misbranded Drugs
- Same as Section 9.

### Section 17A: Adulterated Drugs
- Same as Section 9A.

### Section 17B: Spurious Drugs
- Same as Section 9B.

### Section 17C: Misbranded Cosmetics
- Same as Section 9C.

### Section 17D: Spurious Cosmetics
- Same as Section 9D.

### Section 17E: Adulterated Cosmetics
- Same as Section 9A.

### Section 18: Prohibition of Manufacture and Sale of Certain Drugs and Cosmetics
No person shall manufacture for sale/distribution, sell, stock, exhibit, offer for sale, or distribute:
- Any drug not of standard quality, misbranded, adulterated, or spurious.
- Any cosmetic not of standard quality, misbranded, or spurious.
- Any patent or proprietary medicine without true formula/list of active ingredients.
- Any drug claiming to prevent/cure/mitigate prescribed diseases.
- Any cosmetic containing unsafe/harmful ingredient.
- Any drug or cosmetic in contravention of Chapter IV or rules.
- Any drug or cosmetic imported/manufactured in contravention of Act/rules.
- Any drug or cosmetic except under licence.

**Exceptions**: Small quantities for examination, test, analysis; Central Government may permit non-standard quality drugs with conditions.

### Section 18A: Disclosure of Name of Manufacturer
- Every person not being manufacturer or agent must disclose to Inspector the name, address, and particulars of person from whom drug/cosmetic acquired.

### Section 18B: Maintenance of Records and Furnishing Information
- Licensee under Section 18(c) shall keep records/registers and furnish information as required.

### Section 19: Pleas
- Ignorance of nature, substance, or quality is no defence.
- Drug not deemed misbranded/adulterated/spurious/below standard if innocuous substance added for manufacture/preparation or extraneous substance unavoidably intermixed.
- Person not manufacturer/agent not liable if acquired from licensed manufacturer/distributor/dealer, did not know and could not with reasonable diligence have ascertained contravention, and drug properly stored.

### Section 20: Government Analysts
- State Government may appoint Government Analysts with prescribed qualifications for specified areas/drugs/cosmetics.
- Central Government may also appoint.
- No person with financial interest in import/manufacture/sale of drugs/cosmetics shall be appointed.

### Section 21: Inspectors
- Central/State Government may appoint Inspectors with prescribed qualifications.
- Powers and duties as prescribed.
- No person with financial interest shall be appointed.
- Inspectors are public servants.

### Section 22: Powers of Inspectors
- Inspect premises where drugs/cosmetics manufactured, sold, stocked, exhibited, offered for sale, or distributed.
- Take samples.
- Search persons, places, vehicles, vessels, conveyances.
- Seize stock, records, documents, material objects.
- Require production of records/registers/documents.
- Exercise other necessary powers.

### Section 23: Procedure of Inspectors
- Tender fair price for samples; require written acknowledgment.
- If price refused, tender receipt in prescribed form.
- Divide sample into four portions (or three if taken from manufacturing premises), seal, mark, permit person to add seal/mark.
- Restore one portion to person; send one to Government Analyst; produce one to Court; send third to person whose name disclosed under Section 18A.
- If action under Section 22(c), use despatch, revoke order if no contravention, inform Judicial Magistrate if seized.

### Section 24: Persons Bound to Disclose Place of Manufacture
- Person in charge of premises must disclose place where drug/cosmetic manufactured or kept.

### Section 25: Reports of Government Analysts
- Government Analyst delivers signed report in triplicate to Inspector.
- Inspector delivers one copy to person from whom sample taken, another to person whose name disclosed under Section 18A, retains third.
- Report is evidence unless person notifies intention to adduce evidence in contravention within 28 days.
- Court may send sample to Central Drugs Laboratory for test/analysis; report conclusive.
- Cost paid by complainant or accused as Court directs.

### Section 26: Purchaser Enabled to Obtain Test or Analysis
- Any person or recognised consumer association may submit drug/cosmetic for test/analysis to Government Analyst on application and payment of fee.

### Section 26A: Power to Prohibit Manufacture in Public Interest
- Central Government may regulate, restrict, or prohibit manufacture, sale, or distribution of any drug/cosmetic if it involves risk, lacks therapeutic value, or contains ingredients without therapeutic justification.

### Section 26B: Power to Regulate in Emergency
- Central Government may regulate or restrict manufacture, sale, or distribution of essential drug in emergency due to epidemic or natural calamities.

### Section 27: Penalty for Manufacture, Sale, etc., of Drugs in Contravention
- **Adulterated/spurious drug likely to cause death or grievous hurt**: Imprisonment not less than 10 years, extendable to life; fine not less than ₹10 lakh or 3 times value of drugs confiscated, whichever more.
- **Adulterated drug (not covered above) or without valid licence**: Imprisonment not less than 3 years, extendable to 5 years; fine not less than ₹1 lakh or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Spurious drug (not covered above)**: Imprisonment not less than 7 years, extendable to life; fine not less than ₹3 lakh or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Other contraventions**: Imprisonment not less than 1 year, extendable to 2 years; fine not less than ₹20,000.
- Compensation to victim/relative from fine.

### Section 27A: Penalty for Manufacture, Sale, etc., of Cosmetics
- **Spurious/adulterated cosmetic**: Imprisonment up to 3 years; fine not less than ₹50,000 or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Other contraventions**: Imprisonment up to 1 year, or fine up to ₹20,000, or both.

### Section 28: Penalty for Non-Disclosure of Name of Manufacturer
- Imprisonment up to 1 year, or fine not less than ₹20,000, or both.

### Section 28A: Penalty for Not Keeping Documents
- Imprisonment up to 1 year, or fine not less than ₹20,000, or both.

### Section 28B: Penalty for Contravention of Section 26A
- Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to ₹5,000.

### Section 29: Penalty for Use of Government Analyst's Report for Advertising
- Fine up to ₹500.

### Section 30: Penalty for Subsequent Offences
- Enhanced penalties for repeat offences.

### Section 31: Confiscation
- Stock liable to confiscation.
- Implements, machinery, receptacles, packages, coverings, animals, vehicles, vessels, conveyances also liable in certain cases.
- Court may order confiscation of non-standard quality/misbranded/adulterated/spurious drugs/cosmetics.

### Section 31A: Application to Government Departments
- Chapter IV applies to Government departments except Section 31.

### Section 32: Cognizance of Offence
- Prosecution instituted by Inspector, gazetted officer, person aggrieved, or recognised consumer association.
- No court inferior to Court of Sessions shall try offence.

### Section 32A: Power of Court to Implead Manufacturer
- Court may proceed against manufacturer/agent if satisfied from evidence.

### Section 32B: Compounding of Certain Offences
- Offences under Section 13(1)(b), 28, 28A may be compounded by Central/State Government.
- Not available for subsequent offences.

### Section 33: Power of Central Government to Make Rules
- Rules for manufacture, sale, distribution: establishment of laboratories, qualifications of Government Analysts and Inspectors, methods of test/analysis, forms of licences, records, labelling, packing, etc.

### Section 33A: Chapter Not to Apply to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Drugs
- Chapter IV does not apply to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drugs, except as otherwise provided.

## Chapter IVA: Provisions Relating to Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs

### Section 33B: Application of Chapter IVA
- Applies only to Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs.

### Section 33C: Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board
- Constituted by Central Government to advise on technical matters.
- Members include Director General of Health Services, Drugs Controller India, Principal Officer dealing with Indian Systems of Medicine, Director of Central Drugs Laboratory, Government Analyst, Pharmacognosist, Phyto-chemist, representatives from Ayurvedic/Unani/Siddha Pharmacopoeia Committees, teachers, industry representatives, practitioners.
- Term: 3 years; eligible for re-nomination.

### Section 33D: Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Consultative Committee
- Advisory committee for uniformity in administration.
- Consists of two Central Government representatives and one representative of each State Government.

### Section 33E: Misbranded Drugs
- Same as Section 9 for Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani drugs.

### Section 33EE: Adulterated Drugs
- Same as Section 9A for Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani drugs.
- Explanation: Natural decomposition not deemed adulteration unless due to negligence and injurious to health.

### Section 33EEA: Spurious Drugs
- Same as Section 9B for Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani drugs.

### Section 33EEB: Regulation of Manufacture for Sale
- No person shall manufacture for sale/distribution any Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug except in accordance with prescribed standards.

### Section 33EEC: Prohibition of Manufacture and Sale of Certain Drugs
No person shall:
- Manufacture for sale/distribution any misbranded, adulterated, or spurious Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug.
- Manufacture any patent or proprietary medicine without true list of all ingredients.
- Manufacture any Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug in contravention of Chapter IVA or rules.
- Sell, stock, exhibit, offer for sale, or distribute any drug manufactured in contravention of Act/rules.
- Manufacture for sale/distribution any drug except under licence.

**Exceptions**: Vaidyas and Hakims manufacturing for their own patients; small quantities for examination, test, analysis.

### Section 33EED: Power to Prohibit Manufacture in Public Interest
- Central Government may prohibit manufacture, sale, or distribution of any Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug if it involves risk or lacks therapeutic value.

### Section 33F: Government Analysts
- Central/State Government may appoint Government Analysts with prescribed qualifications.
- No person with financial interest in manufacture or sale of any drug shall be appointed.

### Section 33G: Inspectors
- Central/State Government may appoint Inspectors with prescribed qualifications.
- Powers and duties as prescribed.
- No person with financial interest in manufacture or sale of any drug shall be appointed.
- Inspectors are public servants.

### Section 33H: Application of Sections 22, 23, 24, and 25
- These sections apply to Inspectors and Government Analysts under Chapter IVA with modifications.

### Section 33I: Penalty for Manufacture, Sale, etc., of Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Drugs
- **Misbranded/adulterated/without valid licence**: Imprisonment up to 1 year; fine not less than ₹20,000 or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Spurious**: Imprisonment not less than 1 year, extendable to 3 years; fine not less than ₹50,000 or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Contravention of Section 33EED notification**: Imprisonment up to 3 years; fine up to ₹50,000 or 3 times value, whichever more.
- **Other contraventions**: Imprisonment up to 6 months; fine not less than ₹10,000.

### Section 33J: Penalty for Subsequent Offences
- Enhanced penalties for repeat offences.

### Section 33K: Confiscation
- Stock liable to confiscation.

### Section 33KA: Disclosure of Name of Manufacturer
- Same as Section 18A.

### Section 33KB: Maintenance of Records
- Same as Section 18B.

### Section 33L: Application to Government Departments
- Chapter IVA applies to Government departments except Section 33K.

### Section 33M: Cognizance of Offences
- Prosecution instituted by Inspector with previous sanction of authority specified under Section 33G(4).
- No court inferior to Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate First Class shall try offence.

### Section 33N: Power of Central Government to Make Rules
- Rules for establishment of laboratories, qualifications of Government Analysts and Inspectors, methods of test/analysis, forms of licences, conditions, fees, packing, labelling, records, etc.

### Section 33O: Power to Amend First Schedule
- Central Government may add to or amend First Schedule after consultation with Board and 3 months' notice.

## Chapter V: Miscellaneous

### Section 33P: Power to Give Directions
- Central Government may give directions to State Governments for execution of Act.

### Section 34: Offences by Companies
- Every person in charge of and responsible to company at time of offence deemed guilty.
- Defence: Offence committed without knowledge or despite due diligence.
- Director, manager, secretary, or other officer with consent/connivance/neglect also deemed guilty.

### Section 34A: Offences by Government Departments
- Authority specified by Central Government or head of department deemed guilty.
- Defence: Offence committed without knowledge or despite due diligence.

### Section 34AA: Penalty for Vexatious Search or Seizure
- Inspector exercising powers without reasonable ground, vexatiously, or causing injury without reason: Fine up to ₹1,000.

### Section 35: Publication of Sentences
- Court may order publication of offender's name, residence, offence, and penalty at offender's expense.

### Section 36: Magistrate's Power to Impose Enhanced Penalties
- Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate First Class may pass any sentence authorised by Act.

### Section 36A: Certain Offences to be Tried Summarily
- Offences punishable with imprisonment up to 3 years (except certain offences) tried summarily.
- Magistrate may pass sentence up to 1 year.
- If nature of case requires higher sentence, Magistrate may recall witnesses and proceed as regular trial.

### Section 36AB: Special Courts
- Central/State Government may designate Courts of Sessions as Special Courts for trial of offences relating to adulterated/spurious drugs.

### Section 36AC: Offences to be Cognizable and Non-Bailable in Certain Cases
- Offences relating to adulterated/spurious drugs are cognizable and non-bailable.
- Bail restrictions apply.

### Section 36AD: Application of Code of Criminal Procedure
- CrPC applies to proceedings before Special Courts.
- Special Court deemed Court of Sessions.
- Public Prosecutor/Special Public Prosecutor appointment.

### Section 36AE: Appeal and Revision
- High Court may exercise powers under CrPC Chapters XXIX and XXX.

### Section 37: Protection of Action Taken in Good Faith
- No suit/prosecution/legal proceeding against person for anything done in good faith under Act.

### Section 38: Rules to be Laid Before Parliament
- Rules laid before Parliament for 30 days; may be modified/annulled.

# Part 2: The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945

## Part I: Preliminary

### Rule 1: Short Title, Extent and Commencement
- These Rules may be called the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.
- They extend to the whole of India.

### Rule 2: Definitions
- **Act**: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
- **Central Licence Approving Authority**: Drugs Controller, India, or Joint/Deputy Drugs Controller (India) appointed by Central Government.
- **Director**: Director of Central Drugs Laboratory.
- **Form**: Form set forth in Schedule A.
- **Homoeopathic medicines**: Drugs recorded in Homoeopathic provings or therapeutic efficacy established through long clinical experience as recorded in authoritative Homoeopathic literature; prepared according to Homoeopathic pharmacy techniques; includes combinations; excludes parenteral route.
- **Laboratory**: Central Drugs Laboratory.
- **Registered Homoeopathic medical practitioner**: Registered in Central or State Register of Homoeopathy.
- **Phytopharmaceutical drug**: Purified and standardised fraction with defined minimum four bio-active or phyto-chemical compounds of an extract of a medicinal plant or its part, for internal or external use; excludes parenteral route.
- **Registered medical practitioner**: Holding qualification under Indian Medical Degrees Act, 1916, or Indian Medical Council Act, 1956; registered in medical register (excluding Homoeopathic); dentist under Dentists Act, 1948; veterinary practitioner with approved qualifications.
- **Retail sale**: Sale other than wholesale dealing.
- **Sale by way of wholesale dealing**: Sale to person for purpose of selling again; includes sale to hospital, dispensary, medical, educational, or research institution.
- **Schedule**: Schedule to these Rules.
- **State Government** (in relation to Union Territory): Administrator.

## Part II: The Central Drugs Laboratory

### Rule 3: Functions
- Analyse/test samples sent under Section 11(2) or 25(4).
- Carry out other duties entrusted by Central Government or State Government (with Central Government permission and after consultation with DTAB).

### Rule 3A: Functions at Specific Institutes

  - Phase I: Form 44 with fee ₹50,000 and data as per Schedule Y.
  - Phase II: Based on Phase I data, fee ₹25,000.

- Ethics Committee informs licensing authority about approved cases and potential overlap.

### Rule 122-DAB: Compensation in Case of Injury or Death During Clinical Trial
- Free medical management for injury as long as required or till established injury not related to clinical trial (whichever earlier).
- Financial compensation for clinical trial related injury (over and above medical management expenses).
- For non-permanent injury: Compensation commensurate with nature of injury and loss of wages.
- For clinical trial related death: Nominee(s) entitled to financial compensation (over and above medical management expenses).
- Expenses on medical management and financial compensation borne by sponsor.
- Clinical trial related injury/death includes:
  - Adverse effect of investigational product(s).
  - Violation of approved protocol, scientific misconduct, or negligence by sponsor/representative/investigator.
  - Failure of investigational product to provide intended therapeutic effect where standard care available but not provided as per protocol.
  - Use of placebo where standard care available but not provided as per protocol.
  - Adverse effects due to concomitant medication excluding standard care necessitated as part of approved protocol.
  - Injury to child in-utero because of parent's participation in clinical trial.

### Rule 122-DAA: Registration of Ethics Committee
- Application for registration in accordance with Appendix VIII of Schedule Y.
- Licensing Authority may grant registration with conditions.
- Ethics Committee reviews and approves clinical trial, carries ongoing review.
- For serious adverse event: Analyze and forward opinion as per Appendix XII of Schedule Y.
- Allow inspectors/officials to enter premises, inspect records, provide replies.

### Rule 122-E: Definition of New Drug
- **New drug** includes:
  - Drug (including bulk drug substance or phytopharmaceutical drug) not used in country to any significant extent under prescribed/recommended conditions and not recognized as effective and safe by Licensing Authority for proposed claims (limited use with permission excepted).
  - Drug already approved by Licensing Authority for certain claims, now proposed to be marketed with modified or new claims (indications, dosage, dosage form including sustained release, route of administration).
  - Fixed dose combination of two or more drugs, individually approved earlier for certain claims, now proposed to be combined for first time in fixed ratio, or ratio of ingredients in already marketed combination proposed to be changed, with certain claims (indications, dosage, dosage form including sustained release, route of administration).
- All vaccines and Recombinant DNA (r-DNA) derived drugs shall be new drugs unless certified otherwise by Licensing Authority.
- New drug continues to be considered new drug for 4 years from date of first approval.

### Rule 122-EA: Definitions (Blood Banks)
- **Apheresis**: Process by which blood drawn from donor, after separating plasma/platelets/leucocytes, is re-transfused simultaneously into donor.
- **Autologous blood**: Blood drawn from patient for re-transfusion unto himself later.
- **Blood**: Whole human blood drawn from donor and mixed with anti-coagulant.
- **Blood bank**: Place/organization/unit/institution for collection, apheresis, storage, processing, distribution of blood and/or preparation, storage, distribution of blood components.
- **Blood component**: Drug prepared, obtained, derived, or separated from unit of blood drawn from donor.
- **Blood product**: Drug manufactured or obtained from pooled plasma of blood by fractionation, drawn from donors.
- **Cord blood bank**: Place/organization/unit for collection, processing, testing, banking, selection, release of cord blood units.
- **Donor**: Person who voluntarily donates blood after declared fit after medical examination, without accepting consideration in cash or kind (benefits/incentives like pins, plaques, badges, medals, certificates, time-off, membership, gifts of little intrinsic value not considered consideration).
- **Leucapheresis**: Process by which blood drawn from donor, after leucocyte concentrates separated, is re-transfused simultaneously into donor.
- **Plasmapheresis**: Process by which blood drawn from donor, after plasma separated, is re-transfused during same sitting into donor.
- **Plateletpheresis**: Process by which blood drawn from donor, after platelet concentrates separated, is re-transfused simultaneously into donor.
- **Professional donor**: Person who donates blood for valuable consideration, in cash or kind, from any source, on behalf of recipient-patient; includes paid donor or commercial donor.
- **Replacement donor**: Donor who is family friend or relative of patient-recipient.
- **Umbilical cord blood**: Whole blood including Hematopoietic Progenitor Cells collected from placental and/or umbilical cord blood vessels after umbilical cord clamped.

- Licensee of Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Tibb drug not in conformity with sub-rules (1B) and (1C) shall seek renewal of licence with appropriate name within 1 year from commencement of Drugs and Cosmetics (4th Amendment) Rules, 2015. Exception: Batch manufactured prior to commencement.
- Contravention of rules (1B) to (1D) punishable under Section 33-I.
- Manufacture under direction and supervision of competent technical staff (whole-time employee):
  - Degree in Ayurveda or Ayurvedic Pharmacy, Siddha or Unani system of medicine from University/State Government/Statutory Faculties/Councils/Boards recognized by Central/State Government; or
  - Diploma in Ayurveda, Siddha or Unani system of medicine granted by State Government; or
  - Graduate in Pharmacy/Pharmaceutical Chemistry/Chemistry/Botany from recognized University with 2 years' experience in manufacture of Ayurvedic/Siddha/Unani drugs; or
  - Vaid or Hakim registered in State Register of Practitioners of indigenous systems of medicines with 4 years' experience in manufacture; or
  - Qualification as Pharmacist in Ayurvedic (including Siddha) or Unani systems of medicines with 8 years' experience in manufacture as recognized by Central Government.
- Competent technical staff to direct and supervise manufacture of Ayurvedic drugs shall have qualifications in Ayurveda; Siddha drugs shall have qualification in Siddha; Unani drugs shall have qualification in Unani.

### Rule 157A: Maintaining Records of Raw Material
- Each licensed manufacturing unit shall keep record of raw material used in proforma in Schedule TA for all raw materials utilized in preceding financial year.
- Submit by 30th June of succeeding financial year to State Drug Licensing Authority of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drugs and to National Medicinal Plants Board or nominated agency.

### Rule 158: Conditions of Licence
- Maintain proper records of details of manufacture and tests of raw materials and finished products.
- Allow Inspector to enter, inspect premises, take samples of raw material and finished products, inspect records.
- Maintain Inspection Book in Form 35.

### Rule 158A: Conditions of Loan Licence
- Licence deemed cancelled/suspended if licence in Form 25D of manufacturing facility availed is cancelled/suspended.
- Comply with Act and Rules.
- Maintain proper records of details of manufacture and tests.
- Allow Inspector to inspect registers/records, supply information.
- Maintain Inspection Book in Form 35.

### Rule 158(B): Guidelines for Issue of License
- Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani Medicines under Section 3(a): As per text.

- For Balya/Poshak medicines: Photo-copy of textual reference of ingredients; safety studies if contains Schedule E(1) ingredients; textual indications: safety and effectiveness study not required.
- For Saundarya Prasadak: Photo-copy of textual reference; safety studies if contains Schedule E(1) ingredients; textual indications: safety and effectiveness study not required.

- State Drug Controller or Licensing Authority shall issue Free Sale Certificate in Form 26 E2-I (original license holder) or Form 26 E2-II (loan license) and Non-Conviction Certificate in Form 26 E3 or format specified by importing country/tenderer within 15 days from date of application after fulfilment of requisite formalities.

### Rule 159: Cancellation and Suspension of Licences
- Licensing Authority may cancel/suspend for non-compliance after opportunity to show cause (not less than 15 days from receipt of notice).

### Rule 160: Identification of Raw Materials
- Raw materials used in preparation of Ayurvedic (including Siddha) or Unani drugs shall be identified and tested, wherever tests available for genuineness; records maintained.

## Part XVI(A): Approval of Institutions for Carrying Out Tests on Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs

### Rule 160-A: Application for Grant of Approval
- Form 47 to Licensing Authority (approving authority).
- Inspection fee: ₹6,000 (drugs in First Schedule).
- Renewal after expiry within 6 months: ₹6,000 + ₹1,000 per month.

### Rule 160-B: Form of Approval and Conditions
- Approval in Form 48.
- Conditions:
  - Premises well lighted, properly ventilated, air-conditioned where necessary.
  - Adequate space with separate sections for Chemistry, Pharmacognosy, Ayurveda/Siddha/Unani, Microbiology, Sample Room, Office-cum-Record Room (minimum 800 sq. ft.).
  - List of experts (Chemist, Botanist, expert in Ayurveda/Siddha/Unani or Pharmacist with degree in Chemistry, Botany, Ayurveda/Siddha/Unani/Bachelor in Pharmacy with 2 years' experience).
  - Adequate equipment for tests (list of equipment recommended).
  - Testing under active direction of expert.
  - Testing by qualified persons.
  - Books of standards and reference books.
  - Standard Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs (Reference samples).

### Rule 160C: Duration of Approval
- Valid for 3 years from date granted/renewed.
- If application for renewal made before expiry or within 6 months after expiry (with additional inspection fee), approval continues until orders passed.
- Deemed expired if renewal application not made within 6 months after expiry.

### Rule 160D: Conditions of Approval
- Provide and maintain adequate staff, premises, equipment as specified in Rule 160-B.
- Proper storage facilities.
- Maintain records of tests for 2 years (expiry date) or 3 years (other substances).
- Allow Inspector to enter, inspect premises, equipment, testing procedures; inspect registers/records; supply information.
- Report changes in person-in-charge of testing, expert staff, material alterations in premises/equipment.
- Furnish reports in Form 50.
- If sample not of standard quality: Furnish approving authority and licensing authority of State where manufacturer/sender located with copy of test report with protocols.
- Comply with Act and Rules.
- Maintain inspection book.

### Rule 160-E: Inspection Before Grant of Approval
- Approving authority causes laboratory to be inspected jointly by Inspectors appointed/designated by Central and State Governments.
- Inspector examines premises, equipment, professional qualifications of expert staff.

### Rule 160F: Report of Inspection
- Detailed report to approving authority.

### Rule 160G: Procedure of Approving Authority
- If satisfied: Grant approval in Form 48.
- If not satisfied: Reject application, inform reasons, conditions to be satisfied.

### Rule 160H: Application After Rejection
- Within 6 months from rejection, applicant may inform conditions satisfied and deposit inspection fee ₹2,000.
- Approving authority may cause further inspection and grant approval in Form 48.

### Rule 160-I: Renewal
- On application, approving authority causes inspection.
- If satisfied conditions complied with: Issue certificate of renewal in Form 49.

### Rule 160J: Withdrawal and Suspension of Approvals
- Approving authority may withdraw/suspend approval for non-compliance after opportunity to show cause.
- Appeal to State Government within 3 months (disposed in consultation with panel of competent persons appointed by Department of Indian Systems of Medicine & Homoeopathy).

## Part XVII: Labelling, Packing and Limit of Alcohol in Ayurvedic (Including Siddha) or Unani Drugs

### Rule 161: Labelling, Packing and Limit of Alcohol
- Conspicuously displayed on label of container/package:
  - True list of all ingredients with botanical names of plant-based ingredients, plant part(s), form of ingredients, quantity of each ingredient, reference to method of preparation as detailed in standard text and Adikarana in authoritative books in First Schedule.
  - For Patent or Proprietary Ayurveda, Siddha or Unani drugs: True list of all ingredients with botanical names, plant part(s), form of ingredients, quantity.
  - If list large: May be printed separately and enclosed with packing; reference made on label.
- Container of medicine for internal use made from Schedule E(1) substance: 'Caution: To be taken under medical supervision' in English and Hindi.
- Particulars printed/written in indelible ink on label of innermost container and any other covering:
  - Name of drug (for Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani drugs: same as mentioned in authoritative books in First Schedule).
  - Correct statement of net content in metric system.
  - Name and address of manufacturer.
  - Manufacturing licence number (preceded by 'Manufacturing Licence Number' or 'Mfg. Lic. No.' or 'M.L.').
  - Distinctive batch number.
  - Date of manufacture (date of completion of final products or date of bottling/packing for issue).
  - 'Ayurvedic medicine' or 'Siddha medicine' or 'Unani medicine' as case may be.
  - 'FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY' if for external application.
  - Free samples: 'Physicians sample. Not to be sold' overprinted.
  - Preparations (Asavas) with high content of alcohol as base: Maximum size of packing as specified (Karpur Asava 15 ml, Ahiphenasava 15 ml, Margamadasava 15 ml).
  - Preparations containing self-generated alcohol: Maximum content of alcohol and maximum size of packing as specified (Mritsanjivani Sura 16% 30 ml; Mahadrakhashasava 16% 120 ml).
- Nothing requires labelling of transparent cover/wrapper/case used solely for packing, transport, delivery.

### Rule 161A: Exemption in Labeling and Packing for Export
- Labels/packages/containers for export may be adapted to meet law of country of export.
- Particulars required on container and every other covering:
  - Name of Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani drug (single or compound formulations).
  - Name, address of manufacturer and licence number.
  - Batch/lot number.
  - Date of manufacture with 'Best for use before'.
  - Main ingredients (if required by importing country).
  - For export.
- Code number as approved by Licensing Authority if required by consignee to not label with name/address of manufacturer.
- Rule 161 not applicable to medicine made up ready for treatment supplied on prescription of registered medical practitioner if labelled with name/address of supplier and 'For External Use Only' if for external application.

### Rule 161B: Date of Expiry and Shelf Life
- Date of expiry conspicuously displayed on label of container/package; after date of expiry, medicines not in circulation.
- Shelf life/date of expiry for Ayurvedic medicines as specified (Churna/Kwatha Churna: 2 years; Gutika: 3 years; Gutika with Rasa: 5 years; Rasaashadhies: No expiry; Asava Arista: No expiry; Avaleha: 3 years; Guggulu: 5 years; Mandura-Lauha: 10 years; Ghrita: 2 years; Taila: 3 years; Arka: 1 year; Dravaka/Lavana/Ksara: 5 years; Lepa Churna: 3 years; Dant Manjan Powder: 2 years; Dant Manjan Paste: 2 years; Lepa Guti: 3 years; Lepa Malahar/Liniment/Gels/Lotions/Creams: 3 years; Varti: 2 years; Ghana Vati: 3 years; Kupipakva Rasayan: No expiry; Parpati: No expiry; Sveta parpati: 2 years; Pisti and Bhasma: No expiry; Svarna/Rajata/Lauha/Mandura/Abhraka bhasma etc.: No expiry; Naga/Vanga/Tamra Bhasma: 5 years; Capsules soft gelatin: 3 years; Capsules hard gelatin: 5 years; Syrup/liquid oral: 3 years; Ear/Nasal drops: 2 years; Eye drops: 1 year; Khand/Granule/Pak: 3 years; Dhoopans-Inhalers: 2 years; Pravahi Kwatha: 3 years).

## Part XVIII: Government Analysts and Inspectors for Ayurvedic (Including Siddha) or Unani Drugs

### Rule 162: Duties of Inspectors
- Inspect premises licensed for manufacture at least twice a year.
- Send detailed report to controlling authority after each inspection.
- Take samples of drugs manufactured and send for test/analysis.
- Institute prosecutions.

### Rule 162A: Qualifications of State Drug Licensing Authority
- Ayurvedic/Siddha/Unani qualifications as per Schedule II of Indian Medicine Central Council Act, 1970 or B.Pharma (Ayurveda) of recognized University.
- At least 5 years' experience in Ayurveda/Siddha/Unani drug manufacturing or testing or enforcement or teaching/research on clinical practice.

### Rule 163: Procedure for Despatch of Sample to Government Analyst
- Sent by registered post or by hand in sealed package with memorandum in Form 18-A, outer cover addressed to Government Analyst.
- Package and outer cover marked with distinguishing number.
- Copy of memorandum and specimen impression of seal sent separately.
- On receipt, Government Analyst or authorised officer opens package and records condition of seals.
- After test/analysis, one copy of results supplied to sender in Form 13-A; copy sent simultaneously to Controlling Authority and Drugs Controller, India.

### Rule 163A: Functions of Pharmacopoeial Laboratory for Indian Medicine
- Functions as Central Drugs Laboratory for testing/analysis of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani Drugs.
- Functions:
  - Develop Pharmacopoeial standards and draft monographs and amendments with standardized methods.
  - Act as Central Appellate Drug Laboratory for testing.
  - Analyse/test samples sent under Section 11(2) or 25(4).
  - Maintain reference museum and herbarium for ASU drugs.
  - Run training centre for quality control methods.
  - Carry out other duties entrusted by Government of India.
