## Chunk AY-EDL-001 — Foreword and Preface

**Source pages:** 5–8  
**Topics:** essential medicines, AYUSH, procurement, public health, universal health coverage, EDL purpose

**Summary:**  
The Department of AYUSH, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India, has updated the Essential Drugs Lists (EDLs) for Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, and Homoeopathy (ASU&H). The EDL is a guiding tool for procurement and stocking of generic medicines in public health facilities. It aims to ensure availability, safety, efficacy, quality, and cost-effectiveness. The list was last formulated in 2000; this update (2013) incorporates stakeholder inputs, pharmacopoeias, formularies, and regional preferences. The EDL is not a standard list but a carefully selected range of system-wise medicines to guide need-based procurement.

**Key points:**
- EDL = Essential Drugs List for AYUSH systems.
- Guiding tool for procurement and stocking in dispensaries, hospitals, PHCs, CHCs, District Hospitals.
- Based on safety, efficacy, quality, cost-effectiveness.
- Updated from 2000 list; stakeholder consultations.
- Includes generic medicines from pharmacopoeia, formulary, authoritative texts.
- States can procure from EDL or their own resources.
- EDL-based procurement ensures transparency and optimal use of resources.

**Notable quotes:**
> “Essential medicines act as a powerful means to promote health equity and are selected with due regard to disease prevalence, evidence of efficacy and safety, and comparative cost-effectiveness.”

> “AYUSH Essential Drug Lists were last formulated in the year 2000 and their updating was overdue.”

## Chunk AY-EDL-003 — Guidelines for Use of EDL

**Source pages:** 10  
**Topics:** procurement guidelines, licensing, quality standards, unit pack sizes, storage, dispensing

**Summary:**  
Guidelines for using the EDL: prefer EDL for selection and procurement; ensure medicines meet licensing and quality standards under Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940; select medicines based on priority health needs; procure in standard unit pack sizes; use small dispensable bags for 3–4 days; indications/contraindications are illustrative; distribute under medical supervision; care for storage and expiry dates.

**Key points:**
1. EDL preferred for selection and procurement.
2. Ensure genuineness and quality standards (Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940).
3. Select based on priority health needs and collective recommendations of in-service doctors.
4. Procure in standard unit pack sizes (weekly requirement).
5. Use small dispensable bags for 3–4 days.
6. Indications, contraindications, precautions are general/illustrative.
7. Distribute under medical supervision.
8. Take due care of storage conditions and expiry dates.
9. Spoiled or expired medicines should not be used or dispensed.

**Notable quotes:**
> “It is imperative to ensure that medicines being procured are genuine and meet the licensing requirements and quality standards as mentioned in the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Rules thereunder.”
