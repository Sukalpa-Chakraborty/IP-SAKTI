# FDA Good Guidance Practices Final Rule (2000) and Related Federal Register Documents

## Document Overview

This file consolidates three documents published in the Federal Register on September 18, 2000:

1. **FAA Amendment to Class E Airspace; Fairfield, IA** – Direct final rule; confirmation of effective date.

The primary content is the FDA Good Guidance Practices final rule. The FAA and PDMA sections are included for completeness.

## Part 2: FDA Good Guidance Practices – Final Rule

### Agency
Food and Drug Administration, Department of Health and Human Services.

### Action
Final rule.

### Summary
FDA is amending its administrative regulations to codify policies and procedures for the development, issuance, and use of guidance documents. This action complies with the Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act of 1997 (Modernization Act). The intended effect is to make procedures clear to the public.

### Dates
Effective **October 19, 2000**.

### Contact
LaJuana D. Caldwell, Office of Policy (HF-27), Food and Drug Administration, 5600 Fishers Lane, Rockville, MD 20857, 301-827-7010.

### I. Background

- Section 405 of the Modernization Act (Public Law 105-115) added statutory provisions on guidance documents to the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (the act) in section 701(h) (21 U.S.C. 371(h)).
- FDA proposed changes to 21 CFR part 10 on February 14, 2000 (65 FR 7321).
- Public comments were accepted until May 1, 2000.

#### B. Guidance Documents Resulting From International Negotiations

- FDA participates in international harmonization efforts (e.g., ICH).
- Draft documents negotiated internationally may not comply with all provisions of §10.115(i)(1) and (i)(2).
- Final rule modified: When issuing draft guidelines from international negotiations, FDA need not apply §10.115(i)(1) and (i)(2).
- Final guidelines from international negotiations must comply with all provisions of §10.115(i).

**§10.115 – Good Guidance Practices**

- (a) What are good guidance practices? GGP's are FDA's policies and procedures for developing, issuing, and using guidance documents.
- (b) What is a guidance document?
  - (1) Documents prepared for FDA staff, applicants/sponsors, and public describing agency's interpretation or policy on a regulatory issue.
  - (2) Include documents relating to design, production, labeling, promotion, manufacturing, testing; processing, content, evaluation/approval of submissions; inspection and enforcement policies.
  - (3) Do not include internal FDA procedures, agency reports, general information documents, speeches, journal articles, editorials, media interviews, press materials, warning letters, memoranda of understanding, or communications to individual persons or firms.
- (c) Special terms:
  - (1) Level 1 guidance documents: initial interpretations; changes in interpretation/policy more than minor; complex scientific issues; highly controversial issues.
  - (2) Level 2 guidance documents: existing practices or minor changes; all not classified as Level 1.
  - (3) "You" refers to all affected parties outside FDA.
- (d) Are you or FDA required to follow a guidance document?
  - (1) No. Not legally enforceable; do not bind public or FDA.
  - (2) You may use alternative approach complying with statutes and regulations; FDA willing to discuss.
  - (3) Guidance represents current thinking; FDA employees may depart only with justification and supervisory concurrence.
- (e) Can FDA use other means to communicate new policy? No, must follow GGP's when communicating new regulatory expectations to broad public audience for first time.
- (f) How can you participate?
  - (1) Provide input on guidance under development.
  - (2) Suggest areas for development.
  - (3) Submit drafts; mark "Guidance Document Submission"; send to Dockets Management Branch.
  - (4) Suggest revision or withdrawal of existing guidance.
  - (5) Annual list of possible topics published in Federal Register and Internet.
  - (6) Contact center or office responsible.
  - (7) If FDA agrees to draft/revise, you can participate under (g).
- (g) FDA's procedures:
  - (1) Level 1:
    - (i) Early input before draft.
    - (ii) After draft: publish notice in Federal Register; post on Internet; make hard copy; invite comment.
    - (iii) May hold public meetings or present to advisory committee.
    - (iv) Review comments; prepare final version incorporating changes.
  - (2) No prior public participation if not feasible or appropriate.
  - (3) Procedures when no prior participation:
    - (i) After preparing: publish notice; post on Internet; make hard copy; immediately implement; invite comment.
    - (ii) Review comments; revise when appropriate.
  - (4) Level 2:
    - (i) After preparing: post on Internet; make hard copy; immediately implement unless indicated otherwise; invite comment.
    - (ii) Review comments; revise when appropriate.
  - (5) You can comment at any time. FDA will revise when appropriate.
- (h) How to submit comments?
  - (1) Send to Dockets Management Branch (HFA-305), 5630 Fishers Lane, rm. 1061, Rockville, MD 20852.
  - (2) Identify docket number or title.
  - (3) Comments available to public per §10.20(i).
- (i) Standard elements:
  - (1) Must include: term "guidance"; issuing center(s)/office(s); activity and people to whom applies; prominent nonbinding statement; date of issuance; note if revision and identify replaced document; word "draft" if draft.
  - (2) Must not include mandatory language such as "shall," "must," "required," unless describing statutory or regulatory requirement.
  - (3) Draft guidance from international negotiations need not apply (i)(1) and (i)(2); final must comply.
- (j) Who can approve issuance? Each center/office must have written procedures; approval by appropriate senior officials.
- (k) How will FDA review and revise existing guidance? Periodic review; revise when statute/regulations change; you may suggest revisions.
- (l) How will FDA ensure staff follow GGP's? Training; centers/offices monitor.
- (m) How to get copies? Hard copy and Internet.
- (n) How will FDA keep you informed? Internet list updated within 30 days; annual comprehensive list in Federal Register; lists include name, dates, how to obtain.
- (o) What if you believe someone at FDA is not following GGP's? Contact supervisor; then next highest; contact ombudsman; may ask Office of Chief Mediator and Ombudsman.

## Key RAG Retrieval Notes

- **FDA GGP Final Rule:** Effective October 19, 2000. Codifies policies for guidance documents.
- **Guidance Document Definition:** Documents describing FDA's interpretation or policy on regulatory issues; not binding.
- **Level 1 vs Level 2:** Level 1 – initial interpretations, significant changes, complex science, controversial. Level 2 – existing practices, minor changes.
- **Public Participation:** Level 1 – draft published for comment; Level 2 – posted and implemented, comments invited.
- **Standard Elements:** Must include "guidance", issuing office, applicability, nonbinding statement, date, revision note, "draft" if draft.
- **Nonbinding Effect:** Guidance does not bind FDA or public; alternative approaches allowed if compliant with law.
- **Dispute Resolution:** Contact supervisor, ombudsman, Chief Mediator and Ombudsman.
- **Conforming Amendments:** Replaced "guidelines" with "guidance documents" across 21 CFR parts.
- **International Negotiations:** Draft documents from international negotiations exempt from certain standard elements; final must comply.
- **FAA Amendment:** Class E airspace, Fairfield, IA; effective November 30, 2000; updates FAA Order 7400.9G to 7400.9H.
- **PDMA Public Hearing:** Docket No. 92N-0297; relates to wholesale distribution and blood derivatives.
