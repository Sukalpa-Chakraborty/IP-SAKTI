# FSSAI Guidance and Notification on Nutraceuticals – An Insight

**Author:** Dr Swathi Putta, UGC-PDFWM, Pharmacology Division, A U College of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Andhra University

## Overview

A nutraceutical is a food or food related component that intends for health benefits even in treatment and prevention of ailments. Many people rely on dietary supplements as alternative therapy – medicinal herbs and nutraceuticals. Rising demand worldwide.

- Annual growth of nutraceuticals in India: ~25%, similar to Japan
- Many new companies began manufacturing nutraceuticals in India
- Pharma companies and FMCG suppliers key to success of Indian nutraceuticals market
- Market divided into functional foods and beverages and dietary supplements
- Over 64% of nutraceuticals market in India occupied with vitamin and mineral supplements
- Market growth: $1 billion in 2008 → $1.8 billion in 2013 → crossed $2 billion in 2014 → expected to top $4 billion by 2018

## Regulatory Framework

Nutraceuticals are considered as foods by the FSS Act, 2006, Rules and Regulations, 2011.

FSSAI has issued regulations for:
- licensing and registration of food business
- manufacturing, packing and labelling
- food product standard, food additives
- prohibition and restriction on sales
- contaminants, toxins and residues
- laboratory and sampling analysis

FSS Act consists of 21 chapters – Article 22 (4th Article context) covers nutraceuticals, dietary supplements, functional foods – these products can be produced / manufactured, marketed (sold) or distributed (import) by any company.

Nutraceuticals Regulations, 2016, under 'General Requirements':
- (21) mere combination of vitamins and minerals formulated in tablets, capsules, syrup formats shall not be covered in any categories of these regulations except when vitamins and minerals are added [as specified].

FSS issued regulations for food or health supplements, nutraceuticals, foods for special dietary uses, foods for special medical purpose, functional foods, and novel food in 2015.

Novel foods claimed – must include:
- (a) chemical composition of the engineered food
- (b) surface modification / surface chemistry
- (c) primary particle size
- (d) solubility
- (e) digestibility
- (f) amount of nanomaterial if any in the food product
- (g) specific claim, if applicable

## Schedules – Permitted Ingredients (Food Act)

| Schedule | Content |
|----------|---------|
| Schedule I | Vitamins and minerals |
| Schedule II | Essential amino acids and other nutrients |
| Schedule IV | List of plants and botanical ingredients |
| Schedule VI | List of ingredients as nutraceuticals |
| Schedule VII | List of strains as probiotics |
| Schedule VIII | List of prebiotic compounds |

Rules:
- No ingredient other than those in Schedule VI shall be used as nutraceuticals with standardisation to marker compounds specified and at daily usage levels specified therein
- Only nutraceuticals or extracts of ingredients specified in Schedule IV can be used in nutraceuticals
- Ingredients of plant or botanical origin in Schedule IV and Schedule VI may be used either in given form or extract, subject to extractive ratios in relation to daily usage value
- Only additives in Schedule VA or VE or VF should be used for nutraceuticals formulation

## FSSAI Notice 31 Dec 2018 – Third Notification on Nutraceutical Foods Regulation

1. Allowing use of ingredient 'Vitamin D3' from lichen (*Cladonia rangiferina*) as vegetable source in products under Nutraceutical Regulations, until proposed amendments are finalised and notified
2. Subject to compliance, mustard powder will continue to be used as ingredient
3. Discontinue certain ingredients mentioned in notice
4. Allow FBOs to sell existing formulations with simple combinations of vitamins and minerals, only up to one recommended dietary allowance, in dosage forms such as tablets, capsules, syrups – effective for 3 months from notification date, or until further orders, whichever earlier

## 14 Ingredients Directed to Stop / Discontinue – Lacking Scientific Data for Safe Usage

FSSAI directed to discontinue:
1. Raspberry ketone
2. Silica
3. Angelica sinensis
4. Paullinia cupana
5. Saw palmetto
6. Notoginseng [notoginsing in source]
7. Chlorella growth factor
8. Pine bark extract from *Pinus radiata*
9. Pine bark extract from *Pinus pinaster*
10. Vitamin D3-veg
11. Chaga extract
12. *Oxalobacter formigenes*
13. Phytavail iron
14. Tea tree oil

Also asked to discontinue as health supplements (use in products not prohibited, but cannot claim as supplement or nutraceuticals):
- Artichoke [antichoke in source], kale powder, *Salvia hispanica*, cashew fruit, passion fruit, kiwi fruit extracts, broccoli and enzymes including pectinase and xylanase

No further manufacturing of products using these ingredients until assessed and approved. FBOs directed to furnish information and data within one month.

## Labelling Requirements

Label of nutraceutical products should mention:
- "Nutraceuticals"
- Recommended Usage
- Warning for risk of excess consumption

Prohibitions on labelling claims:
- Cure of disease claims, e.g. "Prevents bone fragility in post menopausal women"
- Implied cures through pictures, vignettes or symbols

## Conclusion

Increased demand evidences possible success in prevention and treatment of diseases. Need regulatory authorities on product quality and safety to minimise adverse events, toxicity, adulteration, misuse, overdose. As food products move country to country, maintaining safety and quality per respective government guidelines is important. New and existing companies should follow FSSAI guidance.
