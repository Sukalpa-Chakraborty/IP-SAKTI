# Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP): An Overview

**Source PDF:** `GMP.pdf`
**Author:** Dr. Jill Shukla

## Definition

GMP is that part of Quality Assurance which ensures that products are consistently produced and controlled to the quality standard appropriate to their intended use and as required by the marketing authorization.

Good Manufacturing Practices (GMPs) are regulations describing methods, equipment, facilities, and controls required for producing:
- Human and veterinary products
- Medical devices
- Processed food

Usually seen as "cGMP" – where c = current, to emphasize expectations are dynamic. Designed to minimize risks in pharmaceutical production that cannot be eliminated through testing final product.

Regulations with force of law require manufacturers, processors, packagers of drugs, medical devices, some food, and blood to take protective steps to ensure products are safe, pure, effective.

Require quality approach to manufacturing, enabling minimization / elimination of contamination, mix-ups, errors. Protects consumer from ineffective or dangerous product.

GMP addresses: record keeping, personnel qualifications, sanitation, cleanliness, equipment verification, process validation, complaint handling.

In short: GMP makes difference between nearly right and exactly right.

## Why GMP?

- Final testing cannot ensure quality, efficacy and safety
- Final testing may not detect contamination, error, etc.
- Conformance to predetermined specification
- Minimize contamination e.g. microbial contamination
- Eliminate error
- Produce product of consistent quality
- Government requirement
- Ensure quality product
- Reduce rejects, recalls
- Satisfied customers
- Company image and reputation
- Maintain manufacturing consistency

## Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)

- Portion of FDA
- Collection of final regulations published in Federal Register (daily published records of proposed rules, final rules, meeting notices, etc.)
- Divided into 50 titles
- Current GMP regulations appear in Part 210 (Title 21) published by USFDA

## Evolution of GMP

- 1963: first GMP publication – USA
- 1971: first revision
- 1978: Major revision
- Current regulation in Part 210 (Title 21) CFR by USFDA

## Main Risks Without GMP

- Unexpected contamination causing damage to health or death
- Incorrect labels – patients receive wrong medicine
- Insufficient or too much active ingredient – ineffective treatment or adverse effects

## Principles of GMP

- Design and construct facilities and equipment properly
- Follow written procedures and instructions
- Document work
- Validate work
- Monitor facilities and equipment
- Write step-by-step operating procedures and work instructions
- Design, develop and demonstrate job competence
- Protect against contamination
- Control components and product related processes
- Conduct planned and periodic audits

## PART I – Good Manufacturing Practices for Premises and Materials

### 1. General Requirements

#### 1.1 Location and surroundings
Factory buildings shall be situated / have measures to:
- Avoid risk of contamination from external environment
- Avoid factories producing obnoxious odors, fumes, dust, smoke, chemical or biological emissions

#### 1.2 Building and premises
Designed to permit manufacturing in hygienic conditions:
- Compatible with other manufacturing operations
- Adequately provided with working space
- Avoid risk of mix-ups, contamination
- Designed to avoid entry of pests, birds, rodents etc. Interior surface smooth, free from cracks
- Production and dispensing area well lighted, ventilated, proper air handling
- Proper drainage as specified for product categories
- Walls and floors free from cracks and open joints for easy cleaning

#### 1.3 Water system
- Validated system for treatment to render water potable
- Potable water for all operations except cleaning/washing. Storage tanks cleaned periodically, records maintained

#### 1.4 Disposal of waste
- Sewage/effluents per Environment Pollution Control Board
- Bio-medical waste per Bio-Medical Waste Rules 1996
- Records maintained, proper storage of waste

### 2. Warehousing area
- Adequate areas for various categories
- Designed for good storage conditions
- Quarantine area clearly demarcated, restricted to authorized persons
- Separate sampling area for active raw materials and excipients

### 3. Production area
- Design to allow preferably uni-flow with logical sequence
- Separate facilities for contamination-causing / potent products: β-lactam, sex hormones, cytotoxic substances
- Service lines well designed, colour-identified, flow direction marked

### 4. Ancillary areas
- Rest/refreshment rooms separate
- Changing, clothes storage, washing, toilet – accessible and adequate
- Animal housing isolated per Rule 150-C(3) of D&C Rules 1945

### 5. Quality control area
- QC labs independent of production; separate areas for physico-chemical, biological, microbiological, radio-isotope analysis
- Adequate space to avoid mix-ups/cross-contamination
- Design considers construction materials, ventilation, separate AHUs, radioisotope areas, water of appropriate quality

### 6. Personnel
- Manufacture/testing under direct supervision of qualified technical staff
- QA & QC personnel qualified and experienced
- Adequate numbers proportional to workload
- Training appropriate to duties

### 7. Health, clothing and sanitation
- Personnel handling beta-lactam tested for penicillin sensitivity; handlers of sex hormones, cytotoxic, potent drugs periodically examined
- Pre-employment medical including eye exam; free from TB, skin, communicable diseases
- Clothing: protection of operator/product, special protective clothing for highly potent/risk products, no movement between different product areas, garments cleaned
- Health exams on recruitment and regularly
- Training: induction including personal hygiene, written hand-wash procedures before entering manufacturing area
- Illness: staff with illness/open lesions not to handle starting materials, intermediates, finished products

### 8. Manufacturing operations & controls
- Operations by trained personnel under approved technical staff approved by Licensing Authority
- Materials/containers labelled: product name, batch number/size, manufacturing stages
- Non-aseptic products free from pathogens like Salmonella, E. coli, Pyocyanea, etc.
- Prevent mix-up/cross-contamination via air-handling, pressure differential, segregation, status labelling, cleaning; SOPs and records

### 9. Sanitation in manufacturing premises
- Cleaned per validated procedures
- Areas not used as storage/thoroughfare
- Routine sanitation program drawn up and observed
- Well-lighted production area especially for visual on-line controls

### 10. Raw materials
- Inventory + records per Schedule U
- Appropriate storage, FIFO / FEFO (first in / first expiry – first out)
- Each batch checked, storage area labelled; separate areas for "under test", "approved", "rejected"; dry, clean, orderly, controlled temp/humidity where needed
- Only QC-released materials within shelf-life used; formulation shelf-life not to exceed active raw material
- Containers on raised platforms/racks, not floor

### 11. Equipment
- Located, designed, constructed, adapted, maintained to suit operations; minimize errors, permit cleaning/maintenance, avoid cross-contamination, dust build-up
- Balances/measuring equipment of appropriate range/accuracy/precision in stores, production, IPC; calibrated per SOP, records
- Non-toxic / edible grade lubricants where possible
- Defective equipment removed or labelled

### 12. Documentation and records
- Essential part of QA, covers all GMP aspects
- Aim: define specs for materials, manufacturing/control methods, ensure personnel know release decision info, audit trail for defective batch investigation
- Approved, signed, dated by authorized persons; design/review/control per rules
- Records made at time of operation; traceable; retained at least 1 year after finished product expiry

### 13. Labels and other printed materials
- For identification and use; bright colours, legible
- All containers/equipment labelled; colour-coded labels
- Printed packaging/leaflets stored separately to avoid mix-up
- Packaging/labelling/release after QC approval
- Receipt/use records maintained

### 14. Quality assurance
- Understand QA/QC issues, requirements on organization, procedures, processes, resources, develop corrective actions
- Principles: wide-ranging concept covering all matters influencing product quality; totality of arrangements to ensure quality for intended use; incorporates GMP + product design/development
- QA system requirements: correct development, managerial responsibilities, SOPs, correct starting materials, stage controls, correct processing/check before release, release by authorized person, storage/distribution, self-inspection

### 15. Self-inspection and Quality audit
Self-inspection team + audit to improve system:
- Ensures compliance, continuous improvement
- Covers production/QC to detect GMP shortcomings
- Recommends corrective action with timetable
- Additional inspections on recalls, repeated rejections, announced NRA inspections

Written instructions cover: personnel, premises/facilities, building/equipment maintenance, starting/finished storage, equipment, production/IPC, QC, documentation, sanitation/hygiene, validation/revalidation, calibration, recall, complaints, label control, previous inspection results/corrective steps

Quality Audit: examination/assessment of quality system to improve; by outside/independent specialist or management team; also for suppliers/contractors. Types: Internal, External, Regulatory

### 16. Quality Control System
- Concerned with sampling, specification, testing, documentation, release; involved in all quality decisions, not just lab
- Establish, evaluate, validate, implement QC procedures/methods
- Batches released after QC certification; reference/retained sample per batch
- Lab divided into chemical, instrumentation, microbiological, biological testing; adequate reference sample storage
- Authorized dated specs for materials, products, reagents
- Stability studies to assign shelf-life; records
- QA in-charge investigates complaints
- Instruments calibrated, procedures validated before routine use + periodic
- Pharmacopoeias, reference standards/spectra, books available

### 17. Specifications
- Raw & packaging material
- Product containers & closures
- In-process & bulk products
- Finished product
- Preparation of containers & closures

### 18. Master formula records
For all manufacturing procedures per product, batch size. Includes:
- Product name + reference code, patent/proprietary + generic name, dosage form description
- Name, quantity, reference of all starting materials
- Expected final yield & principal equipment
- Detailed SOP with time per step
- Storage, containers, labelling requirements
- Packaging detail + specimen labels

### 19. Packaging records
Authorized packaging instructions per product/pack size/type:
- Product name/description, volume in final container
- Complete packaging materials list with quantities, size, type
- Packaging operations description
- In-process controls detail

### 20. Batch packaging record
- Kept per batch/part batch, based on packaging instructions, designed to avoid transcription error
- Before packaging: checks/record that equipment/workstations clear of previous products/documents/materials, clean and suitable

### 21. Batch processing records
Based on approved master formula. Before processing: clearance checks recorded. During processing record at time of action, dated/signed:
- Product name, batch number, date/time commencement
- Operator initials per significant step, checker where appropriate
- Batch no., equipment used, IPQC records, yield at critical stages, special problems

### 22. SOPs and Records

22.1 Receipt: SOPs for receipt of raw/primary/printed packaging, internal labelling, quarantine, storage, instruments/equipment

22.2 Sampling: method, equipment, anti-contamination precautions, quantity/pooling, sterile/hazardous precautions

22.3 Batch numbering: detail per intermediate/bulk/finished batch, applied to processing + packaging stages, includes allocation date, product identity, batch size

22.4 Testing: written procedures for testing at stages, methods/equipment, recorded tests

### 23. Reference samples
- Each lot of active ingredient sufficient for all tests except sterility/pyrogens/BET retained 3 months after expiry of last batch from that ingredient
- Finished samples stored in same/simulated marketed containers

### 24. Reprocessing and recovery
- Written QA-approved procedures specifying conditions/limitations; validated
- Reprocessing authorized/recorded, investigated, stability evaluated
- Residue recovery if permitted per master records in subsequent batches

### 25. Distribution records
- Batch tested/approved/released before dispatch; random pre-dispatch inspection
- Records allow traceability to retail for recall

### 26. Validation and Process validation
- Essential GMP part per pre-defined protocols; written report documented
- Periodic revalidation; critical processes validated prospectively/retrospectively
- New master formula/method demonstrated suitable; significant changes (equipment/materials) validated

### 27. Product Recalls
- Prompt effective recall system informing stockists, wholesalers, suppliers to retail in shortest period via print/electronic media
- Distribution records available; effectiveness evaluated; recalled products segregated securely pending decision

### 28. Complaints and Adverse Reactions
- All quality complaints reviewed/recorded per written procedures, investigated by designated personnel, records of investigation/remedial action
- Serious ADRs reported forthwith to Licensing Authority with comments/documents
- Written recall procedures

### 29. Site Master File
Succinct factual GMP document covering:
- General information, personnel, premises, equipment, sanitation, documentation, production
- QC: quality system + QC activities, release procedure
- Loan licence: GMP compliance assessment
- Distribution, complaints, recall
- Self-inspection
- Export: products, complaints/recall if any

## Different Subparts of Part I

- PART IA: Sterile products, parenteral (SVP/LVP), sterile ophthalmic
- PART IB: Oral solid dosage (tablets/capsules) – general Part-I +: General, Sifting/mixing/granulation, Compression, Coating, Hard gelatin filling, Printing, Packaging (strip/blister)
- PART IC: Oral liquids (syrups, elixirs, emulsion, suspension)
- PART ID: Topical/external (creams, ointments, pastes, emulsions, lotions, solutions, dusting powders)
- PART IE: Metered dose inhalers (MDI)
- PART IF: API / Bulk drugs – general +: building/civil works, utilities, equipment design/size/location, in-process controls, containers/closures

## Common Problems in GMP Execution

1. Organization: lack of commitment, resources
2. Layout: no quarantine, insufficient EM, cracked floor
3. Equipment: no calibration, no balance check, rusty, improper parts storage
4. Lab testing: poor reference standard keeping, poor data recording, unlabelled reagents
5. Documentation: no signature/countercheck, improper corrections, no written procedure, incomplete complaints, outdated training, no review
6. Labelling: unclear status, poor control, insecure release labels, inadequate reconciliation, unlabeled defective equipment
7. Validation: insufficient validation, insufficient raw data, no programme

## GMPs of Different Countries Compared

At high level similar – require proper design/maintenance/cleaning, approved SOPs, independent Quality unit, trained personnel/management.

- WHO GMP used in 100+ countries, mainly developing world
- EU-GMP more compliance requirements than WHO; USFDA also stricter
- Australia, Canada, Japan, Singapore highly developed
- UK: Medicines Act 1968, "Orange Guide" – Rules and Guidance for Pharmaceutical Manufacturers and Distributors

## Enforcement

- EU: National Regulatory Agencies (e.g. UK MHRA)
- Australia: TGA
