## Abstract

Google Patents (www.google.com/patents) includes over 8 million full-text patents (as of 2014). Works like Google search. Lets users search USPTO, EPO, etc. Study overviews how to use, advanced techniques not well-documented, suggestions for improvement, compares citation counts for LIS journals. Concludes free alternative / complement to other patent databases. Advantages: easy interface, fast engine, convenient PDF images, fast PDF downloads.

**Keywords:** Google Patents; Patent analysis; Patentometric

## Introduction

Patents: detailed documents allowing registration nationally/internationally for specified time. Important for knowledge production/commercialization, competitiveness, consumer value. Accessibility important for research, innovation, development.

Google Patents indexes patents and applications (not yet granted) from USPTO and EPO, taken from their databases. USPTO/EPO grants are public domain. Located at www.google.com/patents. Launched Dec 14 2006 using Google Books technology – scrolling, zoom (Banks 2006; Wikipedia 2013).

Allows full-text search of USPTO and EPO to find/identify/obtain patents, ranking by relevance. Allows summary, claims, images, PDF view/download/print, online reading.

'Prior Art Finder' tool: find documents related to application/grant, determine novelty, find multiple sources existing at filing time (Orwant 2012). Identifies key phrases, combines into query, displays results from Patents, Scholar, Books, web.

Each record includes: summary (publication number, type, application number, publication/filing/priority date, also published as, inventors, original assignee, external links), title, description, claims, images/drawings, citations, 'referenced by', classifications, legal events.

## Search Techniques

Works like normal Google + patent features. Two ways: simple and advanced.

### Simple Search
Like normal Google. Query e.g. cigarettes lung cancer, liver tumors alcohol consumption – finds patents with at least one keyword. Click hit shows title, abstract, publication number/type, application number, dates, inventors, applicant, classifications, legal events, external links, etc.

Searchable by patent number, inventor, filing date, assignee, classification code (US/international), keyword. Single search-box.

### Useful Search Tips (Table 2)

- `+` : search stop words – e.g. `+II, +G +8`
- `-` : remove word/phrase – `eyes diseases -animal`
- `OR` (caps) : expand – `mouse OR mice`
- `" "` : phrase – `"Gamma knife"`
- `~` : similar terms – `~surgery`, `~pediatric plastic surgery`, `otoplasty ~surgery`
- `intitle:` : term in title – `intitle:iphone`, `intitle:colon intitle:cancer`
- `allintitle:` : all words in title – `allintitle: colon cancer`, `allintitle: "Plastic surgery"`
- `inventor:` / `ininventor:` : inventor name – `ininventor:Microsoft`, `ininventor:"Shai Dekel"`
- `*` : wildcard for proximity, replaces single word in quoted phrase – `"post-plastic * surgery"`, `"skin ulcers ** reductive potential"` etc.

Boolean AND (+), OR (|), NOT (-), phrase quotes fine-tune queries. ENTER defaults to search. First link gives original patent URL.

### Tips for Better Searching

1. **Keyword rules:** exact/specific – `foot` won't return `feet`/`football`; not case sensitive; order matters especially phrase – `oncology surgical` vs `surgical oncology` differ.
2. **AND assumption:** default AND – `orthopedic surgery` = both words. Force term with `+` no space: `eye surgery +laser`, `Vitamin +B +12`.
3. **NOT:** `-` no space – `Rabies -animal` excludes animal.
4. **Exact phrase:** quotes – `"life-cycle management engine"`, `["lean integration"]`; multiple quoted strings all must appear (implied AND): `"child care" "swing chair"`.
5. **OR / |:** caps OR or vertical bar – `mouse OR mice`, `tooth | teeth`.
6. **Inventor search:** first name/initial + last, or last alone; `ininventor:` erratic but usable: `ininventor:"Sergey Brin"`, `ininventor:Brin`, `ininventor:Wuerstlin AND ininventor:Feichtmayr`, with OR for either. Apostrophe/hyphen/space variants: `ininventor:"R Obrien" OR ininventor:"R O'Brien"`, `inauthor:"Lopez-Gonzalez" OR inauthor:Lopezgonzalez`. `*` for middle name: `"Lopez * Gonzalez"`. Useful orders: first-middle-last etc. Diacritics searchable: `ininventor:Jorgensen OR ininventor:Jørgensen`. Search all variations as appears in source patent.
7. **Date search:** via Advanced Patent Search limit by filing/issue date range.

### Advanced Patent Search
Combine patent number, title, inventor, original assignee, US/international classification, patent type/status, date, restrict date by. Find patents citing particular patent, context of citations. URL: http://www.google.com/advanced_patent_search. Simple-box commands also work: `ininventor:"Michael J. Jackson"`, `inassignee:"Lockheed Martin Corporation"`, combined with AND.

## Limitations and Suggestions

### Pros
- Google basic/advanced screens, simple intuitive UI, skim overview, hyperlinks for navigation
- Very good response time/precision when well adjusted; speed promotes usability
- Translation especially European patents (origin language + English; EPO official: English, French, German)
- Truncation e.g. `metasearch*` but doesn't work well

### Cons
- Imperfect indexing, scanning errors
- No most recent patents, only first version
- No international (non Euro-American) patents at time (hoped expansion)
- No CREATE ALERT (vs Scholar's Alerts via Google Account); citation alert by email useful; suggest Scholar tools: My Citations, My updates, Alerts, Metrics
- Want cited notification; "Cited by" feature; IPC classification for patentometric/intelligence analysis

Other improvements suggested:
1. Cover non Euro-American patents
2. Disambiguate same-name inventors/subfields
3. Checkpoints for language, country, advanced techniques
4. Wildcard e.g. `Metaphysi*`
5. Enhance similar-patent / related retrieval
6. Improve/expand 'cited by' + comprehensive database

## Patent Citations

Citations, especially to scientific references, track science-technology relation. Examiners review prior art, list on front page to ensure novelty/usefulness. Examiner refs determinative vs applicant refs; examiners can add/remove (Narin & Olivastro 1998; Van Looy et al. 2006).

Citations to journal in patents indicates industry-technology orientation.

Example LIS field phrase search:
- JASIST (J Am Soc Inf Sci Tec) – >22,343 citations – technical, industry-oriented
- Journal of Documentation – ~250 – theoretical

## Conclusion

Despite limits, unique advantage is international coverage; alternative interface to USPTO/EPO. Returns patent as published, easy drawings/PDF. Facilitates patent and journal citation analysis. Difficult to compare vs others due to format/coverage – good complement to commercial/government DBs. May become important for patent citation analysis / intelligence / patentometrics.
