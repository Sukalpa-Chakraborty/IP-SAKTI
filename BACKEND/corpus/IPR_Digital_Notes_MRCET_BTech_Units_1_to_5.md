## UNIT-I – Introduction to IPR

**Definition:** IP law deals with legal rights to creative works/inventions; controls who uses new products, artistic works, designs; allows creators to profit.

IPR = rights over creations of mind, usually exclusive for certain period; includes copyrights, patents, trademarks, trade secrets; enforceable via lawsuit; encourages innovation without fear of theft.

**Examples:**
- Patents – features/devices/processes (most applicable for manufacturers; utility/design/plant)
- Trademarks – distinctive words/logos
- Design Rights – 3D shape/look
- Copyright – recorded/written material (drawings, documentation, digital data)

Four main types: Patents (exclude making/selling/using), Trademarks, Trade Secrets, Copyrights.

IP = intangible creations: copyrights, patents, trademarks + trade secrets, publicity, moral rights, unfair competition; music, literature, discoveries, words/phrases/symbols/designs. Term “intellectual property” 19th century, common late 20th century.

**Purpose:** Encourage variety of intellectual goods via limited property rights → economic incentive → profit → innovation → technological progress.

**Intangibility issues:** Indivisible (unlimited consumption without depletion); appropriation problem (vs fenced land, info easily replicated); balance – strong enough to encourage creation but not prevent wide use – focus of modern IP law. Administered via WIPO treaties.

### IP Law Basics

**Patent Law:** Conditions – patentable subject matter, utility, novelty, non-obviousness (US). Examiner judges; grant presumes patentable but errors (missed prior art) can lead to infringement litigation.

**Copyright Law:** Exclusive reproduce/distribute/perform/display/license + derivatives; fair-use exceptions (e.g. reviews). Must be original + concrete medium; no notice/registration required for coverage (US 17 USC 101-810, federal, preempts state; covers architecture, software, graphics, film, sound). Tasks: availability search, licensing, litigation, registration/transfers/notices.

**Trademark Law:** Distinctive symbols/pictures/words identifying origin; federal/state registration → exclusive use on intended + related products; portfolio management critical.

### Role in Innovation / New Product Development

Innovation = developing new idea + putting into practice; bringing valuable new products (goods/services) from concept to market to meet customer needs; marketing communicates that value.

Types: product vs process; radical/basic vs incremental; disruptive vs sustaining; non-tech: marketing, institutional, complementary. Focus here technological innovation – principal determinant of firm performance in knowledge economy. Debate: IP necessary to encourage creativity vs hinders competition – need empirical review (not covered).

Managing innovation better = survival. IP tools help take tech to market beyond just breakthroughs – interactive stages from idea → R&D → launch; value chain stages. IP helps navigate “valley of death” (invention → launch where most collapse for lack of support/viability).

### IP and E-Commerce

IP = industrial property (patents, trademarks, designs, geographical indications, utility models, trade dress, IC topographies, unfair competition/undisclosed info/trade secrets) + copyright/related rights. Asset as/more valuable than physical; traded/licensed standalone.

Why mutual importance:
- E-commerce sells IP-based products (music, photos, software, designs, training, systems) – IP is main value; must protect via tech + law or piracy destroys business
- IP makes e-commerce work: software, networks, chips, routers, UI protected; trademarks/branding/goodwill essential; unfair competition law
- Based on licensing – many technologies per product, outsourcing/sharing via licensing; otherwise high-tech impossible; SME collaboration
- Valuation – e-commerce value largely IP (portfolios, trademarks)

**Value in international commerce:** Not inherently valuable – value = strategic exclusion aligned with business objectives.

**International issues:** Industrial nations pushed IPR in Uruguay Round GATT – exchange patent/copyright access like goods market access; extend beyond Paris (patents) / Berne (copyright) to near-global GATT members. Developing countries resist – harm tech progress, want opt-out. Free trade ↑ competition; IPR extension lets domestic monopolists extend monopoly overseas – province of multinationals, not atomistic trade theory. Little empirical on trade/investment flows vs IPR pattern; paper models US 1982 exports/affiliate sales/licensing + joint tariff/IPR/affiliate policy decisions, cluster analysis (Sections II-III referenced).

## UNIT-II – Parties, Ownership, Licensing

**Ownership scenarios:** (1) university owns, (2) sponsor owns, (3) joint. Universities generally require faculty… Industry-sponsored research should not be trade secrets (blocks publication, universities + FOIA cannot guard). Some sectors claim sponsor owns as payer.

## UNIT-III – Copyrights and Patents (detailed)

### Copyright Fundamentals (US)
Protection for original works of authorship – literary, dramatic, musical, artistic, etc. Need guidelines; visual art, recordings, broadcasts, literary – need not be published but registration imperative for full protection.

**Ownership:** Automatic on creation in tangible form per 1976 Act, no notice needed. Creator owns except: work-made-for-hire (independent contractor parts, translation, film/screenplay part, compilation, atlas, instructional text, test/key, supplementary intro/afterword/appendix – employer owns; employee creations – employer owns). Joint ownership – two authors intending inseparable product – share equally.

**Registration:** Must register with US Copyright Office to defend; statutory damages/fees only for registered; within 3 months of publication or before infringement.

**Owner rights:** Duplicate, distribute/sell, display/perform publicly, adapt/change; profit freely.

**Transfer:** 1. Assignment/sale (e.g. to publisher for contract/money), 2. At death (will/intestate), 3. Mortgage (security for loan), 4. Involuntary (divorce/bankruptcy/foreclosure). Retain publish/duplicate/defend/sell/monetize while in effect.

**Originality (Indian Act Sec 13):** Copyright subsists in original literary/dramatic/musical/artistic, cinematograph films, sound recordings throughout India. Original ≠ novel/inventive thought – just not copied, originates from author (effort/skill).

Cases:
- *University of London Press v Tutorial Press*: maths question papers – though from existing knowledge, formulation requires effort/skill → protectable; originality = effort, not originality of ideas.
- *Burlington Home Shopping v Rajnish Chibber*: customer database – skill/labour → original; “sweat of the brow” threshold.
- *Feist v Rural Telephone*: telephone directory – rejected sweat, requires “modicum of creativity”; mere alphabetical list not original.
- *Eastern Book Co v D.B. Modak (SCC)*: copying SC judgments + headnotes/cross-refs/paragraph divisions – respondents copied additions with skill/creativity, not just judgments (govt/judicial decisions not copyrightable) → infringement; test = skill + judgment, not mere labour.

**Reproduction Rights (1976 Act Sec 106):** Exclusive to reproduce in copies/phonorecords; prepare derivatives; distribute (sale/transfer/rental/lease/lending); perform publicly (literary/musical/dramatic/choreo/pantomime/film/AV); perform sound recordings via digital audio transmission.

**Moral Rights (visual arts only, Sec 106A):** Attribution/paternity + integrity. Visual art = painting/drawing/print/sculpture single or ≤200 signed/numbered (sculpture cast/carved/fabricated ≤200), or still photo for exhibition single or ≤200 signed/numbered (Sec 101). Rights: claim ownership; prevent false attribution; prevent attribution after distortion/mutilation prejudicial to honour; prevent intentional distortion/mutilation prejudicial; prevent destruction of recognized stature (intentional/grossly negligent). Not for literary/musical; often waived in licences.

**What is reproduction?** Duplicate/transcribe/imitate/simulate substantial part in fixed material object. Owner can make/stop copies; cannot stop mere reading aloud or choreographing unless public performance violates. Infringement test (*Arnstein v Porter*): (1) copying – admission or circumstantial (access + similarities; dissection/experts; if no access, striking similarity precluding independent creation), (2) unlawful appropriation – substantial similarity of copyrightable expression to ordinary lay hearer (no dissection/experts). Plaintiff proves direct/circumstantial access+similarity or striking similarity.

**Public Performance Right:** One of six rights: reproduce, derivatives, distribute, perform publicly, display publicly, digital transmit (sound recordings only).

**Ownership issues:** Creator owns; joint authorship / work-for-hire complexities (joint, standard, importance, software example referenced).

**Notice:** Must be original, fixed tangible, within categories. Pre-Berne (US joined Mar 1989) notice required; after – not required but beneficial; relevant for older works.

### Patent

Exclusive to exclude making/using/selling/importing for ~20 years in exchange for enabling public disclosure. Employees often assign via contract. Mostly civil – holder sues infringers. Essential in some industries, irrelevant others. Application needs claims defining invention (many claims, each property right) meeting novelty/usefulness/non-obviousness. TRIPS: available in all tech fields if new/inventive/industrially applicable (country variations); minimum 20 years.

**Foundations (US-centric):** Constitutional/Natural/Property right (only patents/copyrights in Constitution; basis creation/production); trade secrets natural right; patent = social contract giving up secret; all inventions combine known elements/steps/connections; not monopolies; use natural phenomena (no magic), predictable elements (physics), claims define invention (like equation, every word meaningful), invention implies useful/objective result, inventor = first creator → novelty.

**Searching:** Exclusive utility (processes/articles/machines/improvements – protects method) vs design (ornamental appearance). Need thorough novelty search (large orgs/lawyers/SMEs/researchers in patent DBs – technical + legal info).

**Transfer of Ownership (property general, India context):** Owner has possession/use/transfer/rent; transfer via gift/sale – sale deed/conveyance, competent to contract, POA (manage vs sell distinction); agreement to sell on non-judicial stamp precedes sale deed (evidence base); must stamp/register at sub-registrar (prevent fraud, reliance, clear title); unregistered real-estate transfer invalid; stamp duty per State.

## UNIT-IV – Cyber Law

**Definition:** Law dealing with Internet/tech/electronic – computers, software, hardware, IS; aka Internet Law. Prevents/reduces large-scale cybercriminal damage – protects access, privacy, communications, IP, speech re Internet/websites/email/phones/storage. Traffic ↑ → legal issues; varies by jurisdiction – enforcement hard, restitution fines→imprisonment.

**Cyber crime:** Unlawful acts where computer is tool/target/both; traditional (theft/fraud/forgery/defamation/mischief – IPC) + new-age (IT Act 2000).

**IT Act 2000 (No 21 of 2000, notified 17 Oct 2000):** Primary cybercrime/e-commerce law, based on UNCITRAL Model Law on E-Commerce 1996 (UN GA Res 30 Jan 1997). Contentious – stringent privacy, intermediary liability for illegal content.

History: Budget session, President K.R. Narayanan, Minister Pramod Mahajan finalization. Original: e-documents, e-signatures, authentication, penalties for breach (system damage, cyber terrorism), regulator monitoring/rulemaking. 2008: expanded “communication device” to mobiles, IP owners responsible for distributed content. 2011: stringent personal-info collection. Sec 66A (controversial): “offensive messages” illegal, server owners liable even if unauthorized (e.g. porn traced to server) – 3 years to life + fines + corporate admin/bureaucratic burden.

**Applicability:** Companies doing business in India – registered/outsourced/servers in India; covers online exchanges/e-documents. Mere customers in India insufficient; need service/sales + servers. Example: Instagram (US, no servers – not held) vs Snapdeal (Indian – held, Privacy Policy).

**Cybercrime definition:** Computer + network crime – used in commission or target. Offences vs individuals/groups with criminal motive to harm reputation/physical/mental/loss via telecom (Internet chat/email/boards/groups, mobile Bluetooth/SMS/MMS). Threatens personal/national security/financial health. High-profile: hacking, copyright infringement, mass surveillance, sextortion, child porn/grooming, privacy interception (Halder & Jaishankar – gender perspective: cybercrime against women – intentional psychological/physical harm via networks). Govts + non-state: espionage, financial theft, cross-border – cyber warfare if nation-state. McAfee 2014: $445B annual global damage; $1.5B US card fraud 2012; CSIS/McAfee 2018: ~$600B (~1% GDP).

**Classifications:**
- Financial fraud: dishonest misrepresentation causing loss – unauthorized alteration (low skill, employee pre-entry/false data/instructions), altering/destroying/suppressing/stealing output to conceal, altering/deleting stored data; bank fraud, carding, identity theft, extortion, classified theft; phishing/social-engineering scams.
- Cyber terrorism: FBI/CIA concern – ↑ scans since 2001, organized mapping of critical systems. Definition: intimidate/coerce govt/org via computer attack for political/social objectives (Parker 1983 – via cyberspace/resources). Even propaganda (holiday bomb post) counts; hacking/fear/power/info/ruin/robbery/blackmail. DoD: Estonia 2007 (Russia alleged), Georgia Aug 2008 kinetic+non-kinetic, Ukraine Dec 2015 power grid (first successful grid attack, Russia attributed) – future warfare cyberspace operations.
- Cyber extortion: (DoD context above)

**E-commerce:** Buying/selling online – mobile commerce, EFT, SCM, Internet marketing, online transaction processing, EDI, inventory, auto data collection. Web (also email) for ≥part of lifecycle. Examples: Amazon books, iTunes music, customized liquor. Areas: online retail, e-markets, auctions. Supported by e-business. Uses: direct retail (sites/apps/live chat/bots/voice), marketplaces (B2C/C2C), B2B, demographic data (web/social), B2B EDI, email/fax marketing (newsletters), pretail launches, online financial/currency exchanges.

**Data Security:** Protecting from unauthorized access/corruption through lifecycle – encryption, tokenization, key management across apps/platforms. Why: global IT cyber-defence for brand/IP/customer/critical infrastructure – people/process/tech. Solutions (Micro Focus example, 80 patents/51 yrs): cloud access security, encryption/tokenization, HSM (financial/compliance), key management, end-to-end enterprise protection, payments (P2PE/tokenization, PCI reduction), Big Data/Hadoop/Teradata/Vertica/IoT, mobile app, browser (card/PII to trusted host), email/mobile messaging encryption (PII/PHI).

**Confidentiality:** ≈ privacy – prevent to wrong people, allow right people; categorize by damage, stringent accordingly; training (risks, passwords, social engineering); e.g. bank account/routing online; encryption, IDs/passwords, 2FA norm, biometrics/tokens/fobs/soft tokens; minimize places/transmissions; extreme – air-gapped, disconnected, hard-copy only.

**Information Privacy (data privacy/protection):** Collection/dissemination vs tech/expectation/law/politics. Concerns wherever PII/sensitive collected/stored/used/destroyed (digital or not) – poor disclosure control = root. Sources: health, criminal justice, finance, genetics, residence/geo, breach, location/geo, cookies/browsing, research. Challenge: use while protecting preferences/PII – computer/data/information security (software/hardware/HR); laws constantly changing – track compliance; academia IRBs ensure privacy/confidentiality.

**International aspects:** No single definition – illegal Internet-mediated in global networks, no cyber-borders. Challenges domestic/international law/enforcement; criminals exploit lenient/untraceable jurisdictions. Developed/developing recognize economic/political/public threats; complexity → need cooperation. Orgs/govts set global legislative/enforcement standards regionally/internationally – China-US cooperation (top two sources) notable. ICT + global standards for interoperability/security. Countermeasures: legal (legislation) + technical (tracking, content control, proxies, forensics, encryption/plausible deniability); heterogeneity → focus legislative/regulatory cooperation.

**Internet Crime:** Any crime/illegal online via/using Internet; multiple global legislation/oversight; experts use IDS/packet sniffers. Strong branch: identity theft, scams, cyber-talking; geographic dispersion complicates penalty (e.g. Nigerian 419). FBI/FCC dedicated experts. Legislation: US CFAA Sec 1030 (amended 2001 Patriot Act), CAN-SPAM 2003, PROTECT IP 2011; Canada spread (Websense 2001) under review. Types: bullying/harassment, extortion, bomb threats, classified theft, password trafficking, trade-secret theft, personal-data hacking, copyright/software piracy, counterfeit trademarks, weapon trafficking, child porn, card theft/fraud, phishing, domain hijacking, viruses. Prevention: vigilance – never share name/address/birth/SSN to unknown; suspect exaggerated claims.

## UNIT-V – New Developments

### Trademark – Internet
Owners struggle with electronic/Internet issues. Internet origins: 1970s DoD military/research nuclear-survivable network; 1980s NSF expansion (govt/universities); early 1990s global communication (messages/pictures/text/web advertising).

**Domain Names:** Presence starts with address – locator + origin/goodwill (trademark). Two parts: gTLD right of dot (.gov/.com) + second-level left (kraft in kraft.com). Disputes: registered mark vs similar/identical domain.

**ICANN:** Govt-created non-profit, board partly elected by Internet community, to resolve registration/use problems. Authorized .com/.org/.net (1-year renewable); representation of lawful purpose/no known infringement; added .biz/.info + 5 others.

**Protecting – Cybersquatting:** Registering well-known marks to divert via confusion. Three approaches: (1) Federal Trademark Dilution Act action, (2) civil suit under Anticybersquatting Consumer Protection Act (ACPA), (3) ICANN arbitration (UDRP).

- Dilution (15 USC 1125(c)), ACPA (15 USC 1125(d)): plaintiff proves (i) distinctive/famous mark, (ii) domain identical/confusingly similar, (iii) bad-faith registration.
- UDRP (1999): (i) identical/confusingly similar to complainant mark, (ii) registrant no legitimate interest, (iii) bad-faith use.

### Copyright – New Developments
- Clothing useful article not copyrightable, but lace design as writing incorporated in wedding dresses protectable (NY federal) – enjoined copier; detailed embroidery/2D graphics on garment may be copyrightable.
- Type fonts not protectable, but generating software protectable (California).
- Stephen King *Riding the Bullet* Internet-only – cracked, free copies, stronger encryption; 2000 serial suspended for unpaid downloads.
- NET Act late 1997 (Clinton, amending 18 USC 2319): enhanced criminal penalties even without profit; limitations 3→5 years; federal action vs online violation (same as other media).
- Sep 1999: relaxed encryption export + new law enforcement anti-terror/criminal computer powers + FBI code-cracking unit.
- Mid-2000 E-SIGN (Electronic Signatures in Global/National Commerce Act): e-signatures = paper.
- 2000 Chicago “Pirates with Attitude” (17, incl Intel/Microsoft ex-employees) – thousands of software – NET Act prosecution.
- Copyright Office recommends amending Sec 110 for distance-learning transmission if conditions met.

### Patent – New Developments
Patent Act flexible, no statutory overhaul for tech advances.

- **Business method/software:** Cutting-edge; conventional wisdom – unless high commercial value, protection counterproductive (PTO ~2 years ≈ software obsolescence).
- **Biotechnology:** Medicines/science/agri/pharma – genes curing disease, crops resisting drought/disease feeding population → ↑ patents/litigation.
- **AIPA 1999** (most significant in 20 years): Inventors’ Rights Act 1999; First Inventor Defense 1999; Patent Term Guarantee 1999; Domestic Publication of Foreign-Filed Applications 1999; Optional Inter Partes Re-examination 1999.
- **International protection:** US patent only US – need per-country/regional filing. Paris Convention, European Patent Organization, TRIPS, Patent Law Treaty, Foreign Filing Licenses, US filings by foreigners.
- **EPO (1973):** Uniform Europe system – single filing (Munich/Hague/Berlin/national offices) – granted valid in designated EPO states as national patent.

### IP Audits
Companies miss brochure copyright, trade-secret measures; unaware of value. Law firms offer audit (like financial audit) to uncover protectable IP. Due diligence review on acquisition – investigate target IP to ensure benefits, avoid inherited infringement.
