## 1. Introduction to Intellectual Property Rights

**Intellectual Property (IP):** Intangible creation of the human mind, usually expressed or translated into a tangible form that is assigned certain rights of property.

**Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):** Rights given to people over the creation of their minds. They usually give the creator an exclusive right over the use of his/her creations for a certain period of time.

**Classification of IPRs:**
- **Industrial Property:** Inventions (patents), trademarks, industrial designs, geographical indications.
- **Copyright:** Literary and artistic works (novels, poems, plays, films, musical works, drawings, paintings, photographs, sculptures, architectural designs). Related rights: performers, producers of phonograms, broadcasters.

**Rights protected under IP:**
1. Patents
2. Copyrights
3. Trademarks
4. Industrial designs
5. Protection of Integrated Circuits layout design
6. Geographical indications of goods
7. Biological diversity
8. Plant varieties and farmers rights
9. Undisclosed information

**IPR as Instruments of Development:**
- Key drivers of economic performance in R&D-based growth models.
- Intellectual property policies affect the extent and nature of investments by multinational enterprises.

**Global Intellectual Property Trends (2009):**
- Over 3 million trademark applications filed worldwide.
- China accounted for 50% of total industrial design filing activity.
- India: 1,41,943 trademark applications; 34,287 patent applications; 6,092 industrial design applications.

**Durations of IP Protection:**
| IP Type | Duration |
|--------|----------|
| Patent | 20 years from filing date |
| Trademark | 10 years from application date (renewable) |
| Copyright | Generally 60 years |
| Geographical Indication | 10 years (renewable) |
| Chip Layout Design | 10 years from filing or first commercial exploitation |
| Plant Varieties | 18 years for trees/vines; 15 years for other crops and extant varieties |

## 2. History of IPR in India

### 2.1 Patents
- **1856:** First patent legislation in India (Act VI of 1856) to encourage inventions.
- **1859:** Act XV of 1859 – exclusive privileges for useful inventions; priority period extended to 12 months.
- **1872:** Patterns and Designs Protection Act (Act XIII) – included designs.
- **1883:** Act XVI – protection for novelty disclosed in exhibitions (6 months grace).
- **1911:** Indian Patents and Designs Act (Act II of 1911) – Controller of Patents established.
- **1949:** Justice Bakshi Tek Chand Committee – review of patent law.
- **1957:** Justice N. Rajagopala Ayyangar Committee – recommended major changes.
- **1970:** Patents Act, 1970 – repealed 1911 Act (patents only); came into force 1972.
- **1994, 1999, 2002, 2005:** Amendments to comply with TRIPS.
- **2005:** Patents (Amendment) Act, 2005 – product patents in drugs, pharmaceuticals, agro chemicals.

### 2.2 Copyright
- **1847:** Copyright law entered India during East India Company regime.
- **1914:** New Copyright Act – extended UK Copyright Act 1911; introduced criminal sanctions.
- **1957:** Copyright Act, 1957 – came into force 1958.
- **Amendments:** 1983, 1984, 1992, 1994, 1999, 2012.

### 2.3 Trademarks
- **1860:** Statutory Trademarks Law dates back to 1860.
- **1940:** Indian Trademarks Act passed (based on English Trademarks Act).
- **1958:** Trademark and Merchandise Act – registration and better protection.
- **1999:** Trademarks Act, 1999 – compliance with TRIPS; came into force 2003.

## 4. Copyright

**Definition (Section 14):** Exclusive right to do or authorise doing of acts in respect of a work or substantial part thereof.

**Classes of works protected:**
1. Original Literary Work
2. Original Dramatic Work
3. Original Musical Work
4. Original Artistic Work
5. Cinematograph Films
6. Sound Recording
7. Computer Programme

**Ownership:**
- Author is first owner, except:
  - Work made in course of employment (employer owns).
  - Photograph, painting, portrait, engraving, cinematograph film made for valuable consideration (person at whose instance made owns).
  - Government work (Government owns).
  - Public undertaking work (public undertaking owns).
  - International organisation work (organisation owns).

**Assignment (Section 18):**
- Must be in writing, signed by assignor or authorised agent.
- Must identify work, specify rights, duration, territorial extent, royalty.
- If assignee does not exercise rights within 1 year, assignment lapses.
- Period of assignment: 5 years unless specified.
- Territorial extent: India unless specified.

**Term of Copyright (Sections 22–29):**
- Generally 60 years.
- Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic: 60 years from year following death of author.
- Cinematograph films, sound recordings, photographs, posthumous, anonymous, pseudonymous, government, public undertakings, international organisations: 60 years from date of publication.
- Broadcast reproduction right: 25 years from beginning of calendar year following broadcast.
- Performer's right: 25 years from beginning of calendar year following performance.

**Rights of Broadcasting Organisation:**
- Broadcast reproduction right for 25 years.
- Prevents re-broadcasting, causing broadcast to be seen/heard on payment, making sound/visual recording, reproduction, selling/hiring.

**Rights of Performers:**
- Performer's right for 50 years (text says 25 years in one place, but later says 50 years; according to Copyright Act, it is 50 years).
- Prevents making sound/visual recording, reproduction, broadcast, communication to public.

**Computer Software:**
- Protected under Copyright Act, 1957.
- Amendments in 1994 made law one of the toughest.
- Illegal to make or distribute copies without authorization.
- Civil and criminal remedies; minimum jail 7 days, extendable to 3 years.

**Copyright Infringement:**
- Making infringing copies for sale/hire.
- Permitting place for performance.
- Distributing infringing copies.
- Public exhibition.
- Importation.

**Remedies:**
- Civil: injunctions, damages, accounts.
- Criminal: imprisonment (min 6 months, fine min ₹50,000; second offence min 1 year, fine ₹1 lakh).

**International Copyright:**
- Works of countries in International Copyright Order protected as Indian works.
- Berne Convention, Universal Copyright Convention, TRIPS.

**Licences:**
- Chapter VI of Copyright Act.
- Compulsory licence for translation after 3 years for teaching, scholarship, research.
- Copyright Board grants licence.

**Registration:**
- Not mandatory; registration with Copyright Office provides prima facie evidence.
- Procedure: Form IV, separate applications, fee, power of attorney.
- Time: 2–3 months normally.
- Fees: ₹50 for literary/artistic work; ₹400 if used with goods; ₹600 for cinematograph film; ₹400 for sound recording.

**Copyright Board:**
- Quasi-judicial body constituted 1958.
- Chairman (High Court judge level) and up to 14 members.
- Powers: hear appeals, rectification, disputes on assignment, grant compulsory licences, fix royalties.

**Copyright Enforcement Advisory Council (CEAC):**
- Set up 1991 to review enforcement.

**Copyright (Amendment) Bill, 2012:**
- Changes: definition of commercial rental, joint ownership, rights of composers/lyricists, assignment, copyright societies.

## 5. Patents

**Definition:** Grant for an invention by Government to inventor in exchange for full disclosure.

**Invention (Section 2(1)(j)):** New product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application.

**What is not an invention (Section 3):**
- Frivolous inventions
- Contrary to natural laws
- Contrary to public order or morality
- Mere discovery of scientific principle
- Discovery of living thing or non-living substance in nature
- New property or new use for known substance (unless new product or new reactant)
- Mere admixture
- Mere arrangement/duplication of known devices
- Method of agriculture or horticulture
- Medicinal, surgical, curative, prophylactic, diagnostic, therapeutic treatment
- Plants and animals (except micro-organisms)
- Mathematical/business method, algorithms, computer programme per se
- Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic work
- Presentation of information
- Topography of integrated circuits
- Traditional knowledge

**Patent System in India:**
- Governed by Patents Act, 1970 (amended 2005) and Patents Rules, 2003 (amended 2006).
- Administered by Controller General of Patents, Designs, Trademarks and Geographical Indications, under DIPP, Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
- Offices: Kolkata (Head), Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai.
- Patent Information System (PIS) at Nagpur.

**Jurisdiction of Patent Offices:**
| Office | Territorial Jurisdiction |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Mumbai | Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Goa, Chhattisgarh, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli |
| Chennai | Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Pondicherry, Lakshadweep |
| New Delhi | Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttaranchal, Delhi, Chandigarh |
| Kolkata | Rest of India |

**Types of Patent Applications:**
1. Ordinary Application
2. Patent of Addition
3. Divisional Application
4. Convention Application
5. National Phase Application under PCT

**Who can apply:** Inventor, assignee, legal representative.

**Procedure:**
- File application with appropriate office.
- Pay fee.
- Publication after 18 months from filing/priority.
- Request for examination in Form 18 within 48 months.
- First Examination Report (FER) within 6 months.
- Amend within 12 months.
- Grant after 6 months from publication.

**Withdrawal:**
- At least 3 months before first publication (18 months).
- Can withdraw before grant.
- Withdrawn after publication cannot be re-filed.

**Opposition:**
- Pre-grant: any person can oppose after publication but before grant.
- Post-grant: within 1 year from grant.
- Grounds: wrongful obtaining, prior publication, prior claim, public knowledge, obviousness, not an invention, insufficient description, false information, etc.

**Grant:** After all requirements met; letter patent issued; entry in register; notified in Patent Office Journal.

**Term:** 20 years from filing date.

**Renewal:** Every year; first renewal for third year before expiration of second year. Grace period 6 months with extension fee. Restoration within 18 months of lapse.

**Documents for filing:**
- Form 1 (application)
- Form 2 (provisional/complete specification)
- Drawings (if necessary)
- Abstract
- Form 3 (foreign patent details)
- Priority document (if claimed)
- Form 5 (declaration of inventorship)
- Power of attorney
- Fee

**Rights of Patentee (Section 48):**
- Product patent: exclusive right to prevent making, using, offering for sale, selling, importing.
- Process patent: exclusive right to prevent using process, using/offering/selling/importing product obtained directly by process.
- Two or more persons: equal undivided share.

**Compulsory Licence (Sections 82–94):**
- After 3 years from sealing, if reasonable requirements not satisfied, not available at affordable price, not worked in India.
- Special provision for national emergency, extreme urgency, public non-commercial use.
- Section 92A: compulsory licence for export of pharmaceutical products to countries with public health problems.
- Terms: royalty reasonable, non-exclusive, non-assignable, for balance term, predominant supply in Indian market.

**Paris Convention:**
- India became member on 7 December 1998.
- Article 5A allows compulsory licences for abuse (failure to work).
- Insufficient working: no compulsory licence before 4 years from filing or 3 years from grant.

**TRIPS:**
- Patent protection for at least 20 years.
- Compulsory licences allowed under Article 31.

**Patent Agent:**
- Registered with Indian Patent Office.
- Eligibility: citizen of India, 21 years, degree in science/engineering/technology, passed qualifying exam or 10 years as examiner/Controller.
- Not mandatory to engage agent; non-residents must file through agent or give address for service in India.

**Infringement:**
- Unauthorized making, importing, using, offering for sale, selling.
- Remedies: interlocutory injunction, damages, account of profits.

**Penalties:**
- Contravention of secrecy provisions: imprisonment up to 2 years or fine or both.
- Falsification of entries: imprisonment up to 2 years or fine or both.
- Unauthorized claim of patent rights: fine up to ₹1,00,000.
- Wrongful use of "patent office": imprisonment up to 6 months or fine or both.
- Refusal/failure to supply information: fine up to ₹10,00,000; false information: imprisonment up to 6 months or fine or both.
- Practice by non-registered patent agents: fine ₹1,00,000 first offence, ₹5,00,000 subsequent.
- Offences by companies: person in charge deemed guilty unless proves due diligence.

**E-filing:**
- Class 3 Digital Signature required.
- Online registration, secure login, download client software, fill offline, generate XML, digitally sign, upload, pay via EFT.

## 6. Trademarks

**Definition:** Visual symbol (word, signature, name, device, label, numerals, combination of colours) used to distinguish goods/services of one undertaking from others.

**Key Features:**
- Must be distinctive.
- Must be used in commerce.

**Types:**
- Trademark
- Servicemark
- Collectivemark
- Certification Mark

**Functions:**
- Identifies goods/services and origin.
- Guarantees unchanged quality.
- Advertises goods/services.
- Creates image.

**Law:** Trade Marks Act, 1999 and Trade Marks Rules, 2002.

**Who can apply:** Any person claiming to be proprietor.

**Jurisdiction:** Appropriate office based on principal place of business in India.

**Trademarks Registry Offices:**
| Office | Jurisdiction |
|-------|-------------|
| Mumbai (Head) | Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Goa |
| Delhi | Jammu & Kashmir, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi, Chandigarh |
| Kolkata | West Bengal, Orissa, Bihar, Jharkhand, North Eastern States |
| Ahmedabad | Gujarat, Rajasthan, Daman & Diu, Dadra & Nagar Haveli |
| Chennai | Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Pondicherry, Lakshadweep |

**Application Particulars:**
- Graphic representation of trademark.
- Five additional representations.
- Transliteration/translation for non-Hindi/English scripts.
- Priority claim under Paris Convention.

**Series Registration (Section 15(3)):** For marks differing in statement of goods, number, price, quality, names of places, non-distinctive matter, colour. Form TM-8 or TM-37.

**Collective Marks (Sections 61–68):** Owned by association; used by members; indicates trade connection. Form TM-3.

**Registration Procedure:**
- Application filed at Head Office or branches.
- Examination for distinctiveness, deceptiveness, conflicting marks.
- Acceptance and publication in Trade Marks Journal.
- Opposition within prescribed period.
- Counterstatement within 2 months.
- Evidence, hearing, decision by Hearing Officer.
- Appeal to Intellectual Property Appellate Board.

**Correction/Amendment:** Form TM-16 before registration.

**Renewal:** Every 10 years from application date; Form TM-12.

**Offences & Penalties:**
- Falsification of trademark; falsely applying trademark.
- Punishment: min 6 months imprisonment, extendable to 3 years; fine min ₹50,000, extendable to ₹2 lakh.
- Second offence: min 1 year imprisonment, extendable to 3 years; fine min ₹1 lakh, extendable to ₹2 lakh.

**E-filing:**
- Class 3 Digital Signature.
- Online registration, secure login, new reference number, update details, final submission, online payment.

## 7. Designs

**Definition:** Features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornamentation or composition of lines or colours applied to an article, whether 2D or 3D, by industrial process, appealing to and judged solely by the eye.

**Exclusions:**
- Mode or principle of construction.
- Mere mechanical device.
- Trademark or artistic work.

**Law:** Designs Act, 2000; Designs Rules, 2001.

**Essential Requirements:**
- New or original.
- Not disclosed to public prior to filing.
- Significantly distinguishable from known designs.
- Not scandalous or obscene.
- Not mere mechanical contrivance.
- Applied to an article and appeals to the eye.
- Not contrary to public order or morality.

**Exclusions from Scope:**
- Books, jackets, calendars, certificates, forms, dressmaking patterns, greeting cards, leaflets, maps, plan cards, postcards, stamps, medals.
- Labels, tokens, cards, cartoons.
- Any principle or mode of construction.
- Mere mechanical contrivance.
- Buildings and structures.
- Parts of articles not manufactured and sold separately.
- Variations commonly used in trade.
- Mere workshop alterations.
- Mere change in size.
- Flags, emblems, signs.
- Layout designs of integrated circuits.

**Who can apply:** Any person, legal representative, assignee.

**Register of Designs:** Maintained by Patent Office, Kolkata.

**Procedure:**
- Form 1 with fee.
- Representation in quadruplicate (33 cm × 20.5 cm).
- Statement of novelty and disclaimer.
- Power of attorney (if necessary).
- Priority documents (if convention application).

**Cancellation:** Form 8 with ₹1,500 fee; grounds: previously registered, published, not new/original, not registrable, not a design.

**Piracy:** Applying design or fraudulent imitation to article for sale; importing for sale; publishing/exposing for sale.

## 8. Geographical Indications of Goods

**Definition:** Indication identifying goods as originating from a territory where a given quality, reputation or characteristic is essentially attributable to geographical origin.

**Examples:** Solapur Chaddar, Solapur Terry Towel, Basmati Rice, Darjeeling Tea, Kanchipuram Silk Saree, Alphanso Mango, Nagpur Orange.

**Law:** Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999; Rules, 2002.

**Registration:** Not compulsory; valid for 10 years, renewable.

**Procedure:**
- Form GI-1A to ID with ₹5,000 fee.
- Triplicate with Statement of Case and 5 additional representations.
- Typewritten in Hindi or English on strong paper (33 cm × 20 cm) with 4 cm left margin.
- Signed by authorised signatory.
- Statement of user with affidavit.
- Content: statement of quality/reputation, 3 certified copies of class of goods, geographical map, particulars of appearance, particulars of producers, affidavit, standard benchmark, mechanism to ensure standards, 3 certified copies of map, particulars of human skill.

**Infringement:** Unauthorized use misleading public as to geographical origin; unfair competition; passing off.

## 9. Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design

**Definition:** Layout of transistors and other circuitry elements, including lead wires, expressed in any manner in semiconductor integrated circuits.

**Law:** Semiconductor Integrated Circuits Layout-Design Act, 2000.

**Registry:** SICLDR, Department of Information Technology, Ministry of Information Technology.

**Criteria for Registration:**
- Original.
- Not commercially exploited anywhere in India or convention/reciprocal country.
- Inherently distinctive.
- Capable of being distinguishable from any other registered layout design.

**Duration:** 10 years from filing or first commercial exploitation, whichever is earlier.

**Person Entitled:**
- Creator.
- Indian national or national of country according reciprocity.
- Principal place of business in India or place of service in India.

**Steps for Registration:**
1. Filing application at SICLD Registry.
2. Registrar accepts/refuses/modifies.
3. Advertised within 14 days.
4. Opposition within 3 months.
5. Counterstatement within 2 months.
6. Hearing.
7. Decision on originality.
8. Appeal to Appellate Board or Civil Court.

**Documents:** 3 sets of 2D/3D drawings or photographs of masks; semiconductor integrated circuit; related information. Secrecy request possible with blocked drawings.

**Prohibition:** Not original; commercially exploited; not inherently distinctive; not distinguishable.

**Penalties:** Infringement: imprisonment up to 3 years or fine ₹50,000 to ₹10 lakh or both. False representation: imprisonment up to 6 months or fine up to ₹50,000 or both.

## 10. Biological Diversity

**International Commitments:**
- Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) – India party since 5 June 1992; ratified 18 February 1994.
- Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety – signed 23 January 2001; ratified 11 September 2003.

**Law:** Biological Diversity Act, 2002; Biological Diversity Rules, 2004.
- State rules: Kerala 2005, Sikkim 2006, Nagaland 2010, Rajasthan 2010, A.P. 2009, West Bengal 2005, U.P. 2010, Maharashtra 2008, etc.

**Key Provisions:**
- Section 2(b): Biological diversity definition.
- Chapter II: Regulations for access.
- Section 3: Prior permission of NBA for foreigners, NRIs, foreign companies for access.
- Section 4: Prior approval for transferring research results.
- Section 5: Exemption for collaborative research projects approved by Central Government.
- Section 6: Prior approval of NBA before applying for IPR.
- Section 7: Prior intimation to SBB by Indian citizens/companies for commercial utilization.
- Section 24: SBB power to prohibit/restrict activities.

**Exclusions:**
- Biological resources normally traded as commodities.
- Traditional uses.
- Collaborative research projects approved by Central Government.

**Penalties:**
- Non-compliance with NBA approval: imprisonment up to 5 years or fine ₹10 lakh or both.
- Non-intimation to SBB: imprisonment up to 3 years or fine ₹5 lakh or both.

**National Biodiversity Authority (NBA):**
- Established 1 October 2003.
- Functions: lay down procedures, advise Government, encourage SBBs, build database, create awareness.

**State Biodiversity Boards (SBB):** Matters relating to access by Indians for commercial purposes.

**Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs):** Local self-government institutions for conservation, sustainable use, documentation.

## 11. Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights

**Law:** Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001.

**Objectives:**
- Establish effective system for protection of plant varieties.
- Recognise and protect rights of farmers.
- Protect plant breeders' rights.
- Facilitate growth of seed industry.

**Definition of Variety:** Plant grouping within a single botanical taxon of lowest known rank, defined by characteristics, distinguishable, considered a unit, includes propagating material, extant variety, transgenic variety, farmers' variety, essentially derived variety.

**Duration of Protection:**
- Trees and vines: 18 years.
- Other crops: 15 years.
- Extant varieties: 15 years from notification under Seeds Act, 1966.

**Registration:** Confers exclusive right to produce, sell, market, distribute, import, export.

**Who can apply:** Breeder, successor, assignee, farmer/group, authorised person, university/publicly funded agricultural institution.

**Criteria for New Variety:**
- Novelty: not sold/disposed of before filing (India: 1 year; outside India: trees/vines 6 years, others 4 years).
- Distinctiveness: clearly distinguishable.
- Uniformity: sufficiently uniform.
- Stability: essential characteristics unchanged after repeated propagation.

**Prerequisites for Application:**
- Denomination.
- Affidavit that variety does not contain terminator technology.
- Complete passport data of parental lines.
- Characteristics for Novelty, Distinctiveness, Uniformity, Stability.

**Application Procedure:**
- Triplicate, signed.
- Submit hard copy Monday–Thursday 10:00–15:00.
- Max 3 applications per day.
- ₹200 fee per application.
- First come first serve.
- Acknowledgement slip.
- Processed under Rule 29(2) of Rules, 2003.

**Authority:** Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Authority, Ministry of Agriculture.

## 12. Undisclosed Information (Trade Secrets)

**Definition:** Information intentionally treated as secret, capable of commercial application with economic interest.

**Examples:** Technical data, internal processes, methodologies, survey methods, new invention not yet patented, customer lists, process of manufacture, techniques, formulae, drawings, training material, source code.

**Protection:**
- No specific legislation in India.
- Common law approach; Contract Act, 1872 covers many aspects.
- Protection lasts indefinitely if confidentiality not breached.
- Proof of creation: mailing information to oneself, depositing dated copy with third party.

**TRIPS:** Article 39 requires protection of undisclosed information.

## 14. The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS)

**Administered by:** World Trade Organization (WTO).
**Negotiated:** Uruguay Round of GATT (1986–1994).
**Coverage:**
1. Copyright
2. Geographical indications
3. Industrial designs
4. Integrated circuit layout-designs
5. Patents
6. New plant varieties
7. Trademarks
8. Undisclosed or confidential information

**Provisions:**
- Copyright: Part II Section 1 (Articles 9–14).
- Trademarks: Part II Section 2 (Articles 15–21).
- Geographical Indications: Part II Section 3 (Articles 22–24).
- Industrial Designs: Part II Section 4 (Articles 25–26).
- Patents: Part II Section 5 (Articles 27–34).
- Layout Designs: Part II Section 6 (Articles 35–38).
- Undisclosed Information: Part II Section 7 (Article 39).

**Patent Protection:** At least 20 years.
**Compulsory Licences:** Allowed under Article 31.

## 15. World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)

**Established:** 1970; specialized agency of UN since 1974.
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland.
**External Offices:** Rio de Janeiro, Tokyo, Singapore, New York.
**Mission:** Promote innovation and creativity for economic, social, cultural development.

## 16. Intellectual Property Treaties

### 16.1 Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property
- Signed: 20 March 1883.
- Administered by WIPO.
- Applies to patents, marks, industrial designs, utility models, trade names, geographical indications, repression of unfair competition.
- India membership: 7 December 1998.
- Key principles: national treatment, right of priority, independence of patents, parallel importation, protection against false indications and unfair competition.

### 16.2 Berne Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works
- Signed: 1886.
- Three basic principles: national treatment, automatic protection, independence of protection.
- India membership: 1 April 1928.
- As of March 2012: 165 contracting parties.

### 16.3 Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT)
- Done at Washington: 19 June 1970.
- Administered by WIPO.
- Enables single international application for patent protection in multiple countries.
- International phase and national/regional phase.
- 2010: ~164,300 PCT filings.

### 16.4 Patent Law Treaty (PLT)
- Adopted: 1 June 2000, Geneva.
- Purpose: harmonize and streamline formal procedures.
- India not a contracting party.

## 17. Commercialisation of Intellectual Property Rights

**Methods:**
- Sale or assignment.
- Licensing.
- Partnership, joint venture, spin-off company.

**Commercialization:** Process of turning invention or creation into commercially viable product, service, or process.

**Valuation:**
- Essential for using intellectual assets internally and in transactions.
- Relevant for licensing in/out, assignment, collateral, securitization, financing, taxation, bankruptcy, infringement litigation.

**Concepts of Valuation:**
- **Replacement cost:** Value to owner based on replacement cost.
- **Market value:** Based on comparable property prices.
- **Fair value:** Equitable to both parties.

**Methods of Valuation:**
- **Market-based:** Uses recent similar market transactions.
- **Cost-based:** Determines actual historical cost or replacement cost.
- **Income-based:** Based on predicted future cash flow from exploitation.

## Key RAG Retrieval Notes

- **IPR definition:** Rights given to people over creations of their minds.
- **Types:** Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, Industrial Designs, IC Layout Designs, Geographical Indications, Biological Diversity, Plant Varieties, Undisclosed Information.
- **Durations:** Patent 20 years; Trademark 10 years; Copyright 60 years; GI 10 years; Chip Layout 10 years; Plant Varieties 18/15 years.
- **Laws:** Copyright Act 1957; Patents Act 1970; Trade Marks Act 1999; Designs Act 2000; GI Act 1999; SICLD Act 2000; PPV&FR Act 2001; Biological Diversity Act 2002.
- **Administrative Bodies:** DIPP, Controller General, Copyright Office, PPV&FR Authority, SICLDR, NBA, IPAB.
- **International Agreements:** Paris Convention, Berne Convention, PCT, PLT, TRIPS, CBD, Nagoya Protocol.
- **TRIPS:** Covers 8 IP categories; patent protection min 20 years; compulsory licences allowed.
- **WIPO:** UN specialized agency; administers treaties; global registration systems.
- **Commercialisation:** Licensing, assignment, joint ventures; valuation methods: market, cost, income.
