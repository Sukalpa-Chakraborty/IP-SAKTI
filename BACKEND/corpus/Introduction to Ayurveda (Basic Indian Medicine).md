## 4. Chapter One: Let Us Ask

### 4.1 Definition of Ayurveda
- Etymologically: **Ayur** = Life, **Veda** = Science or Study
- Ayurveda = **The Science of Life** or **The Science of Living**
- Akin to Biology but differs: covers knowledge of **healthy living** as well as disease

### 4.2 Twin Concerns of Ayurveda
1. To impart knowledge enabling a **healthy person to maintain health**
2. Knowledge directed to the **cure of disease**

### 4.3 Fundamental Principles
- Ayurveda is not merely a science of drugs, herbs, or remedies — it has a much wider connotation
- Fundamental principles are **unchangeable** and not affected by "winds" of doctrine
- Additions and amendments have been made to applications without altering the essence

### 4.4 Definition of Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Vyadhi** | That which brings about different forms of unhappiness |
| **Roga** | That which causes discomfort |

### 4.5 The Five Basic Factors (Pancha Mahabhutas)
| Sanskrit | English |
|----------|---------|
| Prithvi | Earth |
| Jala | Water |
| Agni | Fire |
| Akasa | Ether |
| Vayu | Air |

- The whole universe is composed of the same five factors
- Food, water, air — all composed of the same five chief components
- Foundation of Ayurvedic thinking: **harmony between microcosm and macrocosm**

### 4.6 The Three Somatic Doshas (Tridoshas)
| Dosha | Composition |
|-------|-------------|
| **Vayu** | Air + Ether |
| **Pitta** | Fire + Water |
| **Kapha** | Water + Earth |

- When normal in proportion and action → **health**
- When deranged → **disease**
- Control both body and mind
- Disease classified as **Sharirika** (somatic) and **Mansika** (mental)

### 4.7 Physical Constitution (Deha-prakriti)
- Three main types: **Vatika, Paitika, Kaphaja**
- Determined at conception by predominant Dosha
- **Unchangeable** from birth to death
- No medicine can change Deha-prakriti
- Each constitution has different reaction to drugs
- Ideal Ayurvedic physician treats the **patient as a whole**, not just the disease

### 4.8 The True Medicine
> "It is the correct medicine and the pure medicine which cures a particular disease and doesn't give rise to other side reactions or diseases; it is the impure drug which temporarily cures the disease or suppresses the symptoms and at the same time gives rise to other side reactions."

### 4.9 Definition of Health
- **Ayur** = Contact of body with soul resulting in the state of living
- **Health** = Natural state of all three aspects: body, mind, and soul (Prasanna — Indriya — Mana — Atma)
- Disease = When this natural state comes in contact with unhappiness (Dukhasamyoga)

### 4.10 Historical Background
- **Vedic Period**: Ayurveda is an **Upaveda** or branch of **Atharva Veda**
- Four Vedas: **Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva**
- Atharva Veda deals in great detail with medicines and etiology of diseases
- Description of skeleton in Atharva Veda strikingly resembles Atreya in Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita
- Generally calculated as **two thousand years before Christ**
- Ayurveda has existed in India for a much longer period than any other system of medicine

### 4.11 Mythological Origin
- **Brahma** (creator) → **Prajapati Daksha** → **Ashwinikumaras** → **Lord Indra** → **Bharadwaja** → **Atreya**
- Atreya had six pupils: Agnivesha, Bhela, Jatukarna, Parasara, Harita, Ksharpani
- Agnivesha Samhita = **Charaka Samhita** (general medicine)
- **Dhanwantri** (Divodas, Kashiraja) → **Sushruta** → **Sushruta Samhita** (surgery)
- **Vagbhata** → **Ashtanga Sangraha** → **Ashtanga Hridaya** (poetry form)

### 4.12 Eight Branches of Ayurveda (Ashtanga Ayurveda)
| Branch | Domain |
|--------|--------|
| 1. Kaya | General Medicine |
| 2. Shalya | Major Surgery |
| 3. Shalakya | ENT, Eye, Mouth Diseases |
| 4. Bhuta Vidhya | Psychiatry |
| 5. Kaumara Bhritya | Paediatrics |
| 6. Agada | Toxicology |
| 7. Rasayana | Rejuvenation / Tonics |
| 8. Vajikarana | Aphrodisiacs |

### 4.13 Scientific Nature of Ayurveda
- Charaka described **six virtues** of a skilful physician:
  1. Vidya (Knowledge)
  2. Tarka (Logic)
  3. Vigyana (Science)
  4. Smriti (Remembrance)
  5. Taptarata (Adaptability)
  6. Kriya (Practical Demonstration)
- Sushruta: "One who is an expert only in theories and has no practical knowledge should be sentenced to death"
- Charaka: "The whole of suffering which clings to mind and body has ignorance for its basis and all happiness is founded in clear scientific knowledge."

## 5. Chapter Two: The Basic Differences

### 5.1 Ayurveda vs. Modern Medicine
- Ayurveda treats about **90% of the rural population in India**
- Not just effective drugs — a complete system with:
  - Accurate diagnosis before treatment
  - Two distinct methods of examination: **patient and disease**
  - **Nidana Panchaka** (five aspects):
    1. Etiology (causes)
    2. Purva Rupa (premonitory symptoms)
    3. Rupa (symptoms)
    4. Samprapti (pathology)
    5. Upashaya (soothing drugs, diet, practices)

### 5.2 Seasonal Divisions
- Year divided into **six seasons**, each of two months
- Each season has special diet, clothing, rules for healthy living
- **Transition period**: Last 8 days of ending season + first 8 days of starting season
- Seasonal disorders: **Prakrita Roga** (natural disease)

### 5.3 Diet in Ayurveda
- Detailed analysis of properties of each food
- **Pathya** = Soothing diet
- **Apathya** = Prohibited diet
- Half of treatment follows through diet

### 5.4 Strength of the Patient
- **Shamana** = Milder type of treatment
- **Shodana** = Cleansing treatment (emetics, laxatives, purgatives, enema)
- No treatment recommended that decreases patient's strength
- Aim: Make patient **resistant to disease**

### 5.5 Surgery in Ayurveda
- Sushruta = **Father of Modern Surgery**
- 125 instruments described
- Operations: hernia, hydrocele, piles, cataract, plastic surgery
- **Simha-mukha yantra** (lion forceps) resembles modern instruments
- King Ashoka stopped Ayurvedic surgery due to pain (no anaesthetics)
- Surgery then spread to the West

### 5.6 Obstetrics and Gynaecology
- Rules for post-delivery care
- Description of spermatozoon, ovum, menstrual cycle
- Foetal growth month by month
- Hygiene for pregnant woman
- Disorders during pregnancy and after labour

## 6. Chapter Three: Theory of Five Basic Factors

### 6.1 Purusha (Human Being)
- Field of treatment
- Composed of **five basic factors + soul**
- Sankhya philosophy: Purusha composed of 25 elements
- Ayurvedic definition: Compound of five basic factors and the soul

### 6.2 Relation of Doshas to Mahabhutas
| Dosha | Composition |
|-------|-------------|
| Vayu | Air (or Air + Ether) |
| Pitta | Fire + Water |
| Kapha | Water + Earth |

### 6.3 Seven Body Constituents (Dhatus) and Their Predominant Elements
| Dhatu | Predominant Basic Factor |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Rasa | Water |
| Rakta | Fire |
| Mamsa | Earth |
| Meda | Water + Earth |
| Asthi | Earth + Air |
| Majja | Water |
| Shukra | Water |

### 6.4 Sense Organs (Gnanendriyas) and Elements
| Organ | Predominant Element |
|-------|---------------------|
| Eye | Fire |
| Ear | Ether |
| Nose | Earth |
| Tongue | Water |
| Skin | Vayu |

### 6.5 Properties and Functions of Five Basic Factors

#### Earth (Prithivi)
- **Properties**: Bulky, dense, heavy, hard, inactive, motionless, rough, firm, slightly astringent, mostly sweet, tendency to fall down
- **Function**: Makes body steady, strong, heavy, hard
- **Body parts**: Nails, bones, teeth, muscles, skin, faeces, hairs, ligaments
- **Sense**: Smell

#### Water (Jala)
- **Properties**: Dense, heavy, soft, inactive, flowing, moist, viscid, cold, slimy, slightly astringent, sour, salty, mostly sweet
- **Function**: Makes body viscid, moist, compact; makes liquid parts flow
- **Body parts**: Chyle, blood, fat, lymph, Kapha, Pitta, urine, sweat
- **Sense**: Taste

#### Fire (Agni/Tejas)
- **Properties**: Penetrative, light, active, rough, dry, hot, clear, slightly sour, salty, mostly pungent, tendency to go upwards
- **Function**: Combustion, digestion, body heat, manifests parts, imparts glow
- **Body parts**: Heat of Pitta, radiance of body
- **Sense**: Colour/Sight

#### Air (Vayu)
- **Properties**: Penetrative, light, rough, dry, cold, transparent, slightly bitter, mostly astringent
- **Function**: Makes body non-viscid, dry, light
- **Body parts**: Exhalation, inhalation, opening/closing of eyelids, contraction, expansion, movement
- **Sense**: Touch

#### Ether (Akasha)
- **Properties**: Penetrative, light, soft, smooth, transparent, sound predominant, taste unmanifested, instantaneous effect
- **Function**: Makes body soft, porous, light
- **Body parts**: Pores and channels of body
- **Sense**: Sound/Hearing

### 6.6 Charaka's Description
> "Those parts of the body which are specially bulky, motionless, solid, heavy, rough and hard — such as nails, bones, teeth, muscles, skin, faeces, hairs, ligaments — they are PARTHIVA; smell and sense of smell are also EARTHY."

### 6.7 Primary Qualities of Bhutas
| Bhuta | Quality |
|-------|---------|
| Earth | Roughness |
| Water | Liquidity |
| Air | Expansion |
| Fire | Heat |
| Ether | Non-resistance |

### 6.8 Note on "Bhuta" Translation
- "Bhuta" has been translated as "element" — this is **misleading**
- Bhutas are not elements in the chemical sense
- What is regarded as element is not earth as we know it but in its **pristine, pure state**
- All gross matter is **pentabhutas** (compounds of five Bhutas)

## 7. Chapter Four: Anatomy in Ayurveda

### 7.1 Definition of Sharira
- **Sharira** = "That which gradually decays"
- Sum total of products of five elements
- Purusha = Combination of five elements + Jivatma

### 7.2 Philosophical Basis
- **Prakriti** (latent supreme nature) — progenitor of all created things
- Three fundamental virtues: **Sattva, Rajas, Tamas**
- Eight-fold categories: Avyakta (unmanifest), Mahat (intellect), Ahamkara (egoism), five Tanmatras
- **Purusha** = Self-conscious subjectivity

### 7.3 Twenty-Four Categories (Tattvas)
- Avyakta, Mahat, Ahamkara, five Tanmatras, five gross material principles
- Eleven organs of cognition: ears, skin, eyes, tongue, nose, speech, hands, genitals, anus, feet, mind
- Five sense organs (Gnanendriyas): ears, skin, eyes, tongue, nose
- Five operative organs (Karmendriyas): speech, hands, genitals, anus, feet
- Mind (Mana) partakes of both

### 7.4 Body Division
- Six major parts: two upper extremities, two lower extremities, trunk, head and neck
- Dissection advocated for thorough knowledge

### 7.5 Sushruta on Dissection
> "A surgeon who desires to obtain thorough knowledge of the body must dissect a dead body and see each and every structure and organ in it. Whatever is actually seen and observed during dissection and what is theoretically learnt from books on anatomy should supplement each other."

### 7.6 Method of Dissection
- Secure dead body with all limbs intact
- Death not due to poison
- No illness of long duration
- Not of a very old person
- Maceration process

### 7.7 Body Components
| Component | Count | Names |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Doshas** | 3 | Vata, Pitta, Kapha |
| **Dhatus** | 7 | Rasa, Rakta, Mamsa, Meda, Asthi, Majja, Shukra |
| **Malas** | 3 | Urine, Faeces, Sweat |
| **Pranas** | 12 | Agni, Soma, Vayu, Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, 5 senses, Atma |

### 7.8 Upadhatus (Sub-Body Constituents)
| From Dhatu | Upadhatu |
|------------|----------|
| Rasa | Stanya (breast milk), Artava (menstrual fluid) |
| Rakta | Kandras, Siras |
| Mamsa | Vasa, Cutis |
| Meda | Snayus |
| Asthi | Teeth |
| Majja | Kesha (hairs) |
| Shukra | Ojas |

### 7.9 Embryology
- Seeds mature at **25 (man) and 16 (woman)**
- Foetus does not develop if parents below this age
- Semen has quality of Jalamahabhuta
- Artava is red and like Agni
- **Sex determination**: Semen exceeds Artava → male; Artava more powerful → female; equal → impotent
- Even nights → male child; odd nights → female child

### 7.10 Foetal Development Month by Month
| Month | Development |
|-------|-------------|
| 1st | Kalala (soft small mass) |
| 2nd | Five elements fuse; compact ball (male), Pesi (female), irregular (sexless) |
| 3rd | Five bud-like protrusions (head and limbs); 11 indriyas appear |
| 4th | All organs clearer; heart ready; longings of pregnant woman |
| 5th | Mental power develops; flesh and muscular parts grow |
| 6th | Strength accumulates; complexion imparted; feelings evident |
| 7th | Complete physical structures |
| 8th | Ojas unstable; child usually does not survive |
| 9th-10th | Live child may be born |
| 11th-12th | Rare delivery |

### 7.11 Intra-Uterine Life
- Excreta very little; Vayu finds no entrance to colon
- Mouth covered by Jarayu; throat by mucus
- Child does not breathe or cry in foetal life
- Head directed downwards, face to back of mother
- Natural descent head-foremost

### 7.12 Nutrition of Foetus
- Father contributes: hair, beard, bones, nails, teeth, blood-vessels, muscles, semen
- Mother contributes: flesh, blood, fat, marrow, brain, heart, liver, spleen, intestines, anus
- Rasa (nutrient fluid) produces compactness, strength, complexion
- Vital force produces acuteness of senses, perception, longevity
- Assimilatory factors produce vigour, health, strength, colour, intellect

### 7.13 Seven Layers of Skin
- Described with names
- Arrived at without modern microscope

### 7.14 Sex Determination Signs
- Male child: secretion first in right breast, heaviness of right eye, walks with right foot forward, desires masculine-named articles
- Female child: opposite conditions
- Twins: middle part of abdomen sinks inwards like saucer

### 7.15 Maternity Care
- **Sutikagara** = Maternity home
- Dietary restrictions before and after delivery
- Construction of maternity homes with equipment, medicines, instruments

### 7.16 Semen Examination
| Deranged by | Colour | Symptoms |
|-------------|--------|----------|
| Vayu | Reddish black | Pain at ejaculation |
| Pitta | Yellowish/bluish | Burning, sucking pain |
| Kapha | White | Pain/itching |
| Blood | Bloody hue | Putrid smell, large quantity |

### 7.17 Healthy Semen and Artava
- **Semen**: Transparent like crystal, fluid, glossy, sweet, smell of honey
- **Artava**: Red like blood of hare, washings of shellac, leaves no stains

### 7.18 Rules for Conception
- Husband: anoint body with ghee, eat boiled sali rice, milk, clarified butter
- Wife: anoint body with oil, eat food with oil and Masha pulse
- Visit on 6th, 8th, 10th, or 12th night of menses for male child
- Visit on 5th, 7th, 9th, or 11th day for female child
- Avoid 1st, 2nd, 3rd day (shortens life)
- 4th day: child lives long, well developed

### 7.19 Pumsavana Sanskara
- 3-4 drops of Lakshmana or Sahadevi juice mixed with milk
- Poured into right nostril of enceinte
- For conception of male child

### 7.20 Four Factors for Conception
1. Ritu (menstrual period)
2. Kshetra (healthy womb)
3. Ambui (nutrient liquid)
4. Bija (healthy semen)

### 7.21 Asthi (Osteology)
- Bone = hardest structure; main support of body
- Vayu present in bones
- Malas of bones: nails and hairs
- Produced from Meda
- Prithvi Mahabhuta in excess
- Paternal part in foetus
- **Varieties**: Kapala (flat), Ruchaka (lustrous), Taruna (cartilaginous), Valaya (curved), Nalaka (long tubular)
- **Number**: Vedic 360; Charaka 360; Sushruta 300; Modern 203 (disparity due to inclusion of cartilages, teeth)

### 7.22 Sandhi (Joints) — Arthrology
- Joint = Structure where epiphysical ends kept together by ligaments
- Lubrication by **Shleshaka Kapha**
- When deficient: cracking sound due to friction
- **Types**: Sthira (immovable), Chastavantha (movable)
- Diseases: Rheumatism (Sandhivata), Gout (Vatarakta)

### 7.23 Snayus (Tendons/Muscles)
- Form the muscular system
- Fleshy organs tied to bone by Snayu and Sira
- Formed from Meda
- **Number**: 900 (600 in extremities, 230 in trunk, 70 in neck)
- Vayu causes all movements

### 7.24 Peshi (Muscles)
- Vayu enters fleshy mass and separates Peshi
- Enclose Siras, Snayus, bones, joints
- **Number**: Male 500; Female 540 (additional 40 in breasts, vagina, uterus)

### 7.25 Srotas (Channels)
- **Types**: Bahir-Mukha (external orifices), Yoga-vaha
- **Nine Bahir-mukhas**: 2 ears, 2 eyes, mouth, 2 nostrils, anus, urethra
- Females additionally: 2 breasts, genetic passage
- Carry Dhatus to final stage of conversion
- **Sushruta's 11 Srotas**:
  1. Prana (bronchi) — roots in heart and pulmonary arteries
  2. Annavaha (food) — roots in stomach and intestines
  3. Udakavaha (water) — roots in palate and Kloma
  4. Rasavaha (serum) — roots in heart and serum-carrying vessels
  5. Raktavaha (blood) — roots in spleen and liver
  6. Mamsavaha (muscle) — roots in Snayu, nerves, Tvak
  7. Medavaha (fat) — roots in Kati (waist) and kidneys
  8. Mutravaha (urine) — roots in bladder and penis
  9. Purishavaha (stool) — roots in anus and large intestines
  10. Shukravaha (semen) — roots in breasts and testes
  11. Artava-vaha (menstrual) — roots in uterus and Dhamanies

### 7.26 Marmas (Vital Points)
- Sites where fleshy parts, Siras, Snayus, bones intermingle
- Contain elements of Soma, Vayu, Tej, Gunas of Sattva, Rajas, Tamas, individual soul
- **Number**: 107 (11 in each extremity, 12 in abdomen/thorax, 14 in back, 37 in neck and upwards)
- **Classification by injury**:
  - Sadhya phranahara: immediate death
  - 33: death after some interval
  - 3: death if foreign body removed
  - 44: permanent loss of limb/activity
  - 8: intense pain

### 7.27 Sira (Veins/Vessels)
- **Number**: 700
- **Classification**: Vayu-carrying, Pitta-carrying, Kapha-carrying, Rakta-carrying
- **Colours**: Vata-vaha (vermillion), Pitta-vaha (blue, warm), Kapha-vaha (white, cold), Rakta-vaha (red, neither hot nor cold)
- Important verse: No single Sira carries only one Dosha; all carry all Doshas

### 7.28 Dhamani (Arteries)
- **Number**: 24
- All originate in navel region
- Distinction between Siras, Dhamanies, Srotas is valid (different natures, origins, functions)

### 7.29 Circulation
- **Shabda-Arch-Jala santanvat**: Flow of sound, fire-flames-light, water-liquid
- Three directions: downward, upward, transverse
- Arch (fire) → arteries; Shabda (sound) → veins; Jala (water) → capillaries
- Speed: Light fastest, sound slower, water slowest

## 8. Chapter Five: Outlines of Tridosha Theory

### 8.1 The Three Somatic Doshas
- Vayu, Pitta, Kapha
- Normal balance → health
- Imbalance → disease (Roga)
- Products of combination of five basic factors
- Have physical properties and corresponding actions
- Special seats though prevailing everywhere
- Production from daily food intake continuously going on

### 8.2 Comparison with Cosmic Forces
| Dosha | Cosmic Force |
|-------|--------------|
| Sharira Vayu | Wind |
| Sharira Pitta | Sun |
| Sharira Kapha | Moon |

### 8.3 Physical Properties
| Dosha | Properties |
|-------|------------|
| **Vayu** | Dry, expanding, non-sticky, light, cold in action, penetrative, Rajas predominating; no taste or colour |
| **Pitta** | Lightly viscid, flowing, non-sticky, active, hot in action and touch, Sattwa predominating; bitter taste, smell of raw meat, red/yellow/green colour; normal quantity 5 Anjalies |
| **Kapha** | Viscid, motionless, sticky, slimy, heavy, cold in touch and action, inert, soft, Tamas predominating; sweet taste, white colour; normal quantity 6 Anjalies |

### 8.4 Five Types of Vayu
| Type | Seat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **Prana** | Brain, face, thorax, ears, nose, tongue; special seat: heart | Maintains heart, mind, senses, intellect, arteries, veins, nerves; aids spitting, sneezing, respiration, eructation, pushing food down |
| **Udana** | Umbilicus, thorax, throat; special seat: larynx, lungs | Produces sound and speech; gives strength to mind, memory, intellect |
| **Samana** | Umbilical region; connected with stomach and small intestine | Stimulates digestion; controls flow of liquids; separates essential from waste; moves in whole digestive tract |
| **Apana** | Lower digestive tract (large intestines, urinary system, reproductive system) | Holds faeces, urine, menses, semen; expels them at proper time; holds foetus for 9 months; normal delivery |
| **Vyana** | Whole body; local seat: heart | Carries food juice (Rasa) and blood throughout body; quick-moving; 5 actions: contraction, expansion, upward, downward, general movements; helps perspiration, eyelid movement, yawning, sperm movement |

### 8.5 Five Types of Pitta
| Type | Seat | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| **Pachaka** | Duodenum (between amashaya and pakvashaya) | Main digestion; divides food juice into fine and waste parts; helps other four Pittas |
| **Ranjaka** | Liver and spleen | Imparts red colour to Rasa (food juice) |

## 10. Chapter Seven: On Personal Hygiene

### 10.1 Charaka's Promise
> "He who rightly observes the rules of health laid down here will not be deprived of 100 years of healthy life."

### 10.2 Measure in Diet
- Eat substances of suitable quality
- Observe measure determined by strength of one's own gastric fire
- Proper measure = Food that, when taken, is digested in due time without impairing health
- Heavy articles: 1/2 or 1/3 measure of full meal
- Light articles: Not in excess

### 10.3 Foods to Take in Measure
**Light items**: Green gram, common quail, grey partridge, antelope, rabbit, wapiti, Indian sambar, sali rice, sastika rice

**Heavy items**: Sugarcane juice, pastry, milk, til, black gram, flesh of aquatic and wet-land animals

### 10.4 Foods to Avoid Habitually
- Dried meat, dried vegetables
- Flesh of emaciated animal
- Coagulated milk, cream, cheese, pork
- Cow's and buffalo's flesh, fish
- Curds, black gram, wild barley

### 10.5 Foods That Can Be Taken Habitually
- Sastika or sali rice, green gram, rock salt
- Embelic myrobalan, barley, rain water
- Milk, ghee, flesh of jangala animals, honey

### 10.6 Seasonal Regimen

#### Winter (Shishira and Hemanta)
- Gastric fire enhanced; capable of digesting heavy food
- Take unctuous, acid, salt juices of flesh of fatty animals
- Spit-roasted flesh of burrowing and tearer animals
- Potion of madira and seedhu wines, honey
- Milk, sugarcane juice preparations, animal fat, oil, new rice, warm water
- Inunction, oil-massage, oil shampoo, hot house sudation, sunbaths, warm cellars
- Warm, thick clothing
- Anoint body with thick paste of eagle-wood
- May indulge in sexual enjoyment to heart's content
- Avoid: light and Vata-provoking eatables/drinks, draughts, restricted diet, diluted demulcent drinks

#### Spring (Vasantha)
- Accumulated Kapha liquefied by sun's hot rays → many diseases
- Perform purificatory procedures (emesis)
- Avoid: heavy, acid, unctuous, sweet articles; day sleep
- Resort to: physical exercises, dry massage, smoking, gargles, collyrium, frequent ablutions with warm water
- Anoint body with sandalwood paste
- Eat: barley or wheat, flesh of wapiti, rabbit, Indian antelope, grey quail, grey partridge
- Drink: wholesome seedhu or honey wine
- Enjoy: youthful loveliness of women, woods

#### Summer (Grishma)
- Sun drinks up unctuous element of earth
- Foods/drinks: sweet, cool, liquid, unctuous
- Cold demulcent drinks with sugar, flesh of jangala animals/birds, ghee, milk, sali rice
- Little or no wine (or diluted with water)
- Avoid: salty, sour, pungent, hot foods; exercise
- Smear sandal paste; sleep by day in cool apartment
- Night: terrace cooled by moon rays, open to breezes
- Adorn with pearls and gems; fans, cool hands with sandal water
- Abstain from sexual intercourse
- Seek coolness of woods, waters, flowers

#### Rains (Varsha)
- Digestive power weakens
- Vata and other constitutions provoked
- General rule: **moderation**
- Avoid: watery demulcent drink, day sleep, frosts, river-water, exercise, sun, sexual intercourse
- Take: foods/drinks well seasoned, with honey
- On cold days: unctuous articles with pronounced acid and salt tastes
- Old barley, wheat, sali rice, flesh and well-seasoned soups of jangala animals
- Drink: honey-wine or rain water/well-water/lake-water (boiled and cooled) with honey
- Resort to: friction-massage, dry massage, bathing, perfumes, garlands, light clean habiliments, rain-house

#### Autumn (Sharada)
- Body habituated to cold of rains; suddenly heated by sun → Pitta provoked
- Partake: sweet, light, cooling, slightly bitter, Pitta-curative eats/drinks
- Beginning of autumn: flesh of grey quail, grey partridge, black buck, wild sheep, wapiti, hare, sali rice, barley, wheat
- Resort to: ghee medicated with bitter articles, purgation, depletion of blood
- Avoid: exposure to sun, animal fats, oils, frosts, flesh of aquatic/wet-land animals, alkalis, curds, day-sleep, easterly winds

### 11.3 Three Analogies for Dhatu Nourishment

#### 1. Milk and Curd Analogy (Kshira-Dadhi-Nyaya)
- Whole Rasa turned into blood as a whole
- Blood turned into flesh as a whole
- Like milk → curd → butter → ghee
- **Commentators' view**: Partially acceptable; whole food juice does not turn into whole blood

#### 2. Pigeons-in-the-Snare Analogy (Khale-Kapot-Nyaya)
- Food juice goes to different constituents by different channels at different times
- Like pigeons from different places falling into same snare at different times
- Rasa nearest → nourished first
- Blood, flesh, etc. deeper → longer routes → longer time
- Shukra nourished last

#### 3. Irrigation Channel System (Kedar-Kaulya-Nyaya)
- Rasa goes to Rakta first, nourishes it
- Then goes to Mamsa, nourishes it
- Then Meda, etc.
- Like stream of water flooding one field after another
- **Most acceptable theory** — preferred by Chakradatta and Shiva Dasa

### 11.4 Nourishment of Tridoshas
- Vayu, Pitta, Kapha formed at conception
- Increase and develop by food juice from mother in womb
- After birth: nourished by milk and mixed food
- Food consists of five basic factors
- Local natural digestion produces Kapha, Pitta, Vayu
- Outside air mixes with Prana and Udana Vayu
- Fresh Pitta in duodenum nourishes Pachaka Pitta
- Pitta produced during digestion of blood as Mala

### 11.5 Dhatu Agni (Seven Digestive Fires)
- Each Dhatu has its own Agni
- Food juice digested by Rasa Agni → Rasa Dhatu
- Rasa Dhatu divided into three parts:
  1. Finest → nourishes Rakta Dhatu
  2. Usual → nourishes own Rasa Dhatu
  3. By-product → Kapha Mala

- Rakta Dhatu divided into three parts:
  1. Finest → Mamsa Dhatu
  2. Usual → own Rakta
  3. By-product → Pitta

### 11.6 Thirteen Agnis
- Jathara Agni (1)
- Dhatu Agnis (7)
- Bhuta Agnis (5)

### 11.7 Four States of Agni
| State | Cause |
|-------|-------|
| **Manda** (Slow) | Kapha preponderance |
| **Tikshana** (Acute) | Pitta predominance |
| **Vishama** (Unsteady) | Vata preponderance |
| **Sama** (Normal) | Normal proportions of all Doshas |

### 11.8 Four Types of Digestive System
- Harder (Vata type) — strong purgative needed
- Milder (Pitta type) — mildest purgative has strong action
- Medium (Kapha type) — medium action purgative
- Sama (Normal)

## 12. Chapter Nine: Diet

### 12.1 General Principles

- Both vegetarian and non-vegetarian foods considered

### 12.2 Charaka's General Observations
- Water moistens, salt liquefies, alkali digests, honey synthesizes

- Grape-wine stimulates digestion, treacle causes accumulation of morbid doshas
- Curds cause edema, green pinyaka causes depression
- Soup of black gram increases fecal matter
- Alkalis injurious to vision and semen
- All acid taste substances (except pomegranate and emblic myrobalan) → Pitta promoters
- All sweet taste substances (except honey, old sali rice, sastika rice, barley, wheat) → Kapha promoters
- All bitter taste substances (except sprout of country willow, guduchi, wild snake-gourd) and all pungent taste substances (except long pepper and ginger) → Vata stimulants and anaphrodisiac

## 14. Chapter Eleven: The Art & Science of Pharmacy

### 14.1 Traditional Preparations

#### Powder (Choorna)
- Raw materials cleaned, converted to fine powder by manual labour
- Filtered through cloth, kept in clean vessel
- Dry herbs used
- Five parts of tree: root, stem, flower, leaves, bark
- Named after chief ingredient or action
- Expiry: Not more than six months

#### Pills (Vati/Guti)
- Dry herbs powdered, mixed in juices of fresh plants
- Paste formed, pills prepared with thumb and first two fingers
- Dried in sunlight or shadow
- Various sizes

#### Decoctions
- Prescribed dose of powder + 8-16 times water
- Boiled till reduced to 1/4th or 1/8th
- Filtered
- Fresh decoction prepared each time
- Sometimes drugs kept immersed in water for 24 hours before

#### Asavas and Arishtas (Tinctures)
- Self-generated alcohol in mild proportion (6-12%)
- Named after major ingredient (e.g., Drakshasava)
- Considered purely as medicines, not drinks

#### Lehyams (Jellies)
- Syrup base with all ingredients
- Jelly prepared
- Also known as Awalehya
- Remain unspoiled for months
- Resemble jam, tomato sauce, ketchup

#### Thailam and Ghritam (Medicated Oils and Ghee)
- Decoction of drug reboiled with oil or ghee as base
- Reboiling until water part evaporated
- Essence remains
- Named after chief ingredient or action
- Consumed internally or used for external massage

#### Metallic Preparations
- Metals: Gold, silver, copper, iron, tin, etc.
- Purification process: Shodana
- Metal turned into thin leaves and fine wires
- Heated, immersed in plant juices
- Made into fine powder, mixed with herb juices
- Further heated → oxide of metal
- **Loha Bhasma** (iron oxide): Does not cause gastric upset, assimilated in blood quickly, increases haemoglobin fast
- **Nagarjuna**: Master brain of Rasa Vaidyas, father of Rasa Vaidyas (1300-1400 A.D.)
- Mercurial preparations: Usually oxides
- Doses: 1/2 grain to 1/16-1/8 grain for children
- Strict dietic restrictions: Avoid pungent, sour, spicy, hot dishes; give sweets, milk, ghee, butter, butter-milk

#### Other Preparations
- **Manjan**: Tooth powders
- **Anjan**: Eye applications (powder and honey-like forms)
- **Vartis**: Suppositories
- **Arkas**: Distilled waters
- **Ksharas**: Salts of various drugs
- **Makaradhwaja**: Special preparation with mercury, sulphur, gold

### 14.2 Makaradhwaja Preparation
- Mercury and sulphur separately purified
- Mixed → Kajjali (black sulphide of mercury)
- Black powder + gold retained for 72 hours
- Sulphur burnt away, red mercuric oxide collected at neck of bottle
- Types: Dwiguna, Shadaguna, Shodashaguna, Shataguna
- Potency: Shataguna > Shodashaguna > Shadaguna > Dwiguna

### 14.3 Need for Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia
- State and Central Governments making efforts
- Standard Ayurvedic pharmaceutical preparations to be enlisted
- Formulae, methods, doses, descriptions
- Standardization of drugs needed

## 15. Chapter Twelve: Examination of a Patient

### 15.1 Three Means of Investigation
1. **Authoritative instruction** (Aptopadesha)
2. **Direct observation** (Pratyaksha)
3. **Inference** (Anumana)

### 15.2 Charaka's Injunctions
> "The intelligent physician should endeavour to investigate correctly each disease by the use of three means... Having considered the disease conditions in all its correlations and from as many points of view as possible, the physician skilful at interpretation should thereafter determine the nature of the disease (diagnosis) and then the line of treatment."

### 15.3 Ten Factors for Investigation
1. Physical constitution
2. Essential make-up
3. Compactness
4. Bodily proportions
5. Homologation
6. Psychic make-up
7. Gastric capacity
8. Capacity for exercise
9. Age
10. Disease condition

### 15.4 Evaluation of Patient
- **First**: Vitality as a whole man (constitution, essential make-up, compactness, bodily proportions, homologation, psychic make-up, gastric capacity, capacity for exercise, age)
- **Second**: Disease condition (disease process, etiological factors, constitutional factors, body constituents, curability/incurability)
- **Third**: Expectation of life (astrological, physiognomical, somatological, evil prognostic data)

| **Nose** | Body odours, pleasant/unpleasant, inexplicable, persisting |
| **Ears** | Gurgling of intestines, cracking of joints, heart/lung sounds, voice characteristics, percussion sounds, wheezing, abnormal speech |
| **Tongue** | Taste changes, vitiation of body-fluid, sweetening of body-fluid, vital blood, bilious blood |

- Understanding → purposeful nature of action
- Passion → strength of attachment
- Ignorance → lack of understanding
- Anger → violent action
- Grief → dejection
- Joy → exhilaration
- Pleasure → sense of satisfaction
- Fear → despair
- Fortitude → cheerfulness
- Vitality → enthusiasm for enterprise
- Resolution → absence of vacillation
- Inclination → likes
- Intelligence → power of comprehension
- Wits → correct recognition
- Memory → power of recollection
- Modesty → bashfulness
- Character → conduct
- Aversion → avoidance
- Motive → subsequent performance
- Steadiness → absence of fickleness
- Docility → compliance
- Age, predilection, homologation, etiological factors → stage of life, residence, homologatory signs, type of pain
- Disease with latent symptoms → homologatory/non-homologatory tests
- Degree of morbidity → intensity of provocative factors
- Imminence of death → evil prognostic signs
- Expectation of recovery → auspicious inclination
- Clarity of mind → absence of disorder

### 15.7 Symptoms of Increased/Decreased Doshas

#### Vayu
- **Increased**: Harshness of speech, emaciation, swarthiness, throbbing of limbs, desire for hot things, insomnia, lack of vitality, constipated stools, tremors, constipation, impairment of sense organs, giddiness, depression, roughness/hardness of skin, loss of consciousness, ostalgia, atrophy of bone marrow, fear, anxiety, grief
- **Decreased**: Diminished movements, depression, stupefaction, weaker limbs, predisposition to Kapha diseases, anorexia, nausea, variability of gastric appetite

#### Pitta
- **Increased**: Pyrexia, desire for cold things, decreased sleep, loss of strength, weakness in senses, fainting, yellow colouration of faeces/urine/eyes/skin, excessive hunger, thirst, burning sensation, hyperacidity, impairment of Oja, bitter taste in mouth, oliguria, yellowness of body/limbs
- **Decreased**: Dyspepsia, anorexia, indigestion, roughness of body, tremors, heaviness, pallor of nails/eyes, increase of mucus, hypothermia, lack of lustre, alidity, stiffness, irregular pain and burning

#### Kapha
- **Increased**: Coldness, heaviness, depression, torpor, overadhesion of joints, ptyalism, lethargy, dullness of appetite, dyspnoea, cough, pallor of stools, corpulence, blocking of channels, nausea, fainting, atony of limbs, whiteness of skin, somnolence
- **Decreased**: Dryness, absence of Kapha in usual seats except stomach, thirst, giddiness, debility, looseness of joints, wakefulness, cramps, tachycardia, dehydration, aching, burning, heat, trembling, loss of consciousness, fuming

### 15.10 Eight-Fold Examination (Recent)
1. Pulse (Nadi)
2. Stool (Mala)
3. Urine (Mutra)
4. Sputum
5. Skin
6. Voice
7. Tongue
8. Eyes

### 15.11 Pulse Examination
- **Vata type**: Snake-like motion
- **Pitta type**: Jumping action like frog
- **Kapha type**: Slow and steady like elephant or swan
- Three fingers used on radial artery
- First finger = Vata, Second = Pitta, Third = Kapha
- Determine where pulsation is more prominent
- Pulse hot/cold, thick/thin, deep/superficial, regular/irregular

### 15.12 Examination of Stool and Urine
- Stool: Solid/semi-solid/liquid, frequency, sticky/non-sticky, mucus-laden, colour, smell, worms
- Urine: Colour, smell, quantity, burning sensation, blood, pus

### 15.13 Sama and Nirama Conditions
- **Sama**: Undigested food juice (Ama) present
- **Nirama**: Fine food juice without undigested portion
- Applies to all Doshas, Dhatus, Malas
- Sama stool: Offensive smell, sticky, not watery, does not float on water
- Patient with Sama: Fever, lassitude, heaviness of body

### 15.14 Srota-Dushti (Channel Defects)
- Channels (Srotas) carry food juice, blood, chyle, Vayu, Pitta, Kapha
- Blockade (Srotarodha) gives rise to many diseases
- 13 Srotas described:

### 15.15 Curability/Incurability
- **Easily curable**: Mild causes, premonitory symptoms, symptoms; morbific factor not homologatory; accessible place; localized; recent; no complication; single Dosha; body able to withstand treatment; fourfold requisites available
- **Formidable**: Moderate strength; one of triad (season, constitution, susceptibility) homologatory; gravid/aged/children; not much aggravated by complication; requiring operative/caustic/cauterizing procedures; beyond incipient stage; difficult location; two body systems; not very chronic; two Doshas
- **Incurable but palliable**: Patient still possessed of part of life-span; needs strict regimen; slight relief but readily aggravated; deep-seated; many body elements; vital parts and joints; constantly relapsing; long-standing; two Doshas
- **Incurable and immitigable**: Beyond stage of treatment; spread to all systems; sudden and extreme excitement, restlessness, stupefaction; destroy sense faculties; weakened constitutions; fatal prognostic signs

## 16. Chapter Thirteen: Treatment

### 16.1 General Principles
- When Dosha or Dhatu aggravated → use substance with opposite Gunas
- When deficient → augment with similar properties
- Sushruta: Waning Dosha → nourish; aggravated → calm down; highly increased → relieve excess; equilibrium → maintain
- **Cause of increase**: Similarity
- **Cause of disease**: Dissimilarity

### 16.2 Four Pillars of Treatment
1. **Physician** (Vaidya)
2. **Drugs** (Dravya)
3. **Attendant** (Upasthata)
4. **Patient** (Rogi)

### 16.3 Qualifications

#### Physician
- Clear grasp of theoretical knowledge
- Wide practical experience
- Skill
- Purity of body and mind

#### Drugs
- Abundance
- Applicability
- Usability in multifarious modes
- Richness of quality

#### Nurse/Attendant
- Knowledge of nursing
- Skill
- Affection for patient
- Cleanliness

#### Patient
- Recollection
- Obedience to instructions
- Courage
- Ability to describe ailments

### 16.4 Charaka on Physician's Place
> "The four basic factors, endowed with the abovementioned sixteen qualities are responsible for success in treatment. Of these four, however, the physician occupies the chief place, being at once the knower of disease and drugs, the instructor of the attendant and patient and prescriber of medicine and regimen."

### 16.5 Warning Against Quacks
> "It is better for the quack to offer himself up in the fire than enforce his treatment on the patient. Like a blind man groping about with his hands in fearful uncertainty, or like a rudderless canoe left to the mercy of the winds, the ignorant physician proceeds in his work full of uncertainty and fear."

### 16.6 Classification of Diseases
- **Curable**: Easily cured, cured with difficulty
- **Incurable**: Palliable, absolutely irremediable
- **Curables classified by treatment**: Mild, moderate, strong

### 16.7 Panchakarmas (Five Therapeutic Actions)
1. **Vamana** (Emetic) — Specific for Kapha aggravation
2. **Virechana** (Purgative) — Specific for Pitta aggravation
3. **Basti** (Enema) — Specific for Vayu aggravation
   - Anuvasana (Oily enema)
   - Niruhana (Dry enema)
4. **Nasya** (Errhine)
5. **Raktamokshana** (Blood-letting)

### 16.8 Purva Karmas (Preparatory Actions)
1. **Snehana** (Lubrication) — Ghee, oil, fats, marrow
2. **Swedana** (Fomentation) — Various heating measures

### 16.9 Shamana and Shodhana
- **Shamana**: Palliative; subsides aggravated condition; causative factors remain; disease may recur
- **Shodhana**: Curative; removes Doshas from body; no relapse
- Exemptions from Shodhana: Children, women, old persons, too much debilitated

### 16.10 Specific Measures for Doshas

#### For Vayu
- Lubrication (Snehana)
- Fomentation (Swedana)
- Mild clarification (Mridu samshodhana)
- Sweet, sour, salt tastes
- Enemas (hot and unctuous)
- Sweet and tonic wines
- Mutton soup, juices, oily enemas, til oil
- Deepan and Pachan drugs
- Comfort (Sukha)
- Vata samshamana varga

#### For Pitta
- Ghee (best Pittahara)
- Sweet and cold purgations
- Sweet, astringent, bitter tastes
- Aromatics
- Wearing flowers, pearls, red corals
- Chandan and Kapur paste
- Moonlight
- Cool breeze
- Cold bath
- Milk (best Pitta-subsiding drink)
- Soothing conversation with friends and wife
- Company of wife, children

#### For Kapha
- Vamana (emetics)
- Honey (best antidote)
- Dry, sour, pungent, hot substances
- Bitter, astringent, pungent tastes
- Old wines
- Sexual intercourse
- Keeping awake
- Dieting and fasting
- Anti-obesity treatment

### 16.11 Langhana (Fasting)
- Fasting or extremely light diet
- For fever, etc. where Doshas in heavy proportions
- Advocated by modern Naturopaths (Bernard Macfadden)

### 16.12 Treatment of Women and Children
- 20 types of Yoni Rogas (5 Vata, 5 Pitta, 5 Kapha, 5 Tridosha)
- Menorrhagia, haemorrhage, dysmenorrhoea, amenorrhoea
- Leucorrhoea
- Vandhyatwa (sterility) — causes in both man and woman
- Children's categories: Milk only, milk and food, food only
- Teething troubles

1. **8-year-old girl with asthma** — Recovered with Abharaka Bhasma, Makaradhwaja, Chyawanaprash; eosinophil count returned to normal

  1. Sama Awastha (undigested)

  - Kalabala pravritta (Seasonal)

  - Swabhavabala pravritta (Natural — thirst, hunger, sleep, old age, death)

### 16.16 Seats of Disease (Roga Marga)
1. **Shakha**: External path (skin and dhatus except Rasa)
2. **Madhya**: Vital parts (Marmas)
3. **Koshtha**: Internal organs

## 17. Chapter Fourteen: Rasayanas and Vajikaranas

### 17.1 Rasayana (Rejuvenation)

#### Definition
- **Deha** = "to grow" (anabolic)
- **Sharira** = "to be rendered to pieces" (katabolic)
- Body is product of diet; disease results from faulty diet
- **Rasayana**: Promotive of longevity and health, preservative of youth, dispersive of somnolence/torpor/fatigue, restorative of Tridosha balance, curative of flabbiness, stimulative of gastric fire, promotive of lustre/complexion/voice

#### Two Types
1. **Kutipraveshika**: Special cottage, strict rules, patient stays with physician
2. **Vata Atapika**: Ordinary normal life, professional activities

#### Purification Before Rasayana
1. Snehana and Swedana (lubrication and perspiration)
2. Powder of Harde, Sindhawa (Amalki), Gur, Vacha, Vavadinga, Haridra, Pepper, Sunthi
3. Barley gruel mixed with ghee for 3 nights/5 days/7 days
4. Until intestines purged of all faecal accumulations

#### Analogy of Cloth
> "If a particular cloth is to be coloured and if that cloth is not clean but dirty, the colour will not be fast, whereas if the cloth is cleaned first and then coloured, it will catch the fast dye... Similarly if the body is not pure and clean but is full of Malas, then the effect of the Rasayanas will not be so marked."

#### Code of Conduct
- Speak truth, free from anger
- Abstain from alcohol and sexual congress
- Hurt no one, avoid overstrain
- Tranquil heart, fair-spoken
- Devoted to holy chants and cleanliness
- Endowed with understanding, given to alms-giving
- Diligent in spiritual pursuits
- Delights in reverencing gods, cows, Brahmans, teachers, seniors, elders
- Attached to non-violence, compassionate
- Moderate and balanced in waking and sleeping
- Regular taking of milk and ghee
- Conversant with science of clime, season, dosage
- Versed in propriety, devoid of egotism
- Blameless conduct, wholesome eating
- Spiritual temperament, attached to elders, believers, self-controlled
- Devoted to scriptural texts

#### Rasayana Drugs

**Harde (Chebulic Myrobalan)**
- Foremost place
- Contains all Rasas except Salt
- Hot, beneficial, corrective of all Doshas
- Light, stimulative of gastric fire and digestion
- Promotive of life, roborant, auspicious, best of vitalizers
- Promotive of intellect and sense vigour
- Quick remedy in: Skin disorders, gulma, distension, consumption, anaemia, piles, intoxication, assimilation disorders, diarrhoea, chronic/irregular fever, cardiac disorders, head diseases, anorexia, cough, urinary anomalies, abdominal/splenic disorders, mucus discharge, hoarseness, complexion impairment, jaundice, helminthiasis, edema, asthma, vomiting, impotency, lassitude, srota avarodha, fluid collection, stupefaction of memory
- **Contraindications**: Dyspeptics, consumers of dried food, weakened by indulgence in women, drunkards, poison addictors, afflicted with hunger/thirst/heat

**Amla (Emblic Myrobalan)**
- All virtues of Harde but **Shita Veerya** (cold)
- Where hot action required → Harde
- Where cold action required → Amla
- Where combined action required → Both

**Chyawanprashawaleh**
- Famous elixir
- Ingredients: Bael, wind killer, Indian trumpet flower, white teak trumpet flower, heart leaved sida, four Parnis, ticktrefoil, painted leaved uraria, wild green gram, wild black gram, long pepper, small caltrops, Indian night shade, yellow berried night-shade, galls, ground phylanthus, grapes, cork swallow wort, german iris, eagle-wood, chebulic myrobalan, heart-leaves moon-seed, Riddhi, Jivaka, Rsabhaka, zedoary, nut grass, hog's weed, Meda, cardamom, sandalwood, blue lily, white yam, Vasaka root, Kakoli, small stinking swallow wort + 500 emblic myrobalan fruits
- Boiled in 1,024 tolas water
- Fried in 48 tolas ghee and oil
- Boiled with 200 tolas candied sugar
- Mixed with 24 tolas honey, 16 tolas bamboo manna, 8 tolas long pepper, 4 tolas cinnamon bark, cardamoms, cinnamon leaves, fragrant poon
- **Benefits**: Cure of cough and dyspnea, body-builder for emaciated, pectoral lesions, old, children; subdues loss of voice, chest/heart diseases, thirst, rheumatic disorders; intelligence, memory, lustre, immunity, longevity, increased sense power, amative delights, gastric fire, complexion, peristalsis

**Triphala**
- Chebulic myrobalan + Beleric myrobalan + Emblic myrobalan
- Equal proportions, minutely powdered
- 3-6 grams with ghee and honey morning and evening
- Makes bowels clean, increases appetite, improves digestion, removes blood impurities and excessive heat
- Useful for constipation

**Shilajit (Mineral Pitch)**
- Astringent, slightly acid, pungent post-digestive
- Neither very hot nor very cold
- Derived from four minerals: gold, silver, copper, iron
- Vitalizer, virilific, remedial agent
- Impregnation method: Immerse in lukewarm decoction, remove, reimmerse, repeat 7 days
- **Types**:
  - Gold ore: Sweet, slightly bitter, china rose colour, pungent post-digestive, cold
  - Silver ore: Pungent, white, cold, sweet post-digestive
  - Copper ore: Peacock's throat hue, bitter, hot, pungent post-digestive
  - Iron ore: Gum guggul hue, bitter, slightly saltish, pungent post-digestive, cold (best)
- **Course**: Maximum 7 weeks, Medium 3 weeks, Minimum 1 week
- **Dose**: 4 tolas, 2 tolas, 1 tola
- **Dietary restrictions**: Avoid irritating and heavy articles; horse-gram prohibited; milk, butter-milk, meat juices, gruel, water, cow's urine, decoctions recommended
- Charaka: "There is no curable disease on the earth which mineral pitch cannot cure."

### 17.2 Vajikarana (Virilification)

#### Definition
- Creates spontaneous pleasurable excitement
- Bestows considerable sexual stamina
- Person most liked by opposite sex
- Increases seminal secretions even in aged persons
- Makes semen fertile and productive of children
- Establishes him surrounded by excellent offspring
- Gives reputation, prosperity, health, strength
- **Central idea**: Maintain the family (Apatyakara treatment)

#### Charaka on Vajikarana
> "The man of self-restraint should seek always to maintain his virility by means of virilification as righteousness, wealth, affection and good repute are dependent on the maintenance of healthy manhood."

> "The best means of stimulating one's manhood is an exhilarating sexual partner in the wife."

> "The man without progeny is like a solitary tree that yields no shade... The man of numerous progeny is praised as one possessing a multiplicity of forms, faces, structures, actions, eyes, intellects and souls."

#### Six Forms of Impotency (Sushruta)
1. **Mental**: Cessation of sexual desire due to bitter thoughts or forced intercourse with disagreeable woman
2. **Loss of Saumya Dhatu**: Excessive pungent, acid, saline, heat-making articles
3. **Virile**: Loss of semen in persons addicted to excessive sexual pleasure
4. **Local disease**: Longstanding disease of male generative organ or destruction of local marma
5. **Congenital**: From very birth
6. **Voluntary suppression**: Strong man observing perfect continence or utter apathy

- Congenital and local marma destruction: **Incurable**
- Rest: **Curable**
