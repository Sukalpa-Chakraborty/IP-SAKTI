## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Scope

The VCS-I-ABS scheme applies to Bio-Resources and/or associated knowledge-based businesses that have been authorised by the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) and its constituent bodies for access to genetic and biological resources.

### 1.2 Objective

- Recognise non-monetary benefits extended by approved entities into an incentive scheme.
- Create a differentiator for certified entities to gain market leadership and access newer markets.
- Provide consumers a choice to purchase commodities from entities that have assisted communities from where bio-resources were procured.
- Make India a hub of ABS-compliant entities by certifying businesses and granting them the ABS logo.

### 1.3 Background

- Humans have used biological/genetic resources and traditional knowledge for thousands of years.
- Traditional Knowledge (TK) includes knowledge related to genetic resources, traditional medicine, agricultural practices, traditional cultural expression, and folklore.
- Biological resources provide food crops, cash crops, clothes, medicines, toiletries, cosmetics, beverages, paper, and other products.
- Modern science can help harness biological/genetic resources and associated TK for economic benefits and community livelihoods.

### 1.4 Need

- Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) is the first international agreement for conservation, sustainable use, and fair and equitable benefit sharing.
- CBD recognises State sovereignty over natural resources and requires Prior Informed Consent (PIC) and Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT).
- Nagoya Protocol provides the structure of the ABS system.
- The scheme aims to move beyond compliance and incentivise individuals, communities, and institutions under the ABS ambit.

### 1.5 Solution

- India is a mega-biodiverse country.
- Biological Diversity Act 2002 (BDA) was enacted following CBD.
- Guidelines on Access to Biological Resources and Associated Knowledge & Benefit Sharing Regulation, 2014 were enacted following the Nagoya Protocol.
- Five categories eligible for ABS approval:
  1. Research or bio-survey and bio-utilisation for research.
  2. Commercial utilisation or bio-survey and bio-utilisation for commercial utilisation.
  3. Transfer of result of research.
  4. Obtaining Intellectual Property Rights (IPR).
  5. Transfer of accessed Biological Resources and/or associated knowledge to third party for research/commercial utilisation.
- NBA, in consultation with QCI, developed the framework for incentivisation.
- QCI, UNDP, and NBA signed an agreement to design VCS-ABS under the BIOFIN Project.

### 1.6 Modalities

- Level of compliance for non-monetary benefits makes businesses eligible for the ABS logo.

### 1.8 Key Definitions

- **Benefit claimants:** Conservers of biological resources, their byproducts, creators and holders of knowledge and information relating to the use of such biological resources, innovations and practices associated with such use and application.
- **Biological diversity:** Variability among living organisms from all sources and the ecological complexes of which they are part; includes diversity within species, between species, and of ecosystems.
- **Biological resources:** Plants, animals, micro-organisms or parts thereof, their genetic material and by-products (excluding value-added products) with actual or potential use or value; does not include human genetic material.
- **Bio-survey and Bio-utilization:** Survey or collection of species, subspecies, genes, components and extracts of biological resource for any purpose; includes characterisation, inventorisation and bioassay.
- **Biodiversity Management Committee (BMC):** Local body constituted within its area for promoting conservation, sustainable use, and documentation of biological diversity.
- **Commercial utilization:** End uses of biological resources for commercial utilization such as drugs, industrial enzymes, food flavours, fragrance, cosmetics, emulsifiers, oleoresins, colours, extracts, and genes used for improving crops and livestock through genetic intervention; excludes conventional breeding or traditional practices.
- **Fair and Equitable Benefit Sharing:** Sharing of benefits as determined by NBA under Section 21, BDA.
- **Foreign entities:** As per Section 3, BDA.
- **Local bodies:** Panchayats and Municipalities.
- **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA):** Established under Section 8, BDA.
- **Research:** Study or systematic investigation of any biological resource or technological application using biological systems, living organisms or derivatives.
- **State Biodiversity Board (SBB):** Established under Section 22, BDA.
- **Sustainable use:** Use of components of biological diversity in a manner and rate that does not lead to long-term decline.
- **Value added products:** Products containing portions or extracts of plants and animals in unrecognizable and physically inseparable form.

### 1.9 Exclusions from Approval under BDA

The following do not require NBA or SBB approval:

- Collaborative research projects approved by concerned Ministry/Department.
- Local people and communities, including growers, cultivators, vaids and hakims practising indigenous medicines, except for obtaining IPR.
- Accessing biological resources for conventional breeding or traditional practices in agriculture, horticulture, poultry, dairy farming, animal husbandry, or beekeeping.
- Publication of research papers or dissemination of knowledge in seminars/workshops as per Central Government guidelines.
- Accessing value-added products.
- Human genetic material.
- Biological resources normally traded as commodities notified under Section 40 of BDA.

### 1.10 Factors NBA May Consider

- Cultivated, domesticated, or wild.
- Rare, endemic, endangered, or threatened species.
- Accessed directly through primary collectors or through intermediaries.
- Developed or maintained under ex-situ conditions.
- High value/importance to livelihoods of local communities.
- Restricted under the Act or any other law.
- Exempted under Section 40 of the Act.
- Included in Annex I to ITPGRFA.
- Included in CITES Appendices.

### 1.11 Restrictions on Access

- Endangered taxa.
- Endemic and rare species.
- Adverse effect on livelihoods of local people.
- Adverse environmental impact difficult to control/mitigate.
- Biological/genetic erosion or affecting ecosystem function.
- Use contrary to national interest and international agreements.

### 1.12 Acronyms

ABS, BD, BDA, BDR, BR, BMC, CBD, CC, IPR, MAT, MTA, NBA, PBR, PIC, QCI, SBB, SC, TSG, TC, TK, UNDP, VCS-I-ABS.

## 2. Governing Structure

### 2.1 Objective

Define roles of organisations/committees involved in operating the scheme for incentivisation of entities approved for access to biological resources.

### 2.2 Structure

- **Multi-Stakeholder Steering Committee (MSC):** Apex level.
- **Technical Committee (TC):** Supported by QCI secretariat.
- **Certification Committee (CC):** Supported by QCI secretariat.
- **Secretariat:** Quality Council of India (QCI).

### 2.3 Appointment of Committees – General Rules

- Balance of interests; no single interest predominates.
- Key interests include Ministries, Government Departments, NBA, SBB, BMC, users of biological resources, certification bodies, accreditation bodies, regulatory bodies, academic/research bodies, medicinal plant sector, consumer organisations.
- Individual experts only when suitable organisation representative is not available.
- Members vacate on leaving organisation; fresh nomination sought.
- Member organisations nominate principal and alternate representative.
- Committees reconstituted every two years.

### 2.4 Multi-Stakeholder Steering Committee (MSC)

**Membership:**

- Chairperson: seasoned professional.
- International Organisations: UNDP, UNCTAD, Bio trade, etc.
- Ministries: Ministry of Tribal Affairs, National Medicinal Plant Board, etc.
- Regulatory Bodies: NBA, SBB.
- Government Institutions: NMPB, CSIR-TKDL, etc.
- Academic Institutions: FRLHT, T.N. Dr. Ambedkar Law University, etc.
- Technical experts as invitees.
- Secretariat: QCI.

**Terms of Reference:**

- Overall development, modification, supervision of the Scheme.
- Decide on recommendations from TC and CC.
- Constitute additional committees.

**Meetings:** At least once every year.

### 2.5 Technical Committee (TC)

**Membership:**

- Chairperson: person of eminence.
- Regulatory Bodies: NBA, SBB, BMC, Government Institution.
- Government Institutions: NMPB, MoA.
- Industry Associations: AMMOI, AHNMI, ADMA, etc. by rotation.
- ABS organisations: Chhattisgarh MFP Federation, GMCL, Vinayak Herbal, by rotation.
- Accreditation Bodies: NABCB.
- Secretariat: QCI.

**Terms of Reference:**

- Define certification criteria and resolve related issues.
- Assist Certification Committee in finalising quality-related issues for ABS.

**Meetings:** At least once every year.

### 2.6 Certification Committee (CC)

**Membership:**

- Chairperson: person of eminence.
- Regulatory Body: NBA, SBB by rotation.
- Government Institutions: NMPB, etc.
- Academic Institutions/Knowledge Body: GNLU, etc.
- Industry Association: FICCI.
- Accreditation Boards: NABCB.
- Conformity Assessment Body Association: certification bodies active in NRM.
- NGO in NRM/ABS by rotation.
- Secretariat: QCI.

**Terms of Reference:**

- Develop, maintain, revise certification scheme.
- Develop guidance document for manufacturers.
- Develop requirements for certification bodies.
- Develop Quality Assurance Protocol.
- Develop process for permitting certified projects to use Certification Mark.
- Any other certification issue.

**Meetings:** At least once every year.

### 2.7 Roles of Organisations

- **NBA:** Principal authority; monitors scheme; formal and binding intervention; uses good offices to enable incentivisation.
- **QCI:** Scheme Owner; owns Certification Mark(s); establishes MSC; provides Secretariat; sets up TC and CC; manages scheme on behalf of NBA; enters legally enforceable agreements.
- **NABCB:** Accredits certification bodies and inspection bodies to appropriate international standards.
- **Applicant:** Responsible for understanding scheme, submitting documentation, engaging with NBA, SBB, BMC; QCI/CBs register compliance and raise non-conformances.

### 2.8 Complaints

- All components must have complaints system.
- Complaints to NBA referred to QCI.
- QCI refers to appropriate body and monitors.
- Statement on complaints reported to MSC.

### 2.9 Appeals

- Appeals from manufacturing units, CBs, laboratories.
- Appeals against TC/CC decisions handled by MSC.
- Appeals against MSC decisions handled by independent appeals panel appointed by MSC Chairperson.
- Appeal handled independently of personnel involved in original decision.
- Statement of appeals forwarded to QCI and may be placed before MSC.

## 3. Certification Criteria

### 3.1 Scope

- Applicable to entities involved with Bio-Resources and/or associated traditional knowledge-based businesses authorised by NBA and constituent bodies.
- Only NBA-authorised entities eligible; others excluded.
- Can be used for benchmarking domestic requirements or harmonised with international standards.

### 3.2 Objectives

- Strengthen adoption and uptake of ABS requirements in India.
- Provide voluntary mechanism for ABS users.
- Grant certification of compliance to help access new markets or earn premium.
- Provide minimum requirements for bio-resource sustainability, traceability, and social/business requirements.
- Cover eight main principles through 45 clauses.
- Assist certification bodies assign 1, 2, and 3 stars based on compliance.

### 3.3 Normative References

- Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing, 2010.
- Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
- National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 – insertion of new Section 52A in BDA.
- Biological Diversity Rules, 2004.
- Guidelines on Access to Biological Resources and Associated Knowledge & Benefit Sharing Regulation, 2014.
- Plantation Act, 1950.
- Factories Act, 1948.
- Labour Code, 2020.

### 3.4 Certification System

Two-step process:

1. Initial approval of entity by NBA – mandatory.
2. Voluntary intervention with respect to community benefit – VCS-I-ABS.

### 3.5 Approval Process Forms

- **Form I:** Access to Biological Resources and associated TK (foreign entity for research/commercial utilisation/bio-survey/bio-utilisation).
- **Form II:** Transfer of results of research to foreign nationals, companies, NRIs for commercial purposes.
- **Form III:** Seeking prior approval of NBA for applying for IPR.
- **Form IV:** Third-party transfer of accessed biological resources and associated TK.
- **Form A:** Indian citizen/body corporate registered in India applying to SBB for prior intimation before obtaining biological resource for commercial utilisation.
- **Form B:** Non-commercial research or research for emergency purposes outside India by Indian researchers/government institutions.

### 3.6 Certification Principles

1. **BD – Bio-Resource Sustainability:** Access must ensure conservation and sustainability of ecosystem.
2. **TR – Traceability:** Identification and tracking of bio-resources used; stakeholders aware.
3. **TK – Traditional Knowledge:** Recording TK utilised; combat bio-piracy; create awareness.
4. **COM – Community Development:** Better livelihood options; enhance living standards.
5. **GI – Gender Inclusion:** Mainstream women; participation and decision-making; skill development; safe working environment.
6. **RB – Responsible Business:** GRM, child labour issues, green environment.
7. **VCS – Compliance to any Voluntary Certification:** Recognise compliance to other voluntary certifications.

### 3.7 Compliance Requirements

- Criteria marked Critical, Major, Minor.
- **Critical:** 100% compliance of all applicable Critical indicators compulsory.
- **Major:** 90% compliance of all applicable Major indicators compulsory.
- **Minor:** 75% compliance of all applicable Minor indicators compulsory.
- **Timelines to close issues:**
  - Critical: 30 days
  - Major: 3 months
  - Minor: 12 months

### 3.8 Labelling Requirements

- Provide information to secondary/tertiary processors, aggregators, buyers, retailers.
- Transaction documents must bear Certification Mark (VCS-I-ABS).
- Off-product material such as invoices, letterhead, promotional material may be labelled.
- Must ensure labelling requirements of country where produced and sold.

## Annex A: ABS Certification Criteria – Requirements

### Table 01: Foreign Entities – Commercial Utilization

**Form I**

- Full particulars of applicant.
- Details of access: scientific name, traditional use, geographical location, nature of TK, quantity, time span, authorised person, purpose.
- National institution participating in R&D.
- Primary destination and R&D location.
- Economic and other benefits including IPR.
- Biotechnological, scientific, social benefits.
- Estimation of benefits to India/communities.
- Benefit sharing:
  - Purchase price: traders 1–3%; manufacturers 3–5%.
  - Ex-factory sale price: turnover up to ₹1 crore – 0.1%; ₹1–3 crore – 0.2%; above ₹3 crore – 0.5%.
- Upfront payment for high economic value resources.
- Fee: ₹10,000 by cheque/demand draft.
- Consultation with BMC.
- NBA to ensure benefit sharing criteria.
- Time period: NBA to finish application within 6 months.
- Measures for conservation and protection.
- Grant/rejection/revocation of approval.
- Publication and monitoring of compliance.

### Table 02: Foreign or Indian Entities – Transfer of Result

**Form II**

- Full particulars of applicant.
- Details of research results.
- Biological resources and/or associated knowledge used.
- Geographical location.
- Traditional knowledge details.
- Institution where R&D carried out.
- Details of transferee.
- Economic, biotechnological, scientific benefits.
- Benefit sharing: 3–5% of monetary consideration.
- Upfront payment for high economic value.
- Fee: ₹5,000.
- Consultation with BMC.
- NBA to finish application within 3 months.
- Grant/rejection of approval.

### Table 03: Foreign or Indian Entities – Obtaining IPR

**Form III**

- Full particulars of applicant.
- Details of invention.
- Biological resources and/or associated knowledge used.
- Geographical location.
- Traditional knowledge details.
- Institution where R&D carried out.
- Benefits expected.
- Benefit sharing:
  - Applicant commercialises: 0.2–1.0% of annual gross ex-factory sale minus government taxes.
  - Assigns/licenses to third party: 3–5% of fee received; 2–5% of royalty annually.
- Fee: ₹500.
- NBA to finish application within 3 months.
- Grant/rejection of approval.

### Table 04: Foreign or Indian Entities – Transfer of Material

**Form IV**

- Full particulars of applicant.
- Details of biological material and TK accessed.
- Access contract details.
- Benefits and benefit-sharing already implemented.
- Third party particulars.
- Purpose of transfer.
- Benefits to third party.
- Agreement between applicant and third party.
- Estimation of benefits to India/communities.
- Benefit sharing: 2–5% of any amount and/or royalty received from transferee.
- Fee: ₹10,000.
- NBA to dispose application within 6 months.
- Grant/rejection of approval.

### Table 05: Indian Entities – Research & Commercial Utilization

**Form A and SBB-prescribed form**

- Full particulars of biological resource: common name, scientific name, plants/animals/parts traded, specific purpose.
- Locations/source.
- Quantity procured.
- Rate per unit.
- Name of SBB.
- Prospective buyers/users if known.
- No fees applicable.
- Benefit sharing:
  - Purchase price: traders 1–3%; manufacturers 3–5%.
  - Ex-factory sale price: turnover up to ₹1 crore – 0.1%; ₹1–3 crore – 0.2%; above ₹3 crore – 0.5%.
- Upfront payment for high economic value/high conservation value.

### Table 06: Non-Commercial Research or Emergency Research Outside India

**Form B – Self declaration to NBA**

- Name of applicant (Indian researcher/government institution).
- Address.
- Institution in India.
- Supervisor/Head of Institution.
- Institution/organisation receiving biological resources.
- Funding agency.
- Brief description of research.
- Details of biological resources: scientific/common name, location, quantity, duration.
- Emergency purpose details.
- No fees applicable.
- NBA to dispose application within 45 days.

## Section 4: Certification Process

### 4.1 Objective

Ensure objective assessment and certification of VCS-I-ABS for business entities approved by NBA for access to biological resources.

### 4.2 Scope

Covers certification process for business entities compliant to ABS norms laid by NBA under BDA 2002 for incentivisation after grant of logo.

### 4.3 Certification for Grant of ABS Logo

- Business entity must meet compliance criteria for use of ABS logo on-product and off-product.
- Open to all legal entities engaged in collection and harvesting of biological resources from India and other countries.
- Information available on QCI website.
- Certification carried out by CBs approved by QCI and constituent Board.
- CBs should have extension of scope within accreditation for ISO/IEC 17065.

### 4.4 Certification Process

- Application after NBA approval.
- Legal entity applies to approved CB.
- Contractual relationship and Certification Agreement with CB.
- Entity requirements:
  - Documented administrative structure.
  - Written signed contracts with community.
  - Register of all community attributes and sites.
  - Unique client number.
  - Information required for registration.
  - CB maintains publicly available certification processes.
  - CB responds to enquiries within 7 days.
  - Application form requirements.
  - Declaration of judicial proceedings.
  - Certification granted only against latest criteria.
  - Review within 7 days.
  - Registration within 7 days.
  - Antecedent verification.
  - Rejection/closure conditions.
  - Refund of application fee.

### 4.5 Quality Management System of Entity

- Management and organisation structure.
- Responsibility and duties.
- Competency and training of staff.
- Quality Manual.
- Document Control.
- Records: minimum 3 years.
- Complaint Handling.
- Internal Audits and Inspections.
- QMS Audit at least annually.
- Communities and Collection Location Inspections at least once a year.
- Non-conformities and Corrective Action Systems.
- Product Traceability and Segregation.
- Sanctions and Non-Conformances.

### 4.6 Pre-assessment

- Optional.
- CB checks applicant's state of preparedness.
- Only one pre-assessment.

### 4.7 Initial Evaluation

- Single stage initial evaluation by competent team.
- Conducted within 3 months of registration.
- CB communicates team composition and duration.
- Timings and date decided in consultation.
- Inspection timings.
- First inspection timing for multiple produce certification.

### 4.8 Evaluation Process

- Witness processes covering as many criteria as possible.
- Nonconformities classified as critical, major, minor.
- Facility evaluation/audit.
- CB reviews internal evaluation reports.
- External QMS Audit annually.
- External collector inspection: random sample minimum square root of total registered communities.
- Compliance for certification:
  - Critical: 100%
  - Major: 90%
  - Minor: 75%
- Internal self-assessment quality assurance at least once a year.

### 4.9 Grant of Certification

- CB grants certification after complete compliance and satisfactory resolution of nonconformities.
- No conditional grant.
- Certificate uniquely identified; scope, criteria, effective date, validity date, name and address.
- Brand names mentioned.
- Effective date not before decision date.
- Certificate valid for 3 years.
- List of collectors and sites annexed.
- Scope linked to community where entity procures material.

### 4.10 Surveillance Evaluation

- At least once a year; gap not exceeding one year.
- Grace period of one month may be allowed.
- Full checklist and verification annually.
- Check compliance, internal self-assessment, nonconformities, complaints, production and consignees.
- Nonconformity categorised and reported.
- Frequency may be increased with justification.
- Infructuous visit may lead to another surveillance evaluation.

### 4.11 Suspension of Certification

- Due notice of at least one week; serious failures may not require notice.
- Issued for unsatisfactory performance during two consecutive surveillance evaluations or voluntary request.
- Time period for correction not exceeding 6 months.
- During suspension, entity prevented from using logo/trademark/license/certificate.
- Root cause analysis and corrective actions required.
- Suspension revoked after verification.
- Suspension not to exceed 6 months; else cancellation.

### 4.12 Cancellation of Certification

- Issued when corrective action not shown after suspension and 6 months elapsed; nonconformity leads to doubt about integrity; major contractual nonconformities.
- Total prohibition of use of logo/trademark/license/certificate.
- Re-submission after 12 months.
- Entity must resolve nonconformities or appeal in writing.
- CB may cancel at request of certified client for natural calamities or closure.

### 4.13 Recertification

- Certificate revalidated every year; recertification at end of 3 years.
- CB sends recertification notice at least 4 months prior to expiry.
- Entity applies at least 3 months before expiry.
- Review based on surveillance reports, nonconforming products, suspension, corrective actions, complaints, adverse information.
- No conditional recertification.
- If unsatisfactory, CB withholds recertification and gives time for corrective actions.
- Verification and decision within 3 months of expiry.
- If not completed, certificate stands expired.

### 4.14 Change of Ownership/Name

- New owner submits proof and acceptance to agreement.
- Change of name informed with documentary evidence.

### 4.15 Extension of Scope

- Additional produce/varieties included after ascertaining resources and technical skills.
- Extension clearly mentioned in certificate; validity unchanged.

### 4.16 Certificate

- CB provides certification document with name, location, dates, expiry, unique ID, criteria, scope, CB details, marks, revised document distinction.
- Effective date not before decision.
- Signed by authorised individual(s).

### 4.17 Fee

- Fee charged without discrimination.
- Fee structure publicly accessible.
- Consent obtained prior to grant; changes communicated.

### 4.18 Withdrawal of Certified Product

- Documented procedures for withdrawal.
- Identify events, responsible persons, notification mechanism.
- Procedure tested at least annually.

### 4.19 Subcontractors

- Procedures for subcontracted services.
- Records of competency.
- Subcontractors work per group's QMS and agreements.

## Section 6: Rules for Use of Certification Mark

### 6.1 Purpose

- Certified entities allowed to use Certification Mark.
- Must enter written contract with QCI.
- Certification Mark is protected mark owned by QCI.

### 6.2 Scope

Covers requirements for use of Mark with respect to certified VCS-I-ABS.

### 6.3 Prerequisites

- Certified under Scheme by NABCB accredited/QCI approved CBs.
- Sign agreement with QCI for use of Mark.
- Application in prescribed format.
- CB makes provision in its system and legally enforceable contract.

### 6.4 Certification Marks and Certificate

- Combination of products/parts complying with criteria does not always constitute finished product complying as a whole.
- Entity responsible for ensuring VCS-I-ABS model complies.
- Additional scope requires application to QCI, informing NBA, depositing benefit sharing.
- Certification marking is key indicator but not proof.
- Certificate of conformity issued for product type.
- Mark may be used as photographic reduction/enlargement; colour scheme same.
- Certificate issued by certifying CB carries appropriate mark.

### 6.5 Conditions for Use

- Sign legally enforceable agreement with QCI.
- Pay annual fee to QCI through CB.
- May use in publicity material, pamphlets, letterheads, stationery, media.
- May use certificate as part of publicity material.
- Must not give impression non-certified products/offices/related companies are certified.
- No misleading claims.
- Must not bring Scheme Owners, QCI, NBA, SBB/BMC into disrepute.
- Violations may lead to corrective actions, suspension, or withdrawal.
- Upon suspension/cancellation/withdrawal, discontinue use of Mark and advertising.
- Retail products may carry ABS logo only when 70% of ingredients are sourced from ABS-compliant business entities.
- NBA may declare list of species of importance.
- If ingredient less than 70%, CB documents composition and rationale by referring to classical text.
- ABS logo uniform; provision to declare percent of ingredients.

### 6.6 Process for Signing Contract Between QCI and Certified Entity

- CB requires entity to fill contract form in duplicate.
- CB forwards to QCI with details.
- Upon receiving signed contract from QCI, CB issues certificate and informs entity.
- Certification validity commences from day contract with QCI is signed.
- Annual fee submitted to QCI.
- CB informs QCI about changes in certification status.
- Contract valid as long as entity holds valid certification.

### 6.7 Contract Terms

- Applicant agrees to comply with Scheme requirements, pay annual fee, use Mark only for certified scope, not make misleading claims, inform QCI of changes, allow announced/unannounced/decoy assessments, discontinue use upon suspension/withdrawal/cancellation.
- Disputes governed by Laws of India; jurisdiction of courts in Delhi.
- Applicant indemnifies QCI.

## Section 7: Portfolio of Incentivisation

### 7.1 Scope

- Applies to Bio-Resources and/or associated knowledge-based businesses approved by NBA.
- Entities assessed based on stated criteria for incentivisation.
- Portfolio offered to entities approved for access to Bio-resources.
- Objective: incentivise entities for care and compassion for communities; enhance quality of life.

### 7.2 Background

- Humans have used biological/genetic resources for thousands of years.
- Globalization and trade make commodities of interest in national and international markets.
- Multinational companies have accessed resources without involving original stakeholders.
- Need for uniform protection regime for biological/genetic resources and associated TK.
- Need for incentivisation mechanism to encourage responsible entities to go beyond Act.

### 7.3 The Need

- Protection regime consolidated internationally and implemented nationally.
- Incentivisation mechanism for biodiversity conservation.
- Motivate entities and consumers to adopt conservation.
- Measures spell out activities required from individuals, institutions, industries.
- Identification of new opportunities and dissemination of information.
- Synergy with sustainable use.
- Contribute to Sustainable Development Goals.
- Capacity building, public awareness, effective implementation.
- Thematic incentive measures with non-monetary benefits and green branding.

### 7.4 The Solution

- NBA in consultation with QCI developed framework for BDA voluntary incentivisation certification scheme.
- Framework integrates provisions of Act; encourages approved entities to strengthen community institutions and livelihoods.
- Design and development through standardized system.
- ABS as strong selling point in global market.

### 7.5 Certification Criteria

- NBA-approved entities eligible.
- Compliance levels:
  - Critical: 100%
  - Major: 90%
  - Minor: 75%

### 7.6 Portfolio of Incentivisation

1. **Public Procurement:** Preference for logo holders in public procurement through GeM.
2. **Pre-requisite/conditionality for funding:** Prioritise ABS-compliant entities for funding; awards/grants may require ABS logo.
3. **Regulator to take cognizance and reduce oversight:** NBA may reduce oversight; simplify IPR procedures; relax Form III fee.
4. **Tool for monitoring and evaluation:** Third-party certification covers regular supervision, monitoring, evaluation; cost saving.
5. **Incentives by Government while allocating space in International and National events:** Facilitation such as stalls, boarding, lodging, travel fare.
6. **ABS Logo Promotion:** Nationwide campaign by NBA and Ministry of Consumer Affairs.
7. **Fast tracking ABS logo holders as 'Green Companies':** Recommend as green companies or sustainability-driven listed companies.
8. **Reduces legal burden related to ABS:** Third-party validation establishes transparent business practice.
9. **Facilitating Regional Trade – SAARC/BIMSTEC/ASEAN region:** Potential recognition across member nations; facilitate sustainable trade in genetic resources.

## Key RAG Retrieval Notes

- **Scheme name:** Voluntary Certification Scheme for Incentivisation of Access and Benefit Sharing (VCS-I-ABS).
- **Regulator:** National Biodiversity Authority (NBA).
- **Scheme owner:** Quality Council of India (QCI).
- **Accreditation:** NABCB.
- **Legal basis:** Biological Diversity Act 2002; Biological Diversity Rules 2004; ABS Guidelines 2014; Nagoya Protocol; CBD.
- **Eligibility:** Entities approved by NBA for access to biological resources.
- **Certification levels:** Critical 100%, Major 90%, Minor 75%.
- **Certification validity:** 3 years, with annual surveillance and yearly revalidation.
- **Logo usage:** 70% ingredients must be sourced from ABS-compliant entities; NBA may declare species of importance.
- **Benefit sharing percentages:** Vary by form and category (e.g., 1–3% for traders, 3–5% for manufacturers, 0.1–0.5% based on turnover, 0.2–1.0% for IPR commercialisation, 2–5% for transfer of material).
- **Governing bodies:** MSC, Technical Committee, Certification Committee, QCI Secretariat.
- **Complaints and appeals:** Handled by QCI, MSC, and independent appeals panel.
