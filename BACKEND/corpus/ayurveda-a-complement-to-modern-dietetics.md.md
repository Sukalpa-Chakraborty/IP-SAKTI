## Chunk AY-CD-008 — Tri-Dosha Theory and Pancha-mahabhutas

**Source pages:** 20–24  
**Topics:** Tri-Dosha, Pancha-mahabhutas, five elements, dosha qualities, body types

**Summary:**  
Humans are tied to their environments through the five great elements (Pancha-mahabhutas): Earth (Prithvi) – solidity/stability; Water (Jala) – liquidity; Fire (Agni) – transformation; Air (Vayu) – movement; Ether (Akash) – emotions/thoughts. Attributes: hot/cold, wet/dry, heavy/light, gross/subtle, dense/flowing, static/mobile, dull/sharp, soft/hard, smooth/rough, cloudy/clear. Five elements and 20 attributes categorize into three doshas: Vata (ether+air) – movement & communication; Pitta (fire+water) – metabolism; Kapha (water+earth) – structure & cohesion. Table of dosha features: body frame, weight gain, food intake, metabolic capacity, disease resistance, cellular functions, disease predisposition, general qualities.

**Key points:**
- Five elements: Earth, Water, Fire, Air, Ether.
- 20 attributes pair opposites.
- Vata = ether + air; Pitta = fire + water; Kapha = water + earth.
- Vata: thin, irregular, cold, dry, quick; prone to arrhythmias, developmental, neurological.
- Pitta: medium, intense, hot, moist, sharp; prone to ulcers, skin, bleeding.
- Kapha: broad, heavy, cold, wet, slow; prone to obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular.

**Table — Dosha Selected Features:**

| Feature | Vata | Pitta | Kapha |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body frame | Thin | Medium | Broad |
| Weight gain | Resistant | Fluctuating | Tendency to gain |
| Food intake | Frequent, variable, irregular | Intense hunger | Low digestive capacity |
| Metabolic capacity | Irregular | Fast | Slow |
| Disease resistance/healing | Poor | Good | Excellent |
| Cellular functions | Cell division, signaling, movement, cognition | Metabolism, energy balance, immunity | Anabolism, growth, storage, stability |
| Disease predisposition | Arrhythmias, developmental, neurological | Ulcer, skin diseases, bleeding disorders | Obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular |
| General qualities | Cold, dry, clear, quick | Hot, moist, sharp, oily | Cold, wet, heavy, soft, dense, oily, slow |

## Chunk AY-CD-009 — Prakriti: Constitutional Uniqueness

**Source pages:** 25–28  
**Topics:** Prakriti, Vikruti, genotype, phenotype, epigenetic determinants, AyuSoft, biochemical individuality, disease susceptibility, genomics

**Summary:**  
The 3 doshas produce 7 body types (Prakritis): Vata, Pitta, Kapha, Vata-Kapha, Vata-Pitta, Pitta-Kapha, Vata-Pitta-Kapha. Prakriti imbalance results in Vikruti (disease). Doshic proportions determined at conception and influenced by epigenetic determinants, parental health/age, intra- and extra-uterine environment, diet, stress, lifestyle, environmental pollutants, maternal gut microbiota. AyuSoft Prakriti assessment tool. Biochemical individuality: gene studies show differences between dosha types in biochemical parameters, gene expression, drug metabolism; Pitta types are fast metabolizers. Disease susceptibility: Kapha individuals, particularly males, to cardiovascular disease; Vata-Pitta subtype prone to rheumatoid arthritis; Pitta individuals higher hemoglobin, more oxidative stress; 78 genes differentially expressed linked to chronic metabolic diseases. Study: 300 males >25 with CAD; dyslipidemia and HOMA-IR in KaphaVata; Tc, LDL, TG, VLDL, inflammatory markers higher in VataKapha.

**Key points:**
- 7 Prakritis from 3 doshas.
- Prakriti = genotype; Vikruti = disease.
- Influenced by epigenetics, parental health, uterine environment, diet, stress, lifestyle, pollutants, microbiota.
- AyuSoft assessment tool.
- Pitta fast metabolizers.
- Kapha males prone to CVD.
- Vata-Pitta prone to rheumatoid arthritis.
- Pitta more oxidative stress.
- KaphaVata: dyslipidemia, HOMA-IR.
- VataKapha: higher Tc, LDL, TG, VLDL, inflammatory markers.

**Notable quotes:**
> “Prakriti imbalance results in Vikruti characterized by disease.”

> “Doshic proportions are determined at conception and influenced by epigenetic determinants.”

Digestion of Ama before Panchakarma therapies can be done.

- Vriddhi = excess; Kshaya = deficiency.

## Chunk AY-CD-013 — Disease Etiology: Ancient vs Modern — Diabetes and Cancer

**Source pages:** 34–35  
**Topics:** diabetes, Madhumeha, cancer, Prakriti, doshas, dhatus, Ama, inflammation, immunotherapy

**Summary:**  
Ancient concept of diabetes (Madhumeha): excess intake of dairy, sugar, fresh grains; lack of exercise; psychological stress; Kapha – low Agni, low Ojas. Accumulation, aggravation, migration, localization of Ama; Pitta; hyperglycemia/hypoinsulinemia; Vata; genetic predisposition due to defects in sperm or ovum; negative intra-uterine environment; epigenetic changes. Modern concept: high calorie unhealthy diet, disturbed macronutrient metabolism, obesity, glycosuria, proteinuria, inflammatory/hepatic/gallbladder complications, free radical insult, metabolic disturbances, loss of immunity, insulin-dependent disease, metabolic syndrome, macro/micro vascular complications. Cancer: modern concepts vs Ayurvedic concepts. Ayurvedic: cancer results when abnormal interactions between Prakriti (genotype) and environmental factors vitiate doshas and impair immunity. Abnormalities besides aberrant cell growth/death cause cancer. Epigenetic regulation, diet, environmental factors, immunity affect phenotypes. Interaction between vitiated doshas and weak tissues (Dhatus) manifests as cancers of specific organs. Shared molecular pathology between cancer and metabolic syndrome. Links between improper diet, digestion, metabolism, inflammation, disease. “Ama” may be novel biomarker for early inflammation. Therapies indirectly target cancer tissue by eliminating vitiated doshas, rejuvenating dhatus, restoring immunity. Anti-inflammatory and antidiabetic drugs indirectly destroy cancer tissue. Immunotherapy. Cancer vaccines.

**Key points:**
- Ancient diabetes concept: Kapha, low Agni, low Ojas, Ama, Pitta, Vata.
- Modern diabetes: high calorie diet, obesity, inflammation, metabolic syndrome.
- Ayurvedic cancer concept: Prakriti + environment vitiate doshas, impair immunity.
- Ama as potential biomarker for early inflammation.
- Ayurvedic therapies target doshas, dhatus, immunity.
- Anti-inflammatory and antidiabetic drugs may indirectly destroy cancer tissue.

**Notable quotes:**

## Chunk AY-CD-015 — Integration of Diet — “AHAAR”

**Source pages:** 38–42  
**Topics:** AHAAR, food as medicine, what/how much/when to eat, taste to balance dosha, food qualities and prakriti

**Summary:**  
Food as medicine in Ayurveda: primary mode of treatment; physiological conditions (e.g., lactation); specific disease conditions; adjuvant to other treatments; preparation of Rasayanas; precursor for specific treatments (e.g., Panchakarma). “What to eat,” “How much to eat,” “When to eat.” Food quality, quantity, kind, source, season, taste. Freshness and seasonality important. One third of stomach solid, one third liquid, one third empty for proper movement of doshas. Dosha qualities of foods used therapeutically: bitter, astringent reduce Pitta; plant-based diets contribute to Sattvik mind. Food preparation, consumption according to gender and life-cycle needs. Rasayanas in early life can mitigate genetic predispositions, restore health, boost immunity. Food avoidance = reduction in quantity and frequency, not abstinence. Daily judicious use of spices and herbs (phytochemicals, antioxidants). Synergistic food and herbal combinations. Meal time important: large healthy mid-day meal when Pitta (digestive capacity) is greatest, lighter breakfast and evening meal.

**Taste to Balance Dosha:**

| Taste | Examples | Vata | Pitta | Kapha |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweet | Sugar, milk, butter, rice, bread | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Sour | Yogurt, lemon, cheese | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |
| Salty | Salt | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |
| Pungent | Spices, pepper, ginger | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| Bitter | Spinach, other greens | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Astringent | Beans, walnuts | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ |

Example: Sweets decrease or pacify Pitta while salty foods increase Pitta. If Pitta is aggravated, increase sweet while decreasing salty foods.

**Major Food Qualities and Prakriti:**

| Food Quality | Examples | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dry | Beans, potatoes, barley, corn | ↑ Vata |
| Cold | Beverages; raw foods | ↑ Vata |
| Light | Corn, spinach, millet, barley | ↑ Vata |
| Heavy | Table sugar | ↑ Kapha |
| Oily | Dairy, meat, fatty foods | ↑ Kapha/Pitta |
| Hot | Beverages, warm cooked foods | ↑ Pitta |

**Key points:**
- AHAAR = food as medicine.
- What, how much, when to eat.
- One-third solid, one-third liquid, one-third empty.
- Tastes balance doshas.
- Bitter/astringent reduce Pitta.
- Plant-based diet → Sattvik mind.
- Spices/herbs provide phytochemicals and antioxidants.
- Mid-day meal largest when Pitta strongest.
