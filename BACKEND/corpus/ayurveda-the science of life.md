## Editorial: A Holistic Opportunity

Modern medicine initially used the concept of the “family doctor” to reach people. With the rise of super-specialities, this concept was largely abandoned, especially in the West. Physicians became less accessible to the masses.

New trends suggest people are turning back to general practitioners and showing interest in alternative and holistic medicine. This is good news for Ayurveda. Ayurveda has always relied on easily and locally available, cheap resources to prepare medicinal formulations that cured even difficult diseases. This virtue should be propagated to give Ayurveda its due among medical sciences.

The Global Ayurveda Summit, organized by CII, Department of AYUSH, and the Government of Kerala in Kochi on March 23-25, is expected to produce new initiatives. ## Cover Story: Spring in the Autumn

### Geriatric Care in Ayurveda

**Compilation:** K Govindan Nampoothiry  
**Consultants:** Dr Rajeev Pillai, Dr Raj Kumar, Dr Shaji Varghese, Dr M Prasad

Childhood is a time of innocence and discovery. Youth is a phase of excitement and adventure. Middle age is a period of career, family, and responsibilities. Old age is a time to relax and slow down. But over-enthusiasm and over-exertion of youthful days can take a toll on the body and mind. Geriatrics focuses on ailments of old age.

“Geros” in Greek means “old man” and “iatros” means “healer.” Geriatrics deals with problems and diseases specific to old age: how to prevent them or manage them if they have already set in. *Jara chikitsa*, one of the eight branches of Ashtanga Ayurveda, deals with measures for retaining agility and vitality in old age. It suggests this can be achieved through medicines, food, and control over body and mind.

Ayurveda stresses that the mind exerts the deepest influence on the body. A state of balanced awareness leads to right attitudes, right actions, and a higher state of health.

### Tridoshas and Phases of Life

Ayurveda holds that the three Humours or Tridoshas coordinate and enable changes in the body.

- **Kapha:** Growth-oriented; oversees anabolism.
- **Pitha:** Ensures sustenance and metabolism.
- **Vata:** Denotes deterioration; presides over catabolism.

They exert influence differently at different phases:

- **Early years:** Kapha dominates.
- **Middle age:** Pitha takes the upper hand.
- **Old age:** Vata takes over.

Old age is dominated by Vata, wherein metabolism slows down and energy resources dry up. Ayurveda approaches geriatrics by helping the body retain its Pitha-Kapha combination and keep Vata from over-exerting itself.

Classical texts by Charaka and Vagbhata begin with chapters such as “How to live long” and “What prolongs life,” enumerating measures that help prevent diseases and preserve health.

### How the Cell Ages

Almost all living cells undergo transformation:

- **Anabolism:** Build-up
- **Metabolism:** Conversion
- **Catabolism:** Break-down

In the initial stage, structures that form the skeleton of the cell accumulate. Assimilation and nourishment take place at maximum pace. Cells have maximum nourishment, good metabolic power, and easily utilisable energy resource.

As maximum nourishment is reached, metabolic activities effecting conversion gradually dominate. This results in maturation of cells. Later, energy resource is used up, the cell becomes worn out, metabolic activity fails, energy production stops, and cell death occurs.

### Diseases of Old Age

Diseases under geriatrics include joint pains, eye diseases, hypertension, and mental anxiety. It is difficult to identify one causative gene or triggering factor. It is an outcome of many factors, including improper food and lack of exercise. Natural degenerative changes in old age are accentuated by these factors.

With age, metabolic activity becomes moderate and energy source declines. Treatment focuses on increasing digestive and metabolic power and supplementing energy requirements through internal and external use of suitable fatty substances.

### Rasayana Chikitsa

Rasayana chikitsa enhances health and vitality, revitalizing tissues worn out or in the process of wearing out due to disease and ageing. It:

- Enhances the immune system
- Arrests ageing
- Gives lustre to the skin
- Gives youthful energy
- Nourishes blood and body tissues (Sapta dhatus)
- Eliminates senility and other diseases of old age

The therapy includes immuno-modulation, antioxidant action, adaptogenic effects, and more.

Panchakarma is effective for age-related health problems. Mismanagement, medicine side effects, and iatrogenic diseases have sidelined natural old age diseases. Lack of awareness of healthy food and regimen, fast food, heavy spicy food, and artificial environment have added new diseases under geriatric ailments.

### Live Long, Healthy: Ayurvedic Tips

- Use ghee, buttermilk, sesame oil, soup, and water prepared with dry ginger and coriander judiciously.
- Attend calls of nature on time.
- Exercise regularly to increase appetite and energy source.
- Apply sesame oil externally to prevent degenerative changes of skin, joints, nerves, and blood vessels.
- Oil application should be done on an empty stomach when digestive power is normal.
- Walk regularly after the onset of appetite and before food to increase metabolic power, blood circulation, absorption, and energy utilisation.
- Practise wholesome food and regimen during middle age.
- Avoid immune suppressant drugs and treatment.
- Do not interfere with natural body processes.
- Administer rejuvenative and aphrodisiac medicines during young and middle age.
- Practise yogic exercises, listen to music, read, and play games for mental strength.
- Good friendship reduces stress.
- Avoid stressful situations, inappropriate acts, intoxicating beverages, and non-vegetarian predominant diet.

## Age-Related Eye Diseases

### Be Far-Sighted

The eyes of a 60-year-old may not be as powerful as those of an 18-year-old. Degeneration varies according to climate, food habits, lifestyle, and hereditary factors.

According to Dr K Prasad, chief physician, Sunethri Ayurveda Hospital, Thrissur:

- Cataract, macular degeneration, and glaucoma are the main diseases that blind aged people.
- Diabetic patients have a high chance of developing diabetic retinopathy.
- Early diagnosis and right corrective measures are significant.
- Prevention is key. Rasayana chikitsa is suggested for eye ailments in middle age.

### Cataract (Linganaash)

Cataract is caused by aggravation of Vata. Vitiated Vata dries up the liquid that keeps the eye lens and retina supple. The lens loses moisture and suppleness, becomes opaque, and leads to cataract.

**Causes:**

- Temperature variations
- Inflammation
- Constipation
- Decreased ability of the capsule covering the eye to allow fluids to enter and exit
- Diabetes
- Vitamin deficiency
- Irregularities in digestion

**Symptoms:**

- Vision becomes hazier
- Difficulty focusing on objects
- Eyes become sensitive to light
- Double vision in one eye

**Management:**

- Oil bath with Thriphaladi coconut oil or Neelibhringadi coconut oil
- Intake of Triphala choornam
- Drinking Triphala water
- Kajayapanam
- Chandrodaya Varti as collyrium
- Nasyam with Seetharandathi thailam
- Netra dhara is effective

**Food Notes:**

- Avoid food that aggravates Kapha-Pitha dosha.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Avoid curd.
- Avoid fast foods, sour food, and cold drinks.
- Avoid sleeping during the day.
- Avoid bathing at noon.
- Limit alcohol.
- Avoid smoking.
- Eat lutein-rich foods: spinach, mangoes, cucumber, tomato, sweet potatoes, carrots, oranges.
- Avoid reading or working in dim light or very bright sunlight.
- Limit TV and computer use.

### Macular Degeneration

Age-related Macular Degeneration is a condition where aged people lose vision in the centre of the visual field—the macula. It is primarily caused by lack of essential fats that nourish eye tissues.

**Symptoms:**

- Blurred vision
- Distorted vision
- Difficulty differentiating colours

**Types:**

- **Dry AMD:** Breakdown of light-sensitive cells in the macula. Treatment: Nasyam with Ksheerabala 101 and sirodhara.
- **Wet AMD:** Formation of new unwanted blood vessels under the macula. Treatment: Takradhara, netra dhara, anjanam with ilaneer kuzhambu.

### Glaucoma

Glaucoma is a silent disease caused by pressure rise inside the eye due to vitiated Kapha. The exit route for fluid gets blocked, and increased pressure may damage the optic nerve.

**Symptoms:**

- Blurred vision
- Nausea
- Headaches
- Falling of peripheral vision

**Treatment:** Sirodhara, sirovasti, and nasyam.

### Eye Exercises

- Look right as far as possible for two seconds.
- Look left for two seconds.
- Look front again.
- **Palming:** Rub palms together for 8-10 seconds. Close eyes and gently place palms over eyes for one minute. Repeat 2-3 times.

## Fighting Rheumatism

Many aged people suffer from rheumatic diseases that affect mobility. The most common are osteoarthritis and osteoporosis.

A freely movable joint has cartilages to prevent friction between two bones. Synovial fluid ensures nourishment and removal of wastes. Degenerative changes in old age may prevent synovial fluid from performing its duty, resulting in osteoarthritis or osteoporosis. All degenerative joint diseases result in joint deformities and abnormal body postures.

Osteoarthritis and osteoporosis, along with stroke and parkinsonism, are four main diseases triggered by aggravated Vata.

### Treatment Approach

Treatment for osteoarthritis is generally spread over three phases and may take eight months to a year.

1. **First phase:** External application of selected oils and internal administration of anti-inflammatory analgesics to bring down pain and inflammation.
2. **Second phase:** Anti-degenerative treatments including kizhi, pizhichil, and oil massage. This increases lubrication and flexibility of joints.
3. **Third phase:** Internal administration of medicines including kashayams.

For osteoporosis, treatment aims at lubricating affected parts. Maintaining Kapha-Pitha balance is important. Oil bath and strict diet help.

### Oil Bath (Abhyangam)

Oil bath helps lubricate tissues and stimulates lymph nodes in joints. Daily application of oil on head, ears, hands, etc., can keep off diseases to an extent. Commonly used oils: Asnanadi, Dhanwantharam, Balaaswangandhadi.

### Food Advice

- Vata-predominant people should take food enriched with oil and ghee.
- If diabetic or having cholesterol, follow the physician’s diet.
- Avoid junk food that increases cholesterol.
- Mutton nourishes tissues. Buttermilk, orange juice, grape juice, and watermelon juice are advised in summer.
- Avoid curd as it slows blood circulation.

A guiding principle of Ayurveda is that the mind exerts the deepest influence on the body. Disease is seen as the result of emotional imbalance and stress, as well as climatic variations, bacterial attack, and nutritional deviance.

Mental strength results from well-formed structures. The mind is present in all cells. As metabolic power and energy source decline in cells, immunity and selective absorptive and expulsive power decrease. Discriminative capacity of cells deteriorates in old age.

All sense organs gradually deteriorate. Degenerative changes in the eye produce cataract, long sight, and presbiopia. Hearing, taste, and smell decrease. Skin becomes rough and hard; touch sensation declines.

Aged people may lack receptivity. Loss of memory, orientation, and increased selfishness are prominent in individuals with a Vata constitution. They may get irritated and become emotionally fragile.

### Satwavajaya

Ayurveda addresses mental disturbances and management of emotional stress through Satwavajaya. Unvirtuous acts lead to a perturbed mind, imbalance in the system, and inability to discern good from bad.

Helpful practices:

Ayurveda is “irreligious” in the sense that one may follow one’s chosen faith and religion.

## Pranayama

**Author:** A P Jayadevan, yoga trainer and research scholar, Sree Sankaracharya Sanskrit University, Kalady, Kerala.

Without breathing, life ceases within minutes. Most people use only a fraction of their full breathing capacity. Stress, poor posture, long hours of sitting, and bad habits affect proper breathing. Improper breathing creates an imbalance in the oxygen/carbon dioxide ratio, resulting in hyperventilation and dizziness.

The brain requires more oxygen than any other organ. Lack of oxygen results in mental sluggishness, negative thoughts, and depression. Poor oxygen supply affects other parts of the body. Proper oxygen supply allows the body to metabolise food efficiently and rid itself of toxic by-products.

*Hatha Yoga Pradeepika* says: “As long as there is breath in the body there is life. When breath departs, life also departs. Therefore regulate the breath.”

Pranayama is controlled intake and outflow of breath. Prana is the subtle life-giving element extracted from air. The more life-force in the body, the more “alive” one is. Prana is present in all forms of nourishment but is most accessible and constant in air.

The ancient sages knew that all bodily functions are performed by five types of vital energy. Prana, mind, and breath are closely connected. Pranayama regulates the flow of Prana and bestows a calm mind.

An average person takes around 500 cubic centimetres of air during normal inhalation. In deep breathing, intake increases up to 3,000 cubic centimetres—about six times greater. Pranayama helps the respiratory and circulatory systems function effectively.

### Abdominal Breathing

- Sit cross-legged or lie flat on the back in corpse pose.
- Place one hand on the abdomen.
- Inhale slowly and deeply through the nose, feeling the abdomen expand and rise while keeping the chest still.
- Exhale, feeling the abdomen sink.
- Practise ten cycles.

**Benefits:** Brings air to the lowest part of the lungs, exercises the diaphragm, relaxes mind and body, massages internal organs, calms emotions, induces good sleep.

### Rib Cage Breathing

- Sit cross-legged or lie in Savasana.
- Place hands on the sides of the ribs.
- Gently contract the abdomen.
- Inhale slowly through the nose into the rib cage.
- Feel ribs expand outward and chest open.
- Exhale, feel ribs contract inward.
- Repeat five times.

**Benefits:** Relaxes mind and body, strengthens lungs.

### Complete Breathing

- Lie flat on the back in corpse pose.
- Place one hand on the abdomen and the other on the rib cage.
- Inhale slowly through the nose: abdomen expands first, then rib cage, then upper chest.
- Exhale slowly, emptying lungs from top to bottom.
- Keep body without jerks.
- Make inhalation and exhalation uniform.
- Do not hold breath.
- Repeat five times.

**Benefits:** Can be used anywhere to calm mind and body. Use before meditation and asana practice.

**Caution:** Learn Pranayama from an authentic teacher. Improper practice can lead to long-lasting adverse effects.

## Neurological Disorders in Geriatrics

**Author:** Dr G S Lavekar, Director-General, Central Council for Research in Ayurveda and Siddha, New Delhi.

The 21st century is witnessing a decline in fertility and an increase in life expectancy. Morbidity and mortality are shifting from communicable to non-communicable diseases. Neurological disorders are significant causes of morbidity among the aged.

### Insomnia

Half of all people over 65 suffer from chronic sleep disturbance. Melatonin, produced by the pineal gland at night, regulates the sleep-wake cycle. A study found that melatonin replacement therapy improved sleep quality in elderly insomniacs.

**Ayurvedic treatment:**

- Abhyanga (body massage with medicated oils)
- Padabhyanga (foot massage)
- Shiro abhyanga (head massage)
- Shirodhara (pouring medicated oil/liquid on head)

### Alzheimer’s Disease

Alzheimer’s is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common cause of dementia in the elderly. Risk factors include age, genetics, head trauma, and elevated plasma homocysteine. Free radicals are linked to age-related disorders.

**Research findings:**

- *Withania somnifera* active principles showed antioxidant activity comparable to deprenyl.
- *Bacopa monniera* and *Valeriana wallichii* extracts attenuated ischaemia-reperfusion induced cerebral injury in animal studies.
- These findings support therapeutic use of *Withania somnifera* as an Ayurvedic Rasayana and Medhyarasayana.

## Classic Formulations: Ghritham

**Author:** Dr M S Noushad, Deputy Manager, R&D Wing, Nagarjuna Ayurvedic Group, Thodupuzha.

Ayurveda says Ghritham is better than three types of fat/oil derivatives:

- Vasa (fat from animal flesh)

### Mode of Preparation

- Dosage varies according to body constitution and disease severity.
- According to Sharngadhara Samhitha, dosage is up to 48 grams. Generally 10-15 grams is administered.
- In Snehapana, a single dose may be 25 ml, 50 ml, 100 ml, etc.
- Used in peptic ulcer, chronic wounds, joint pains, skin diseases, insanity, and epilepsy.

## Medicinal Plants: Herbs to the Rescue of Farmers

**Author:** Dhanya M

Ayurveda employs thousands of herbs and plants in medicines and cosmetics. India has over 17,000 species of flowering plants; over 6,000 are estimated to be of medicinal value.

The National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB), under the Department of AYUSH, coordinates matters related to medicinal plants. Its functions include:

- Guidance in formulation of proposals and schemes
- Identification, inventorisation, and quantification of medicinal plants
- Promotion of cooperative efforts among collectors and growers
- Matters relating to import/export of raw material and value-added products

### The Andaman Project: Noni

*Morinda citrifolia*, commonly known as noni, is a tree in the coffee family, Rubiaceae. It grows extensively in India. It favours shady forests and open rocky or sandy shores. It produces fruits all year round. Noni is rich in macro and micro nutrients and vitamins. It is used for cold, chronic pains, asthma, high blood pressure, hypertension, and arthritis.

After the 2004 tsunami, almost 16 per cent of agricultural land in Andaman and Nicobar was destroyed. Dr D R Singh’s team at Central Agricultural Research Institute (CARI), Port Blair, identified noni growing abundantly. They collected and conserved germplasm and selected genotypes suitable for saline-affected land.

## Beauty Care: Alopecia Areata

**Author:** Dr Devidasan Vellodi, Senior Consultant Physician, AVN Arogya Ayurvedic Clinic, Kochi.

Alopecia areata is a non-scarring, inflammatory hair loss seen in men, women, and children. It manifests as patches of hair loss on the scalp and other parts of the body. In severe cases, it can progress to complete loss of all body hair. It is not life-threatening but can have psychological and sociological effects.

### Hair Growth Phases

- **Anagen:** Growing phase, lasts 2-6 years, average 3 years.
- **Catagen:** Transitional phase, lasts 1-2 weeks.
- **Telogen:** Resting phase, lasts 3-4 months.

In a normal scalp, 90 per cent of hair is in anagen phase; 10 per cent in catagen or telogen.

Alopecia areata affects the hair follicle as it enters anagen. Inflammatory cells of the immune system infiltrate around anagen hair follicles and stop hair fibre production. It is thought to be an autoimmune disorder.

### Symptoms

- Small, soft, bald patches, usually round but can take any shape
- Mostly affects scalp and beard, but can occur on any hair-bearing part
- Different skin areas with hair loss and re-growth at the same time
- May go into remission or be permanent
- Area may tingle or be mildly painful
- Hair falls out over a short period
- Loss commonly occurs more on one side of the scalp

### Causes

Alopecia areata is not communicable. It occurs more frequently in people with affected family members, suggesting heredity. It is slightly more likely in people with relatives with autoimmune diseases. An unknown environmental trigger such as emotional stress combined with hereditary factors is believed to be the cause.

### Ayurveda View

Hair follicle contains Bhrajak Pitha. When Pitha at the root of hair increases with Vata, it results in hair fall. Kapha and Rakta block hair follicles, preventing fresh growth.

Terms used:

- **Indralupta:** Hair falls suddenly in a ring shape; affects beard.
- **Khalitya:** Long continuous process of hair fall; affects scalp.
- **Ruhya:** Affects entire body.

### Types of Alopecia

- **Alopecia areata:** One or more round spots on scalp.
- **Diffuse alopecia areata:** Hair lost diffusely over whole scalp.
- **Alopecia areata monolocularis:** Baldness in only one spot.
- **Alopecia areata multilocularis:** Multiple areas of hair loss.
- **Alopecia areata barbae:** Limited to beard.
- **Alopecia areata totalis:** Loss of all scalp hair.
- **Alopecia areata universalis:** Loss of all body hair.

### Treatment

Ayurveda recommends special oils made from specific natural ingredients.

**Malathyadi thailam:** Apply on scalp, massage gently, keep 20 minutes, wash. Main ingredient: *Jasminum grandiflorum*.

**Hibiscus shampoo:** Boil 3-4 flowers or 5-6 leaves in water. Cool, squeeze juice, use as shampoo.

**Hasti dantha mashi:** Burned fine powder of elephant teeth. Apply over affected area with juice of *Eclipta alba*.

**Internal medicines:** Mandura bhasma, Loha bhasma, Navayasa choorna, Amalaki rasayana. Combination of *Eclipta alba* choornam and Amalaki churnam is effective.

**Panchakarma therapy:** Nasyam with anu thailam; blood letting; Ksheera vasti (medicated enema using herbal decoction, milk, and ghee).
