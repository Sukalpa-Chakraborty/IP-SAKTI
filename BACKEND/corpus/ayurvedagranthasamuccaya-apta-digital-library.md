# Ayurvedagranthasamuccaya (APTA Digital Library): A Digital Library of Classical Ayurveda Texts

## 1. Document Overview

This paper describes the **Ayurvedagranthasamuccaya (APTA Digital Library)**, a searchable digital bouquet of classical Ayurveda books developed by the **Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS)**. The portal is designed for Ayurveda students, teachers, practitioners, and researchers. It enables reading, searching, and learning classical Ayurveda texts in a digital environment.

The portal was developed as a web-based application module. It includes 35 books with 68,415 slokas, including major texts like Carakasamhita, Sushrutasamhita, Ashtangahridaya, and various Nighantus.

## 2. Structure of Classical Ayurveda Texts

- Ayurveda Samhita is well-structured into "Shana/Adhyaya/Adhikarana" pattern, resembling a relational database.
- Language is Sanskrit; converted using **ITRANS** (Indian languages TRANSLiteration) for English characters.
- Digital versions created by typing and rigorous proofreading.

## 3. Benefits of APTA Digital Library

- Simultaneous search in all texts with a single search.
- Results displayed in **Key Word in Context (KWIC)** format.
- Search for up to five keywords at once.
- **Salaka pariksha** (ancient method of examination) employed as an edutainment tool: quiz-like method with random software-generated questions.
  - Reference identification of a random verse.
  - Quiz on definitions of predefined terms.
  - Identification of hidden part of a verse.
  - Identification of previous or next verse.

## 4. Status of the Portal

- Available for public use since **2021**.
- Access at: **http://ccras.res.in/ccras_ebooks/**
- Search and Salaka quiz modules accessible without login.
- Login required for 'Read Samhita' module.
- Includes 35 books with 68,415 slokas.
- Major texts: Carakasamhita, Sushrutasamhita, Ashtangahridaya, Ashtangasangraha, Madhavanidana, Bhavaprakasha, Sarangadharasamhita, and major Nighantus (Abhidhanamanjari, Amarakosha, etc.).

## 5. Timelines of Development

- Initiated by CCRAS in **2018** through project "Preparation of Ayurveda Grantha Samuccaya (Ayurveda E-book Portal)."
- Completed in **2020**.
- Released in **2021** by Shri Shripad Naik, then Minister of State (Independent Charge), Ministry of Ayush.

## 6. Key Features

- All major Ayurveda Samhita on a single web-based platform.
- Search results in **KWIC format**.
- View texts in different scripts: Devanagari, Phonetic English, IAST, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Tamil, Gurmukhi, Bengali, Gujarati.
- View with or without commentaries/variant readings.
- Navigation: thumbnail or tabular lists, dropdowns for section (sthana), chapter (adhyaya), or context (adhikarana).
- Read in classic, light, or dark themes.
- Search: simple phonetic search or multi-word search up to five terms.
- Search results from verses and commentaries in separate tabs.
- Random Sutra link displays a random verse.
- Four edutainment features: reference identification, quiz on definitions, hidden part identification, previous/next verse identification.
- Header buttons: Read Samhita, Phonetic Search, Salaka Quiz, Login.
- Footer links: Random Sutra, Search, Help, Foreword, Copyright Policy, Development Team.

## 7. Use-Case Scenarios

- **Education:** Use in digital classrooms for reading (patha) and discussions (avabodha). Ideal for seminars, workshops, and online/offline training programs.
- **Research:** Literature search for postgraduate and Ph.D. theses. Collect classical references on a topic by searching with different keywords and synonyms.
- **Clinical Practice:** Identify possible formulations for patients. Search for symptom combinations to shortlist possible diagnoses. Find formulations for multiple comorbidities.
- **Decision Support:** Use as a decision support system by searching for symptom combinations.
- **Study of Terminologies:** Study usage of terminologies in classical texts, frequency, and contextual meanings.
- **Classroom Assessment:** Use Salaka pariksha for quizzes.

**Examples:**
- Search for "vAr" (vari), "jal" (jala), "amb" (ambu), "pay" (paya) gives comprehensive references on "water."
- Search for "ras" gives contexts like "juice," "mercury," etc.
- Search for "jvar" (Jvara) and "PAND" (Pandu) gives formulations with both indications.
- Search for "meh" (prameha) and "jvar" (jvara) gives Abhayadimodaka.
- Search for "PAND" (pandu) and "kusht" (kushtha) gives Narayanacurna, Hapushadicurna, etc.
- Search for "haridr" (haridrA) and "citrak" (citraka) gives Candraprabhaguggulu, Dhatturataila, etc.
- Search for "guru" (Aguru) and "tail" (taila) gives Aguruvadi taila.
- Search for "laghu" gives >1900 results; "balya" gives godhumavihita bhakshya, masha vataka, laksha, etc.

## 8. Limitations

- Efficient search requires correct **ITRANS spelling**.
- Search terms are identified based on exact spelling matches; synonyms are not automatically included.
- Sanskrit grammar peculiarities: initial vowels may be skipped due to sandhi; declension forms need to be considered.
- Typing/proofreading errors in the database limit search results.
- Based on published critical editions; existing mistakes may be reproduced.
- Advanced users can search more precisely than novices.

## 9. On-boarding

- Limited to pre-registered users for 'Read Samhita' module.
- Search and Salaka quiz pages are fully accessible without registration.

## 10. Development

- Web application module developed in-house by CCRAS.
- Database developed through typing, proofreading, and conversion into database form.
- Architecture diagrams in Figs. 2 and 3 of the original paper.

## 11. Achievement and Awards

- Awarded **Silver Award** of the Ministry of Ayush under "Awards for Excellence in IT in AYUSH Sector 2019."

## 12. Future Plans and Prospects

- Inclusion of more books: Rasashastra, other major books in First Schedule of Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
- Better search options: common search for all synonyms.
- Mobile application version.
- Linking with NAMAsTE Portal.
- Analytics features (common search terms).
- Training modules.
- Elective courses by NCISM on literary search of Ayurvedic Classics.
- Text-to-speech, AI/ML for automated translation.

## 13. Conclusion

The Ayurvedagranthasamuccaya (APTA Digital Library) is a noteworthy example of integrating modern technology with ancient science. It harnesses software technologies for Ayurveda education, research, and practice. Awareness and capacity building are needed for best utilization.
