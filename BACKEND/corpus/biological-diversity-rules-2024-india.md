# The Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023 and the Biological Diversity Rules, 2024

## 1. Document overview

The Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 were notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) on **22 October 2024** in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3, Sub-section (i), vide **G.S.R. 665(E)**, in supersession of the Biological Diversity Rules, 2004. They are made in exercise of the rule-making power conferred by **section 62 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002** and give effect to the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Parent statute | Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (as amended by the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023) |
| Principal rules | Biological Diversity Rules, 2024 — G.S.R. 665(E), 22 October 2024 |
| Superseded | Biological Diversity Rules, 2004 |
| Commencement | On expiry of 60 days from gazette notification — reported by practitioners as **22 December 2024** (one widely circulated summary says 25 December 2024; check the notification's own commencement clause) |
| Amendment since | Biological Diversity (Amendment) Rules, 2025 — G.S.R. 295(E), 6 May 2025 (certificate of origin for cultivated medicinal plants: Rule 19, First Schedule entries, Forms 11, 11A and 12) |
| Administering body | National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), with State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) and Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) at local level |
| Commencement of the 2023 Amendment Act | Reported as **1 April 2024** (notification dated 18 January 2024); the Amendment Act itself was published on 3 August 2023 |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** almost every Ayurvedic product starts with a biological resource obtained from India — a plant, an extract, a microbial strain, or associated traditional knowledge. Whether access, research, commercialisation, patenting or export triggers a prior approval, a prior intimation, a registration, a fee or a certificate of origin is decided under this framework. A 2024 rewrite moved nearly all of it online, differentiated fees by applicant type, and added defined decision timelines.

## 3. What the framework regulates (described by subject)

The Act is structured around obligations that attach to a person's *status* and *purpose*, not to the value of the resource. The provisions below are described by subject rather than by section number where the numbering has changed across the 2023 amendment; read the consolidated Act text before citing a section.

1. **Prior approval for access.** A foreign individual, foreign organisation, or an Indian organisation *controlled* by foreign interests must obtain prior approval of the NBA before obtaining biological resources or associated traditional knowledge from India for research, bio-survey or bio-utilisation.
2. **Prior approval for commercial utilisation.** The same class of persons must obtain prior approval before commercially utilising biological resources obtained from India or results of research based on them.
3. **Prior intimation to the State Biodiversity Board.** Indian persons and local bodies have an obligation to give prior intimation to the concerned SBB before commercial utilisation of biological resources obtained from India, subject to the exemptions in the Act (commonly summarised as codified traditional knowledge, local communities and cultivators of biodiversity, vaidyas/hakims and registered AYUSH practitioners practising for livelihood, and — since the 2023 amendment — **cultivated medicinal plants and their products**, the last only on production of a **certificate of origin** from the BMC).
4. **Approval before obtaining intellectual property rights.** Prior NBA approval is required before applying for a patent or other IP right in India or abroad based on biological resources or associated traditional knowledge obtained from India; Indian applicants instead follow a registration route plus an undertaking (see section 6 below).
5. **Intimation after the IP right is granted.** Applicants must inform the Authority within a prescribed period after the grant of the IPR — the 2024 Rules set this at **45 days**.
6. **Restriction or prohibition of access.** The Authority may restrict or prohibit access to biological resources that are threatened, endemic, or whose access would cause genetic erosion, adverse environmental impact, harm to local community livelihoods or socio-cultural concerns, or be contrary to national interest.
7. **Regulation of use of foreign resources in India.** Persons using in India biological resources or associated traditional knowledge obtained from a foreign country must declare that use, so it can be notified on the Access and Benefit-Sharing Clearing-House of the Convention on Biological Diversity.
8. **Benefit sharing.** Where approval is given, benefit sharing arises out of the agreement on mutually agreed terms between the Authority and the applicant. The quantum and modality are agreement-specific; the Act does not prescribe a single percentage.
9. **Penalties.** The Act penalises contraventions of its core obligations. The 2024 Rules list the factors to be considered in determining the quantum of penalty for the contraventions referred to in section 55 of the Act (including the amount of disproportionate gain or unfair advantage, where quantifiable); the penalty for those contraventions is reported as **not less than ₹1 lakh and extendable to ₹50 lakh** — verify against the penalty clause of the consolidated Act before quoting a figure.

## 4. What changed in the 2024 Rules

| Theme | Rules 2004 (as generally understood) | Rules 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Filing | Largely physical; cheques/demand drafts in favour of NBA | **All applications online on the NBA web portal** unless physical filing is expressly allowed; all payments electronic to the **National Biodiversity Fund** |
| Forms | Older form set | Simplified forms with drop-downs, differentiated **by purpose of access** |
| Fees | Flat fee (e.g. access permission reported at ₹10,000 for all applicants) | **Differential fees** — separate fee for individuals and for entities, and separate fee per purpose |
| Decision time | Access: "as far as possible" within 180 days; no clear timelines downstream | **90 days** for access decisions; defined timelines for every subsequent step; **180 days** for IPR-related approvals |
| Transparency | Granted approvals published | **Both granted and rejected** approvals published on the NBA website |
| Emergencies | No specific expedite power | **Chairperson may expedite** approval in emergencies such as epidemics and pandemics |
| Extensions/revival | Not specified | **No general extension provision**; a closed application can be **revived on payment of half the fresh-application fee** within the prescribed period |
| Cultivated medicinal plants | Exemption route existed but procedure was thin | **Certificate of origin** procedure via the BMC (Rule 19; procedure reworked in 2025) |

## 5. Application routes and forms

The forms below are as described in practitioner summaries of the 2024 Rules. Form numbers and purposes should be confirmed against the Rules and their Schedules in the gazette text.

| Form | Purpose | Reported fee (individual / entity) |
|---|---|---|
| Form 1 | Prior permission to **access** biological resources or associated knowledge for research, bio-survey or bio-utilisation | ₹10,000 / ₹20,000 |
| Form 2 | Prior permission to access for **commercial utilisation** | Prescribed in the First Schedule (higher than Form 1) |
| Form 3 | Prior approval to **share or transfer results of research** (commercial or otherwise) | ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 |
| Form 4 | **Transferee registration** to use results of research for further research | ₹2,000 / ₹5,000 |
| Form 5 | Prior approval for the **transferee** to use results of research for commercial utilisation | ₹10,000 / ₹20,000 |
| Form 6 | Prior approval for the **transferee** to use results of research to obtain IPR | ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 |
| Form 7 | **Foreign applicant**: prior approval before grant of IPR based on Indian biological resources / traditional knowledge / digital sequence information | ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 |
| Form 8 | **Indian applicant**: registration before grant of IPR (with undertaking to seek prior approval before commercialisation) | ₹2,000 / ₹5,000 |
| Form 9 | Prior approval for an **Indian applicant to commercialise the IPR** | ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 |
| Form 10 | Declaration for **use in India of a biological resource or associated TK obtained from a foreign country** (section 36A route) | Nil |
| Form 11 | **Book of cultivated medicinal plants** maintained by the BMC (amended 2025) | Nil |
| Form 11A | **Application for certificate of origin** for cultivated medicinal plants, to the BMC (added 2025) | **₹200** |
| Form 12 | **Certificate of origin** issued to the applicant (added 2025) | Nil |
| Form 13 | Permission to **carry or send a biological resource outside India** for non-commercial research or emergency research | ₹1,000 |

Notes on the routes most relevant to an Ayurvedic product developer:

- **Research access vs commercial access are different forms.** A licence to collect samples for a study (Form 1) does not cover selling a finished product made from them (Form 2, and later Form 9 where the IPR route applies).
- **Results of research travel with obligations.** The 2024 Rules put a duty on the *transferee* as well as the transferor: a foreign collaborator who receives research results must register and seek approval before further research, commercial utilisation or IP protection.
- **Digital sequence information is in scope.** The 2024 Rules expressly cover digital sequence information on biological resources accessed from India, including material deposited in repositories outside India.
- **Section 3(2) possession carried over.** Persons (including Indian entities with foreign shareholding) who were already in possession of biological resources obtained from India before 1 April 2024 are reported to still need approval for research, commercial utilisation, bio-survey and bio-utilisation — the Rules provide a no-penalty route to apply.

## 6. IPR: registration, approval, commercialisation and post-grant intimation

The single most misunderstood part of the framework for an Indian Ayurvedic company is that **being Indian does not remove the obligation** — it changes the *form* of it.

| Step | Foreign individual / entity (or foreign-controlled Indian entity) | Indian applicant |
|---|---|---|
| Before filing the IP application | **Prior approval** in Form 7 | **Registration** in Form 8, plus an undertaking that prior approval will be obtained before commercialisation |
| Decision period | Reported as **180 days** from receipt of a complete application | Same route |
| After the IP right is granted | Inform the Authority within **45 days** | Inform the Authority within **45 days** |
| Before commercialising the IP | Covered by the approval | **Separate prior approval** in Form 9 |
| Scope | Patents and other IP based on biological resources, associated TK and digital sequence information obtained from India | Same |

Practical consequences:

1. **A patent application filed without the required approval/registration is a compliance defect independent of the patent's merits.** Record the NBA reference number with the patent docket.
2. **The undertaking in Form 8 becomes live at commercialisation.** A product launch, a licence, a royalty deal or a sale of the patent can each be a commercialisation event.
3. **The 45-day post-grant intimation runs from grant, not from issue of the certificate.** Diary it at grant, in India and abroad.
4. **Licence/assignment to a foreign party can itself be a transfer of results of research**, requiring Form 3 and possibly Form 5/Form 6 on the transferee's side.

## 7. Certificate of origin for cultivated medicinal plants

This is the provision that most directly concerns a manufacturer buying cultivated ashwagandha, sarpagandha, or any other notified/cultivated medicinal plant.

- **Statutory basis:** the exemption for prior intimation to the SBB was widened by the 2023 Amendment Act to include *cultivated medicinal plants and their products*, conditional on a **certificate of origin issued by the Biodiversity Management Committee (BMC)**.
- **Procedure (Rule 19, as substituted in 2025):**
  1. The BMC maintains a **book containing details of cultivated medicinal plants in Form 11** on the web portal (fields: common and botanical names, period of cultivation, approximate quantity produced annually in a financial year).
  2. An application for the certificate is made to the BMC in **Form 11A** on the web portal along with a fee of **₹200** (First Schedule entry 11A). Fields include applicant name, contact details, address, State of the SBB, District and name of the BMC, and the request for access to cultivated medicinal plants with quantity and species.
  3. On the basis of the entries in the Form 11 book, the **certificate of origin is generated in Form 12** on the portal (unique ID / QR code digitally assigned; issued for a stated quantity; signed by the authorised person of the BMC). Fee for the certificate itself: **Nil**.
- **What the earlier (2024) version required:** a self-declaration in physical or digital form containing the applicant's and cultivator's name and address, geographical location including survey number, extent of area under cultivation, species and parts cultivated, and approximate quantity to be accessed. The 2025 substitution moved this to the online Form 11A/11/12 workflow.
- **Cultivated is not the same as exempt.** The certificate evidences *cultivation under the BMC's jurisdiction*; it does not by itself waive other obligations (labelling, licensing, SBB local-area conditions, or approval obligations triggered by foreign-controlled ownership).

## 8. Restrictions, prohibitions and expedited approvals

The Authority may restrict or prohibit access where, broadly: the species is threatened or endemic; access would adversely affect the livelihood or socio-cultural aspects of local communities; access would cause adverse environmental impact that is difficult to control or mitigate; access would cause genetic erosion or affect ecosystem function; or the purpose is contrary to national interest or to international agreements to which India is a party.

The 2024 Rules add a clarification that where a threatened or endemic species is **cultivated**, access to the cultivated resource may be permitted on the merits of each case for a specific period — an exception that matters for cultivated-stock supply chains.

Emergency handling: the Chairperson of the NBA may expedite approvals in situations such as epidemics and pandemics. Separately, Form 13 covers carrying or sending biological resources outside India for non-commercial research or urgent emergency studies, with approval reported within **45 days** and a requirement to deposit voucher specimens in designated national repositories where applicable, with proof endorsed to the Authority.

## 9. Timelines and closure mechanics

| Stage | Reported timeline |
|---|---|
| Decision on access application (Forms 1 and 2) | **90 days** from receipt of the application in complete form |
| Decision on IPR-related applications (Forms 7, 8) | **180 days** |
| Decision on commercialisation of IPR by an Indian applicant (Form 9) | **180 days** |
| Emergency/non-commercial export approval (Form 13) | **45 days** |
| Applicant to answer a query from the Authority before the application is closed | **30 days** (IPR applications: 90 days) |
| Applicant to sign the draft agreement after communication | **60 days** (IPR applications: 90 days) |
| Revival of a closed application | **Half the fresh-application fee**, within **90 days** of closure |
| Inform the Authority after grant of the IPR | **45 days** |
| Extension of timelines | **No specific provision** for extension in the 2024 Rules |

An approval, once granted, takes the form of an **agreement on mutually agreed terms** signed by an authorised officer of the Authority and the applicant. Benefit sharing is negotiated in that agreement.

## 10. Compliance failure modes seen in practice

1. **Treating "Made in India" as a full exemption.** Indian applicants must still register before filing (Form 8) and obtain approval before commercialising the IPR (Form 9).
2. **Filing a patent first, NBA paperwork later.** The approval/registration obligation attaches *before* the application; the 45-day intimation attaches *after* grant. They are different duties.
3. **Missing the certificate of origin for cultivated stock** and then claiming the cultivated-medical-plants exemption without BMC evidence.
4. **Sharing research results with a foreign collaborator** under an MTA or research agreement without Form 3, and without checking whether the transferee needs Forms 4-6.
5. **Assuming a licence covers commercialisation.** Research access approval does not authorise sale of a product.
6. **Exporting a sample for testing or a collaborative study** without checking the Form 13 / voucher-deposit requirements.
7. **Ignoring digital sequence information** — deposits of sequence data in foreign repositories are within scope.
8. **Letting a closed application lapse.** Closure for non-response is common; revival is available but time-limited and fee-based.
9. **No owner for the NBA docket.** Approvals, undertakings, agreements and intimations are spread across a product's life; assign them to a named compliance owner alongside the patent and FSSAI/AYUSH files.

## 11. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is my entity an "Indian applicant" or a foreign/foreign-controlled entity for this resource? (Shareholding and control, not just incorporation, matter.)
- [ ] Which **purpose** am I doing — research access, bio-survey, commercial utilisation, transfer of results, IPR, commercialisation of IPR? Each has its own form and fee.
- [ ] Do I need **prior approval before filing** the patent/design/plant-variety application, or registration plus undertaking? (Forms 7 or 8.)
- [ ] Have I diarised the **45-day post-grant intimation** for every jurisdiction in which the right grants?
- [ ] Has the **Form 9 commercialisation approval** been obtained before launch, licensing or assignment?
- [ ] For cultivated medicinal plants: does the supplier have a **BMC certificate of origin (Form 12)** generated against the Form 11 book, and does the quantity match my order?
- [ ] If I share results of research with a collaborator, have **transferor and transferee** obligations (Forms 3-6) been cleared?
- [ ] Is any **digital sequence information** or foreign repository deposit involved?
- [ ] Is any part of the resource or knowledge sourced **from outside India** and used here (Form 10 declaration)?
- [ ] Is the species subject to a **restriction or prohibition**, or is it a cultivated stock that may qualify for the cultivated-species exception?
- [ ] Are the approval, agreement, undertaking and any **benefit-sharing commitment** recorded in the product passport and the IP docket?
- [ ] Has anyone checked the current **First Schedule fees** — fees have been revised more than once since 2024?

## 12. Interfaces with other regimes

| Regime | Interface |
|---|---|
| Nagoya Protocol / Convention on Biological Diversity | The Act implements India's access-and-benefit-sharing commitments; Form 10 declarations feed the CBD ABS Clearing-House. India's obligations also continue under the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. |
| Patents Act, 1970 | The patent applicant's NBA approval/registration and the post-grant intimation are separate from the patent office's own disclosure practice. WIPO's 2024 Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge adds an international disclosure-of-origin obligation that is separate again. |
| Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 | Registration of a variety and access to a biological resource are distinct; farmers' and breeder's rights do not waive biodiversity approvals. |
| Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 / AYUSH licensing | Manufacturing and licensing obligations under the Schedule T / Form 24-D route are independent of biodiversity approval. |
| FSS (Ayurveda Aahar) Regulations, 2022 | Food products made from Ayurvedic recipes still need the biodiversity clearances above where the resource route is triggered. |
| State-level rules | Some States have their own biodiversity rules (e.g. West Bengal, 2005) and local-area conditions imposed by SBBs. |
