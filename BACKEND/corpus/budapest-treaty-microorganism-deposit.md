# Budapest Treaty (1977) — International Recognition of the Deposit of Microorganisms for Patent Procedure

## 1. What this instrument is

The **Budapest Treaty on the International Recognition of the Deposit of
Microorganisms for the Purposes of Patent Procedure** was done at **Budapest on
28 April 1977** and **amended on 26 September 1980**; it entered into force on
**8 May 1980**. It is administered by the **International Bureau of WIPO**.

**The problem it solves:** where an invention cannot be described in a patent
application in such a way that a person skilled in the art can carry it out
(Chapter II biological inventions — pure cultures, cell lines, plasmids,
hybridomas, fungal/bacterial strains used in Ayurvedic fermentation or
bioconversion), the applicant must **deposit** the biological material with a
recognized culture collection. Before the Treaty, each country demanded its own
deposit. Under the Treaty, **one deposit with any International Depositary
Authority (IDA) is recognized by every Contracting State for patent-procedure
purposes**.

**India** became a Contracting State in **2001** (verify the exact date of
accession/entry into force in WIPO's list of Contracting States at the
source_url) and hosts **three IDAs** (Section 8).

## 3. Article 3 — Recognition and effect of the deposit

**Article 3(1)(a):** Contracting States which allow or require the deposit of
microorganisms for the purposes of patent procedure **shall recognize** such a
deposit made with **any** international depositary authority, **irrespective of
whether that authority is in or outside their territory**. Recognition
includes:

- recognition of the **fact and date of the deposit** as indicated by the IDA;
- recognition that **what is furnished as a sample is a sample of the deposited
  microorganism**.

**Article 3(1)(b):** a Contracting State may require a **copy of the receipt**
of the deposit issued by the IDA (the "international depositary receipt" — see
Rule 7).

**Article 3(2)** addresses the effect of a **new deposit** under Article 4.

**Practical meaning for an Indian applicant:** deposit once with an IDA (for
example MTCC, Chandigarh), file the **receipt** with the complete specification
(the Patents Act, 1970, Section 10 requires the depositor's name, address and
the accession number of the deposit), and the same deposit is effective in the
US, EPO, Japan, Korea, China and every other Contracting State — provided the
national office accepts Budapest deposits (most do; check the office's
particular requirements, e.g. whether a *pro forma* deposit is accepted on
filing).

## 4. Article 4 — New deposit

Article 4 permits a **new deposit** where the original IDA **can no longer
furnish samples** of the deposited microorganism — because it has ceased to
have IDA status (Article 8), or because the microorganism is no longer viable
despite the required care, or where the IDA refuses/ceases to operate. The new
deposit:

- must be made **promptly** with **another** IDA;
- must reproduce **without modification** the description/contents of the
  original deposit (or the last amended description);
- the receipt for the new deposit must be accompanied by a copy of the receipt
  of the previous deposit and the most recent viability statement (Rules 6 and
  7);
- the **date of the original deposit** is preserved (so disclosure/priority is
  not lost).

## 5. Article 5 — Export and import restrictions

Contracting States undertake to **permit and not to impede the import and
export** of microorganisms deposited (or to be deposited) under the Treaty, and
of their progeny, where needed to comply with the Treaty — subject to
reasonable safeguards. This matters for Ayurvedic/biological inventions where
a strain must be shipped to a foreign IDA or to a national office.

## 6. Article 6 and Article 7 — IDA status: requirements and acquisition

- **Article 6(1)** — the institution must be equipped and staffed to safely
  store microorganisms, and must have the capacity to make deposits for
  industrial property purposes.
- **Article 6(2)** — the **declaration of assurances** must state that the
  institution complies and will continue to comply with requirements including:
  it is **available, for deposit purposes, to any depositor** (no
  nationality/residence discrimination); it **accepts and stores** deposited
  microorganisms and issues a **receipt**; it **tests viability** and issues a
  **viability statement**; it **furnishes samples only to those entitled** to
  receive them under the Regulations; it **keeps the contents confidential**
  until the relevant application is published; it has **continued existence**
  as far as the Contracting State can assure; it **surrenders IDA status** if
  it ceases to comply.
- **Article 7** — the Contracting State in whose territory the institution is
  located transmits a **written communication** to the Director General of WIPO
  with the declaration of assurances; on finding compliance, the International
  Bureau **publishes** the communication, and status is acquired **on the date
  of publication** (or the later date indicated). **Article 9** allows
  intergovernmental industrial property organisations (EPO, ARIPO, EAPO, OAPI)
  to furnish assurances for institutions in their territories.
- **Article 8** — the Assembly may **terminate or limit** an authority's status
  where the Article 6 requirements are no longer complied with.

**Scale:** around **50 IDAs** in some **20+ countries** hold status (WIPO's
IDA list is updated periodically — check the current list at the source_url).

## 7. Regulations — Rules 6 to 12 (the operational rules)

| Rule | Subject | Key content |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule 6** | Requirements or Formalities upon Deposit | The IDA examines the deposit for compliance (form, viability, description), accepts or refuses it, and communicates any **scientific description / proposed taxonomic designation** (Rule 6 communication); a *pro forma*/filling-up of missing data procedure exists. |
| **Rule 7** | Receipt of Deposit | The IDA issues an **international depositary receipt** (standard form) stating the date of receipt, accession number, depositor, and the deposit's status; a receipt for a **new deposit** (Article 4) must be accompanied by a copy of the previous receipt and the latest viability statement. |
| **Rule 8** | Later indication or amendment of the scientific description / taxonomic designation | The depositor may communicate or amend the description later; the IDA issues an **attestation** of the data and date. |
| **Rule 9** | **Storage of microorganisms** | **9.1 Duration:** the microorganism shall be stored with all care necessary to keep it viable and uncontaminated for a period of **at least 30 years after the date of the deposit**, or **at least five years after the most recent request for the furnishing of a sample**, whichever is **later**. **9.2 Secrecy:** the IDA keeps the deposit confidential and gives no information except as permitted by Rule 11. |
| **Rule 10** | **Viability test and statement** | **10.1** the IDA must test viability promptly after deposit (and after any transfer), at reasonable intervals, at any time for technical reasons, and on the depositor's request. **10.2** the IDA issues a **viability statement** to the depositor promptly after deposit, on request, and to any party that received a sample under Rule 11 (a statement of whether the microorganism is or is no longer viable, with IDA details and test date). |
| **Rule 11** | **Furnishing of samples** | **11.1** samples to **interested industrial property offices** (offices to which the Treaty applies, where the sample is needed for patent procedure — furnishing to such offices is **free of charge**). **11.2** samples to the **depositor** (on request) or to a third party **with the depositor's written authorization**. **11.3** samples to **parties legally entitled** (e.g. persons legally entitled in the relevant Contracting State, on certification by the requesting office as to their entitlement). **11.4** common form/certification rules. |
| **Rule 12** | **Fees** | An IDA may charge fees for (i) **storage**, (ii) the Rule 8 attestation, (iii) issuance of viability statements (in certain cases), (iv) furnishing of samples in certain cases, and (v) communication of information under Rule 7.6. **The storage fee must cover the whole duration of storage** (a once-and-for-all payment) and **may not vary** by the depositor's nationality or residence. |

**Related rule:** **Rule 5.1** requires that where an IDA is about to cease
functioning, **all deposited microorganisms be transferred** to another IDA.

## 8. India's three International Depositary Authorities

| IDA | Host institution / location | IDA status acquired | WIPO notification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MTCC — Microbial Type Culture Collection and Gene Bank** | CSIR-Institute of Microbial Technology (IMTECH), Sector 39-A, Chandigarh | **4 October 2002** (first IDA in India; notified as being the 7th in Asia and 34th in the world) | Budapest Notification No. 204 |
| **MCC — Microbial Culture Collection** (now hosted as the National Centre for Microbial Resource, NCMR) | National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS), Savitribai Phule Pune University campus, Ganeshkhind, Pune | **9 April 2011** | Budapest Notification No. 272 |
| **NAIMCC — National Agriculturally Important Microbial Culture Collection** | ICAR-National Bureau of Agriculturally Important Microorganisms (ICAR-NBAIM), Maunath Bhanjan, Uttar Pradesh | **28 July 2020** (third IDA of India) | Budapest Notification No. 338 |

**Also relevant to Indian applicants:**

- **MTCC is a designated national repository** of the National Biodiversity
  Authority for microbial cultures of Indian origin under the Biological
  Diversity Act, 2002 (and its own guidance states that microbial cultures of
  Indian origin should be deposited there before filing a patent involving them
  — check the current NBA/MTCC guidance and the Biological Diversity Rules).
- **MCC/NCMR (Pune)** and **NAIMCC** were also designated/recognised as
  national repositories under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (verify
  individual orders and current status).
- **Foreign IDAs remain valid for Indian patents** — an Indian applicant may
  deposit with any IDA anywhere (for example ATCC in the USA or DSMZ in
  Germany), subject to Indian requirements for furnishing samples to the Indian
  Patent Office and to any Indian biosecurity/biodiversity approval for
  exporting the material.

## 9. Interaction with Indian patent law and practice

| Requirement | Source |
|---|---|
| Duty to disclose the deposit — name and address of the depository institution and **accession number** in the complete specification | Patents Act, 1970, **Section 10** (statement of deposit); Patents Rules, 2003 (Form and timing; verify current rule numbers) |
| Amendment of the complete specification after a new deposit (Article 4) | Patents Act, 1970, **Section 59** (amendment restrictions) with the Controller's permission |
| Sufficiency of disclosure standard | Patents Act, 1970, **Section 10(4)** (enablement) |
| Time to furnish the receipt | Rules prescribe filing of the **receipt within a period after the date of filing** of the complete specification (verify the current deadline in the Patents Rules, 2003 as amended) |
| Procedural substitutes where national law does not require deposit | Some offices accept *pro forma* declarations on filing with an undertaking to deposit later — check each target office |
| Biological material of Indian origin, benefit-sharing | Biological Diversity Act, 2002 — NBA approval for obtaining/using/transferring such material (Section 3, Section 4 read with the Rules); see the CBD/Nagoya files in this corpus |

## 10. Deposit checklist (typical sequence)

1. Confirm the invention is **not** enabled by words alone (a written
   description of a known, publicly available strain is not enough — but a
   strain *deposited and made available* to the public under Rule 11.3 may be).
2. Choose an **IDA**: an Indian IDA (MTCC/MCC/NAIMCC) for Indian-origin
   material (avoids biodiversity/export approvals), or a foreign IDA where the
   target office's practice or collaboration requires it.
3. Complete the deposit form with the **scientific description / proposed
   taxonomic designation** (Rule 6 communication).
4. Receive the **international depositary receipt** (Rule 7) and the
   **viability statement** (Rule 10.2).
5. File the complete specification naming the IDA and giving the **accession
   number**, and lodge the receipt within the prescribed period.
6. Keep the deposit alive: storage for at least **30 years** (or 5 years after
   the last sample request, whichever is later — Rule 9.1); the storage fee is
   a single payment covering the whole period (Rule 12.1(b)).
7. If an IDA ceases or a sample can no longer be furnished, make a **new
   deposit** (Article 4) promptly with another IDA and notify the patent
   office.

## 11. What this means for an Ayurvedic biotech invention

1. **Deposit is disclosure, not protection.** It fixes sufficiency of
   disclosure; it does not create rights in the organism.
2. **Timing matters.** The deposit must be made by the applicable filing date
   (or within the office's grace period) for the deposited material to support
   novelty/priority claims.
3. **Use an Indian IDA where possible.** It reduces export approvals, keeps
   the material within the biodiversity-approval perimeter, and speeds
   correspondence — MTCC (Chandigarh), MCC/NCMR (Pune) and NAIMCC (Mau) are
   all designated.
4. **Understand sample release.** Rule 11.2 gives the depositor control until
   the application is published; thereafter Rule 11.1/11.3 release applies —
   licences and agreements should anticipate third-party access to the strain.
5. **Keep the viability statement current.** National examiners and licensees
   ask for it; the IDA must retest at intervals (Rule 10.1).
