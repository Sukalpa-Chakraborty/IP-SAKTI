# Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD, 1992) — Relevant Provisions

## 1. What this instrument is

The Convention on Biological Diversity ("CBD") is a multilateral environmental
agreement opened for signature at the **Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit on 5 June
1992** and entered into force on **29 December 1993**. It has three stated
objectives (Article 1):

1. the **conservation** of biological diversity;
2. the **sustainable use** of its components; and
3. the **fair and equitable sharing of the benefits arising out of the
   utilisation of genetic resources**, including by appropriate access to
   genetic resources and by appropriate transfer of relevant technologies,
   taking into account all rights over those resources and to technologies, and
   by appropriate funding.

**India is a Party** to the CBD (ratified; in force for India in the 1990s —
exact date of ratification/entry into force for India to be confirmed against
the CBD depositary records at the source_url). India's domestic implementing
statute is the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**, with the National
Biodiversity Authority (NBA) as the statutory authority and the Biological
Diversity Rules (as amended).

> The CBD is a *framework* convention. Most day-to-day ABS obligations for a
> company are now read through the **Nagoya Protocol (2010)** — see the
> companion file `nagoya-protocol-2010-access-benefit-sharing.md` in this
> corpus.

## 3. Core principles

### 3.1 Article 3 — Sovereign rights over natural resources

Article 3 recognises that **States have sovereign rights over their own natural
resources** and that the authority to determine access to genetic resources
rests with national governments and is subject to national legislation.

### 3.2 Article 4 — Jurisdiction over components of biological diversity

The provisions of the Convention apply to biological diversity components where
a Contracting Party has **jurisdiction or control** — i.e. it reaches resources
in areas beyond national jurisdiction only to the extent a Party controls them.

### 3.3 Article 5 — Conservation and sustainable use

Each Contracting Party shall, as far as possible and as appropriate: establish
**national strategies, plans or programmes** for the conservation and
sustainable use of biological diversity; integrate conservation and
sustainable use into relevant sectoral or cross-sectoral plans, programmes and
policies; and **co-operate** with other States on trans-boundary and
ecosystem-level conservation.

### 3.4 Article 6 and Article 7 — General measures and identification/monitoring

- **Article 6** — measures for conservation and sustainable use: in-situ
  conservation, sustainable utilisation of components, protection of
  ecosystems and natural habitats, maintenance of viable populations, and
  control of risks arising from living modified organisms.
- **Article 7** — identification and monitoring: identify components of
  biological diversity important for conservation and sustainable use,
  monitor components through sampling and other techniques, maintain and
  organise metadata (taxonomic, ecological, geographic), establish a system of
  areas for in-situ conservation, and promote **research necessary for
  conservation**.

## 4. Article 8(j) — The traditional-knowledge clause

Article 8 (In-situ Conservation) requires Contracting Parties, subject to their
national legislation, to:

> **(j)** respect, preserve and maintain **knowledge, innovations and
> practices of indigenous and local communities** embodying traditional
> lifestyles relevant for the conservation and sustainable use of biological
> diversity and promote their wider application with the approval and
> involvement of the holders of such knowledge, innovations and practices and
> encourage the **equitable sharing of the benefits** arising from the
> utilisation of such knowledge, innovations and practices.

### Why Article 8(j) matters for Ayurveda

- It is the treaty hook under which **traditional knowledge systems** —
  including Ayurveda — are recognised as relevant to conservation and
  sustainable use.
- It links TK to **equitable benefit-sharing**, which is implemented in India
  through the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 and the NBA's access-and-benefit-
  sharing conditions.
- It is a **national-legislation-qualified** obligation: the operative
  requirements are set by the domestic statute (in India: Sections 6, 7 and 21
  of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, read with the NBA's ABS conditions).
- Article 8 also contains: (a) in-situ conservation measures; (b) ecosystem
  conservation; (c) sustainable use of species; (d) incentives for conservation;
  (e) environmental-impact assessment; (f) adverse-impact prevention and
  control; (g) control of pollution; (h) control of invasive alien species;
  (i) prevention of clearance/loss of ecosystems; (k) establishment/maintain
  systems of protected areas; (l) managed-use areas; (m) protected-area
  regulation; (n) impact assessment on protected areas; (o) safeguards for
  essential natural resources.

## 5. Article 9 — Ex-situ conservation and Article 10 — Sustainable use

- **Article 9 (ex-situ conservation)** — adopt measures for ex-situ
  conservation, ideally **in the country of origin**, collect biological
  resources for conservation and sustainable use *in the country of origin or
  otherwise in accordance with that country's legislation*, maintain and
  develop ex-situ collections preferably in the country of origin, cooperate on
  scientific and technical research and training, and manage habitats and
  species outside their natural habitat **safeguarding their natural
  characteristics**.
- **Article 10 (sustainable use of components)** — integrate conservation and
  sustainable use into national decision-making; protect and encourage customary
  use consistent with conservation; maintain and encourage utilisation of
  biological resources in a sustainable manner; provide incentives for
  conservation; and promote use of **indigenous and local knowledge**.

> For an Ayurveda product, Article 9 is the reason that **depositing a
> microorganism or germplasm abroad** can carry ABS conditions attached to the
> country of origin.

## 6. Access and benefit-sharing — Articles 11–15

| Article | Subject | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | In-situ conservation — protected ecosystems | Contracting Parties shall establish systems of protected areas or areas where special measures are needed; rehabilitate degraded areas; manage inter-connected ecosystems. |
| 12 | In-situ conservation — research and training | Promote and co-operate in research consistent with conservation needs, training of personnel, and dissemination of information. |
| 13 | Public education and awareness | Promote and encourage understanding of the importance of, and the measures required for, conservation of biological diversity. |
| 14 | Environmental impact assessment and mitigation | Require appropriate environmental impact assessment for proposed projects likely to have significant adverse effects; provide for public participation; provide for mitigation measures. |
| 15.1 | **Access governed by national legislation** | Recognising the **sovereign rights of States over their natural resources**, the authority to determine access to genetic resources rests with national governments and is subject to national legislation. |
| 15.2 | **Prior informed consent (PIC)** | Each Contracting Party shall endeavour to provide for **prior informed consent** for access to genetic resources. |
| 15.3 | Access subject to mutually agreed terms (MAT) | Where access is granted, it shall be on **mutually agreed terms** (MAT). |
| 15.4 | PIC of provider country | Access, where granted, shall be subject to prior informed consent of the provider country, unless otherwise determined by that Party. |
| 15.5 | Access mechanism | Contracting Parties shall take legislative, administrative or policy measures to provide for access to genetic resources for use in research and development and in accordance with the Convention. |
| 15.6 | Benefit-sharing — MAT | Each Contracting Party shall take legislative, administrative or policy measures to provide that **benefits arising from the use of genetic resources are shared in a fair and equitable way with all Contracting Parties**, particularly developing countries, and that such sharing is on **mutually agreed terms**. |
| 15.7 | Non-monetary benefits | Benefit-sharing includes benefits arising from research and development, including results under Articles 20 and 21, based on material transferred, taking into account any restrictions on transfer. |
| 15.8 | Special case — plant genetic resources | Recognising the special role of **agricultural plant genetic resources**, access and benefit-sharing for PGRFA should be governed by an **exemption system** — which became the **International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture (ITPGRFA, 2001)**, a separate instrument. |

### Practical reading for an Ayurvedic company

- Any request for a **plant, microbial or biochemical sample from an Indian
  source** is governed by national law (in India: the Biological Diversity
  Act, 2002 — NBA prior approval for obtaining biological resources for
  research/commercial use by non-Indian entities, and for transfer of results
  of research).
- Access is on **PIC + MAT**: the provider's consent and a written benefit-
  sharing agreement must be in place **before** collection or transfer.
- **Article 15.8** is why medicinal-plant PGRFA may sit under the ITPGRFA
  Multilateral System rather than bilateral ABS, but only for PGRFA within the
  Treaty's Annex I crop list — Ayurvedic drug plants are largely outside it.

## 7. Article 16 — Technology transfer and Article 17 — Exchange of information

- **Article 16 (access to and transfer of technology)** — technology transfer
  to developing countries shall be on **fair and most favourable terms**,
  including on **concessional and preferential terms where mutually agreed**;
  where technology is **subject to patents and other intellectual property
  rights**, the know-how, genetic resources and technology shall be provided on
  **mutually agreed terms**; Contracting Parties shall co-operate with a view to
  ensuring that such IP rights are **supportive of and do not obstruct** the
  transfer of technology for conservation and sustainable use.
- **Article 17 (exchange of information)** — exchange of scientific,
  technical, socio-economic, legal and other relevant information through
  publications, libraries, data banks, institutions, and clearing-house-type
  mechanisms.

## 8. Article 18 — Co-operation and Article 19 — Biotechnology

- **Article 18 (co-operation)** — facilitation of access to scientific and
  technical co-operation, joint research, biotechnology development and access
  to and transfer of technology, human-resource development, and safety measures
  for research involving living modified organisms.
- **Article 19 (handling of biotechnology and distribution of its benefits)**
  — consideration of measures for **effective participation of Contracting
  Parties** (particularly developing countries) in biotechnological research
  activities, and for the **fair and equitable sharing of benefits** arising
  from such research, on mutually agreed terms.

> **Article 19** is the CBD's biotechnology/ABS bridge. In India, transfers of
> biological resources and of research results for commercial utilisation are
> routed through NBA approvals under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

## 9. Article 20 — Financial resources and Article 21 — Financial mechanism

- **Article 20 (financial resources)** — developed-country Contracting Parties
  shall provide **new and additional financial resources** to enable developing-
  country Parties to meet the agreed full incremental costs of measures under
  the Convention; developing-country Parties' commitment is progressive and
  subject to their ability to mobilise internal resources.
- **Article 21 (financial mechanism)** — a **Financial Mechanism** operating on
  a concessional or grant basis, under the authority and guidance of the COP,
  accountable to the COP — implemented in practice through the **Global
  Environment Facility (GEF)**.

## 10. Relationship to the Cartagena Protocol

The **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2000, in force 11 September 2003)** is a
supplementary agreement to the CBD dealing with the **transboundary movement of
living modified organisms (LMOs)** and advance informed agreement (AIA)
procedures. It does **not** govern access and benefit-sharing — that is the
**Nagoya Protocol (2010)**. The three are separate instruments with separate
obligations:

| Instrument | Subject | Status of India |
|---|---|---|
| CBD (1992) | Conservation, sustainable use, ABS framework | Party |
| Cartagena Protocol (2000) | Biosafety / LMOs | Party (verify current status in CBD depositary records) |
| Nagoya Protocol (2010) | Access to genetic resources and benefit-sharing; TK; digital sequence information (via COP-MOP decisions) | Party (verify against CBD depositary records; see companion file) |

## 11. Institutional arrangements

- **Conference of the Parties (COP)** — supreme governing body; meets
  periodically; adopts protocols, recommendations and financial arrangements.
- **Secretariat** — located in Montreal, Canada.
- **National focal points and national competent authorities** — each Party
  designates a national focal point for information and a national competent
  authority for access and benefit-sharing decisions (the latter is given
  operative effect by the Nagoya Protocol).
- **Clearing-House Mechanism (CHM)** — the Convention's information-sharing
  system.

## 13. What this means for an Ayurvedic product owner

1. **Sovereignty first.** Access to Indian biological resources is governed by
   Indian law (Article 3, Article 15.1). There is no "free" sourcing of plant
   or microbial material for commercial product development.
2. **PIC + MAT.** Consent must precede collection, and the benefit-sharing
   terms must be written and agreed (Article 15.2–15.6).
3. **Article 8(j) is live.** Using classical Ayurvedic knowledge associated
   with a biological resource triggers equitable benefit-sharing obligations
   with the knowledge holders and the community — in India, through the NBA
   and Section 7 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
4. **IP filings must be ABS-clean.** Article 16 requires IP rights not to
   obstruct technology transfer; India's Section 24A–24I regime requires
   approval/disclosure steps **before** filing patents that claim biological
   resources or knowledge associated with them.
5. **Foreign deposits carry conditions.** Depositing an Indian microorganism or
   germplasm abroad (Article 9) needs NBA approval; use an Indian designated
   repository where possible.
6. **Read CBD + Nagoya + Indian Act together.** The CBD states the principle;
   the Nagoya Protocol supplies the operational access/PIC/MAT/compliance
   architecture; the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 supplies the enforceable
   Indian rules.
