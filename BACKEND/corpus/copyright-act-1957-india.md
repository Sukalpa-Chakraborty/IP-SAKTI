# The Copyright Act, 1957 and the Copyright Rules, 2013

## 1. Document overview

The Copyright Act, 1957 (Central Act) consolidates the law of copyright in India, provides for the authors' rights, for the term of protection, for licensing and compulsory licensing, and for civil and criminal remedies. The Copyright Rules, 2013 (as amended) prescribe registration and procedural mechanics.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Enactment | The Copyright Act, 1957 (as amended, notably by the Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012) |
| Subordinate legislation | Copyright Rules, 2013 (as amended) |
| Registration | Register of Copyrights maintained by the Copyright Office (Registrar of Copyrights); registration is **voluntary** and evidentiary, not constitutive |
| Abolished tribunal | The Copyright Board was abolished by the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021 — matters now go to the Commercial Courts/High Court (verify current forum) |

**Scope note:** copyright protects *expression*, not ideas, facts or information. An Ayurvedic **formulation (the recipe, the proportions, the therapeutic concept) is not protected by copyright** — the protection routes for that are patent (if section 3(j)/3(p) is cleared), trade secret, trademark, GI or plant variety rights.

## 2. Subject matter — Section 13

**Section 13 — Works in which copyright subsists:**

1. **Original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works** — copyright subsists in original works of these kinds first published in India (or by a citizen/organisation of a Convention country), subject to the section's conditions.
2. **Cinematograph films** — copyright subsists (subject to the section).
3. **Sound recordings** — copyright subsists (subject to the section).

Plain-language coverage relevant to an Ayurveda enterprise:

| Asset | Work type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Books, papers, pharmacopoeia commentaries written by your team | Literary work | Needs originality of expression (compilation may be protected if there is skill/judgment) |
| Website copy, brochures, labels text, ad scripts | Literary work | Yes |
| Label/carton artwork, illustrations, photographs | Artistic work | Yes — but see the Designs Act interface (section 15) |
| Music/jingles, recorded meditations, podcasts | Musical work / sound recording / performance | Layered rights: lyrics, composition, recording, performance |
| Training videos, brand films, documentaries | Cinematograph film | Multiple rights (script, direction, music, performance) |
| Software (ERP, LIMS, apps, website code) | Literary work (computer program) | Source and object code protected |
| **The formulation itself / a therapeutic method / a concept** | **Not a work** | Ideas, procedures, methods, mathematical concepts and facts are outside copyright |
| Data tables of assay results / facts | Facts not protected | Selection/arrangement may be (database compilation) |

**Section 15 — Designs interface:** copyright does not subsist in an artistic work which is or has been **registered as a design** under the Designs Act, 2000 (and related limits apply where an artistic work is industrially reproduced beyond the statutory threshold) — read section 15 for the exact rule and threshold.

## 3. Ownership — Sections 14 and 17

### 3.1 Section 14 — meaning of copyright

Section 14 lists the **exclusive rights** constituting copyright, varying with the class of work: right of **reproduction**, issuing copies, **performance** (dramatic/musical works), **communication to the public** (including broadcasting and electronic dissemination), making a **cinematograph film** of the work, **sound recording** of the work, and **translation/adaptation** rights (as enumerated for each class).

### 3.2 Section 17 — first owner of copyright

General rule: **the author is the first owner**, subject to the section's exceptions, the principal ones being:

- works made in the **course of employment** under a contract of service/apprenticeship — the employer is the first owner (unless there is an agreement to the contrary);
- certain **commissioned works** — the section has special rules (the commissioning-party rule for certain commissioned works has been the subject of amendment and litigation; **read the current text of section 17 before advising**);
- works produced by/under the direction of an **authority constituted under a law** (Government works) — rules differ.

**Contract hygiene:** for Ayurveda content teams, agencies, photographers, videographers, illustrators and developers, use written assignments/licences; do not rely on the employment presumption for freelancers.

## 4. Term of protection — Section 22 and related sections

- **Section 22 — Literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works:** copyright subsists until the **end of sixty years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the author dies** (i.e. author's life + 60 years). For joint authorship, the death of the **last surviving author** governs (as the section provides).
- **Other works:** cinematograph films and sound recordings are protected for **60 years from the date of publication** (see the sections dealing with films and sound recordings); **broadcast reproduction rights** and **performers' rights** run for a shorter term (commonly stated as **25 years** from the beginning of the calendar year next following the performance/broadcast — see the sections in Chapter VIII).
- **Government/international-organisation works and anonymous/pseudonymous works** have their own periods — see the sections following section 22.
- **Caution:** section numbering for these terms (sections 23 to 27 and Chapter VIII) must be checked in the official text; do not quote a section number for a specific term without verifying.

## 5. Moral rights — Section 57

**Section 57 — Author's right to claim authorship and to restrain distortion, mutilation or modification:**

- the author has the right **to claim authorship** of the work, and
- the right **to restrain or claim damages** in respect of any distortion, mutilation, modification or other act in relation to the work which would be prejudicial to the author's honour or reputation.

Points to remember:

1. Moral rights are **personal to the author** and are generally treated as **non-assignable** (though they can be waiven by agreement in practice — courts have examined waiver clauses; verify before advising).
2. They survive assignment of economic rights.
3. For Ayurveda brands: using a scholar's name on a revised formulation monograph, or altering a guru's written text, can trigger section 57 even if the economic copyright was licensed.
4. Section 57 has also been invoked (controversially) in takedown disputes — it is not a general censorship tool; the "prejudicial to honour or reputation" threshold applies.

## 6. Infringement — Section 51

**Section 51 — Infringement of copyright:** copyright is infringed by any person who, **without a licence or without the authority of the owner**, does or causes to be done any act that only the owner has the exclusive right to do (reproduce, issue copies, perform, communicate to the public, make a film/sound recording, translate/adapt, etc.), or permits any place to be used for infringing communication of a work, and where the act would be an infringement if done without licence.

**Secondary/innocent infringement:** selling, distributing, importing or exhibiting infringing copies is actionable even where the seller is not the primary copier (subject to statutory defences such as innocent purchase).

**Who is liable:** individuals, employers (for acts of employees in the course of employment), platforms/users (for communication to the public), and companies (via general criminal/corporate liability provisions).

## 7. Exceptions and fair dealing — Section 52

**Section 52** contains an extensive list of acts that **do not constitute infringement**, including (by subject):

- fair dealing for **private use, research, criticism, review, news reporting, and education**;
- reproduction for **judicial proceedings or reporting of judicial proceedings**;
- certain acts by **libraries, reading rooms and archives**;
- **performance** in the course of educational activities and by amateur performances;
- **communication of a literary/musical work and sound recordings in the course of instruction** (with conditions on attribution and remuneration);
- **reporting of current events** and inclusion in reports of current events;
- the **editing/reproduction for approved purposes** contemplated in sub-clauses (including reproduction by teachers and students within limits);
- acts necessary for **interoperability, backup copies, observation/testing and security testing of computer programs** (the IT-related clauses);
- certain uses in connection with **disabilities** (accessible-format copies — the 2012 framework);
- **making of copies/performances for non-commercial research, and for private/personal use** (with limits).

**Conditions:** many exceptions require that the use be accompanied by **acknowledgement** of the source and title of the work; commercial exploitation beyond the exception is still infringement. "Fair dealing" in Indian law is **purpose-limited** (the section enumerates purposes) — unlike a free-floating proportionality test.

**Section 52A-type provisions** (optional works for records etc.) and the **compulsory/optional licensing** machinery are separate — see below.

## 8. Compulsory and non-voluntary licences — Sections 31 and 31A

- **Section 31 —** where the owner of copyright has **refused or neglected to grant a licence**, or where the work is required for a purpose covered by the section (including public interest, and where a work is not available to the public at an affordable price, as the section provides), an applicant may approach the authority (formerly the Copyright Board) for a **compulsory licence** on the prescribed conditions. The 2012 amendment adjusted the timing (applications after a period of refusal/neglect — verify the current period).
- **Section 31A —** machinery for works **unpublished or withheld from the public**, enabling a compulsory licence to publish/work the work (read the section for conditions).
- **Forum:** after the abolition of the Copyright Board (Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021), these applications lie to the **Commercial Courts/High Court** framework — verify the current forum and procedure.
- **Practical relevance:** compulsory licensing is a safety valve, not a routine route — copyright owners retain strong control over reproduction and communication of their works.

## 9. Registration and the Register of Copyrights

- **Voluntary registration:** the Copyright Office registers claims in the prescribed form and fee under the Copyright Rules, 2013 (classes include literary, artistic, musical, sound recording, cinematograph film, software, etc.).
- **Evidentiary value:** a certificate of registration is **prima facie evidence** of the particulars entered (see the evidentiary section of the Act — commonly cited as section 48 — verify).
- **Not a precondition to suit:** an owner can sue without registering, but registration strengthens proof of ownership/date.
- **Register entries** (title, author, first publication details, ownership) are open to inspection subject to the Rules.
- **Practical tip for Ayurveda enterprises:** register (i) the brand book/label artwork set, (ii) the software, (iii) the video/marketing content, and (iv) any textbook or monograph authored in-house — cheap, fast evidence.

## 10. Remedies — civil and criminal

### 10.1 Civil remedies

| Remedy | Provision (verify numbering) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Who may institute proceedings | Section 54 (as commonly cited) | Owner, assignee, exclusive licensee and legally authorised persons may sue (read the section for standing rules) |
| Remedies for infringement | **Section 55** | Injunction (temporary and permanent), damages or account of profits (at the plaintiff's election), delivery up/destruction of infringing copies |
| Impounding/ seizure | Sections dealing with search, seizure and delivery up (verify: sections 60/64 and related) | Infringing copies, plates and equipment may be impounded |
| Account/assessment of damages | Provisions in the same Chapter | Court may award additional damages in appropriate cases |
| Groundless threats | Provisions in the Act | Wrongful threats of infringement proceedings may be restrained |

### 10.2 Criminal remedies

- **Section 63 — Offence of infringement:** knowingly infringing copyright (or abetting it) is an offence punishable with **imprisonment and fine**. The Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012 enhanced the minimum and maximum sentences/fines — **read section 63 for the current figures before quoting any number.**
- **Search and seizure:** the Code of Criminal Procedure machinery applies; special provisions allow seizure of infringing copies without warrant in certain circumstances (verify sections).
- **Offences by companies:** directors/managers/secretaries and authorised persons can be liable where the offence was committed with consent/connivance or neglect (as the Act provides).
- **Logistical note:** criminal complaints by the owner are typically preceded by a cease-and-desist; civil injunctions remain the primary commercial tool.

## 11. Digital and IT interface

1. **Communication to the public** includes making works available online/on demand — website, app, marketplace and social-media use is squarely within the owner's rights.
2. **Platform liability:** platforms that host or transmit infringing content face secondary liability analysis (and take-down/notice practices under the Information Technology Act, 2000 framework).
3. **Software and databases:** computer programs are literary work; a licensed (not sold) copy regime applies — EULA terms matter; backups and interoperability have limited statutory exceptions (section 52 clauses).
4. **AI/automated content:** training, generation and editing of content raise authorship questions (human authorship requirement) — treat AI-generated output as high-risk for registration and enforcement; verify current judicial/registry positions.
5. **Moral rights takedowns:** section 57 is increasingly invoked online; the "prejudicial to honour or reputation" element still must be proved.

## 12. Relevance to Ayurvedic businesses — what copyright does and does not cover

**Covered:** formulation monographs and research write-ups, textbooks and course material, marketing copy and advertising scripts, label and packaging *artwork* (subject to the Designs Act interface), photographs of herbs and products, training videos and e-learning modules, website/app content, software (ERP, LIMS, e-commerce), database compilations of phytochemical data, music and recorded lectures.

**Not covered:** the formulation itself (recipe/proportions/therapeutic concept), the underlying facts (a plant's traditional use, a clinical result), a method of treatment, a name or short phrase (that is trade mark territory), the traditional knowledge of a community (see the section 3(p)/TKDL framework), or the idea of a product concept.

**Boundary rules for the platform:**

- To reproduce a passage from a classical Ayurvedic text (Charaka/Sushruta — long in the public domain), no permission is needed **for the original text**, but a modern **translation or commentary** is a separate copyright owned by its translator/commentator until life + 60 years.
- Government publications are often freely usable, but check the specific statutory position for works of Government and international organisations.
- Use licence tracking for third-party images, monographs and datasets; keep licences in the compliance register.

## 13. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is the asset **original expression** (not an idea, fact, method or formula)?
- [ ] Who is the **first owner** (author, employer, commissioning party) — is there a signed assignment/licence from freelancers, agencies, translators and developers?
- [ ] Are **moral rights** cleared (section 57) — attribution and no derogatory editing without consent?
- [ ] Has the work been **registered** with the Copyright Office (evidentiary benefit) and are dates/versions recorded?
- [ ] Is the proposed use within a **section 52 exception** (research/criticism/education/news), with acknowledgement, and not disguised commercial exploitation?
- [ ] Is the term calculated correctly (**life + 60 years** for authored works; other terms for films/sound recordings/broadcasts/performances — verify sections)?
- [ ] Has the **Designs Act overlap** been checked for artwork applied industrially (section 15)?
- [ ] Are contracts with content creators clear on ownership, licence scope, territory and term?
- [ ] For enforcement: have you sent a cease-and-desist, prepared section 55 relief (injunction/damages/account), and considered section 63 criminal remedies for knowing commercial-scale infringement?
- [ ] For online use: takedown readiness (notice-and-take-down) and platform EULA checks.
