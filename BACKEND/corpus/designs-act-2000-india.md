# The Designs Act, 2000 and the Designs Rules, 2001

## 1. Document overview

The Designs Act, 2000 (Act No. 16 of 2000) provides for the protection of new or original designs, being features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament or combination of colours applied to an article, and for the registration of designs and remedies for piracy. It is read with the Designs Rules, 2001 (as amended).

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Enactment | The Designs Act, 2000 (16 of 2000), published in the Gazette of India (Extraordinary) in 2000 |
| Subordinate legislation | Designs Rules, 2001 (as amended from time to time) |
| Office | The Patent Office (Designs wing), under the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks; Controller of Designs administers the Act |
| Application form | Form 1 under the Designs Rules (application for registration of a design) |
| Term | 10 years from registration, extendable by 5 years on application |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** the *look* of a product — bottle shape, jar, carton artwork, device form-factor — is protectable as a design even where the formulation itself is not patentable (section 3(p) bar) and the brand is separately a trade mark.

## 2. Definition of design — Section 2(d)

**"Design" means only the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament or composition of lines or colour applied to any article, whether in two dimensional or three dimensional or both forms, by any industrial process, whether manual, mechanical or chemical, separate or combined, which in the finished article appeal to and are judged solely by the eye; but does not include any mode or principle of construction.**

Elements (each is a retrieval point):

1. **Features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament or composition of lines/colour** — the visual attributes only.
2. **Applied to any article** — a design must be applied to an article; a design in the abstract (unapplied) is not registrable.
3. **Industrial process** — manual, mechanical or chemical; must be capable of reproduction by an industrial process.
4. **Judged solely by the eye** — visual appeal to the eye in the finished article; functionality and how it *works* are outside design law.
5. **Exclusion: mode or principle of construction** — the way an article is built or functions cannot be monopolised as a design (functionality exclusion).

Other definitions (section 2, by subject): "article" includes any substance, artificial or natural, and things capable of being applied to an article; "copyright" (the exclusive right to apply a design to an article); "Controller" (the Controller of Designs); "design class"/classification of goods (prescribed by the Rules); "new or original design" (as defined, judged against prior design art and the statutory tests).

## 3. What is NOT registrable — Section 4

**Section 4 (Prohibition of registration of certain designs)** — a design shall not be registered if:

- (a) it is **not new or original**;
- (b) it has been **disclosed/published before** the relevant date (in India or elsewhere, subject to the statutory exceptions in the Act — read the full clause);
- (c) it is **contrary to public order or morality**;
- (d) it **comprises or contains scandalous or obscene matter**.

**Caution:** a widely repeated secondary citation places these grounds in "Section 13". In the official arrangement of sections, section 4 is "Prohibition of registration of certain designs" (and section 13 deals with procedure for restoration applications). **Verify the section numbering in the official text** — do not quote "section 13" for registrability without checking.

Related bars and interfaces:

- **Trademark overlap:** a design that is essentially a logo/trade mark may be refused or better protected as a trade mark; where a design comprises a well-known sign, examine both statutes.
- **Copyright overlap:** under the Copyright Act, 1957 (section 15 as commonly cited), copyright does not subsist in an artistic work which is or has been **registered as a design** under the Designs Act, and copyright in an artistic work capable of industrial reproduction is lost if it is industrially reproduced beyond the statutory threshold (commonly fifty copies) — **verify the section and the threshold in the Copyright Act text**.
- **Prior design art:** novelty in Indian design practice is assessed against earlier designs in the same class and closely related classes (the Controller's search).

## 4. Registration and its effect (Sections 5 to 11)

| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| 3 | Controller and other officers |
| 5 | **Application for registration** — Form 1, in respect of one article/class (as prescribed), with representations (views) of the design; statement of novelty and disclaimer of functional features; endorsed on the application |
| 6 | Registration to be in respect of a **particular article** (and class) |
| 7 | **Publication of particulars** of registered designs (journal/publication of particulars) |
| 8 | Power of Controller to order substitution of applications etc. |
| 9 | **Certificate of registration** issued to the proprietor |
| 10 | **Register of Designs** maintained by the Controller |
| 11 | **Copyright on registration** — see section 5 below |

**No pre-grant opposition:** the Designs Act does not provide a pre-grant opposition/advertise-and-oppose procedure like trade marks or patents; instead, after registration, any interested person may apply for **cancellation** of the registration (Section 19 — see section 7 below).

## 5. Copyright in a registered design — Section 11 (term: 10 years + 5)

On registration of a design, the registered proprietor has the **exclusive right (copyright) to apply the design** to any article, to import, sell or hire articles bearing the design, and to restrain others from applying it — for the statutory term.

- **Initial term: ten years from the date of registration.**
- **Extension: a further five years** on application made before expiry of the initial term (the section's proviso/mechanism), giving a maximum of **15 years** in total on the standard reading.
- **Caution on further extension:** some commentary refers to a possible "second period of five years" (language echoed in older law). **Confirm the current statutory position in section 11 before asserting that more than one extension is available.**
- **Restoration:** Section 12 provides for restoration of lapsed designs (with the procedure in Section 13) where renewal/extension was missed — verify time limits.

**Disclosure effects (Section 16):** prior disclosure can destroy copyright; the section also addresses the effect of publication/display (including the well-known limited period — commonly twelve months — for filing after certain publications/exhibitions). **Verify the period before relying on it; do not advise a client to publish before filing without checking.**

**Marking (Section 15):** articles sold or delivered must comply with the statutory marking/registration requirements before delivery or sale — proper marking (registration number) preserves remedies against innocent purchasers. Check section 15's exact requirements.

## 6. Application procedure and practice

1. **Filing:** Form 1 (with the prescribed fee under the Designs Rules) to the Controller; the application states the name of the article, class, and includes views of the design (typically four views plus a declaration of novelty/description).
2. **Representation:** drawings/photographs of the design as applied to the article; disclaimers of any part not claimed (e.g. mechanical features) and statement of novelty.
3. **Examination:** formal and novelty examination by the Controller; objections (objection report) may be raised; the applicant responds/amends.
4. **Registration:** if accepted, the Controller registers the design, enters it in the Register of Designs and issues the certificate (Section 9). In straightforward cases registration can be obtained comparatively quickly (weeks to a couple of months) — but this depends on workload and objections; do not promise a date.
5. **Publication:** particulars of registered designs are published (Section 7).
6. **Classification:** goods are classified for design filings under the Rules — check the current classification schedule before filing (do not assume a foreign classification system without verifying).
7. **Exhibition/consortium routes:** Section 21 contains provisions regarding designs exhibited at industrial/international exhibitions (protection of novelty when displayed — verify scope).

## 7. Cancellation, rectification and appeals

- **Section 19 — Cancellation of registration:** at any time after registration, any interested person (or the Controller in certain cases) may apply to the Controller to cancel the registration on grounds such as the design not being new or original, or not being registrable (read the section for the complete grounds and time limits).
- **Section 31 — Rectification of the register** (correction of entries).
- **Section 30 — Entry of assignment and transmission** in the register.
- **Section 36 — Appeals to the High Court** (against Controller's decisions) — note: appeals that formerly went to the Appellate Board framework now lie to the High Courts following the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021; verify the current appellate route.
- **Section 20 — Designs bind the Government** (subject to the statute) — a registered design can be enforced even against government use, subject to the section's terms.

## 8. Piracy and remedies (Section 22), application of patent provisions (Section 23)

**Section 22 — Piracy of registered design:**

- After registration, no person, **without the proprietor's licence**, shall apply the design (or any obvious or fraudulent imitation of it) to any article for sale, or import, sell or publish for sale any article to which the design (or an obvious/frauditious imitation) has been applied.
- **Remedies:** the proprietor may sue for infringement/piracy; the court may grant **injunction** (interim and final) and award **damages**. Practitioner sources report the statutory damages ceiling at **up to ₹25,000 per contravention** (with a higher figure reported for specified/repeat contraventions).
  - **Figures caution:** read section 22 in the official text for the exact amounts and structure; penalty provisions in Indian IP statutes have been amended by reform legislation (e.g. the Jan Vishwas Act, 2023 and earlier decriminalisation packages) — verify before quoting.
- **Section 23 — Application of certain provisions of the Act relating to patents to designs:** certain patent-law procedural provisions apply to designs as if adapted (read this section to determine which patent provisions are imported — it is a gateway section, not a free-standing penalty).
- **Criminal/civil mix:** the Act's remedies are primarily civil (injunction, damages); check for any penalty/offence provisions in the current text before asserting criminal liability.

**Practical enforcement notes:**

- Speed matters: design piracy cases turn on clear visual comparison — keep dated evidence of first publication, application and use.
- Preserve evidence of copying: side-by-side comparisons, launch dates, marketplace listings, wholesale markets.
- Consider **simultaneous** relief: trade mark infringement (if logo used), copyright (if artwork/label copied), and passing off.

## 9. Designs Rules, 2001 (as amended)

The Rules prescribe:

- **Form 1** application and the required representations/views, statement of novelty and disclaimer;
- **classes of goods** for design registration (classification schedule);
- **fees** (filing, extension of term, statement of novelty, etc.) — check the current fee schedule;
- **procedure** for examination, objections, registration, extension of term (the extension application must be made before expiry of the ten-year term), restoration;
- provisions for **agents** (who may practise before the Controller) and for **inspection** of registered designs.

## 10. Interaction with other IP and with Ayurveda products

| Asset | Right | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bottle/jar shape (visual) | **Design** | Must be new/original and judged by the eye; functional shapes excluded (s.2(d) "mode or principle of construction") |
| Bottle shape that customers associate with a brand (source identifier) | **Trade mark** (shape mark) | Functionality and distinctiveness bar; can coexist with design registration in some cases — analyse both |
| Label/carton artwork, illustration, photograph | **Copyright** | Artistic/literary work; lost if industrially reproduced as a registered design (Copyright Act interface) |
| Brand name/logo | **Trade mark** | Section 9/11 of the Trade Marks Act |
| Formulation | **Patent?** | Only if it clears section 3(j)/3(p) — usually not for classical formulations |
| Manufacturing know-how | **Trade secret / contract** | No registration regime in India; confidentiality agreements |
| Origin of a regional product | **GI** | For goods of a place — separate regime |

**Ayurveda-specific applications:**

- Distinctive **churna bottles, lehya jars, oil dispensers, tablet strips, vaporiser/diffuser devices**, and portable "kit" packaging.
- **Device design** for Ayurvedic apparatus (neti pots, shirodhara stands, massage tools) where the visual form is the point.
- Aesthetic carton artwork (but remember copyright and trademark cover different aspects).
- **Not protectable as design:** the formulation inside, the therapeutic method, the functional mechanism (unless it produces a visual effect independent of function).

## 11. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is the asset a **visual feature of an article** (shape/config/pattern/ornament/colour) applied by an industrial process? If not, design law does not apply.
- [ ] Is it **new and original** versus prior designs (search the Register of Designs and design databases — including foreign offices)?
- [ ] Does it include **scandalous/obscene** or **public-order/morality** issues (s.4 grounds)?
- [ ] File **before** any public launch or catalogue publication (disclosure can destroy novelty — s.16 exceptions are narrow; verify the period).
- [ ] Use **Form 1** with a clear statement of novelty and proper views; name the correct article and class.
- [ ] Diary the **10-year term from registration** and file the **extension application (5 years) before expiry** (s.11); check restoration (s.12) if missed.
- [ ] Mark articles with the **registration number** before delivery/sale (s.15) to preserve remedies.
- [ ] Check the **design/trademark/copyright overlap** — file in each regime that applies, and avoid assuming one right covers all.
- [ ] On infringement, act promptly (injunction + damages under s.22); confirm current penalty/damages figures in the official text.
- [ ] For unregistered designs, consider passing off/copyright — but design law itself requires **registration**.
