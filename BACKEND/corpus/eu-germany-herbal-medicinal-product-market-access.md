# EU and Germany — Market Access Framework for Herbal/Ayurvedic Products

## 1. What this file is for

The IP-SAKTI corpus tracks two markets — **India** and **Germany/EU**. This
file maps the **regulatory routes** by which an Ayurvedic or herbal product can
be placed on the EU/German market, and identifies which instrument governs
each route. It is a **legal/regulatory map**, not legal advice: every provision
below must be read against the current consolidated text on EUR-Lex and the
competent authority's published guidance.

**First question — which product category?** The same formula can be a
medicine, a food, a food supplement or a cosmetic depending on how it is
presented, dosed and claimed:

| Route | Trigger (in substance) | Core EU instrument | Core authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Herbal medicinal product** — full marketing authorisation | Therapeutic/prevention claims; or presentation as a medicine | Directive 2001/83/EC, **Article 6** (standard application) | National competent authority (Germany: BfArM) |
| **Well-established use (abridged) authorisation** | Bibliographic/expert evidence; ≥10 years' well-established use within the Community with recognised efficacy and acceptable safety | Directive 2001/83/EC, **Article 10** (abridged/bibliographic route) | National competent authority |
| **Traditional herbal medicinal product (THMP)** — simplified registration | Traditional indications, no medical supervision, ≥30 years' use incl. ≥15 in the Community | Directive 2001/83/EC, **Articles 16a–16h** (inserted by Directive 2004/24/EC) | National competent authority (may refer to EMA HMPC) |
| **Homeopathic medicinal product** — simplified registration | Homeopathic preparation, traditional/limited indications | Directive 2001/83/EC, **Article 14** (as amended) | National competent authority |
| **Food / traditional food from a third country** | Novel food (not consumed significantly in the EU before 15 May 1997) with a documented history of safe food use in a third country | Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 — **Article 3(b)**, **Article 10** (authorisation), **Article 14** (notification of traditional food) | European Commission + EFSA |
| **Food / food supplement** | No therapeutic claim; food category presentation (capsule/tablet/powder dose) | Directive 2002/46/EC (supplements), Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 (information), Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006 (claims), Regulation (EC) No 1925/2006 (fortification) | National authority (Germany: BVL/LKÜ) + EU framework |
| **Food flavouring / ingredient** | Used for flavouring or as a food ingredient | Regulation (EC) No 1334/2008 (flavourings), Directive 2009/32/EC (extraction solvents) | European Commission + EFSA |
| **Cosmetic** | Cleansing/protecting/beautifying presentation; no therapeutic claim | Regulation (EU) No 1223/2009 | National authority (Germany: BASG/BVL surveillance); CPNP notification |

> **Presentation drives classification.** A therapeutic claim, or packaging
> that presents the product as treating disease, makes it a medicinal product
> regardless of how the formula is composed (see the "medicinal claim" test in
> the Directive's definitions — Article 1(8)(a) hazard to health when
> administered — **verify the current consolidated wording**).

## 2. Herbal medicinal products — Directive 2001/83/EC

### 2.1 Key definitions (Article 1)

| Definition | Content (verify numbering against consolidated text) |
|---|---|
| **Medicinal product** | Article 1(8) — substance/composition presented for treating or preventing disease, or for restoring/correcting physiological functions, **or** having pharmacological/immunological/toxicological action, used to make a medical diagnosis or to prevent/achieve a physiological function. |
| **Traditional herbal medicinal product (Art. 1(29))** | A herbal medicinal product that fulfils the conditions laid down in **Article 16a(1)**. |
| **Herbal medicinal product (Art. 1(30))** | A medicinal product **exclusively containing as active ingredients** one or more **herbal substances** or one or more **herbal preparations** (or a combination of both). |
| **Herbal substances / herbal preparations (Art. 1(31) ff.)** | Directly used plants and plant materials (fresh or dried, whole or powdered), and preparations obtained from them (extraction, distillation, expression, fractionation, purification, etc.). |

### 2.2 Routes to market

- **Article 6 — full marketing authorisation.** Complete dossier in the
  required quality, non-clinical and clinical modules (as set out in Annex I of
  the Directive — the "Detailed Guidelines" and, for the technical requirements
  of herbal products, the Annex I requirements inserted by **Directive
  2004/24/EC**). Needed where the product makes **full efficacy claims** or
  where traditional-use criteria are not met.
- **Article 10 — abridged (bibliographic) route.** Test and trial results may
  be replaced by **bibliographic or expert evidence** where the product or its
  active substances have a **well-established medicinal use within the
  Community for at least ten years, with recognised efficacy and an acceptable
  level of safety** (conditions in Annex I).
- **Article 16a — traditional-use registration (THMP).** The central route for
  classical Ayurvedic *herbal* products in the EU. See Section 3.
- **Article 14 — homeopathic medicinal products.** Simplified registration
  where the product is prepared according to homeopathic principles (not the
  route for Ayurvedic products, though some EU products combine traditions).

### 2.3 Obligations that apply to every authorised/registered product

| Requirement | Directive basis (verify numbering) |
|---|---|
| **Marketing authorisation holder / applicant established in the EU/EEA** | Article 4 (applicant establishment) |
| **GMP compliance of manufacturing** | Article 46 ff.; EU GMP Guide (EudraLex Volume 4); importation requirements for third-country manufacturers |
| **Qualified Person (QP) certification** | Article 48 ff. |
| **Pharmacovigilance system and adverse-event reporting** | Title IX / Articles 101 ff. (as amended by Directive 2010/84/EU) |
| **Product information — SmPC and package leaflet (Annex to Directive 2001/83/EC)** | Articles 11, 15; Annex |
| **Language: product information and labelling in the official language(s) of the Member State** | Article 54 ff. (verify) |
| **Packaging: name/address of holder, active substances, excipients, storage, batch, expiry** | Article 54 ff. |

**THMP-specific pharmacovigilance relief:** holders of **Article 16a**
registrations are **not required to submit PSURs**, except where a case under
Article 107b(3)(a) or (b) arises or the authority imposes it (EMA guidance).

## 3. Traditional herbal medicinal products — Articles 16a–16h

### 3.1 Eligibility criteria (Article 16a(1)) — verified against the consolidated text

A product qualifies for **traditional-use registration** only if it meets
**all** of the following:

| Criterion | Article 16a(1) |
|---|---|
| **Indications** | (a) exclusively **appropriate to traditional herbal medicinal products** — intended and designed for use **without the supervision of a medical practitioner** for diagnosis, prescription or monitoring of treatment |
| **Posology** | (b) exclusively for administration in accordance with a **specified strength and posology** |
| **Route** | (c) an **oral, external and/or inhalation** preparation (no parenteral, no ophthalmic) |
| **Tradition** | (d) the period of traditional use laid down in **Article 16c(1)(c)** has elapsed |
| **Data sufficiency** | (e) the data on traditional use are sufficient — in particular the product **proves not to be harmful** in the specified conditions of use and the pharmacological effects or efficacy are **plausible on the basis of long-standing use and experience** |

**Article 16a(2)** — the presence of **vitamins or minerals** with
well-documented safety evidence does **not** disqualify the product, provided
their action is **ancillary** to that of the herbal active ingredient(s).

### 3.2 The 30/15-year rule (Article 16c(1)(c)) — verified

> The applicant must provide **bibliographical or expert evidence** that the
> medicinal product in question, or a corresponding product, has been in
> **medicinal use throughout a period of at least 30 years preceding the date
> of the application, including at least 15 years within the Community**.

- The 30-year use need **not** have been based on a specific authorisation,
  and is satisfied even if the number of ingredients was reduced during that
  period (Article 16c(3)).
- **Where the product has been used in the Community for less than 15 years**
  but is otherwise eligible, the **Member State must refer the product to the
  Committee for Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC)** at EMA (Article 16c(4)),
  which assesses whether the other Article 16a criteria are met.

**Ayurveda implication:** a formula used in India for centuries but only
recently marketed in the EU will normally be **referred to HMPC** — plan for
that step, and assemble **documented evidence of the 30-year use** (classical
texts, pharmacopoeial monographs, ethnographic and clinical literature, prior
marketing in third countries) as **bibliographic/expert evidence**.

### 3.3 What the dossier must contain (Article 16c, with 16b and 16d)

- **Quality/Particulars** — pharmaceutical form, qualitative/quantitative
  composition, excipients, **method of manufacture** (where traditional), data
  supporting the traditional use for the specified constituents, and — for
  combinations — information on the combination as such.
- **Non-clinical and clinical safety** — bibliographic/expert safety evidence;
  **no clinical efficacy trials are required**; efficacy need only be
  **plausible** on long-standing use.
- **Product information** — SmPC and package leaflet in the prescribed format,
  with **traditional-use wording** (indications introduced with, e.g., "used in
  traditional herbal medicine for..." — verify current wording conventions).
- **Community herbal monograph** — where HMPC has established one
  (**Article 16h(3)**), the Member State **takes it into account when examining
  the application**; where **no** Community herbal monograph has been
  established, **other appropriate monographs, publications or data may be
  referred to** (also Article 16h(3)). HMPC's standing duties — including the
  tasks arising under Article 16c(1) and (4) and under Article 16d, drafting
  the Article 16f(1) list, and establishing monographs — are set out in
  **Article 16h(1)**.

### 3.4 Refusal grounds (Article 16e) — verified against the consolidated text

Registration **must be refused** if the application does not comply with
Articles 16a, 16b or 16c, or if at least one of the following is true
(**Article 16e(1)**):

| Ground | Article 16e(1) |
|---|---|
| Composition not as declared (qualitative and/or quantitative) | (a) |
| Indications do not comply with the conditions in Article 16a | (b) |
| The product could be harmful under normal conditions of use | (c) |
| Data on traditional use are insufficient — pharmacological effects or efficacy not plausible on long-standing use and experience | (d) |
| Pharmaceutical quality not satisfactorily demonstrated | (e) |

The competent authority must **notify the applicant, the Commission and any
authority that requests it** of any refusal decision and its reasons
(**Article 16e(2)**).

**Correction to a common mis-citation:** the refusal grounds are in
**Article 16e**, *not* Article 16f. **Article 16f** is a different provision:
it establishes the **list of herbal substances, preparations and combinations
permitted in THMPs** (Article 16f(1), set by the Article 121(2) procedure).
Where the product's substance is on that list, the data required by Article
16c(1)(b), (c) and (d) are not needed and grounds 16e(1)(c) and (d) do not
apply (Article 16f(2)).

### 3.5 Recognition of registrations from other Member States

**Article 16d(1)** — without prejudice to Article 16h(1), **Chapter 4 of
Title III (mutual recognition of authorisations) applies by analogy** to
registrations granted under Article 16a, provided that either (a) a Community
herbal monograph has been established under Article 16h(3), **or** (b) the
product consists of substances/preparations/combinations on the Article 16f
list.

**Article 16d(2)** — for **other** herbal medicinal products under Article
16a, each Member State must, when evaluating an application, **take due
account** of registrations granted by another Member State under the chapter.

There is **no** "reference Member State"/decentralised-procedure mechanism for
THMPs: recognition operates through Article 16d only. **Correction to a common
mis-citation:** Article 16g is *not* the mutual-recognition provision —
**Article 16g** applies the **GMP principles and guidelines (Articles 40–52) by
analogy** to traditional-use registrations and prescribes the **mandatory
labelling/leaflet statement** ("traditional herbal medicinal product for use in
specified indication(s) exclusively based upon long-standing use", with the
"consult a doctor/qualified health care practitioner if symptoms persist"
wording) and the equivalent **advertising statement**.

## 4. Germany as a target market

| Aspect | Position (verify each before use) |
|---|---|
| **Competent authority for medicines** | **BfArM** — Bundesinstitut für Arzneimittel und Medizinprodukte (Bonn). Biologics/vaccines go to the **Paul-Ehrlich-Institut**. |
| **EU-level assessment for monographs** | **EMA — Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC)** issues **Community herbal monographs** and, for THMP referrals, scientific opinions. |
| **Implementing statute** | **Arzneimittelgesetz (AMG)**, together with the statutory ordinances made under it and the EU Directives/Regulations; EU **GMP** (EudraLex Volume 4) applies. |
| **German national monographs** | Germany operates **national monographs on herbal substances** in addition to HMPC Community monographs — check the current publication channel and status at BfArM before citing one. |
| **Language** | Product information (SmPC, package leaflet) and labelling in **German**. |
| **Establishment** | The registration/authorisation holder and (in practice) the importer/QP arrangements must be established in the EU/EEA; an **Indian manufacturer** will need an EU importer/MA holder and GMP verification (inspection or acceptance of the country's GMP status as applicable). |
| **Distribution** | Medicines in Germany are generally subject to **pharmacy availability rules** (Apothekenpflicht) with some free-sale categories — check the product's status; food supplements are ordinary retail goods. |
| **Claims** | The boundary between a medicinal claim and a food claim is enforced by both food and medicines authorities; EU authorities scrutinise "wellness" wording closely. |
| **Surveillance** | Food/feed complaints and border controls via **BVL**; cosmetics market surveillance by the competent Länder/BASG channels (verify current assignment). |

## 5. Novel food route — Regulation (EU) 2015/2283

**Applicability:** many Ayurvedic botanical ingredients (for example
*Withania somnifera*, *Mucuna*, *Coleus/*, or traditional preparations not
used to a significant degree in the EU **before 15 May 1997**) are **novel
foods** and cannot be placed on the market without authorisation.

### 5.1 Verified provisions

| Provision | Content |
|---|---|
| **Article 3** definitions | **(a)** "novel food" includes food not consumed to a significant degree within the Union before **15 May 1997**, plus the category-based exclusions (including the plant categories in points (a)(i)–(x), engineered nanomaterials, etc.); **(b)** **"history of safe food use in a third country"** means safety confirmed with **compositional data and experience of continued use for at least 25 years in the customary diet of a significant number of people in at least one third country**, **prior to a notification referred to in Article 14**; **(c)** **"traditional food from a third country"** — novel food as defined in point (a) other than specified categories, **derived from primary production** as defined in Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, with a history of safe food use in a third country. |
| **Article 4** | Requires **food business operators to verify** whether the food they intend to place on the EU market falls within the scope of the Regulation (novel-food check duty). |
| **Article 10** | **Authorisation procedure** for novel foods (application → Commission → EFSA opinion → Union list). |
| **Article 14** | **Notification of a traditional food from a third country** — the simplified route: applicant, name/description, detailed composition, countries of origin, documented data demonstrating the history of safe food use, and supporting data; if no **duly reasoned safety objections** are raised, the food is added to the Union list. |
| Implementing acts | **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2468** (administrative and scientific requirements for traditional-food notifications), as amended by **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1824**. |

> **Correction to a common misconception:** the "**history of safe food
> use**" standard under EU novel-food law is **25 years in at least one third
> country** (Article 3(2)(b)) — there is **no "15 years in the EU" element**
> on this route. The **30 years including 15 years within the Community**
> requirement belongs to the **THMP medicinal route** (Article 16c(1)(c) of
> Directive 2001/83/EC), not to food law. Do not blend the two.

### 5.2 Practical notes

- Evidence of history of safe food use must be **dietary** (normal diet), not
  medicinal use, and must relate to **food** consumption in the third country
  (India qualifies as a third country for this purpose).
- Novel foods added to the Union list may carry **conditions of use, labelling
  requirements and post-market monitoring** requirements.
- The route is slower than the traditional-food notification where an
  authorisation is needed; the notification route exists precisely to speed
  up traditional foods with solid history of use.

## 6. Food, food supplements and claims

### 6.1 Food information — Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011

Mandatory food information (name, list of ingredients, allergens, quantity,
date, storage, operator identification, nutrition declaration) for pre-packed
foods; **food supplements are a food category** and must be labelled
accordingly. *(Verify the specific Annex requirements for supplements.)*

### 6.2 Food supplements — Directive 2002/46/EC

- Requires that supplements be taken in **small measured quantities** (unit
  dose forms: capsules, lozenges, granules, sachets, etc.).
- **Harmonised positive lists** of vitamins and minerals that may be used,
  together with **source-substance** restrictions.
- **Germany: Nahrungsergänzungsmittelverordnung (NemV)** lays down the
  additional national rules (permitted vitamins/minerals, labelling, naming of
  nutrients in German). *(Verify current Annex lists.)*
- A supplement **may not contain medicinal substances** — for Ayurvedic
  products this excludes any ingredient with pharmacological action presented
  for therapeutic effect.

### 6.3 Health and nutrition claims — Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006

> **Correction:** the EU nutrition and health claims Regulation is
> **Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006** (not 1924/2009) — several secondary sources
> miscite the year. The list of permitted health claims is in **Commission
> Regulation (EU) No 432/2012**, applicable since 14 December 2012.

| Provision | Content (verify exact allocation) |
|---|---|
| **Article 3** | Definitions (nutrition claim, health claim, disease risk reduction claim, etc.). |
| **Articles 4–6** | Conditions for using nutrition claims; conditions for health claims; **substantiation** — claims must be based on generally accepted scientific evidence and be reviewed. |
| **Article 7** | Nutrition information. |
| **Article 10** | Specific conditions: health claims are **prohibited unless authorised and listed**; labelling/presentation must carry the statement on a varied balanced diet and healthy lifestyle, the quantity/pattern of consumption needed for the claimed effect, warnings for persons who should avoid the product, and warnings for excess consumption risk; general "good health" statements must be accompanied by a specific authorised claim. |
| **Article 13(1)** | **General function claims** (growth/development/functions of the body; psychological/behavioural functions; slimming/weight control) — the "**Article 13 list**". |
| **Article 13(5)** | New function claims based on **newly developed scientific evidence and/or proprietary data** (individual application; procedure in Article 18). |
| **Article 14(1)(a)** | **Reduction of disease risk claims** (authorised individually; must be accompanied by the statement that the disease has multiple risk factors). |
| **Article 14(1)(b)** | Claims referring to **children's development and health**. |
| **Articles 15–18** | Authorisation procedure (application → Member State → EFSA → Commission decision). |
| **Article 19** | Modification of an authorised claim. |
| **Article 28** | Trade marks and brand names that are claims are still subject to the Regulation. |

**Ayurveda claims reality check:** classic therapeutic claims ("relieves
*ama*", "balances *dosha*", "treats diabetes") are **not permitted** on foods
or supplements in the EU. Only **listed** claims may be used; botanical
"Article 13" claims were examined in a separate procedure and many botanical
claims were **not authorised** — check the **EU register of nutrition and
health claims** before using any wording.

### 6.4 Fortified foods — Regulation (EC) No 1925/2006

Vitamins and minerals may be added to foods only within the harmonised rules
(Annex lists and conditions); certain substances are restricted or prohibited
(Annex III). *(Verify current annexes before formulating.)*

## 7. Flavourings and food ingredients — Regulation (EC) No 1334/2008

- Applies to **flavourings** and to foodstuffs with flavourings, including
  flavourings derived from botanical source materials — the route many Ayurvedic
  spices and aromatics take into EU food products.
- Core requirements (verify article numbering at EUR-Lex): definitions
  (Article 3); flavourings must be **of appropriate purity and must not
  present a safety concern**; flavourings are assessed in **flavouring group
  evaluations** and, where authorised, appear on the **Community list** of
  flavourings permitted for use in foodstuffs.
- **Constituent limits matter for Ayurvedic botanicals**, for example:
  restrictions/maximum contents for **coumarin** (present in cinnamon), for
  **beta-asarone** (present in *Acorus calamus*/vacha), and for **thujone**
  (present in wormwood-type botanicals) — imposed by the Regulation and its
  amending acts (e.g. the amending regulation setting maximum coumarin and
  thujone contents — **verify its number before citing**).
- **Extraction solvents** used to produce food ingredients are harmonised by
  **Directive 2009/32/EC** (residue limits).

## 8. Cosmetics — Regulation (EU) No 1223/2009

Ayurvedic skincare, hair oils, soaps and balms are usually **cosmetics**:
substances applied to the external parts of the body or to the teeth/mucous
membranes for cleansing, perfuming, protecting or beautifying.

| Article | Requirement (verified numbering) |
|---|---|
| **Article 4** | **Responsible person** — every cosmetic product placed on the EU market must have a **Responsible Person established within the Union**; the RP is liable for compliance (incl. traceability). |
| **Article 5** | Product safety obligation (the product must be safe). |
| **Article 10** | **Safety assessment** — before placing on the market a **safety assessor** prepares the **safety report** (Part A safety information + Part B safety assessment conclusion) after non-clinical and, where necessary, clinical testing. |
| **Article 11** | **Product information file (PIF)** — held by the Responsible Person at the stated address, containing the product description, GMP proof, safety report, method of manufacture, proof of the effect claimed, and animal-testing data where applicable; must be kept for **10 years** after the last batch. |
| **Article 13** | **Notification** — every product must be notified to the Commission through the **Cosmetic Products Notification Portal (CPNP)** before it is placed on the market. |
| **Article 16** | **Nanomaterials** — specific notification requirement for cosmetic products containing nanomaterials (safety, method of manufacture, toxicological data) **6 months** before placing on the market, plus labelling "(nano)". |
| **Article 18** | **Animal testing prohibition** — no tests on animals for the finished cosmetic product or its ingredients; no marketing of products tested on animals for cosmetic purposes (subject to the transitional provisions). |
| **Article 20** | **Claims** — labelling/presentation/advertising must not attribute to a cosmetic product characteristics or functions it does not have, and must not use text/pictures conferring medicinal functions (verify current wording). |
| **Article 21** | Products presented as medicinal or with medicinal properties fall outside the Regulation (they are medicinal products). |

**Practical points for Ayurvedic cosmetics:** the **PIF and safety report are
mandatory** and must be in a language the authority can read; the **responsible
person** and **CPNP notification** are the two structural prerequisites; the
**animal-testing ban** applies to ingredients too (with rare REACH testing
exceptions discussed at EU level) — for Ayurvedic actives, test data must come
from **existing/non-animal** sources.

## 9. End-to-end checklist for an Ayurvedic product entering Germany/EU

| Step | Question | Route |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What claim is being made? | Therapeutic claim → **medicine**; no therapeutic claim → food/cosmetic |
| 2 | Is the formula **exclusively herbal substances/preparations**? | Yes → THMP possible (Article 16a). **No** (contains Rasa Shastra/bhasma, metals/minerals, or non-herbal actives) → **not** a THMP: full authorisation (Article 6), or reclassify |
| 3 | Do the **30 years / 15 years in the EU** criteria hold? | Yes → national THMP registration. EU use < 15 years → **HMPC referral** (Article 16c(4)) |
| 4 | Are the **indications** traditional and self-care appropriate? | Traditional wording only; no diagnosis/prescription/monitoring implications |
| 5 | Route: oral, external or inhalation? | Parenteral/ophthalmic excluded from THMP |
| 6 | If a food: is the ingredient **novel**? | Not used significantly in the EU before **15 May 1997** → novel food check (Article 4); traditional food route with **25-year** history (Articles 3(b), 14) |
| 7 | Are the **claims** authorised? | Check the **EU health-claims register** and **Commission Regulation (EU) No 432/2012**; no therapeutic wording on foods |
| 8 | If a cosmetic: **RP, PIF, safety report, CPNP, (nano) notification** | Regulation (EU) No 1223/2009, Articles 4, 10, 11, 13, 16 |
| 9 | Who is the **EU legal representative/importer**? | Establish the RP/MA holder/importer arrangement before shipment |
| 10 | **Language and labelling** | German product information and labelling; food information under Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 |
| 11 | **GMP / manufacturing evidence** | EU GMP (EudraLex Vol. 4); for medicines, importation and QP certification |
| 12 | **Source/ABS and prior-art position** | Ensure the sourcing of biological material complies with CBD/Nagoya/Biological Diversity Act obligations (see the CBD, Nagoya and case-law files in this corpus) |
| 13 | **IP protection in the market** | Check patent/trademark/design position in Germany/EU before launch (see TRIPS, PCT, Madrid, Hague files) |

## 10. What this means for an Ayurvedic product owner

1. **Pick the route before designing the product.** The claim, dose form and
   composition decide the category; changing route later means re-doing the
   whole dossier.
2. **THMP is the realistic Ayurvedic route for herbal-only products** — but
   the **30/15-year** test and **self-care indications** are hard filters, and
   EU use below 15 years triggers an **HMPC referral**.
3. **Rasa Shastra/mineral preparations cannot use the THMP route** — they are
   not "herbal medicinal products" under Article 1(30); plan accordingly
   (full authorisation or a different category/jurisdiction).
4. **Food is not a soft option.** Novel-food status, the claims Regulation and
   national rules (e.g. Germany's NemV) all bite; many popular botanical claims
   simply do not appear on the authorised list.
5. **Cosmetics carry heavy documentation** — RP, PIF, safety report, CPNP and
   the animal-testing ban — despite being the fastest category commercially.
6. **Germany adds language, authority and distribution specifics** (German
   product information; BfArM; pharmacy rules) on top of the EU framework.
7. **Verify everything at source.** Directive/Regulation numbering and
   implementing acts change; this file states the position as at
   `last_verified` and must be checked against EUR-Lex and authority guidance
   before it is relied on.
