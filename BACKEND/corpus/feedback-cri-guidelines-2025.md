# Feedback on Draft CRI Guidelines 2025

## 1. Document Overview

This is a letter from **Manas Vridhi**, Patent Agent (IN/PA 3475), Managing Associate, Global IP India, addressed to the Controller of Patents, New Delhi. It provides comments and suggestions on the **Draft Guidelines for Examination of Computer-Related Inventions (CRI) – 2025** released by the Indian Patent Office (IPO).

The letter commends the IPO for the draft and offers both positive observations and specific concerns with suggestions for improvement.

## 2. Positive Observations

<!-- chunk_id: cri-feedback-pos-001 -->

1. **Recognition of Judicial Precedents on Technical Effect/Contribution:** Inclusion of recent case laws and alignment with jurisprudence on “technical effect” and “technical contribution” is appreciated.
2. **Updated Definition of CRI:** The new definition is broader and includes AI, blockchain, and quantum computing, improving upon the 2017 guidelines.
3. **Departure from Hardware Requirement:** No longer mandating novelty in hardware; emphasis on technical merit paves the way for genuine software innovations.
4. **Better Real-Life Examples:** Modern use cases improve practical understanding for examiners and applicants.

## 3. Concerns and Suggestions for Improvement

<!-- chunk_id: cri-feedback-concerns-001 -->

### 3.1 Uncertainty in Application of Section 3(k)
- **Concern:** It remains unclear whether the IPO will stop rejecting inventions solely because they are implemented via software/computer programs.
- **Suggestion:** Clarify that mere presence of a computer program does not trigger Section 3(k) if technical effect is demonstrable.

### 3.2 Lack of Discussion on AI-Related Patentability
- **Concern:** Despite AI being in scope, there is no substantive discussion on how AI-related inventions will be examined—a major omission.
- **Suggestion:** Include a dedicated section on AI patentability with practical considerations under Section 3(k), including how AI models, training methods, or AI-assisted systems may qualify based on technical contribution.

### 3.3 Need for Clarity on “Per Se” Interpretation
- **Concern:** Guidelines could further elaborate on how “per se” exclusions under Section 3(k) will be interpreted, especially when a computer program forms part of a larger technical system.
- **Suggestion:** Provide interpretive guidance consistent with EPO or UKIPO approaches, distinguishing clearly between excluded and allowable subject matter.

### 3.4 Impact on Pending or Rejected Applications
- **Concern:** Guidelines do not discuss whether applicants with pending or previously rejected applications under the 2017 CRI regime can request re-examination or reconsideration.
- **Suggestion:** Publish a transition policy or allow re-evaluation upon request.

## 4. Conclusion

The Draft CRI Guidelines 2025 are a progressive and welcome step. They align Indian practice closer to international standards, support innovation, and offer hope for more balanced and technologically informed examination. However, specific improvements—particularly around clarity, AI treatment, and claim type flexibility—would make the guidelines stronger.
