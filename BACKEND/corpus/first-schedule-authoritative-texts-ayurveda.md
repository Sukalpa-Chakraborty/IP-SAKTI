# First Schedule to the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 — Authoritative Books of Ayurveda

## 1. Document overview

The **First Schedule to the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** lists the **authoritative books** of the Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani (Tibb) systems. Those books are the legal reference point for three things that decide how an Ayurvedic product is classified, labelled, licensed and sold: what counts as an *Ayurvedic drug*, what counts as a *classical* (as against *patent or proprietary*) formulation, and what must be stated on the label.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Parent statute | Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (Central Act), read with the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 |
| Schedule | **First Schedule** — names of authoritative books, arranged by system (Ayurveda; Siddha; Unani Tibb) |
| Commencement | The First Schedule came into force with effect from **1 February 1969** (reported consistently in the literature; verify against the appointing notification) |
| Amendment | Amended by gazette notifications over the years — e.g. **G.S.R. 735(E), 28 August 1987** added entries including **Ayurvedic Formulary of India (Part I)** (entry 54-A); the Ministry of AYUSH continues to notify additions |
| Size | Roughly fifty-plus Ayurvedic titles are listed, with Siddha and Unani titles following in their own parts; the current text governs |
| Administered by | CDSCO / Directorate General of Health Services (Drugs Controller General of India) and State Drugs Control Departments; the Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board advises on matters referred to it |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** the First Schedule is where "traditional" becomes "legal". A recipe described in a listed book is a classical formulation; the same ingredients combined differently are a patent or proprietary medicine; and a product that is not manufactured in accordance with a listed book's formulae is not an "Ayurvedic drug" under the Act at all — it is regulated through the proprietary-medicine provisions instead.

## 2. The definitions that hang off the Schedule

As rendered on the CDSCO "Traditional Drugs" page (condensed statutory text — read the consolidated Act for the exact wording):

**"Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug"** — includes all medicines intended for internal or external use for or in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease or disorder in human beings or animals, and **manufactured exclusively in accordance with the formulae described in the authoritative books of the Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Tibb systems of medicine, specified in the First Schedule**.

**"Patent or proprietary medicine"** — in relation to the Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani Tibb systems, all **formulations containing only such ingredients as are mentioned in the formulae described in the authoritative books** specified in the First Schedule, but does **not** include a medicine administered by the parenteral route, and does not include a formulation included in the authoritative books themselves.

**Other role-players created by the same provisions:**

| Concept | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| "The Board" — Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani Drugs Technical Advisory Board | Constituted under section 33C |
| Government Analyst for ASU drugs | Appointed under section 33F |
| Inspector for ASU drugs | Appointed under section 33G |
| Application of the Act's Chapters to ASU drugs | The Act contains a provision limiting the application of certain provisions to Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drugs (with modifications) — described here by subject; check the current wording |

## 3. Classical versus patent or proprietary — the operative distinction

| | Classical formulation | Patent / proprietary medicine |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | **Described** in a book listed in the First Schedule | Not described there — a **new combination** of ingredients that themselves appear in listed formulae |
| Legal character | "Ayurvedic drug" manufactured exclusively per the authoritative book | Patent or proprietary medicine under the Act |
| Label | True list of ingredients, with the common names of the drugs as in the authoritative book; classical identity | True formula or list of ingredients as required for patent/proprietary medicines; brand name; the statutory labelling set |
| Licence | Manufacturing licence for ASU drugs (Form 24-D route under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules) | Same licensing route, but the **label and the claims** differ |
| Evidence of standard | Ayurvedic Formulary of India (formulation) + Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (drug standards) | Same standards; specification must still be justified |
| Typical risk | Being "classical" does **not** immunise the product — claims, advertising and safety duties all still apply | Higher documentation burden (true formula, justification, and the claims boundary) |

**Note on wording:** the exact statutory definition of "patent or proprietary medicine" is longer than the CDSCO rendering above and has been the subject of litigation. Do not quote the definition from this summary; quote it from the consolidated Act.

## 4. What the First Schedule lists

The Ayurveda part of the Schedule is an alphabetical list of classical works. Representative titles (illustrative, not exhaustive — the current Schedule governs):

| Representative entries | Type of text |
|---|---|
| Charaka Samhita; Sushruta Samhita; Ashtanga Hridaya; Ashtanga Samgraha | The foundational clinical and surgical treatises |
| Bhava Prakasha; Nighantu Ratnakara; Brihat Nighantu Ratnakara | Nighantus (materia medica / lexica) |
| Bhaishajya Ratnavali; Chakra Datta; Sharangadhara Samhita; Gada Nigraha; Kupi Pakva Rasayana; Yoga Ratnakara; Yoga Tarangini; Yoga Chintamani; Ayurveda Prakasha; Ayurveda Kalpadruma; Arogya Kalpadruma; Arya Bhishak; Vaidya Chintamani; Vaidyaka Shabda Sindu; Vaidyaka Chikitsa Sara; Vaidya Jiwan; Bharat Bhaishajya Ratnakara; Sarvaroga Chikitsa Ratnam; Yogaratnasangraha; Abhinavachintamani | Formulation and clinical texts |
| Rasa Ratna Samuchaya; Rasa Tarangini; Rasa Yoga Ratnakara; Rasa Yoga Samgraha; Rasendra Sara Samgraha; Rasa Pradipika; Rasa Raja Sundara; Rasa Chandanshu; Rasatantra Sara Siddha Prayoga Samgraha; Rasa Yoga Sagra | Rasa Shastra (herbo-mineral and metallic) texts |
| **Ayurvedic Formulary of India (Part I)** — entry 54-A, added by G.S.R. 735(E), 28 August 1987 | Modern official formulary placed on the Schedule |
| Ayurveda Sara Sangraha — entry 54-B | Later addition |

The Schedule's **Siddha** part and **Unani Tibb** part follow (the Unani heading was substituted by Act 68 of 1982 with effect from 1 February 1983, as reported).

**Important:** the list is **live**. Additions are made by notification, and Ministry of AYUSH proposals to expand it are periodically announced. Always read the current Schedule and its amendment notifications rather than a reproduced copy.

## 5. What follows from the Schedule in the Rules

The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 deal with ASU drugs principally in:

| Part / Rule area | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Part XVI** | Manufacture for sale of Ayurvedic (including Siddha) or Unani drugs — licensing | 
| **Part XVII** | Labelling, packing and limit of alcohol in Ayurvedic (including Siddha) or Unani drugs |
| **Part XVIII** | Government Analysts and Inspectors for Ayurvedic (including Siddha) or Unani drugs |
| Rules 151-160 area | AYUSH manufacturing provisions — licence forms and fees (reported: **Form 24-D** for manufacture for sale, with a loan-licence form alongside; five-year term provisions; Schedule T / Schedule TA) |
| Rules 161-161B area | AYUSH labelling — true list of ingredients with the **botanical name and the part used**, the name of the drug as in the authoritative book, "Ayurvedic/Siddha/Unani" designation, caution wording for **Schedule E-1** substances in English and Hindi, batch and manufacturing details, export marking, and **shelf-life/expiry provisions that differ by dosage form** (some classical categories are treated differently from modern tablets/syrups) |
| Rule 162-164 area | Government Analysts for AYUSH drugs, sample testing, and the AYUSH-qualified licensing cadre |

**Schedule T** — Good Manufacturing Practice for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs — is the GMP standard for this sector, applied through the manufacturing-licence provisions (reported under Rule 157; verify the current rule number and the Schedule's latest amendment, including Schedule TA).

**Schedule E-1** — the list of substances requiring caution — is separate from Schedule E of the FSS (Ayurveda Aahar) Regulations, 2022 (which is a logo). Do not confuse them.

## 7. Interfaces with other regimes

| Regime | Interface |
|---|---|
| **FSS (Ayurveda Aahara) Regulations, 2022** | Food products prepared per Schedule A recipes sit under FSSAI, not here. The dividing line: no disease claims, no Schedule E-1 herbs, no metals/bhasma/pishti, no parenteral use |
| **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** | Advertising restrictions bite on ASU drugs regardless of their classical status |
| **Biological Diversity Act, 2002 / Rules, 2024** | Sourcing botanicals needs access approvals and, for cultivated medicinal plants, a BMC certificate of origin |
| **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** | Where a product is a "new drug" or makes new claims, the clinical-trial regime may engage — check with the licensing authority |
| **Patents Act, 1970** | A classical text in the First Schedule is prior art for novelty and inventive step (see `section-3j-3p-patenting-bar-tkdl-guidance.md`) |
| **Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules** | Net quantity, declaration and other pack particulars on retail packs |

## 8. Common failure modes

1. **Citing a text that is not on the Schedule** as authority for a "classical" claim.
2. **Reproducing an outdated Schedule** — the list has been amended repeatedly; a 1990s copy is not the current law.
3. **Calling a product classical when the formula is in-house** — if the formula is not described in a listed book, it is a patent/proprietary medicine and must be labelled accordingly.
4. **Botanical part omitted from the label** — the part used (root, bark, leaf, fruit, resin) is a required identity element and matters for safety (e.g. where a plant's different parts differ in toxicity).
5. **Schedule E-1 substance used without the prescribed caution** (English and Hindi) and without appropriate controls.
6. **Assuming Schedule T compliance by intent** — the licence requires demonstrated GMP against the Schedule, not a self-declaration.
7. **Wrong shelf-life/expiry treatment** — applying a modern tablet expiry scheme to a classical dosage form, or vice versa.
8. **Parenteral claims or presentation** on a formulation that the proprietary-medicine definition expressly excludes for the parenteral route.
9. **Metallic/bhasma preparations** handled as if they were ordinary herbal products — Rasa Shastra texts on the Schedule do not displace the safety, heavy-metal and labelling controls.
10. **Forgetting the advertising overlay** — a compliant classical label can still be ruined by the advertisement.

## 9. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is the **formula described** in a book listed in the current First Schedule? Record the book, chapter and formula reference.
- [ ] Have I checked the **current Schedule and its amendment notifications**, not a reproduced copy?
- [ ] If the formula is in-house: is the product being labelled and documented as a **patent or proprietary medicine**, with the true formula/list of ingredients?
- [ ] Does the label carry the **common name of each drug as per the authoritative book**, plus the **botanical name and part used**?
- [ ] Does the label carry the **"Ayurvedic/Siddha/Unani"** designation, batch, manufacturing and licence particulars, and the correct **expiry/shelf life for that dosage form**?
- [ ] Does any ingredient appear in **Schedule E-1**? If so, is the prescribed caution displayed and the handling control in place?
- [ ] Is the manufacturing licence on the correct **form and term** (reported Form 24-D route), and is the site compliant with **Schedule T**?
- [ ] Do the **claims** avoid disease cure/treatment promises and stay inside the Drugs and Magic Remedies restrictions?
- [ ] Is the **API/AFI standard** for each drug substance identified and on file (with the spec and test method)?
- [ ] Have **biodiversity clearances** for the sourced botanicals been obtained?
- [ ] If a **foreign market** is targeted, have the exporting country's herbal/traditional-medicine requirements been checked (the First Schedule is an Indian concept)?
- [ ] Is the classification (food / drug / device) decided and consistent across label, licence, claims and dossier?
