# Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahara) Regulations, 2022

## 1. Document overview

The Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahara) Regulations, 2022 place food products prepared under classical Ayurvedic recipes inside the food law system, with their own definition, permitted categories, labelling rules and claims boundary. They are made under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Short title | Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahara) Regulations, 2022 |
| Notifying authority | Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) |
| Date of issue | Reported as **5 May 2022**; gazette notification published **9 May 2022** (Gazette of India notification series) |
| File number | F. No. Stds/SP-05/A-1.Y(01) (as printed on the notification text circulated by compliance trackers) |
| Parent law | Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 |
| Key schedules | **Schedule A** — authoritative books of Ayurveda; **Schedule B** — categories and requirements; **Schedule C** — additives; **Schedule D** — standards (microbiological/contaminant); **Schedule E** — logo |
| Subject | Definition of Ayurveda Aahara, permitted formulations, restrictions, labelling, claims, expert-committee route |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** this is the dividing line between a *food* and a *drug* in India for Ayurvedic products. A chyawanprash-style spread, a laddu, a herbal tea or a recipe-derived drink sits here; a product that treats, cures or prevents disease, uses metals/bhasma, or uses herbs listed in Schedule E-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act sits on the drug side instead.

## 2. Definition of "Ayurveda Aahara"

As circulated in the regulations:

> "Ayurveda Aahara" means a food prepared in accordance with the recipes or ingredients or processes as per method described in the authoritative books of Ayurveda listed under **Schedule A** of these regulations, including products which have other botanical ingredients in accordance with the concept of Ayurveda Aahara, but **does not include** Ayurvedic drugs or proprietary Ayurvedic medicines and medicinal products, cosmetics, narcotic or psychotropic substances, herbs listed under **Schedule E-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945, metals based Ayurvedic drugs or medicines, **bhasma or pishti**, and any other ingredients notified by the Authority from time to time.

Elements to retrieve:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recipe / ingredient / process | The preparation must trace to a described method, not merely to an ingredient list |
| Schedule A books | The classical texts the recipe must come from (the schedule names the authoritative Ayurvedic texts) |
| Other botanical ingredients | Permitted where consistent with the concept of Ayurveda Aahara |
| Carve-outs | Drugs, proprietary Ayurvedic medicines, medicinal products, cosmetics, narcotic/psychotropic substances, Schedule E-1 herbs, metals-based medicines, bhasma/pishti, and anything else notified |

**Drafting note:** Schedule E-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (poisonous/serious-alarm substances) and Schedule E of these Regulations (the logo) are different schedules with similar names — a common source of labelling errors.

## 3. Core obligations

1. **Formulate to Schedule B.** Food Business Operators shall formulate Ayurveda Aahara in accordance with the categories and requirements specified in **Schedule B**. FSSAI has issued, under Note (1) of Schedule B, a definitive list of products under a category (reported as Category A) drawn from the Schedule A texts — check the current list before positioning a product.
2. **Infant bar.** No person shall manufacture or sell Ayurveda Aahara intended for administration to **infants up to the age of twenty-four months**.
3. **Manufacturing standard.** The FBO shall manufacture Ayurveda Aahara in accordance with **Schedule 4 of the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011**.
4. **No added vitamins, minerals or amino acids.** Provided that **natural vitamins and minerals** already present in the Ayurveda Aahara may be declared on the label. (Fortification of an Ayurveda Aahara product is therefore not the route; the natural nutrient content may be declared.)
5. **Purity criteria.** The FBO **may** adopt purity criteria for ingredients as per the Food Safety and Standards Regulations, or as generally accepted by pharmacopoeias — namely the **Indian Pharmacopoeia, Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India, British Pharmacopoeia, United States Pharmacopoeia** — or relevant **BIS** specifications, or the **Quality Standards of Indian Medicinal Plants published by the ICMR**. The FBO shall provide information on the purity criteria adopted **at the time of licensing and for any subsequent changes**.
6. **Compliance condition of sale.** No person shall manufacture, pack, sell, offer for sale, market or otherwise distribute or import Ayurveda Aahara unless the product complies with these Regulations.
7. **Prior approval where Schedule B requires it.** Where prior approval is needed under Schedule B, it is obtained in accordance with the **Food Safety and Standards (Approval for Non-Specific Food and Food Ingredients) Regulations, 2017**.
8. **Expert Committee.** FSSAI constitutes an **Expert Committee under the Ministry of Ayush**, with relevant experts including FSSAI representatives, which gives recommendations on the approval of claims and products specified in the Regulations, and is also empowered to address concerns about registration, licensing, certification, laboratory accreditation, testing or quality issues relating to Ayurveda Aahara.
9. **Existing products.** An FBO shall inform the licensing authority **in writing** of existing duly licensed food products to be assigned as Ayurveda Aahara; the Licensing Authority shall permit the same, with applicable modifications including labelling, **within thirty days** from the date of the recommendation received from the Expert Committee.
10. **Kind of Business.** FSSAI has introduced a new **Kind of Business for Ayurveda Aahara** on the FoSCoS licensing portal — confirm the correct KoB when licensing rather than forcing the product into a generic food category.

## 4. Labelling requirements

Labelling follows the **Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020**, plus the specific requirements below. Every label shall specify the **intended purpose**, the **target consumer group**, the **recommended duration of use**, and:

- the words **"AYURVEDA AAHARA"** printed in the **immediate proximity of the name or brand name** of the product, **and** the **logo specified in Schedule E**, on the **front of pack**;
- an advisory warning **"ONLY FOR DIETARY USE"** prominently written;
- a statement that the Ayurveda Aahara is **not to be used as a substitute for a varied diet**;
- a warning or other precautions to be taken while consuming, **known side effects**, if any, **contraindications**, and **published product or drug interactions**, as applicable;
- a statement that the product is to be **stored out of reach of children**;
- a warning that the product is **for oral consumption only and not for parenteral use**.

## 5. Claims boundary

> The labelling, presentation and advertisement shall **not claim that the Ayurveda Aahara has the property of preventing, treating or curing a human disease**, or refer to such properties.

Consequences for a product owner:

| Claim style | Status under these Regulations |
|---|---|
| "Supports digestion as part of a balanced diet" (structure/function style, no disease named) | Within the food-claims regime — but check the separate FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018 for the actual permitted wording |
| "Helps manage diabetes / cures constipation / treats anaemia" | Disease claim — not permitted on an Ayurveda Aahara label, presentation or advertisement |
| "Clinically proven to treat X" | Not permitted; also engages the Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 if the product is positioned as a remedy |
| Ayurvedic classical indication quoted as a "use" | Risky: quoting a classical *therapeutic* indication for a food reintroduces the disease claim through the back door |
| Nutrient content claims ("rich in natural vitamin C") | Only for vitamins/minerals **naturally present**; no added vitamins, minerals or amino acids |

Related instruments that also apply to the label and the ad: the FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018, the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020, and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules.

## 6. Where the boundary with the drug side lies

| Question | Food side (Ayurveda Aahara) | Drug side (Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940) |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | Food prepared per a Schedule A recipe | Ayurvedic/siddha/unani drug or proprietary medicine |
| Claim | No prevention/treatment/cure of disease | Therapeutic claims are the point of the product |
| Ingredients | Schedule B categories; no Schedule E-1 herbs, no metals, no bhasma/pishti | Schedule E-1 poisons, metals and bhasma permitted under the drug rules with conditions |
| Licence | FSSAI licence/registration (Ayurveda Aahara KoB, Schedule 4 of the 2011 Licensing Regulations) | Manufacturing licence under the D&C Rules — Form 24-D route, Schedule T good manufacturing practice |
| Infants | Not for infants up to 24 months | Governed by the drug rules and paediatric dosing |
| Standard-setting | FSS standards + pharmacopoeial purity criteria voluntarily adopted | Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India and the First Schedule authoritative texts are the standards |

A single brand sometimes runs both: a food-line SKU under FSSAI and a therapeutic SKU under a drug licence. Keep the two dossiers, labels and ad copy strictly separated.

## 7. Interfaces with the rest of the corpus

- **FSS (Health Supplements, Nutraceuticals, Food for Special Dietary Use, Food for Special Medical Purpose, Novel Food) Regulations, 2022** — the other modern food regime; a product is either an Ayurveda Aahara under these Regulations or fits one of those categories, and the labelling and claims differ.
- **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** — advertising restrictions for specified diseases continue to bite even where the product itself is licensed as food.
- **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024** — sourcing the botanicals still requires the biodiversity clearances/certificate of origin.
- **Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India** and **ICMR Quality Standards of Indian Medicinal Plants** — named in the purity-criteria provision; a spec sheet should cite the one actually used.
- **FSS (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations, 2011** — Schedule 4 manufacturing requirements and the licensing route.

## 8. Common compliance failure modes

1. Label carries the brand name but the words "AYURVEDA AAHARA" are not in the *immediate proximity* of it, or the Schedule E logo is missing from the front of pack.
2. The advisory warning is shortened to "dietary use" or "food supplement" — the prescribed words are **"ONLY FOR DIETARY USE"**.
3. Fortification with added vitamins or minerals, which the Regulations prohibit (only naturally present vitamins/minerals may be declared).
4. Quoting a classical therapeutic indication on pack, which converts a compliant food into a disease-claim case.
5. Selling to infant/baby segments, or making a product suitable for infants under 24 months.
6. No documented purity criteria filed at licensing, or criteria changed without informing the licensing authority.
7. Product made under a recipe that is not in a Schedule A book (or not in FSSAI's Schedule B category list) but marketed as Ayurveda Aahara.
8. Metals, bhasma/pishti or Schedule E-1 herbs in a product still labelled and licensed as food.
9. Licence taken under a generic food category instead of the Ayurveda Aahara Kind of Business.
10. Claims carried over from Ayurvedic marketing copy without review against the Advertising and Claims Regulations.

## 9. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Does the recipe trace to an **authoritative book listed in Schedule A**? Record the text, chapter and recipe reference in the product passport.
- [ ] Is the product in a **Schedule B category**, and is it within FSSAI's published list under Note (1)?
- [ ] Is any ingredient a **Schedule E-1 herb, a metal, a bhasma/pishti, a narcotic/psychotropic substance**? If yes, this is not an Ayurveda Aahara.
- [ ] Are there **added vitamins, minerals or amino acids**? If yes, reformulate or re-classify.
- [ ] Which **purity criteria** did I adopt (IP / API / BP / USP / BIS / ICMR), and is it on file with the licensing authority?
- [ ] Is the licence on the correct **Kind of Business** on FoSCoS, and does the plant meet **Schedule 4** of the 2011 Licensing Regulations?
- [ ] Does the front of pack carry **"AYURVEDA AAHARA" next to the name/brand** plus the **Schedule E logo**?
- [ ] Is the **"ONLY FOR DIETARY USE"** advisory present and prominent, along with the varied-diet statement, side-effect/interaction warnings, keep-out-of-reach-of-children statement and oral-only/not-parenteral warning?
- [ ] Do the label, presentation and advertisement contain **no prevention/treatment/cure claim** for any human disease?
- [ ] Is the intended purpose, target consumer group and recommended duration of use stated?
- [ ] If converting an existing licensed product to Ayurveda Aahara, has the **written intimation to the licensing authority** been made, and has the **Expert Committee** recommendation route run (30-day permit window)?
- [ ] If prior approval is required under Schedule B, has the **2017 Non-Specific Food Approval** route been used?
- [ ] Is the product **excluded from the infant segment** (up to 24 months)?
- [ ] Have biodiversity, sourcing and export obligations been cleared separately?
