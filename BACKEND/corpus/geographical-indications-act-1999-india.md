# The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999

## 1. Document overview

The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 (Act No. 48 of 1999) provides for the registration and protection of geographical indications relating to goods in India, for the encouragement of producers and for penalties for falsification and false application of GIs. It is read with the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Rules, 2002.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Enactment | Act No. 48 of 1999 (WIPO Lex records it as amended up to an amendment Act of 2023 — check the current consolidated text) |
| Subordinate legislation | GI (Registration and Protection) Rules, 2002 (as amended) |
| Registry | The GI Registry (head office at **Chennai**) |
| Registrar | The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks acts as Registrar of GIs (Section 3) |
| Structure | 9 chapters, ~87 sections (arrangement of sections in the official text governs) |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** GI protects *origin* — a product name tied to a place (e.g. Darjeeling Tea, Kashmir Saffron, Tirupati Laddu). It does not protect a formulation, a brand, or a process. Ayurvedic raw drugs with a regional reputation and food/AGRI products derived from Ayurvedic traditions can be GIs; a general "Ayurveda" or brand word can never be one.

## 2. Core definition — Section 2(1)(e)

**"Geographical indication", in relation to goods, means an indication which identifies such goods as agricultural goods, natural goods or manufactured goods as originating, or manufactured in the territory of a country, or a region or locality in that territory, where a given quality, reputation or other characteristic of such goods is essentially attributable to its geographical origin.**

Elements to retrieve and explain:

1. **Indication** — any name, sign or combination (words, maps, symbols) that identifies origin.
2. **Goods** — agricultural goods, natural goods **or manufactured goods** (so processed/medicinal products qualify).
3. **Quality, reputation or other characteristic** — *one* of these is enough; it must be **essentially attributable** to the geographical origin.
4. **Territory / region / locality** — can be as narrow as a village or as wide as a country.

Related definitions in section 2 (by subject): "goods" (any substance manufactured, produced, mined or hand-made, including handicrafts and foodstuffs); "producer" (a person engaged in manufacturing, mining, farming, extracting, or dealing in the goods); "approved article" (a document, notice, label, wrapper, packet, etc.); "geographical indication" as above.

## 3. Why a GI differs from other IP rights

| Feature | Trade mark | GI | Patent | Copyright | Plant variety |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protects | Brand / source identity of one owner | Collective origin link of many producers in a place | Invention (product/process) | Original expression | New plant variety |
| Owned by | One proprietor | Association of producers / authorised users (collective) | Inventor/applicant | Author/owner | Breeder/registrant |
| Assignable | Yes (with goodwill) | **No — assignment/transfer is barred (Section 24)** | Yes | Yes | Subject to rules |
| Duration | 10 years, renewable | 10 years, renewable | 20 years from filing | Life + 60 years | 15 years (18 for trees/vines) |
| Requires use in the place | No | Yes — genuine link to origin | No | No | DUS criteria |

**Key doctrine:** a GI right belongs to the eligible producers collectively; it is a public-law-style right in the community's reputation, not a freely tradable private asset.

## 4. Who may apply — Section 11

**Section 11 (Application for registration):** an application may be made by:

- any **association of persons or producers**, or
- any **organisation** established by or under law, or
- any **authority** established by the Central Government or a State Government, **acting in the interest of the producers of the goods concerned**,

and not by an individual trader alone. The applicant must also be an eligible applicant as defined (essentially, acting in the interest of producers).

Practical points:

- A brand owner, distributor or foreign company cannot register a GI *on behalf of* foreign producers for Indian goods; the applicant must represent the local producers' interest.
- All producers in the area who meet the common characteristics should be able to join — a GI is a collective good.
- Applications may also claim **authorised users** (Section 17): individual producers who actually produce goods conforming to the GI may be registered as authorised users and then have enforcement rights.

## 5. The Register, Registry and Registrar

- **Section 3 — Registrar of Geographical Indications:** the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks is the Registrar.
- **Section 5 — GI Registry and officers:** the Registry with its head office at **Chennai**, with Registrars, Deputy Registrars, Assistant Registrars and examiners.
- **Sections 6 and 7 — Register of GIs:** the Register is divided into **Part A** (GI as applied for/registered?) and **Part B** as provided in section 7; entries record the particulars of each registered GI.
- **Section 8 — Registration to be in respect of particular goods and area:** a GI is registered for a specified product and a specified territory — not for "all goods".
- **Section 9 — Prohibition of registration of certain geographical indications** (what is *not* registrable — see section 9 of this document).
- **Section 10 — Homonymous GIs:** two different places with the same name — the Registry handles homonym situations (with an obligation not to deceive the public).

## 6. What is NOT registrable (Section 9) and related bars

**Section 9 (Prohibition of registration of certain GIs)** — a GI is not to be registered where, in substance:

1. it is likely to deceive or cause confusion as to the true origin, quality or other characteristics of the goods (including where the indication is "misleading" as to origin);
2. it is contrary to any law for the time being in force or opposed to public order or morality;
3. it is not, or ceases to be, a geographical indication for the goods (i.e. it does not identify origin anymore);
4. it is customary in the common language of India as the name of a good / the indication is a "customary" term for the good in the trade (so everyday names and generic terms cannot be monopolised);
5. it is a geographical indication for goods not originating in the territory indicated, or it is a translation or transliteration of such an indication (so false-origin claims are barred);
6. it has ceased to identify the place of origin (e.g. the goods are no longer made in that place).

**Section 25 — Prohibition of registration of geographical indication as trade mark:** a GI may not be registered as a trade mark (and deceptively similar marks for the same goods are barred), which prevents one party privatising a regional name.

**Section 26 — Protection to certain trade marks:** pre-existing trade marks that would deceive the public as to origin are handled specially (protected where rights already existed).

**Practical test for Ayurveda:** the sign must (a) name a real place, (b) for goods made/produced there, (c) with a quality/reputation/characteristic essentially attributable to that origin. "Chyawanprash" is a formulation name, not a place. "Assam Green Tea" style indicators require proof of origin-linkage.

## 7. Procedure for and duration of registration (Sections 11-19)

Chapter III of the Act sets the procedure (section numbers as per the official arrangement of sections):

| Step | Section (as per official arrangement) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Application | 11 | Association/organisation applies in the prescribed form with statement of characteristics, class of goods, producers' particulars, map/area, specimens (content prescribed by the Rules) |
| Withdrawal of acceptance | 12 | Mechanism to withdraw an accepted application |
| Advertisement of application | 13 | The application is advertised in the GI Journal so the public and interested parties can see it |
| Opposition | 14 | Any person interested may give notice of opposition within the prescribed period; hearing and decision follow |
| Correction and amendment | 15 | Amendments/corrections of the application |
| **Registration** | 16 | On acceptance (after opposition), the GI is registered and a certificate issued |
| **Authorised users** | 17 | Producers of the goods may apply to be registered as authorised users (separate application/fee) |
| **Duration, renewal, removal, restoration** | 18 | Registration runs for **ten years from the date of registration**, renewable for further periods of **ten years** on payment of fee; lapse for non-renewal; restoration mechanism |
| Effect of removal | 19 | Consequences of removal for failure to pay renewal fee |

**Duration (Section 18):** **10 years** from the date of registration, **renewable** for successive 10-year periods (a common but incorrect secondary citation is "Section 13" for duration — in the official arrangement, section 13 is "Advertisement of application"; verify numbering in the official text).

## 8. Effect of registration (Chapter IV)

- **Section 20 — No action for infringement of unregistered GI:** remedies for GIs depend on registration; a passing-off style action for an unregistered GI is limited (subject to general law).
- **Section 21 — Rights conferred by registration:** registration confers the exclusive right to use the GI and the right to obtain relief in respect of its infringement.
- **Section 22 — Infringement of registered geographical indication:** using the GI (or a deceptively similar indication) on goods of the same class, or doing any act likely to cause injury to the registered holder, or using a container/pack likely to lead the public to believe the goods originate from the protected place, constitutes infringement.
- **Section 23 — Registration as prima facie evidence of validity.**
- **Section 24 — Prohibition of assignment or transmission:** a registered GI **cannot be assigned, transmitted, franchised or otherwise transferred** — it vests in the eligible producers.
- **Section 66 — Suit for infringement to be instituted before a District Court** (jurisdiction floor).
- **Section 67 — Relief in suit for infringement or passing off:** injunction, damages or account of profits, delivery up/destruction as applicable.

**Exceptions / fair use:** use of a geographical indication otherwise than as an indication of origin (e.g. descriptive use, indication of kind/quality, own name in fair practice) is treated by the Act's provisions on no-offence/limitations (Chapter VIII contains "No offence in certain cases"). Always read those exceptions before asserting infringement.

## 10. Appeals, rectification and ancillary provisions

- **Chapter VI (Sections 27-30):** power to cancel/vary registration and rectify the register, correction of register, alteration of registered GIs, adaptation of entries to classification changes.
- **Chapter VII (Sections 31-36):** appeals and related matters formerly to the Appellate Board. **Note:** the Intellectual Property Appellate Board was abolished by the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021, and appellate functions were transferred to the High Courts — verify the current appellate route before advising.
- **Section 71 — Power to require goods to show indication of origin:** the Registrar/authorised persons may require goods sold in a manner suggesting a false origin to be shown with their true origin.
- **Section 84 — Convention-country applicants:** applicants from convention countries can use the international arrangement machinery.
- **Section 87 — Power to make rules** (the basis of the GI Rules, 2002).

## 11. The GI Rules, 2002

The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Rules, 2002 prescribe:

- the **application form and its contents** (statement of characteristics, class of goods, name/address of applicant and producers, description of the area, map/plan, specimen of the mark, power of attorney);
- **fees** (First Schedule);
- the **advertisement and opposition procedure**, time limits and hearing practice;
- the **Journal** in which applications are advertised;
- forms for authorised users, renewal, rectification and corrections.

**Retrieval note:** always pair the Act with the current Rules for time limits and forms; do not state an opposition period without checking the Rules.

## 12. Relevance to Ayurveda, AYUSH and food products

1. **Origin-linked raw drugs and spices:** regional reputations (e.g. Kashmir Saffron, Darjeeling Tea, regional spices and jaggery-style products) are classic GI candidates; a producer association can seek registration for quality/reputation attributable to the place.
2. **GI is not for formulations:** classical Ayurvedic formulations (Ashwagandha churna, Triphala) are traditional knowledge, not geographical indications. Their protection paths are different (trademark for the brand, patent only if novel/non-traditional under section 3(p), GI only if a *place* is genuinely the source of a distinct product).
3. **GI does not block honest descriptive origin use** by others who genuinely produce in the area (subject to meeting the standard).
4. **No assignment:** a company cannot buy a GI — it can only become an authorised user if it genuinely produces the goods in the area.
5. **Trade mark conflicts:** a trade mark that is identical/deceptively similar to a registered GI for the same goods is barred from registration (Section 25) — relevant when naming Ayurvedic products after places (e.g. "Darjeeling", "Kashmiri").
6. **Export angle:** GIs matter for market access and premium positioning of Indian herbal/agri produce abroad; foreign protection requires separate action in each country (India's GI law has no automatic extraterritorial effect).

## 13. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is the sign a **place name (or other indication of origin)** rather than a brand word? If it is a brand, the route is trade mark, not GI.
- [ ] Is the product actually produced in that place, and can the **quality/reputation/characteristic** be attributed to the origin (evidence: soil/climate, traditional know-how, producer records)?
- [ ] Is the applicant an **association/organisation acting in the interest of producers** (Section 11)?
- [ ] Is the name **customary in the common language** for the goods (Section 9 bar) or likely to deceive the public?
- [ ] Has a search of the GI Register/GI Journal been done (and of the Trade Marks Register, given Sections 25-26)?
- [ ] Renewal diary: **10-year cycle** from the date of registration (Section 18).
- [ ] If a member producer wants to use the GI — is it registered as an **authorised user** (Section 17)?
- [ ] For enforcement — is there a **registered** GI (Section 20 limits unregistered claims) and is suit filed in the **District Court** (Section 66)?
- [ ] Penalty figures and current section numbering verified against the official text (this document omits fine/imprisonment amounts deliberately).
