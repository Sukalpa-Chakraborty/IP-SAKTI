# India's First National Report on the Implementation of the Nagoya Protocol (NR1) – Summary

## 1. Document Overview

This document is a summary/infographic of **India's First National Report (NR1) on the Implementation of the Nagoya Protocol**. It presents progress made in implementing Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) through legal and institutional measures, regulatory procedures, benefit-sharing mechanisms, compliance systems, capacity building, and international cooperation.

- **Reporting Period:** 1 November 2017 – 31 December 2025
- **Submitted to the CBD:** 27 February 2026
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), Government of India
- **Authority:** National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)

## 2. What is ABS?

**Access & Benefit Sharing (ABS)** ensures that biological/genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge are accessed and utilised in accordance with applicable domestic legal requirements, with benefits arising from their utilisation shared fairly and equitably.

### ABS in Simple Terms

1. **Access** – Responsible and regulated access
2. **Utilisation** – Research • Innovation • Commercial use
3. **Benefit Sharing** – Monetary + Non-monetary benefits
4. **Conservation & Community Benefits**

> ABS links the utilisation of biological resources with equity, conservation, sustainable use and community welfare.

## 3. Why is ABS Important for India?

- India is one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries.
- Harbours nearly **8% of global species** within about **2.4% of the world's land area**.
- Rich agro-biodiversity and extensive traditional knowledge systems.
- In India, communities play a crucial role in conservation of biodiversity.
- Their efforts need to be incentivized.
- ABS is one such incentive mechanism.

## 4. Nagoya Protocol & NR1

### The Nagoya Protocol

- Provides a transparent international framework for the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the utilisation of genetic resources.
- **Adopted:** 29 October 2010
- **Entered into force:** 12 October 2014

### Key Elements

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** | Access with the free, prior informed consent of the local communities in accordance with applicable requirements. |
| **Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT)** | Agreed terms & conditions for access and benefit sharing. |
| **Compliance** | Measures to ensure compliance with provider-country requirements. |
| **Monitoring & Traceability** | Checkpoints and the ABS Clearing-House support transparency and monitoring. |

### India & the Nagoya Protocol

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1993** | India signed the CBD |
| **1994** | India ratified the CBD |
| **11 May 2011** | India signed the Nagoya Protocol |
| **9 October 2012** | India ratified the Nagoya Protocol |
| **12 October 2014** | Nagoya Protocol entered into force |

India was among the early countries to ratify the Protocol and became a party.

### Why is NR1 Required?

Parties are required to monitor implementation and periodically report on measures taken to operationalise the Protocol.

### India's Reporting Journey

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2017** | Interim National Report |
| **2017–2025** | First complete reporting cycle |
| **27 Feb 2026** | India submitted NR1 |

NR1 provides a comprehensive account of India's legislative, administrative and institutional measures for implementing ABS.

## 5. India's Legal & Institutional Framework

### Building the ABS Framework

- **The Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (Amended in 2023)
- **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024**
- **ABS Regulations, 2025**

Together, these provide the legal and procedural framework for regulating access and ensuring fair and equitable benefit sharing.

### Three-Tier Institutional System

| Level | Body |
|---|---|
| **Top** | MoEFCC |
| **Middle** | NBA |
| **State/UT** | State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs) / UTBCs |
| **Local** | Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs) – **2.76 LAKH+ BMCs** |

BMCs support local biodiversity governance, preparation of People's Biodiversity Registers and benefit-sharing processes.

### ABS Clearing-House

India is publishing the relevant information in the ABS Clearing-House portal of the CBD as required under **Article 17** of the Protocol, for transparency, information exchange and compliance monitoring.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Legislative, administrative and policy measures published | **32** |
| IRCCs generated by India | **3556** |
| India issued the world's first IRCC in 2014 | |

## 10. RAG Q&A Extraction

### Q: What is ABS?
**A:** Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) ensures that biological/genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge are accessed and utilised in accordance with applicable domestic legal requirements, with benefits shared fairly and equitably.

### Q: What is the Nagoya Protocol?
**A:** An international framework for fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the utilisation of genetic resources. Adopted 29 October 2010, entered into force 12 October 2014.

### Q: When did India ratify the Nagoya Protocol?
**A:** 9 October 2012.

### Q: What is NR1?
**A:** India's First National Report on the Implementation of the Nagoya Protocol, submitted to the CBD on 27 February 2026, covering the reporting period 1 November 2017 – 31 December 2025.

### Q: How many ABS approvals have been granted?
**A:** 12,830 total: 5,913 by NBA (Section 3(2) entities) and 6,917 by SBBs/UTBCs (Section 7 entities).

### Q: What is the total ABS amount accrued and disbursed?
**A:** ₹216.31 crore accrued through NBA approvals; ₹139.69 crore disbursed to benefit claimants. Since inception: ₹263 crore realised and ₹145 crore distributed.

### Q: What is the three-tier institutional system for ABS in India?
**A:** MoEFCC (top), NBA (middle), SBBs/UTBCs (state/UT), and BMCs (local) with 2.76 lakh+ BMCs.

### Q: What are the key elements of the Nagoya Protocol?
**A:** Prior Informed Consent (PIC), Mutually Agreed Terms (MAT), Compliance, Monitoring & Traceability.

### Q: What is the ABS Clearing-House?
**A:** A portal under Article 17 of the Nagoya Protocol for publishing legislative, administrative and policy measures. India has published 32 measures and generated 3556 IRCCs.

### Q: What are the externally supported projects for ABS in India?
**A:** ASEAN-India Cooperation Project, UNEP-GEF-MoEFCC ABS Project, GIZ-ABS Partnership Project, India–Norway Technical & Institutional Cooperation, Indo-German Technical Cooperation.

### Q: How many individuals have been trained in ABS?
**A:** 2,56,393 individuals through 3,723 programmes/workshops.
