# Memorandum of Understanding in the Field of Industrial Property Cooperation between India and Singapore

## 1. Document Overview

This is a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between the **Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion (DIPP)**, Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Government of India, and the **Intellectual Property Office of Singapore (IPOS)**, Ministry of Law, Government of Singapore. The MoU aims to establish a wide-ranging and flexible mechanism for developing and furthering cooperation activities in the industrial property field and related information technology services.

Signed in Delhi on **4th October 2016**.

## 2. Key Provisions

### Paragraph I: Objective
- Establish a wide-ranging and flexible mechanism for cooperation in industrial property and related IT services.

### Paragraph II: Specific Areas of Cooperation
Cooperation may be carried out through:
- Exchange of best practices, experiences, and knowledge on IP awareness among the public, businesses, and educational institutions.
- Exchange of experts.
- Exchange and dissemination of best practices on IP with industry, universities, R&D organisations, and SMEs through programs and events.
- Cooperation in development of automation, modernization projects, new documentation and information systems, and IP management procedures.
- Collaboration in IP-related training for local IP and business communities.
- Any other modality mutually agreed.

The MoU does not oblige the Parties to develop projects in all areas.

### Paragraph III: Competence
- Parties will comply with their respective jurisdictions, institutional directives, and applicable national laws.

### Paragraph IV: Joint Coordination Committee
- A Joint Coordination Committee will be set up to draw up work projects and coordinate implementation.
- Each project will include forecasts, specifications, management, resource assignment, time schedule, etc.

### Paragraph V: Follow-up Mechanism
- Parties will meet as often as agreed to evaluate matters arising from implementation after receiving the Joint Coordination Committee’s report.

### Paragraph VI: Information, Material and Protected Equipment
- Parties will freely exchange available information, except where national legislation restricts.
- Restricted information will not be transferred to a third party without prior written consent.

### Paragraph VII: Financing
- Parties will finance cooperation activities from their respective budgets, subject to availability and national legislation.
- Expenses will be funded as mutually agreed, except when alternative funding mechanisms exist.
- For personnel exchange, terms and conditions will be established in writing.

### Paragraph VIII: Labour Relation
- Assigned personnel remain under the direction and authority of their own institution; no employment relationship is created with the other Party.
- Parties will facilitate movement and stay of assigned personnel.
- Assigned personnel are subject to host country’s immigration, customs, tax, health, and national security provisions.

### Paragraph IX: Dispute Settlement
- Disputes will be settled by mutual agreement in a friendly manner.
- The MoU does not create legally binding obligations or rights under domestic or international law.

### Paragraph X: Final Provisions
- Cooperation continues for four years, automatically extended for further four-year periods unless terminated with 90 days’ written notice.
- MoU may be amended by mutual agreement through exchange of written communications.
- Either Party may terminate at any time with 30 days’ written notice.
- Early termination does not affect completion of any cooperation activity already formalized.

## 4. RAG Q&A Extraction

### Q: What is the objective of this MoU?
**A:** To establish a wide-ranging and flexible mechanism for developing and furthering cooperation activities between India and Singapore in the industrial property field and related information technology services.

### Q: What are the specific areas of cooperation?
**A:** Exchange of best practices on IP awareness, exchange of experts, dissemination of best practices to industry and SMEs, cooperation in automation and modernization, IP-related training, and any other mutually agreed modality.

### Q: How long does the MoU last?
**A:** Four years initially, automatically extended for further four-year periods unless terminated with 90 days’ notice.

### Q: Does the MoU create legally binding obligations?
**A:** No, it explicitly states it does not create legally binding obligations or corresponding rights under domestic or international law.

### Q: Who are the signatories?
**A:** Ramesh Abhishek for India and Lim Thuan Kuan for Singapore.
