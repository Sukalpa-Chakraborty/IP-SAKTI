# Deposition of Microorganisms in Foreign Repositories for Claim of Novel Species

## Source Metadata

- **Document topic:** Deposition of microorganisms in foreign repositories for claiming publication of novel species
- **Authority:** National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), India
- **Legal framework referenced:** Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (BD Act, 2002)
- **International nomenclature framework:** International Bacteriological Code of Nomenclature
- **Authority decisions referenced:** 36th Authority Meeting (6 January 2016), Agenda 36.16.04; 37th Authority Meeting (22 March 2016), Agenda 37.14.02
- **Document language:** English
- **RAG use:** Legal/regulatory procedure and scope-of-applicability retrieval

## 1. Background

Indian researchers/scientists may be required to deposit microorganisms in foreign repositories when claiming a novel species for publication in journals, as required under the International Bacteriological Code of Nomenclature.

The document states that Rules 27 and 30 of the International Bacteriological Code of Nomenclature require deposition of novel microbial species in at least two internationally recognized culture collections, with one collection in the country of origin and another in a foreign country.

When a new taxon is published, a viable culture of the strain must be deposited in at least two publicly accessible culture collections located in different countries.

## 2. Applicability of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002

### 2.1 Foreign deposition for publication of a novel species

At the **36th Authority Meeting**, under **Agenda Item 36.16.04**, the National Biodiversity Authority decided that deposition of microorganisms by Indian researchers/scientists in repositories in other countries, for claiming their novel species for publication in journals as required by the International Bacteriological Code of Nomenclature, **does not come under the purview of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002**.

### 2.2 Prior intimation to the NBA

Subsequently, at the **37th Authority Meeting**, under **Agenda Item 37.14.02**, the Authority decided that the Indian researcher/scientist **shall provide prior intimation to the NBA in Form-C**.

### 2.3 Access to the deposited Indian biological resource by non-Indians

The document further states that **any non-Indian person or entity accessing the deposited Indian biological resource from the foreign repository should obtain prior approval of the NBA under Section 3 of the Biological Diversity Act, 2002**.

## 3. Regulatory Decision Logic

For RAG retrieval, the source establishes the following sequence:

1. **Indian researcher/scientist deposits a microorganism in a foreign repository** solely to satisfy the International Bacteriological Code of Nomenclature requirements for publication of a novel species.
2. This deposition activity **is outside the purview of the BD Act, 2002**, according to the NBA's 36th Authority Meeting decision.
3. The Indian researcher/scientist **must nevertheless provide prior intimation to the NBA in Form-C**, according to the subsequent 37th Authority Meeting decision.
4. A **non-Indian person/entity accessing the deposited Indian biological resource from the foreign repository requires prior NBA approval under Section 3 of the BD Act, 2002**.
