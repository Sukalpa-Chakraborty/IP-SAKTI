# Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing (2010) — Relevant Provisions

## 1. What this instrument is

The **Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable
Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization to the Convention on
Biological Diversity** was adopted at **Nagoya, Japan, on 29 October 2010** and
entered into force on **12 October 2014** (in accordance with its Article 33).
It is a supplementary agreement to the **Convention on Biological Diversity
(1992)** and binds only Parties to that Convention.

**India is a Party to the Nagoya Protocol** (signed at Nagoya; ratified and
bound from 2014). Confirm the exact date of India's ratification and entry into
force against the UN Treaty Collection depositary record and the CBD list of
Parties at the source_url before citing a date. India's domestic implementing
framework is the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** and the Biological Diversity
Rules (as amended), with the **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)** as the
access/benefit-sharing authority and the **ABS Clearing-House** as the
transparency mechanism.

> The Protocol does **not** create a free-standing private right of access. It
> obliges *States* to take legislative, administrative and policy measures; the
> day-to-day duties fall on users of genetic resources and traditional knowledge
> through national law and contracts.

## 2. Objectives (Article 1)

The objective stated in Article 1 is the **fair and equitable sharing of the
benefits arising from the utilization of genetic resources**, including by
appropriate access to genetic resources and by appropriate transfer of relevant
technologies, taking into account all rights over those resources and to
technologies, and by appropriate funding, **in accordance with mutually agreed
terms**. The Protocol also aims to contribute to the conservation and
sustainable use of biodiversity.

## 4. Scope and relationship with other instruments

- **Article 3 (Scope)** — the Protocol applies to **genetic resources within
  the scope of Article 15 of the CBD** and to benefits arising from their
  utilization, and it **also applies to traditional knowledge associated with
  genetic resources** within the scope of the Convention and to benefits
  arising from its utilization.
- **Article 4 (Relationship with international agreements and instruments)** —
  implementation shall be in a mutually supportive manner with other relevant
  instruments; **paragraph 4** allows a **specialized international access and
  benefit-sharing instrument** that is consistent with the Convention and the
  Protocol to displace the Protocol's PIC/MAT requirements for the specific
  genetic resource covered and for the purpose of that instrument (commonly
  discussed in relation to the WHO Pandemic Influenza Preparedness Framework and
  the ITPGRFA).
- **Article 24 (Non-Parties)** — Parties endeavour to co-operate with
  non-Parties to obtain information on access and MAT and to encourage measures
  ensuring compliance by their nationals and entities.

## 5. Article 5 — Fair and equitable benefit-sharing

Article 5 requires Parties, in accordance with domestic law and subject to
established PIC/MAT procedures, to take legislative, administrative or policy
measures for **fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the
utilization of genetic resources**, including benefits obtained from
**commercial and other utilization**. MAT shall include **the type, form,
timing and frequency of benefits**, and, where appropriate, **monetary and
non-monetary benefits**; benefits shall be shared fairly and equitably with the
provider and, where applicable, with indigenous and local communities holding
relevant traditional knowledge.

**The Annex to the Protocol lists monetary and non-monetary benefits**, for
example: cash payments; revenue/profit sharing; royalties; licence fees; equity
participation; payments for research and development; access to and transfer of
technology; contribution to local economies; training and employment; joint
ownership of relevant IP; scientific co-development; and institutional
capacity-building.

## 6. Article 6 — Access to genetic resources

Article 6 requires legislative, administrative or policy measures to provide
**clear and transparent legal conditions** for access, including:

- a **clear and transparent procedure** for obtaining **prior informed consent
  (PIC)** and for issuing a **permit or its equivalent**, indicating at a
  minimum the scope and purpose of access, the resources covered, any
  restrictions, the date of PIC, and a reference to the MAT;
- **provision of information** on how to apply for PIC;
- decision-making within a **reasonable period of time** and at **no cost** to
  the applicant, unless otherwise agreed;
- issue of a **permit or its equivalent** as evidence of the decision to grant
  PIC, with reasons given where access is denied;
- **mutually agreed terms** between provider and user including **benefit-
  sharing**, **conditions for transfer to third parties**, and procedures for
  subsequent changes to MAT;
- requirements for **PIC from the provider before transfer** of genetic
  resources to third parties, unless otherwise provided;
- requirements to maintain and communicate information on **provenance and
  collection** (Art. 6(3) includes collection data and provenance requirements).

A permit or its equivalent issued under **Article 6(3)(e)** and made available
to the ABS Clearing-House constitutes an **internationally recognized
certificate of compliance** (Article 17(2)).

## 7. Article 7 and Article 12 — Traditional knowledge associated with genetic resources

### 7.1 Article 7 — Access to traditional knowledge (the approval requirement)

> "In accordance with domestic law, each Party shall take measures, as
> appropriate, with the aim of ensuring that traditional knowledge associated
> with genetic resources that is held by indigenous and local communities is
> accessed with the **prior and informed consent or approval and involvement of
> these indigenous and local communities**, and that **mutually agreed terms**
> have been established." — Article 7

This is the operative **TK access/approval** provision. It is
**domestic-law-qualified**: the procedure (consent by a statutory authority,
community-level consent, or both) is set by national law.

### 7.2 Article 12 — Traditional knowledge: customary laws, community protocols, minimum MAT requirements

Article 12 requires Parties, in accordance with domestic law, to take into
consideration indigenous and local communities' **customary laws, community
protocols and procedures**; to establish mechanisms to **inform potential users**
of their obligations; to support the development by communities of **community
protocols**, **minimum requirements for mutually agreed terms** to secure fair
and equitable benefit-sharing from TK, and **model contractual clauses**; and
**not to restrict customary use and exchange** of genetic resources and
associated traditional knowledge within and among communities.

### 7.3 Article 16 — Compliance for traditional knowledge

Article 16 mirrors Article 15 for traditional knowledge: Parties shall take
appropriate, effective and proportionate measures to ensure that TK utilized
within their jurisdiction has been accessed with the required consent and MAT
under the provider Party's law.

## 8. Article 8 — Special considerations; Article 9 — Contribution to conservation

**Article 8** addresses how Parties shall frame their ABS legislation. It
requires them to (a) create conditions promoting research that contributes to
conservation and sustainable use, including **simplified access for
non-commercial research**; (b) pay **due regard to cases of present or
imminent emergencies that threaten or damage human, animal or plant health**
(with possible expedited access, including affordable treatments); and
(c) consider the **importance of genetic resources for food and agriculture and
their special role for food security**.

**Article 9 (Contribution to conservation and sustainable use)** — Parties
encourage users and providers to **direct benefits** arising from utilization
towards conservation and sustainable use of biodiversity and towards the
sustainable development of provider countries.

## 9. Article 10 — Global multilateral benefit-sharing mechanism; Article 11 — Transboundary cooperation

- **Article 10** — Parties shall consider the need for and modalities of a
  **global multilateral benefit-sharing mechanism** for benefits arising from
  utilization of genetic resources and associated TK **in transboundary
  situations or where it is not possible to grant or obtain PIC** (the
  "secondary use" gap; being developed under COP-MOP decisions on digital
  sequence information and the multilateral mechanism).
- **Article 11 (Transboundary Cooperation)** — where genetic resources are
  subject to more than one jurisdiction, or where genetic resources and
  associated TK are of **mutual concern**, Parties shall co-operate to ensure
  that PIC is obtained and MAT established where required, and to facilitate
  benefit-sharing; relevant where a medicinal plant spans borders (for example
  trans-Himalayan species shared between India and neighbouring States).

## 10. Article 13–14 — Institutions: focal points, competent authorities, ABS Clearing-House

- **Article 13** — each Party designates a **national focal point** on ABS
  (information on procedures for obtaining PIC and establishing MAT; liaison
  with the Secretariat) and one or more **competent national authorities**
  (grant access, or issue written evidence that access requirements have been
  met; advise on procedures), and notifies the Secretariat of their contact
  details **no later than entry into force for that Party**.
- **Article 14** — the **Access and Benefit-Sharing Clearing-House** is
  established as part of the clearing-house mechanism under Article 18(3) of
  the CBD. Parties make available: legislative/administrative/policy measures;
  information on the national focal point and competent authority(ies); and
  **permits or their equivalent** issued at the time of access as evidence of
  the decision to grant PIC and the establishment of MAT.

## 11. Article 15–18 — Compliance: user-country duties, checkpoints, certificates

| Article | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Art. 15** | **User-country compliance for genetic resources:** each Party takes appropriate, effective and proportionate measures to ensure that genetic resources **utilized within its jurisdiction** were accessed in accordance with **PIC** and that **MAT have been established**, as required by the provider Party's domestic ABS law; measures to address non-compliance; co-operation in alleged-violation cases. |
| **Art. 16** | The same obligation for **traditional knowledge** associated with genetic resources. |
| **Art. 17(1)** | **Monitoring the utilization of genetic resources:** measures to monitor and enhance transparency, including **designation of one or more checkpoints** — points where relevant information on PIC, source, MAT and utilization is collected; Parties may require users to provide that information at a checkpoint; information (including from certificates of compliance) is provided to national authorities, the provider Party and the ABS Clearing-House; checkpoints must be **effective** and may function at any stage of **research, development, innovation, pre-commercialization or commercialization**; Parties take **appropriate, effective and proportionate measures to address non-compliance**; encouraging MAT reporting obligations. |
| **Art. 17(2)** | A **permit or its equivalent issued under Article 6(3)(e)** and made available to the ABS Clearing-House constitutes an **internationally recognized certificate of compliance**. |
| **Art. 17(3)** | The certificate serves as **evidence** that the resource was accessed in accordance with PIC and that MAT were established. |
| **Art. 17(4)** | Minimum contents when not confidential: issuing authority; date of issuance; the provider; unique identifier; the person/entity to whom PIC was granted; the subject-matter/genetic resource covered. |
| **Art. 18** | **Compliance with mutually agreed terms:** each Party shall take legislative, administrative or policy measures to ensure that users subject to its jurisdiction comply with MAT (whether the MAT arise under domestic or foreign law). |

> **Common mis-citations corrected here:** checkpoints = **Article 17(1)(a)**
> (not Article 13); certificate of compliance = **Article 17(2)–(4)** (not
> Article 12); national focal points/competent authorities = **Article 13** (not
> Article 17); user-country compliance = **Article 15** (not Article 10).

## 12. Articles 19–23 — Implementation support

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 19 | **Model Contractual Clauses** — Parties encourage, develop and make available model clauses, especially for research and for TK associated with genetic resources and community-level MAT. |
| 20 | **Codes of Conduct, Guidelines and Best Practices** — Parties encourage the development of these, with the effective participation of indigenous and local communities. |
| 21 | **Awareness-Raising** — education and public awareness on the importance of genetic resources and associated TK. |
| 22 | **Capacity** — capacity-building and institutional strengthening, particularly in developing countries, including human-resource and institutional capacity for ABS. |
| 23 | **Technology Transfer, Collaboration and Cooperation** — effective participation in research and development activities, and transfer of technology to provider countries on fair and mutually agreed terms, including access to and transfer of technology using genetic resources. |

## 13. Articles 25–31 — Finance and institutions

- **Article 25 (Financial Mechanism and Resources)** — the financial mechanism
  of the CBD applies, and Parties provide new and additional financial resources
  to enable developing-country Parties to meet agreed full incremental costs.
- **Article 26 (COP-MOP)** — the Conference of the Parties to the CBD serving as
  the meeting of the Parties to the Protocol is the supreme body; it reviews
  implementation, adopts guidance, and takes budgetary decisions.
- **Article 27 (Subsidiary Bodies)** — the Protocol makes provision for
  subsidiary bodies as may be established (see COP-MOP decisions establishing
  such arrangements).
- **Article 28 (Secretariat)** — the CBD secretariat serves as secretariat to
  the Protocol; the costs of secretariat services are met by the Parties.
- **Article 29 (Monitoring and Reporting)** — Parties submit national reports on
  measures taken to implement the Protocol and on effectiveness.
- **Article 30 (Procedures and Mechanisms to Promote Compliance)** — Parties
  shall take note of procedures and mechanisms to promote compliance, including
  **consideration of dispute avoidance and settlement** procedures.
- **Article 31 (Assessment and Review)** — the COP-MOP periodically assesses the
  effectiveness of the Protocol in achieving its objectives.

## 14. Final clauses

- **Article 32** — signature (opened at Nagoya, 29 October 2010, and thereafter
  at the UN Headquarters).
- **Article 33** — entry into force on the ninetieth day after deposit of the
  fiftieth instrument of ratification, acceptance, approval or accession (for
  later ratifiers: ninetieth day after deposit for that Party).
- **Article 34** — reservations are **not permitted**.
- **Article 35** — withdrawal, effective one year after receipt of the
  notification by the depositary.
- **Article 36** — authentic texts (Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian,
  Spanish).

## 16. What this means for an Ayurvedic product owner

1. **Identify your role.** Sourcing Indian plant material, microbial strains or
   associated classical knowledge makes you a **user** under Indian law; NBA
   approval is needed *before* collection/transfer and before commercialisation.
2. **PIC + MAT before work starts.** Article 6 requires a permit as evidence of
   PIC; Article 5 requires type, form, timing and frequency of benefits in the
   MAT — including non-monetary benefits (technology transfer, training, R&D
   results, joint IP).
3. **Community-held TK needs community consent.** Article 7 (consent or
   approval and involvement of indigenous and local communities) and Article 12
   (community protocols, minimum MAT requirements) apply where the knowledge is
   community-held.
4. **Expect checkpoints and certificates.** Article 17 lets States designate
   checkpoints at funding, research, pre-commercial, commercial and patent-
   filing stages; the certificate of compliance is your portable evidence of
   legality, and Article 18 makes compliance with MAT enforceable against users.
5. **Do not mis-number the provisions.** Cite Article 7 for TK approval,
   Article 15 for user-country compliance, Article 17 for checkpoints and
   certificates, Article 13 for focal points — verify any other numbering
   against the official text at the source_url.
6. **The Protocol sets conditions, not a licence.** It does not itself grant
   access to anyone's resources and does not extinguish IP rights; domestic law
   and the negotiated MAT do.
