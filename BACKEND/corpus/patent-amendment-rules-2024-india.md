# The Patent (Amendment) Rules, 2024 (India)

## 1. Document overview

The Patent (Amendment) Rules, 2024 is a set of amendments to the Patents Rules, 2003, made by the Central Government (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, Ministry of Commerce and Industry) in exercise of the rule-making power under the Patents Act, 1970.

| Item | Position (verify in the official notification) |
|---|---|
| Amends | The Patents Rules, 2003 |
| Made under | Rule-making power of the Central Government under the Patents Act, 1970 (commonly cited as section 159 of the Act) |
| Gazette | Reported as published in the Official Gazette dated **15 March 2024**, effective from that date |
| Parent statute | The Patents Act, 1970 (as amended) |

**Important:** this document is a study summary of the 2024 amendments. Rule numbers, form numbers, timelines and fees change frequently. Always read the current consolidated Patents Rules, 2003 as amended, and the First Schedule (fees), on the official Patent Office site before advising or filing.

## 2. Why the 2024 amendment matters

The 2024 amendment was a procedural/formalities package rather than a change to patentability law. Its purposes were:

1. **Speed up prosecution** by compressing the window to request examination.
2. **Reduce compliance burden** in respect of foreign-filing information (Form 3) and the statement of working of the patent (Form 27 under section 146(2)).
3. **Introduce procedural clarifications** — divisional applications, grace-period procedure, representations against applications, opposition board timelines, certificates of inventorship, and extension mechanics.
4. **Encourage early/long-run fee payment** (renewal-fee discount for advance electronic payment).

Section 3 exclusions (Section 3(j), 3(p), 3(d), etc.), inventive step, novelty, disclosure sufficiency and working requirements are unchanged by this amendment — those live in the Act.

## 3. Key changes at a glance

| Topic | What changed (as reported in reliable practice commentary) | Where in the Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Request for Examination (RFE) | Time to file RFE reduced from **48 months to 31 months** from the date of priority or filing, whichever is earlier | Rule 24B(1)(i) |
| RFE for pre-existing applications | Applications filed before the amendment: the earlier (48-month) time continues | new clause in Rule 24B(1) |
| Foreign filing information (Form 3) | Time to inform the Controller of corresponding foreign applications: **within three months of the first examination report (FER)**; Controller may call for a fresh statement/undertaking (Form 3) within two months; delay may be condoned up to three months on Form 4 | Rule 12(2) and related sub-rules |
| Divisional applications | New sub-rule: the specification of a divisional must **refer to the number of the parent application** from which it is divided | new sub-rule in Rule 13 |
| Grace period | New rule prescribing procedure to claim the **12-month grace period** (specified in the Act's provision on anticipation by publication/working in certain circumstances): application in Form 31 with fee | new Rule 29A |
| Representation against an application (pre-grant) | Streamlined timeline: Controller to dispose of representations (with/without hearing), statements by applicant within two months, reasoned orders within one month | Rule 55 |
| Opposition Board | Report with reasons on each ground to be given **within two months** of receiving papers | Rule 56(4) |
| Certificate of inventorship | New provision: certificate of inventorship / duplicate thereof on Form 8A with fee | new Rule 70A |
| Renewal fees | Renewal fees may be paid in advance for multiple years; **10% reduction where paid electronically for at least four years in advance** | Rule 80(3) |
| Patent agent examination | Revised scheme of the qualifying examination: Paper I (statutes/rules — Patents Act, Patents Rules, Designs Act, Designs Rules), Paper II (drafting/interpretation of specifications), and viva-voce | Rule 110 |
| Statement of working (Form 27) | From annual filing to **once every three financial years**, starting from the year after grant; due within six months of the end of each three-year period; three-month extension available on Form 4; to be filed by patentees **and licensees** | Rule 131 |
| Extension/condonation mechanics | Controller may extend time or excuse delay up to six months on Form 4, requested before the original deadline expires | Rules 137 (as renumbered) and 138 |

**Confidence note:** the items above are corroborated by multiple independent practice-firm summaries of the March 2024 notification. Fees, form numbers and exact sub-rule numbering must still be read from the gazette text.

## 4. Request for examination — the 31-month rule

### 4.1 Ordinary examination

- A patent application is examined only after a **request for examination (RFE)** is filed with the prescribed fee (there is no automatic examination in India).
- Under the amended Rule 24B(1)(i), the RFE must be filed **within 31 months** of the date of priority or the date of filing of the application in India, **whichever is earlier** (for PCT national phase applications, from the international filing date).
- **Consequence of missing the deadline:** the application is deemed to be **withdrawn** (abandoned) — this is a hard, non-extendable statutory outcome under section 11B read with the Rules. Diary the 31-month date at filing, and note that for applications filed before the amendment the earlier time applies.

### 4.2 Expedited examination

- The Rules provide an **expedited examination** route (the rule is commonly cited as Rule 24C) for defined categories of applicants, such as natural persons, startups, small entities, and applications filed in certain fields or by certain categories, on payment of the higher prescribed fee and by filing Form 18 with Form 18A and supporting proof.
- Expedited examination compresses the first examination report timeline substantially compared to the ordinary route.
- **The categories and the fee differential must be checked in the current Rules and First Schedule** — they have been amended repeatedly (2016, 2017, 2018, 2021, 2024).
- Ayurveda-relevant note: a small entity/startup Ayurvedic formulation company can often qualify for expedited examination — check entity classification (individual/startup/small entity/micro enterprise) and file proof of category.

### 4.3 Sequence listings (biological applications)

Sequence-listing requirements for biotech/nucleotide-amino acid applications follow the **WIPO ST.26 standard** as prescribed by the Patent Office, with specific form, text and PDF requirements. **Do not attribute any specific effective date or form number to the 2024 Rules without checking the current IPO notification** — sequence-listing requirements have been the subject of separate amendments and transition dates. If a query concerns a biotech filing, direct the user to the IPO's current form instructions.

## 5. Form 3 — foreign filing information

- Applicants must keep the Controller informed of corresponding applications filed outside India, by country and application number, in **Form 3**.
- Under the amended rule, the applicant must file/update Form 3 **within three months of the first statement of objections (first examination report)**.
- The Controller may, with recorded reasons, require a fresh statement and undertaking (Form 3) within two months of the communication, and may rely on accessible databases to check foreign filings.
- Delay in filing Form 3 may be excused/extended up to three months on **Form 4** (request for extension of time), if made before the deadline.
- **Substantive obligation unchanged:** failure to faithfully disclose foreign filings can lead to sanctions in grant or post-grant challenges — this is a truth-disclosure duty, not a formality.

## 6. Divisional applications

- The new sub-rule requires the specification of a divisional application to **specifically refer to the number of the parent (original) application** from which it is divided.
- The amendment also preserves the applicant's ability to file further applications for matter disclosed in the original (provisional or complete) specification or in a subsequent application — i.e., the "multiple divisionals" practice continues, subject to the controller's directions.
- Strategic point for formulations: divisionals are the standard route to pursue additional claims (e.g., a second formulation, dosage form, or process) disclosed in one application, without adding new matter (adding new matter is prohibited).

## 7. Grace period procedure (new rule)

- The Act provides a **12-month grace period** for certain disclosures (e.g., disclosure at a learned society meeting or an international exhibition held in India or abroad, and certain other prescribed disclosures) so that such disclosure does not destroy novelty.
- The new rule prescribes the procedure: application in **Form 31** with the prescribed fee under the First Schedule, within the statutory period.
- **Do not over-read this:** India's grace period is narrow and does **not** cover general self-disclosure, marketing launch or publication by the applicant in all circumstances. Commercial launches of an Ayurveda product with publicity before filing remain dangerous. Always advise filing before any public launch.

## 8. Pre-grant representation, opposition board and hearings

Amendments to Rules 55 and 56 restructured the handling of **representations against patent applications** (third-party pre-grant submissions):

1. If the Controller finds no prima facie case, the opponent is notified; if no hearing is requested, an order refusing the representation (with reasons) is to be passed within one month; if a hearing is requested, the opponent is heard and an order passed within one month.
2. If a prima facie case is found, the Controller passes a reasoned order within one month and notifies the applicant.
3. On receipt of notice of a representation, the applicant may send a statement and evidence within **two months** (copy to the opponent).
4. After considering statements/evidence and submissions, the Controller may reject the representation, direct amendment, or refuse the application — decision within one month of completion of proceedings.
5. The **Opposition Board** must give a reasoned report on each ground **within two months** of receiving the notice of opposition and documents.
6. Hearing procedure follows the general hearing rules (notice and fee; new publications must be shared at least five days before the hearing).

**Relevance:** pre-grant oppositions are a live tool against dubious "evergreening" or TK-derived patent applications in the Ayurveda space (and, conversely, a company's own applications can be attacked).

## 9. Renewal fees — advance payment discount

- Renewal fees under section 26(1) may be paid for **multiple years in advance**.
- Where renewal fees are paid electronically for **at least four years in advance**, a **10% reduction** applies (per the amended rule).
- Diary management point: renewal runs from the date of filing (term is 20 years from filing), with a window for renewal and a restoration mechanism after lapse — check the current rules on the renewal window (commonly six months before the anniversary) and restoration.

## 10. Statement of working of the patent (Form 27)

- Section 146(2) of the Act empowers the Controller to call for information on the working (commercial utilisation) of a patented invention, and Rule 131 prescribes **Form 27**.
- **Before 2024:** Form 27 was to be filed annually with a statement and undertaking.
- **After the 2024 amendment:** Form 27 is filed **once in every three financial years**, beginning with the financial year following the grant, within six months of the end of that three-year period; a three-month extension is available on Form 4. Both patentees and licensees may be required to file, verified by them or their authorised agents.
- **Why it matters for Ayurveda:** the working statement feeds (a) the Controller's powers to revoke patents for non-working (section 66), (b) **compulsory licensing** under section 84 (which can be invoked if the reasonable requirements of the public are not met or the invention is not available at a reasonably affordable price), and (c) opposition/revocation strategy. A formulation patent that is never worked in India is vulnerable.
- The information sought includes whether the invention is worked in India by working (manufacture) or importation, and the extent to which the public requirement is met.

## 11. Fees

- All fees are in the **First Schedule to the Patents Rules, 2003**, as amended from time to time; fees differ for natural persons/startups/small entities versus others, and between ordinary and expedited examination.
- **Do not quote fee amounts from memory.** Fees were last restructured significantly in 2021 and adjusted in 2024 (including the renewal discount). Read the current First Schedule.

## 12. What did NOT change (common misconceptions)

- The **patentability standards** — novelty, inventive step, industrial application, and the section 3 exclusions (including section 3(d) and section 3(p) for traditional knowledge) — are in the Act and were not altered.
- The **20-year term**, **early publication** on request, **opposition** mechanisms, and the **working requirement** remain as before.
- The amendment did not create any "fast-track grant" — expedited examination still requires a valid Form 18A and category proof.

## 13. Relevance to Ayurvedic / AYUSH innovators

| Scenario | Practical effect of the 2024 Rules |
|---|---|
| New Ayurvedic delivery system or formulation with genuine data | Diary the **31-month RFE deadline**; consider **expedited examination** if eligible; file before public launch |
| Start-up with a herbal extraction process | Entity category proof speeds examination; renewal discount if paying 4+ years electronically |
| Product with foreign co-applicants/filings | Maintain **Form 3** updates within 3 months of the FER |
| Strategy to protect multiple claims from one application | Use **divisionals** (parent number must be recited) |
| Monitoring competitors' dubious TK patents | Pre-grant **representation** timelines are now tighter (Rules 55/56) |
| Portfolio of granted-but-unworked patents | **Form 27** now every 3 years — keep working/affirmative statements accurate |
| Traditional/classical formulation | Not patentable (see the section 3(j)/3(p) document) — the 2024 Rules change nothing here |

## 14. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Filing date/priority date recorded and the **31-month RFE deadline** diarised (with the older 48-month time applied only where the amendment expressly says so).
- [ ] Entity category (individual / startup / small entity / micro enterprise) determined and proof ready — it drives fees and expedited eligibility.
- [ ] Form 3 foreign-filing disclosures up to date (and within 3 months of the FER).
- [ ] Divisional strategy documented, with the parent application number recited.
- [ ] No public launch/marketing before filing (grace period is narrow).
- [ ] Renewal dates and the 4-year advance electronic payment discount tracked.
- [ ] Form 27 statement of working diarised on the three-year cycle and answered truthfully.
- [ ] Current fees taken from the First Schedule as amended — never from an old document.
- [ ] For biotech/sequence matter, check the IPO's current sequence-listing instructions separately.
