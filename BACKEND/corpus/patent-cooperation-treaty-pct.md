# Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT, 1970) — Relevant Provisions

## 1. What this instrument is

The **Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT)** was signed at Washington on **19 June
1970**, entered into force on **24 June 1978**, and is administered by the
**International Bureau of WIPO**. It is a *procedural* treaty: it does not
create a "world patent". It provides a single, uniform procedure for filing
patent applications in up to 150+ contracting States, deferring the cost and
work of national prosecution while preserving the option to enter each national
office later.

**India acceded to the PCT on 7 September 1998**, with the Treaty entering into
force for India on **7 December 1998**. The Indian Patent Office acts as a
**Receiving Office (RO)**, an **International Searching Authority (ISA)** and an
**International Preliminary Examining Authority (IPEA)** — the ISA/IPEA
functions took effect for India from **15 October 2013**.

## 2. Core structure

| Stage | PCT provisions | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Filing | Art. 11 | One application, one language, one set of fees → filing date |
| Search | Arts. 15–16, 18 | International Search Report (ISR) + Written Opinion of the ISA (WO-ISA) |
| Publication | Art. 21 | International publication by WIPO |
| National phase | Arts. 22, 39 | Copies/translations/fees to designated offices (typically **30 months**) |
| Optional Chapter II | Arts. 31–34, 40–41 | Demand for International Preliminary Examination; amendments; second opportunity |
| Amendment | Arts. 28, 41 | Amendment before designated/elected offices |

> Withdrawal of the application, of the demand, or of election, and timing of
> refusals, are governed by the **PCT Regulations** (including Rules on
> withdrawal and on time limits), not by the Treaty articles themselves — check
> the Regulations at the source_url rather than assuming an article number.

## 3. Article 11 — The filing date

Article 11 requires the Receiving Office to accord, as the **international
filing date**, the date of receipt of the **request**, together with whatever
else it receives by the prescribed time:

- the **description** (and claims, drawings, if any) must be received within
  the prescribed period after filing (the Regulations set a 13-day allowance for
  supplementary documentation);
- the applicant must be a **resident or national** of a Contracting State bound
  by the Treaty;
- the application must contain a **designated State** (now effectively all
  States for applications filed on or after the relevant transition), a
  **specification**, and **claims** (may be supplied later within the
  prescribed period), and a **request form**.

The Receiving Office is required to issue a **certificate of filing** showing
the international filing date, and to send the application to the International
Bureau (for ROs in a language other than the PCT minimum languages, with
translation).

## 4. Article 12 — Confidentiality

The international application and its contents are **kept in confidence** by
the Receiving Office, the International Bureau and the ISA from the
international filing date until international publication.

## 5. International search — Articles 15–18

- **Article 15 (International Search Authority)** — each Contracting State
  appoints one or more ISA; if no ISA is appointed, the International Bureau
  performs the search.
- **Article 16 (The international search)** — the ISA searches the relevant
  prior art (published documents in the prescribed categories) with a view to
  establishing whether the invention appears to be **novel and to involve an
  inventive step (and be industrially applicable, where applicable)** — the
  PCT uses " inventive step / industrial applicability" wording that TRIPS
  Members must read consistently with their national standards (TRIPS
  Articles 27 and 33 — see the TRIPS file in this corpus).
- **Article 17 (Procedure before the International Search Authority)** — the ISA
  prepares an **International Search Report (ISR)** with citations and a
  statement of the findings; applicant may file **observations** within the
  prescribed period; the ISR is transmitted to the applicant and the
  International Bureau.
- **Article 18 (Transmission of the international search report)** — the report
  and any observations are transmitted to the applicant and, on publication, to
  each designated office.

**Written Opinion of the ISA:** for applications filed after the transition
from the International Preliminary Examining Authority system, the ISA issues a
**Written Opinion (WO-ISA)** on novelty, inventive step and industrial
applicability, and (if it was a non-English language search, a translation of
the ISR). This is the procedural successor to the International Preliminary
Report on Patentability (IPRP) under Chapter II.

**Indian Patent Office as ISA/IPEA:** the IPO acts as ISA for international
applications filed with the IPO as Receiving Office (and, by agreement, for
certain other filings), and as IPEA for Chapter II demands — effective from
**15 October 2013**. The IPO's fee schedule, technical areas accepted, and any
restrictions are published by the IPO and WIPO; verify the current list of
areas and fees at ipindia.gov.in and wipo.int before filing.

## 6. Article 21 — International publication

The International Bureau **publishes** the international application (with the
ISR and Written Opinion, and any amendments) **18 months after the priority
date**, or earlier at the applicant's request. Publication is in the language
of filing (with a translation of the request and abstract into other languages
as prescribed).

## 7. Article 22 — National phase entry: copy, translation and fee

**Article 22** provides that, if the applicant wishes to obtain the effect of
the international application in a **designated office**, it must furnish,
**not later than the expiration of 30 months from the priority date**:

1. a **copy** of the international application (unless already received);
2. a **translation** of the international application (where the language of
   filing is not the required national language);
3. the prescribed **fee**;
4. any other required documents (e.g. power of attorney, sequence listings,
   declarations).

**India's national phase deadline is 31 months from the priority date** for
applications entering the national phase before the Indian Patent Office (for
both Chapter I entry and Chapter II entry). This is longer than the 30-month
PCT baseline of Article 22 (and Article 39): it follows from India's domestic
patent legislation and the PCT (National Phase) Rules in India. **Confirm the
current Indian deadline and fee schedule at ipindia.gov.in before relying on
it.**

> Common failure mode: relying on Article 22's "30 months" for India. India is
> a **31-month** jurisdiction. Check the current rule text.

## 8. Article 23/24 — Effect and withdrawal before national phase (summary)

- **Article 23** — no designated office shall process the international
  application before expiration of the applicable period (the "stay" of
  national processing), and the international application is not subject to
  withdrawal by the Receiving Office after the international filing date.
- **Article 24** — effects of withdrawal of the international application or of
  designation, subject to the Regulations.

> These are the Treaty's standstill/withdrawal rules; the operational withdrawal
> procedure and deadlines sit in the PCT Regulations (Rules on withdrawal of
> demand, election, and international application).

## 9. Article 27 — Requirements of the national phase; Article 28 — Amendment before designated offices

- **Article 27 (Certain requirements of national law)** — national offices may
  apply their own substantive requirements (formalities, unity of invention,
  fees, subject-matter exclusions such as treatment methods and business
  methods, disclosure requirements, entitlement) in the national phase, subject
  to the Treaty's rules on confidentiality and no additional formal
  requirements before the international phase is completed.
- **Article 28 (Amendment of the international application before designated
  offices)** — the applicant has the opportunity to **amend the claims**,
  description and drawings **before each designated office** on payment of the
  prescribed fee, within the prescribed period (the Regulations set this as not
  later than the expiry of the national phase entry period or a set period
  after the office's request).

**India:** in the national phase the Indian Patent Office applies the Patents
Act, 1970 and the PCT (National Phase) Rules, including the Indian
**non-patentable subject matter** (Sections 3 and 4 — e.g. Section 3(d) on
known substances and mere discovery of a new form, Section 3(h) on methods of
treatment, Section 3(j) on plants/animals) and **Sections 8 and 9 disclosure
duties**. Amending the claims under Article 28 does not remove those national
substantive bars.

## 10. Chapter II — International preliminary examination (Articles 31–34)

- **Article 31 (Demand for international preliminary examination)** — a
  **demand**, filed with an IPEA and accompanied by the prescribed fee, triggers
  Chapter II. It may be filed after the Written Opinion is issued (or after the
  prescribed period), and can be filed by the applicant or the applicant's
  successor in title.
- **Article 32 (International Preliminary Examining Authority)** — each
  Contracting State appoints one or more IPEA; the International Bureau
  performs the function where no IPEA is appointed.
- **Article 33 (Criteria for the international preliminary examination)** — the
  IPEA examines the claims (as amended, if amended) with a view to establishing
  whether the invention, in the **international preliminary examination
  opinion**, presents on the criteria of **novelty, inventive step and
  industrial applicability** in accordance with the provisions of the Treaty.
  Paragraph 3 refers to the standard being **the same as the standard applied
  in the national phase** by the offices concerned (and TRIPS Article 33
  supplies the agreed minimum standard for inventive step/non-obviousness and
  novelty among WTO Members).
- **Article 34 (Procedure before the International Preliminary Examining
  Authority)** — confidentiality of the international application (until
  international publication); the IPEA may invite the applicant to answer
  **written communications** and, where the opinion is adverse, may issue
  **second and further written communications**; **paragraph 2(b)** permits the
  applicant to **amend the claims, description and drawings** (in the Chapter II
  procedure) before the expiration of a prescribed period; the IPEA issues an
  **International Preliminary Report on Patentability (IPRP)** under Chapter II,
  transmitted to the applicant and the International Bureau.

**Why Chapter II matters for a Ayurveda patentee:** a Chapter II IPRP (with an
affirmative opinion on novelty/inventive step/industrial applicability) carries
significant **weight before national offices** in the national phase — a
positive IPRP normally means the office does not raise objections on those
grounds again, subject to its own domestic law (e.g. Indian Sections 3(d), 3(h),
13).

## 11. Articles 39 and 41 — Election and amendment before elected offices

- **Article 39 (Copy, translation and fee, to elected offices)** — if a demand
  was filed, the applicant may obtain the effect of the international
  application in each **elected office** by furnishing, **not later than the
  expiration of 30 months from the priority date** (or, where Article 40(2)
  applies, a later prescribed period): a copy of the international application,
  a translation where required, and the prescribed fee. For offices that
  operate a Chapter II national-phase treatment, the IPRP is also transmitted.
- **Article 41 (Amendment of the international application before elected
  offices)** — the applicant may amend the claims, description and drawings
  before each **elected office** on payment of the prescribed fee, within the
  prescribed period.

**India's elected-office deadline is likewise 31 months** (see Section 7
caveat); verify the current rule.

## 12. Quick reference: PCT → India

| PCT element | India |
|---|---|
| Receiving Office | Indian Patent Office (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) — for residents/nationals of India |
| ISA / IPEA | Indian Patent Office, in force from **15 October 2013** (verify current areas and fees) |
| Language | English (India) |
| International filing fee | PCT fees set by WIPO (reduced for electronic filing; fee relief for certain applicants — verify at WIPO) |
| National phase entry | **31 months** from priority date (Chapter I and Chapter II) — verify current PCT (National Phase) Rules |
| National substantive law | Patents Act, 1970 (Sections 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 46–48, 53, 64, 84, 92) and Patents Rules, 2003 (as amended) |
| Filing via Madrid/Hague | Not applicable — PCT covers patents only |

## 13. What this means for an Ayurvedic inventor

1. **PCT buys time, not a patent.** One international filing gives you up to
   ~30/31 months before you must decide on countries, pay national fees, and
   comply with local substantive law (Article 22 / Article 39).
2. **The ISR and WO-ISA are early risk signals.** They are non-binding but are
   read closely by national examiners (and by investors and licensees).
3. **Chapter II is a strategic option.** A favourable IPRP (Article 33) can
   streamline national prosecution — but Indian Section 3(d), 3(h) and
   disclosure duties (Sections 8, 9, 10) still apply.
4. **Amend early, amend narrowly.** Article 28/41 amendments before national
   offices are the last cheap chance to fix claim scope; and Indian
   post-filing amendments (Section 59) have their own restrictions.
5. **Deposits.** If the invention cannot be disclosed by words alone
   (microorganism, cell line, culture), arrange a **Budapest Treaty deposit**
   and include the receipt in the complete specification.
6. **Check India-specific deadlines at source.** The 31-month Indian national
   phase deadline and the IPO's ISA/IPEA areas/fees are the two items most
   often miscited.
