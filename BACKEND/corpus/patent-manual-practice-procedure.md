\# Manual of Patent Practice and Procedure



\## 1. Document Overview



This manual provides detailed information on practices and procedures followed by the Indian Patent Office for processing patent applications. It incorporates the \*\*Patents Act, 1970\*\* as amended by the \*\*Patents (Amendment) Act, 2005\*\* and the \*\*Patents Rules, 2003\*\* as amended by the \*\*Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2006\*\*.



The manual reproduces successive sections and relevant rules, followed by explanations and past decisions. It is a guide only and does not have the force of law. If inconsistencies exist, the Act and Rules prevail.

\## 3. Chapter I: Introduction



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch1-001 -->



\### History of Indian Patent System



\- First legislation: Act VI of 1856.

\- Act IX of 1857, Act XV of 1859, Act XIII of 1872, Act V of 1888.

\- Indian Patents and Designs Act, 1911 (Act II of 1911).

\- Patents Act, 1970 (Act 39 of 1970).

\- Amendments: 1999, 2002, 2005.

\- Patents Rules: 1972, replaced by 2003, amended 2005 and 2006.



\### Patent Administration



\- CGPDTM appointed under Section 3, Trade Marks Act, 1999.

\- Office under Department of Industrial Policy and Promotion, Ministry of Commerce and Industry.

\- Head Office: Kolkata. Branch offices: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai.



\### Patentable Subject Matter



\- Criteria: novelty, inventive step, industrial application.

\- Not patentable under Sections 3 and 4.



\### Novelty



\- Not anticipated by publication anywhere, use in India, prior claim in India, or traditional knowledge.

\- State of the art includes all matter made available to public before priority date.

\- Determination: search in patent and non-patent literature.

\- Anticipation must be in a single document; mosaic not allowed for novelty.

\- Enabling prior art: disclosure sufficient for skilled person to work.

\- Prior public use in India destroys novelty, except reasonable trial within 1 year under Section 32.

\- Prior claiming: earlier Indian application filed before but published after.



\### Inventive Step



\- Not obvious to person skilled in the art.

\- Mosaicing multiple documents permissible for inventive step if obvious to combine.

\- Person skilled in the art: ordinary practitioner, aware of common general knowledge, no inventive capability.

\- Indicators: distance from prior art, surprising effect, long-felt need, failure of others, complexity, commercial success, cheaper/economical product, prior art motivation.

\- Ex post facto analysis to be avoided.



\### Industrial Applicability



\- Capable of being made or used in an industry.

\- Not applicable to methods of treatment of human/animal body by surgery/therapy/diagnosis.

\- Parts of human/animal body for transplants not industrially applicable.

\- Perpetual motion machines contrary to physical laws not industrially applicable.



\## 4. Chapter II: Inventions Not Patentable



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch2-001 -->



\### Section 3: What Are Not Inventions



\- Frivolous or contrary to natural laws.

\- Contrary to public order, morality, or prejudicial to human/animal/plant life/health/environment.

\- Mere discovery of scientific principle, abstract theory, living/non-living substances in nature.

\- Mere discovery of new form of known substance without enhanced efficacy; new property/new use; mere use of known process/machine/apparatus unless new product or new reactant.

\- Substance obtained by mere admixture resulting only in aggregation of properties.

\- Mere arrangement/re-arrangement/duplication of known devices functioning independently.

\- Method of agriculture or horticulture.

\- Process for medicinal, surgical, curative, prophylactic, diagnostic, therapeutic treatment of humans/animals.

\- Plants and animals in whole or part, seeds, varieties, species, essentially biological processes.

\- Mathematical/business method, computer programme per se, algorithms.

\- Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic work, aesthetic creation.

\- Mere scheme, rule, method of performing mental act, method of playing game.

\- Presentation of information.

\- Topography of integrated circuits.

\- Traditional knowledge or aggregation/duplication of known properties of traditionally known components.



\### Section 4: Atomic Energy



\- No patent for inventions relating to atomic energy under Atomic Energy Act, 1962.



\## 5. Chapter III: Application for Patents



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch3-001 -->



\### Persons Entitled to Apply



\- True and first inventor.

\- Assignee of true and first inventor.

\- Legal representative of deceased person entitled to apply.

\- “True and first inventor” excludes first importer or person to whom invention communicated from outside India.



\### Where to Apply



\- Appropriate office based on residence, domicile, place of business, place of origin of invention, or address for service in India. Rule 4.

\- Jurisdiction:

&#x20; - Mumbai: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Goa, Chhattisgarh, Daman \& Diu, Dadra \& Nagar Haveli.

&#x20; - Delhi: Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu \& Kashmir, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttaranchal, Delhi, Chandigarh.

&#x20; - Chennai: Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Pondicherry, Lakshadweep.

&#x20; - Kolkata: rest of India.



\### How to Apply



\- Form 1, in duplicate, with fee.

\- Provisional or complete specification in Form 2.

\- Drawings, abstract, declaration of inventorship Form 5, proof of right, power of attorney Form 26, Form 3 foreign filing details.



\### Types of Applications



1\. Ordinary application.

2\. Convention application.

3\. PCT international application.

4\. PCT national phase application.

5\. Patent of addition.

6\. Divisional application.



\### Specification and Drawings



\- Provisional specification: describes invention, establishes priority, 12 months to file complete.

\- Complete specification: title, field, prior art, problem, object, statement of invention, detailed description, best method, claims, abstract, drawings.

\- Claims: define scope, clear, succinct, fairly based on description.

\- Unity of invention: single inventive concept.

\- Sufficiency of disclosure: enable skilled person to work invention.

\- Drawings: A4, margins, numbered, reference letters, no descriptive matter except flow diagrams.

\- Abstract: concise summary, preferably 150 words, technical field, problem, solution, principal use.



\### Claims



\- Independent claim: essential novel features.

\- Dependent claims: additional features.

\- Omnibus claim: “substantially as described”.

\- Markush claims: chemical structure with substituents.

\- Claims must not be vague, ambiguous, speculative.

\- Trade marks should not be used unless unavoidable.



\### International Applications Under PCT



\- India became PCT member on 7 December 1998.

\- Receiving Office, International Searching Authority, International Preliminary Examining Authority.

\- International phase: filing, search, publication, preliminary examination.

\- National phase entry in India within 31 months from priority date.

\- Requirements: national fee, translation if not in English/Hindi, priority document, special requirements.



\## 6. Chapter IV: Publication and Examination



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch4-001 -->



\### Publication



\- 18 months from filing or priority, whichever earlier. Section 11A.

\- Early publication: Form 9 with fee.

\- Publication includes application number, date, title, applicant name/address, abstract, priority details.

\- Effects: biological material available; specification/drawings available; provisional rights from publication; no infringement suit until grant.



\### Request for Examination



\- Form 18 within 48 months from priority/filing. Section 11B.

\- If no request, application deemed withdrawn.

\- Deferred examination system.



\### Examination



\- Formal examination: forms, fees, specifications, drawings, claims, proof of right, Form 5, power of attorney, Form 3.

\- Substantive examination: patentability, sufficiency, unity, industrial applicability, classification, novelty search, inventive step, validity of claims, biological material, biodiversity.

\- First Examination Report (FER): gist of objections; 12 months to comply.

\- Anticipation: prior publication, prior claiming.

\- Potential infringement: reference to other patent may be inserted.

\- Substitution of applicant: Section 20.

\- Time for putting application in order: Section 21.



\## 7. Chapter V: Opposition Proceedings



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch5-001 -->



\### Pre-Grant Opposition



\- After publication but before grant.

\- Any person may represent by way of opposition on grounds under Section 25(1):

&#x20; - Wrongful obtaining.

&#x20; - Prior publication.

&#x20; - Prior claiming.

&#x20; - Prior public knowledge/use.

&#x20; - Obviousness/lack of inventive step.

&#x20; - Not an invention/not patentable.

&#x20; - Insufficient description.

&#x20; - Failure to disclose foreign filing information.

&#x20; - Convention application not within 12 months.

&#x20; - Incorrect mention of biological material source.

&#x20; - Anticipation by traditional knowledge.

\- Rule 55: statement and evidence, request for hearing.

\- Controller considers representation after request for examination.

\- Applicant may reply within 3 months.

\- Controller may refuse or require amendment.



\### Post-Grant Opposition



\- After grant, before expiry of 1 year from publication of grant.

\- Any person interested may give notice on grounds under Section 25(2).

\- Form 7, fee, written statement, evidence.

\- Opposition Board constituted.

\- Patentee reply within 2 months.

\- Opponent evidence in reply within 1 month.

\- Hearing, decision to maintain/amend/revoke patent.

\- Cases reported: Novartis, Rickett \& Colman, Crompton Greaves, etc.



\### Wrongful Obtaining



\- Section 26: Controller may treat patent as patent of opponent.

\- Form 12 within 3 months.



\### Mention of Inventor



\- Section 28: request/claim before grant.

\- Form 8.

\- Controller may mention inventor in patent and register.



\## 8. Chapter VI: Anticipation



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch6-001 -->



\- Sections 29–34: exceptions to anticipation.

\- Previous publication: not anticipation if published before 1912, or obtained from applicant without consent.

\- Previous communication to Government: not anticipation.

\- Public display at exhibition: not anticipation if application within 12 months.

\- Public working: not anticipation if reasonable trial within 1 year.

\- Use/publication after provisional specification: not anticipation.



\## 9. Chapter VII: Secrecy Directions



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch7-001 -->



\- Section 35: inventions relevant for defence purposes.

\- Controller may prohibit/restrict publication.

\- Central Government reviews every 6 months.

\- Consequences: no refusal, no publication, no patent grant until directions revoked.

\- Section 39: residents not to apply outside India without prior permission.

\- Form 25 for permission; Controller disposes within 21 days.

\- Contravention: application deemed abandoned; patent liable to revocation.



\## 10. Chapter VIII: Grant of Patents



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch8-001 -->



\- Section 43: grant when application in order.

\- Patent granted with seal, date entered in register.

\- Form of patent: Third Schedule.

\- Amendment of patent granted to deceased applicant: Form 10.

\- Date of patent: date of filing.

\- Form, extent, effect: throughout India, one invention.

\- Conditions: Government use, experiment/research, import of medicine/drug.

\- Rights of patentee: exclusive right to prevent making, using, offering for sale, selling, importing.

\- Foreign vessels/aircraft/land vehicles: temporary use not infringement.

\- Co-owners: equal undivided share; rights.

\- Controller directions to co-owners: Form 11.

\- Term: 20 years from filing.

\- Renewal fees: Rule 80.



\## 11. Chapter IX: Patent of Addition



<!-- chunk\_id: pmp-ch9-001 -->



\- Section 54: improvement/modification of main invention.

\- Same or later filing date than main invention.

\- Not granted before main patent.

\- Term equal to main patent.

\- No renewal fee.

\- Validity not questioned for lack of inventive step over main invention.

\## 21. RAG Q\&A Extraction



\### Q: What are the criteria for patentability?

\*\*A:\*\* Novelty, inventive step, and industrial application. Section 2(1)(j).



\### Q: What is the term of a patent in India?

\*\*A:\*\* 20 years from the date of filing. Section 53.



\### Q: What is the time limit for requesting examination?

\*\*A:\*\* 48 months from priority date or filing date, whichever is earlier. Section 11B, Rule 24B.



\### Q: What is the time limit for national phase entry in India under PCT?

\*\*A:\*\* 31 months from priority date.



\### Q: What is a patent of addition?

\*\*A:\*\* A patent for improvement/modification of a main invention; same term as main patent; no renewal fee.



\### Q: What are grounds for compulsory licence?

\*\*A:\*\* Reasonable requirements not satisfied; invention not available at reasonably affordable price; not worked in India. Section 84.



\### Q: What is the effect of secrecy direction?

\*\*A:\*\* Application not published; no patent granted; Government use allowed; solatium may be paid.
