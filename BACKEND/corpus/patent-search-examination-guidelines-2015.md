# Guidelines for Search and Examination of Patent Applications - Indian Patent Office

## 1. Document Overview

This document provides comprehensive guidelines for the search and examination of patent applications at the Indian Patent Office. Published in 2015 by the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trademarks (CGPDTM), it covers the entire examination process including formal examination, substantive examination, patentability criteria (novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability), inventions not patentable, International Patent Classification (IPC), search strategies, examination report writing, amendments, pre-grant and post-grant opposition procedures, and review of Controller's decisions.

The guidelines are intended to assist Examiners and Controllers in their day-to-day duties and to ensure transparent, uniform, and consistent administration of the Patents Act, 1970 and Patents Rules, 2003.

## 3. Chapter 1: Introduction

### 1.1 Overview of the Indian Patent System
- Patent system provides a bargain: exclusivity for limited period in exchange for disclosure.
- History: Act VI of 1856 → Act V of 1888 → Patents & Designs Act 1911 → Patents Act 1970.
- Amendments: 1999 (w.e.f. 1995), 2002 (w.e.f. 2003), 2005.
- India is a member of WTO, CBD, Paris Convention, PCT, Budapest Treaty.
- TRIPs required product patenting in all sectors including pharmaceuticals and 20-year uniform term.
- India introduced product patenting from 01.01.2005.
- Biological Diversity Act, 2002 introduced for sustainable use of genetic resources.

### 1.2 Importance of Patent Examination
- Bad patents impair market dynamics.
- Examination is the primary gatekeeper of the patent system.
- Patents Act stipulates benchmarks: novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability, sufficiency of disclosure, support of claims.

### 1.3 Necessity of the Guidelines
- Need for transparent, uniform, and consistent administration.
- Earlier guidelines: Manual of Patent Office Practice and Procedure, Traditional Knowledge, Biotechnology, Pharmaceuticals.
- These guidelines encompass all areas of technology.

## 4. Chapter 2: Patent Application Processing Flow

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch2-001 -->

### 2.1 Application Filing - Initial Processing
- Date and serial number accorded.
- PCT national phase and non-PCT applications identified by separate serial numbers.
- Applications digitized, verified, screened, classified, uploaded.
- File wrapper prepared with bibliographic sheet.

### 2.2 Screening and Classification
Screening for:
- International Patent Classification.
- Technical field for allocation to examiner.
- Relevance to defence or atomic energy.
- Correcting/completing the abstract.

Scrutiny of Application:
- Appropriate jurisdiction.
- Proof of right to file (within 6 months).
- Prescribed format (forms, request, petitions, assignment deeds, translation).
- Documents on proper sized paper, typed, properly spaced, signed.
- Abstract, drawings in proper format.
- Meaningful claim(s) present.
- Power of Attorney or attested copy of GPA.
- Form-5 (Declaration of Inventorship).
- Form-6 if invention assigned.

Secrecy Directions and Consequences:
- Controller may issue direction prohibiting publication for defence-related inventions.
- Central Government reviews every 6 months.
- Controller shall not decide grant/refusal while directions in force.

Inventions Relating to Atomic Energy:
- No patent for inventions relating to atomic energy under Atomic Energy Act, 1962.
- Controller refers application to Department of Atomic Energy.
- Central Government's direction is binding; not open to appeal.

Withdrawal of Patent Application:
- Applicant may withdraw at any time before grant.
- If withdrawn within 15 months from filing/priority, application not published.

### 2.3 Publication
Early Publication:
- Form-9 with fee.
- Published ordinarily within 1 month of request.
- Privileges and rights from date of publication.

Publication u/s 11A:
- Published after 18 months from filing/priority.
- Documents laid open to public.
- Applicant has like privileges and rights from date of publication.

### 2.4 Request for Examination
- Form-18 with fee within 48 months from priority/filing.
- Examiner submits report ordinarily within 1 month, not exceeding 3 months.
- Allocation to examiner in order of filing of request.

## 5. Chapter 3: Overview of Patent Examination

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch3-001 -->

### 3.1 Mandate of Law
- Examination under Section 12 of Patents Act, 1970.
- Search under Section 13.
- Examiner reports on matters under Section 12(1)(a) to (d).

### 3.2 Formal Examination
Steps:
- Application, specification, documents in duplicate in prescribed forms.
- Applicant entitled to apply under Section 6.
- Jurisdiction under Rule 4(1)(i) - appropriate office.
- Address for service provided (Rule 5).
- Post-dating of provisional specification (max 6 months, Section 17(1)).
- Complete specification within 12 months from provisional (Section 9(1)).
- Complete specification within 12 months from earliest provisional for cognate inventions (Section 9(2)).
- Complete specification within 12 months from earlier complete treated as provisional (Section 9(3)).
- No provisional specification for convention/PCT national phase applications.
- Power of Attorney or General Power of Attorney.
- Declaration as to Inventorship (Form-5).
- Proof of Right (Section 7(2)).
- Form-3 (foreign filing details).
- Publication under Section 11A.
- Cross-references on file covers.
- Request for Examination (Form-18) and who filed it.
- Priorities claimed and fee paid.

#### 3.2.1 Timelines of Filing Documents and RQs, Forms and Fee, Right to File, Priority Rights
- Time limits incorporated in Annexure-I.

### 3.3 Substantive Examination
- Examiner conducts examination under Section 12.
- Ascertains lawful grounds of objection.

#### 3.3.1 Understanding the Invention
- Complete Specification is a techno-legal document.
- Must fully and particularly describe invention and method of performing.
- Best method of performing must be disclosed (Section 10(4)).
- Biological material: deposit in International Depository Authority under Budapest Treaty.
- Reference in specification within 3 months from filing.
- Source and geographical origin of biological material disclosed.

#### 3.3.2 Sufficiency of Disclosure
- Technical advance, synergistic effect, efficacy must be substantiated.
- Press Metal Corporation Ltd. v. Noshir Sorabji Pochkhanawalla (1982 PTC 259 Bom): Duty to state clearly and distinctly.
- Description should not contain passages confusing scope.
- Technical or specialized terms: clear, straightforward, avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Foreign terms only where no English equivalent.
- Established meanings not used differently.
- Trade names/marks not used in specification.

#### 3.3.3 Understanding the Scope of Claims
- Claims define boundary of protection.
- What is not claimed stands disclaimed.
- Each claim in single sentence, clearly worded.
- Claims clear, succinct, no unnecessary repetition.
- Each claim evaluated on own merit.
- Claims must be fairly based on matter disclosed.

**Scope of Claims:**
- Claims must not be too extensive or too narrow.
- Passages confusing scope or unspecific claims are prejudicial.
- Claim for product, process, apparatus, or all.
- Attributes of claims: preamble "I or we claim", fresh page, serially numbered, additional fee for >10 claims, single inventive concept, clear and complete, specific not vague, no trade marks.

**Structure of Claims:**
- Introductory phrase, body, link ("comprising", "including", "consisting of", "consisting essentially of").
- If improvement, two parts separated by "characterized by" or "wherein".
- Independent claim: essential novel features, characterized with respect to prior art.
- Dependent claims: include all features of independent claim plus additional features.
- Further independent claims justified where inventive concept covers more than one category.

**Statements Not Regarded as Claims:**
- "I claim to be the inventor..."
- "I claim a patent and that no one else shall use..."
- "I claim that the machine described above is quite new..."
- "I claim some reward."
- Claims illustrating efficiency or advantages.
- Claims merely referring back to description.

#### 3.3.4 Single Inventive Concept
- Section 10(5): claims relate to single invention or group linked to form single inventive concept.
- Multiple independent claims allowed if single inventive concept.
- Unity of invention: technical relationship among claimed inventions involving same or corresponding special technical features.
- Lack of unity: a priori (before prior art) or a posteriori (after prior art).
- Examples: A+X, A+Y, X+Y lacks unity a priori.
- Examples of single inventive concept: product and process for manufacture; process and apparatus; product, process, and apparatus.

### 3.4 Patentability Criteria: Novelty, Inventive Step, Industrial Applicability

#### 3.4.1 Novelty of Invention
**General Principle:**
- Invention is new if not anticipated by publication anywhere, prior claimed in India, or part of knowledge (oral or otherwise) in local/indigenous community, or used, before filing/priority date.
- Prior art means everything published, presented, or disclosed to public before filing date.
- Indian Patent Application filed before but published after is considered for prior claiming.
- Prior art anticipatory if all features present in cited prior art.
- Prior art must disclose explicitly or implicitly.
- Mosaicing not followed for novelty.
- Generic disclosure may not take away novelty of specific disclosure.
- Specific disclosure takes away novelty of generic disclosure.
- Onus on applicant if prior art cited but deemed not anticipation under Sections 29-34.

**Determination of Novelty:**
- Search for anticipation by previous publication and prior claim.
- Invention lacks novelty if specified combination of features already anticipated in previous disclosure.
- Anticipatory disclosure must be entirely contained within single document.
- Cumulative effect of disclosures from multiple documents not considered for novelty.
- Prior art drawing may be considered if essential features disclosed clearly.
- Matter part of state of art on date it first becomes available to public.
- Prior publication depends on accessibility, not degree of dissemination.
- Communication to single member of public without obligation of secrecy amounts to making available.
- If claim specifies alternatives or range, not new if one alternative or single example within range known.
- Specific example destroys novelty of generic claim.
- Generic disclosure does not impugn novelty of specific claim.

**Illustrative Cases:**
- Preventing knocking signals in engine: anticipated by document restricting suction air quantity.
- Trailing arm with anti-roll bar suspension: anticipated by document with forward portion pivotally supported.
- Crompton Greaves Ltd. v. BHEL: Prior public knowledge not established by contract terms alone.
- Monsanto v. Coramandal Indag: Butachlor formula published in 1968 report, publicly known.
- Lallubhai Chakubhai v. Chimanal Chunilal: Document accessible to public without trouble.
- Decision Re. Patent Application No. 23077: Document reaching appreciable section of public without secrecy.
- Pope Alliance Corp. v. Spanish River Pulp: Specification must convey same knowledge.
- Decision Re. Patent Application No. 27709: Mosaic of extracts not proper anticipation.
- Decision Re. Patent Application No. 32384: Mosaic of separate steps not sufficient for anticipation.
- Decision Re. Patent Application No. 29089: Prior publication must enable person conversant with art.
- Decision Re. Patent Application No. 29180: Disclosure to selected individuals in Government service not public knowledge.

**Prior Public Use:**
- Prior public use before filing destroys novelty.
- Exception: public working within 1 year before priority date for reasonable trial (Section 32).
- Public user means user in public manner (Lallubhai Chakubhai v. Chimanal Chunilal).
- Use for trade constitutes public user.
- Public sale strong evidence of commercial user.
- Invention deemed publicly used if used in regular business without secrecy in place accessible to persons without confidential relationship.
- Article manufactured under secret process: sale amounts to public user if secret discoverable by examination.
- Monsanto v. Coramandal Indag: Publicly known if known to persons engaged in pursuit of knowledge.
- Ram Narain Kher v. Ambassador Industries: Novelty must be succinctly stated in claim.
- Patent Application No. 26209: Prior use of machine for profit in private premises amounts to public use if no secrecy.
- Patent Application No. 27208: Allegation of "machine similar to applicant's machine" not enough.
- Patent Application No. 31894: Unfair to refuse patent without fair opportunity to judge similarity.
- Bilcare Limited v. Amartara: Novelty and inventive step are mixed questions of law and fact.

**Prior Claiming:**
- Section 13(1)(b): Investigation for prior claiming in Indian complete specification published on or after filing date.
- Conditions: Application X filed in India, filed/priority earlier than application Y, published on or after filing date of application Y.
- Centron Industrial Alliance v. Harbans Lal Malhotra: Later application for razor blades anticipated by prior claiming in earlier application.

#### 3.4.2 Inventive Step
**Concept:**
- Section 2(1)(ja): Feature involving technical advance or economic significance, not obvious to person skilled in art.
- IPAB: Technical advance comparison must not be related to excluded subjects.
- Supreme Court in Biswanath Prasad Radhey Shyam v. Hindustan Metal Industries: Obviousness strictly and objectively judged.
- Test: Whether discovery lies so much out of track of what was known before as not naturally to suggest itself.
- Test: Would competent craftsman with common general knowledge, faced with problem, have said "this gives me what I want?"
- Delhi High Court in F. Hoffmann-La Roche v. Cipla: Normal and grammatical meaning of person skilled in art.
- Points to ascertain inventive step:
  1. Identify inventive concept of claim.
  2. Identify person skilled in art.
  3. Identify relevant common general knowledge at priority date.
  4. Identify differences between state of art and inventive concept.
  5. Viewed without knowledge of invention, do differences constitute obvious steps?

**Illustrative Case Laws:**
- IN/PCT/2002/00020/DEL: Selection of range from prior art not inventive step; mere workshop improvements.
- Patent No. 173953: Specific narrow range not taught by cited documents; opponents failed.
- Patent No. 183455: Prior Sri Lankan and Nigerian patents anticipated invention.
- Ajay Industrial Corporation v. Shiro Kamas: No technical evidence that patent embodied no new discovery.
- Franz Zaver Huemer v. New Yesh Engineers: Arrangement/rearrangement of known device not invention.
- Surendra Lai Mahendra v. Jain Glazers: Indigenous combination of known integers; no inventive faculty.

#### 3.4.3 Industrial Applicability
**Concept:**
- Section 2(1)(ac): Capable of being made or used in an industry.
- Devoid of industrial application does not satisfy definition of invention.
- Industry broad sense: useful and practical, not intellectual or aesthetic.
- Vague and speculative indications not sufficient.
- Methods of testing capable if applicable to improvement/control of product/process.
- Processes contrary to physical laws (perpetual motion) not industrially applicable.
- Methods of treatment of human/animal body by surgery/therapy/diagnosis not industrially applicable.
- Parts/pieces of human/animal body for transplants objected.

**Illustrations:**
- "In any kind of industry" connotes trade or manufacture in widest sense.
- Must be product, but need not be article or substance; must be useful in practical affairs.
- Method of eradicating weeds gives product (improved crop) with economic significance.
- Scheme for exchanging prison sentence for corporal punishment lacks industrial applicability.
- Method for effecting introductions to make friends not industrially applicable.
- Photo-booth camera with folded optical path lacks industrial applicability.

### 3.5 Inventions Not Patentable

**Section 3: What Are Not Inventions**
- (a) Frivolous or contrary to well-established natural laws.
- (b) Contrary to public order, morality, or prejudicial to human/animal/plant life/health/environment.
- (c) Mere discovery of scientific principle, abstract theory, or living/non-living substances in nature.
- (d) Mere discovery of new form of known substance without enhanced efficacy; new property/new use; mere use of known process/machine/apparatus unless new product or new reactant.
- (e) Substance obtained by mere admixture resulting only in aggregation of properties.
- (f) Mere arrangement/re-arrangement/duplication of known devices functioning independently.
- (h) Method of agriculture or horticulture.
- (i) Process for medicinal, surgical, curative, prophylactic, diagnostic, therapeutic treatment of humans/animals.
- (j) Plants and animals in whole or part, seeds, varieties, species, essentially biological processes.
- (k) Mathematical/business method, computer programme per se, algorithms.
- (l) Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic work, aesthetic creation.
- (m) Mere scheme, rule, method of performing mental act, method of playing game.
- (n) Presentation of information.
- (o) Topography of integrated circuits.
- (p) Traditional knowledge or aggregation/duplication of known properties of traditionally known components.

**Section 4: Inventions Relating to Atomic Energy**
- No patent for inventions relating to atomic energy under Atomic Energy Act, 1962.

**Illustrative Examples:**
- 3(a): Perpetual motion machine; machine giving output without input; metric time device (101/Bom/72); making article in one piece (Indian Vacuum Brake Co. v. Laurd).
- 3(b): Device for theft/burglary; counterfeiting currency; gambling; adulteration of food; cloning; house-breaking.
- 3(c): Compound from cerebrospinal fluid of horseshoe crab; extract of Calotrophis gigantea.
- 3(d): Paracetamol analgesic property; ethyl alcohol anti-knocking; Aspirin cardiovascular use; Chloroquine for Sarcoidosis; metric time showing device (101/Bom/72).
- 3(e): Sugar and colorants soft drink; Paracetamol and Ibuprofen composition; synergistic admixtures patentable.
- 3(f): Arrangement/rearrangement of pouch layers; Franz Zaver Huemer; apparatus for metallic bellows; head scarf cum neck covering (ORA/34/09/PT/KOL).
- 3(h): Method of producing mushroom (64/Cal/79); cultivation of algae (445/Del/93).
- 3(i): Medicinal methods; surgical methods; curative methods; prophylactic methods; diagnostic methods; therapeutic methods; animal treatment; cosmetic treatment; surgical/therapeutic/diagnostic instruments patentable.
- 3(j): Ex vivo educated autologous NK T cells; method of producing hybrid seeds; Dimminaco A.G. v. Controller of Patents (living organism in vaccine patentable).
- 3(k): Mathematical methods; business methods; algorithms; computer programme per se.
- 3(l): Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works.
- 3(m): Method of learning language; method of playing chess; method of teaching.
- 3(n): Presentation of information.
- 3(o): Topography of integrated circuits.
- 3(p): Turmeric wound healing; neem pesticidal; pigeon serum for paralysis.

## 6. Chapter 4: International Patent Classification

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch4-001 -->

### 4.1 Importance, Necessity and Details
- IPC established by Strasbourg Agreement 1971.
- Hierarchical system of language-independent symbols.
- Indian Patent Office follows IPC for publication under Section 11A and search.

**Layout of Classification Symbols:**
- Sections: 8 sections (A-H).
- Classes: Section symbol + two-digit number (e.g., H01).
- Subclasses: Class symbol + capital letter (e.g., H01S).
- Groups: Subclass symbol + two numbers separated by oblique stroke (e.g., H01S 3/00).
- Main groups: Subclass symbol + one-to-three digit number + /00 (e.g., H01S 3/00).
- Subgroups: Subclass symbol + main group number + / + at least two digits other than 00 (e.g., H01S 3/02).

**Multiple Classifications:**
- Needed when different categories of subject matter constitute invention information.
- Classifying in function-oriented and application-oriented places.
- Multi-aspect classification.

**Classification of Technical Subjects:**
- Accurate identification of technical subject.
- Function-oriented vs application-oriented places.
- Examples of IPC classification.

## 7. Chapter 5: Search for Novelty and Inventive Step

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch5-001 -->

### 5.1 Concepts Including Search Standards
- Read specification thoroughly.
- Prepare precise and concise statement of technical features.
- Understand what is explicitly and implicitly contained.
- Formulate problem and technical solution.

**Search Query:**
- Structured command with keywords, classifications, operators.
- Search strategy: group of queries for relevant citations.
- Iterative process.
- Record search strategy and databases accessed.
- Language of search: keywords, operators (Boolean, proximity), classification.
- Operators: OR, AND, NOT; F, S, P, D, nD, =nD, W, nW, underscore, parentheses.
- Stemming: use root words.
- Classification: tool to overcome language differences.

### 5.2 Guidance (Search Strategy, Databases, Methodology, Recording, Reporting)
**Novelty Search:**
- Search for all identified technical features and functional relationships.
- Anticipatory disclosure must be entirely contained within single document.
- Documents to search: published before priority date; patent documents filed in India before priority date but published after.
- Search report should include: application number, IPC, relevant citations with number and date, paragraph indicating similarities, categories (X, Y, A, P).

### 5.3 Examples
**Search Example 1: Auto Disabled Syringe**
- Claims: needle, hub, hollow barrel, piston, plunger rod, protrusion with locking step, locking groove.
- Search strategies: using keywords and proximity operators, classification, publication date filters.

**Search Example 2: Unified Model for Authoring and Executing Flow-Based and Constraint-Based Workflows**
- Claims: workflow with structured and unstructured activities, runtime engine.
- Search strategies: keywords "workflow", "unstructured", "activity", "task", etc.

**Search Example 3: Method for Coating a Substrate**
- Claims: atomizing deposition material by ultrasonic signal, directing onto substrate, applying in situ or post-deposition treatment.
- Search strategies: keywords "coating", "atomize", "ultrasonic", "substrate".

**Search Example 4: Thermochromic Material**
- Claims: thermochromic material based on supramolecular gel; electron donor and acceptor.
- Search strategies: keywords "thermochromic", "material", "gel", "electron donor", "electron acceptor".

## 8. Chapter 6: Examination Standards and Official Requirements

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch6-001 -->

### 6.1 All Standard Statutory Warranted Objections
- Examiner prepares report incorporating all statutory objections.
- Objections must have legal basis.
- List of standard objections annexed.
- Report open to judicial scrutiny.
- Quote specific provisions of patent law.

### 6.2 No Unwarranted Objections
- Objections without legal basis not taken.
- Example: demanding NBA approval when source disclosed as outside India.
- Example: insisting on power of attorney when applicant files without attorney.

### 6.3 Clear Explanatory Nature of Objections
- Objections well communicative and definitive.
- Vague objections like "claims do not sufficiently define invention" not acceptable.

### 6.4 Comprehensive Examination Report
- Drawn comprehensively.
- Supported by legal provisions and proper reasoning.
- Mutually contradictory objections avoided.

### 6.5 Effective Maintenance of Objections During Examination Cycle
- Objections maintained and withdrawn only with proper reasoning.

### 6.6 Fresh Objections During Amended Stage
- As far as possible, no fresh objection at subsequent stages.
- If facts altered, fresh objection with proper justification.

### 6.7 Adherence to Patent Act and Rules
- Adhere to procedures established by patent law.
- Not by any other convention without legal basis.

### 6.8 Maintenance of Strict Timelines
- Crux of quality is maintaining timelines.
- Quality component attached to product and services.

## 9. Chapter 7: Amendments

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch7-001 -->

### Guidance
- Request for amendment on Form-13 with fee before or after grant.
- State nature of proposed amendment and reasons.
- Amendment of priority date possible (Section 57(5)).
- Allowed only by way of disclaimer, correction, or explanation.
- For incorporation of actual fact.
- No amendment describing matter not in substance disclosed before amendment.
- Amended claims must fall wholly within scope of claim before amendment (Section 59(1)).
- Amended pages in duplicate with cancelled original pages.
- If after grant, nature of amendment published; opposition by interested person (Section 57(3), 57(4), Rule 81(3)).
- Leave to amend obtained by fraud is ground for revocation (Section 64(1)(o)).
- If suit for infringement/revocation pending, Controller shall not pass order (Section 57(1)).
- Section 57 without prejudice to right to amend to comply with Controller's directions before grant (Section 57(6)).
- Form-13 for amendment; petition under Rule 137 for other documents.

**Case Laws:**
- Orissa Cement v. Belpahar Refractories: Controller's power to allow amendments subject to Section 59 restrictions.
- ORA/17/2009/PT/CH [140/2012] IPAB: Section 57(6) does not permit filing defective application to make good defect later; amendments extending scope prohibited.
- ORA/07/2009/PT/CH [109/2013] IPAB: Amendment narrower than original claim, bringing clarity, considered; must stand test of Section 59.
- OA/4/2009/PT/CH [189/2012] IPAB: Adding new features into claims not permissible under Section 59.
- W.P. (C) 801 of 2011 Delhi HC: Priority date can be amended, but no time limit set for F13; cannot file F13 on application that has ceased to exist.
- ORA/6/2009/PT/CH IPAB: Discretion applied in allowing amendments when inordinate delay; inadvertent error argument not accepted.

## 10. Chapter 8: Pre-grant Disposal Procedure

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch8-001 -->

- Any person may file opposition by representation to Controller after publication but before grant.
- Grounds under Section 25(1).
- Date of grant is date Controller orders grant.
- Pre-grant opposition received after grant returned to opponent.
- Person interested may file post-grant opposition.
- Patent not granted before expiry of 6 months from publication under Section 11A.
- Representation includes statement, evidence, request for hearing.
- Controller considers representation after Request for Examination filed.
- Pre-grant opposition considered along with examiner's report.
- If opposition devoid of merit, opportunity of hearing granted if requested.
- If opposition refused, speaking order within 1 month.
- If opposition has merit, notice to applicant with copy of representation.
- Applicant may reply within 3 months.
- Controller may refuse grant or require amendment.
- After hearing, Controller either rejects representation and grants patent or accepts representation and refuses grant, ordinarily within 1 month.
- Refusal order under Section 15 is speaking order.

## 11. Chapter 9: Examination Report Writing Methodology

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch9-001 -->

### Report of Examiner
- Examiner prepares report after examination in electronic module.
- Sends to Controller.
- After consideration under Section 14, examination report with covering letter generated and sent to address for service.

### Approval/Decision of Controller
- On approval, FER sent to applicant with email intimation.
- Controller intervenes in supervisory capacity if report lacking quality parameters.
- Quality parameters mandatory unless proved unwarranted.

### Application Found in Order for Grant
- Applicant complies with requirements on or before date of putting application in order for grant.
- Office prepares file for grant.
- If amended on last date, applicant submits freshly typed copy of amended portion.

### Consideration of Report by Controller and Issuance of FER
- Controller considers report ordinarily within 1 month.
- List of objections sent as FER.
- If no objection and no pre-grant opposition, patent granted at earliest.
- FER sent even when request for examination filed by person interested.
- FER may contain objections: lack of novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability; subject matter under Sections 3 and 4; non-fulfillment of other requirements.
- Applicant must comply within 12 months from FER; else application deemed abandoned under Section 21(1).
- If response/amendment does not satisfy requirements, opportunity of hearing offered.
- If applicant contests objections or refiles specification, opportunity of being heard if requested.
- Controller may specify/permit amendment and grant patent.
- Controller may refuse grant unless amendments made or requirements complied.
- Controller records reasons for disagreement with Examiner.
- No refusal without opportunity of being heard under Section 14.
- Refusal order under Section 15 is speaking order; appealable before IPAB.

## 12. Chapter 10: Examination Procedure at Amended Stage

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch10-001 -->

- Amended specification examined and investigated like original specification (Section 13(3)).
- Controller exercises discretionary power with care and caution.
- Opportunity of being heard before acting adversely.
- Reasons recorded in file.
- Not applicable to deemed provisions.
- Party desiring hearing requests at least 10 days in advance.
- Controller gives 10 days' notice ordinarily.

## 13. Chapter 11: Abandoning, Refusal and Grant Procedure

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch11-001 -->

### Abandoning
- Application treated as abandoned under Section 21 if not put in order for grant within 12 months.
- Points for review before recommending abandonment: FER/SER sent to proper address; no response within prescribed time limits.

### Refusals
- If response filed but official requirements not complied, case may proceed for hearing under Section 14.
- Case may be refused if applicant fails to comply even after hearing, with speaking order.

### Responding to Arguments Presented
- OA/16/2009/PT/DEL [262/2012] IPAB: Controller cannot say no submission made when specific case projected regarding inventive step.

### Clarity of Text
- OA/16/2009/PT/DEL [262/2012] IPAB: Order should fully describe documents cited; heading for each ground; finding against each head.

### Grants
- Patent granted as expeditiously as possible when:
  - Application not refused by Controller.
  - Application not in contravention of Act.
  - No pre-grant representation pending or disposed in favour of applicant.
- Date of grant is date Controller grants patent in file.
- Patent number simultaneously generated.
- Fact of grant and patent number reflected on official website real time.

### Consequences of Grant
- Serial number allotted; Certificate of Patent generated; entry in e-register.
- Complete specification available to public through official website.
- Application, specification, other documents open for public inspection on payment.
- Fact of grant published in official journal.
- Patentee pays accumulated fee within 3 months from recordal.
- Post-grant opposition under Section 25(2) within 12 months from publication of grant.
- Patentee and licensee furnish statement regarding working of patented invention every 6 months.

## 14. Chapter 12: Disposal of Post-grant Opposition

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch12-001 -->

### Post-grant Opposition
- Any person interested may file Notice of Opposition in prescribed format within 12 months from publication of grant.
- Date of grant is date Controller grants patent.
- Opposition filed after grant treated as post-grant opposition.
- Opponent states nature of interest.
- Person interested includes person engaged in/promoting research in same field; organization with manufacturing/trading/financial interest; possessor of patents relating to same subject.
- Grounds under Section 25(2) only.
- Controller notifies patentee without delay.
- Opponent delivers copy of statement and evidence to patentee.
- Patentee may file reply statement and evidence within 2 months.
- If patentee does not contest or file reply, patent deemed revoked.
- Opponent may file evidence in reply within 1 month.
- No further evidence except with leave or direction of Controller.
- Translation of documents in other languages required.
- Evidence by affidavits (Rule 126); exhibits (Rule 127).

### Constitution of Opposition Board
- Controller constitutes Opposition Board by order.
- Board examines notice and documents under Rules 57-60.
- Submits report with reasons on each ground within 3 months.
- Board consists of 3 members with one as Chairman.
- Examiner who dealt with application during prosecution not included.
- Further evidence forwarded to Board for consideration.

### Hearing in Post-grant Opposition Matters
- Controller fixes date and time for hearing, informs parties at least 10 days in advance.
- Party desiring to be heard informs Controller with notice and fee.
- Controller may require Board members present.
- Controller may refuse to hear party not giving notice and fee.
- Party intending to rely on new publication gives notice at least 5 days before hearing.
- After hearing, Controller decides opposition: revoke patent, order amendments, or refuse opposition.
- Speaking order issued.
- If amendment ordered, patentee submits amended documents within reasonable time.

**Case Laws:**
- OA/4/2009/PT/CH IPAB: Both patentee and opponent entitled to know contents of Opposition Board recommendations before hearing; board members may be present.
- OA/13/2011/PT/MUM IPAB: Prior art citations cannot be ignored even if additional documents filed at appeal stage; contesting party given opportunity to defend.

## 15. Chapter 13: Disposal of Post-grant Amendments

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch13-001 -->

- After grant, patentee may apply in Form 13 for amendment.
- Request states nature of proposed amendment and reasons.
- Amendments allowed only by way of disclaimer, correction, or explanation.
- For incorporation of actual fact.
- No amendment describing matter not in substance disclosed before amendment.
- Amended claims must fall wholly within scope of claim before amendment.
- Application for amendment may be published if substantive.
- Amended pages in duplicate with cancelled original pages.
- Any person interested may file notice of opposition in Form 14 within 3 months from publication.
- Controller notifies applicant; opportunity to applicant and opponent.
- Procedure under Rules 57-63 for post-grant opposition applies.
- Amendments allowed after grant published.
- Leave to amend obtained by fraud ground for revocation under Section 64.
- If suit for infringement or revocation proceeding pending, Controller shall not pass order.

## 16. Chapter 14: Review of Controller's Decision

<!-- chunk_id: patent-guidelines-ch14-001 -->

- Review under Section 77 of Patents Act, 1970.
- File Form 24 within time limits under Rule 130.
- Controller acts in accordance with Rule 130; decides on merits.
- Controller has powers of civil court under CPC, 1908.
- Who may file review petition: any person considering himself aggrieved by decree/order from which appeal allowed but not preferred; by decree/order from which no appeal allowed.
- Grounds for review: discovery of new and important matter or evidence; mistake or error apparent on face of record; any other sufficient reason.
- Party not appealing may apply for review notwithstanding pendency of appeal by other party, except where ground common or respondent can present case to Appellate Court.
- Supreme Court: Error apparent on face of record must be self-evident; not established by lengthy and complicated arguments.

## 19. RAG Q&A Extraction

### Q: What is the objective of these guidelines?
**A:** To assist Examiners and Controllers in search and examination of patent applications, ensuring transparent, uniform, and consistent administration of the Patents Act, 1970 and Patents Rules, 2003.

### Q: What are the criteria for patentability?
**A:** Novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability.

### Q: What is novelty?
**A:** An invention is new if not anticipated by prior publication, prior use, prior knowledge, or prior claiming before the filing/priority date.

### Q: What is inventive step?
**A:** A feature involving technical advance or economic significance that makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art (Section 2(1)(ja)).

### Q: What is industrial applicability?
**A:** Capable of being made or used in an industry (Section 2(1)(ac)).

### Q: What are the categories of documents in a search report?
**A:** X (particularly relevant), Y (relevant in combination), A (background art), P (published between priority date and filing date).

### Q: What is the time limit for filing a request for examination?
**A:** Within 48 months from the date of priority or filing, whichever is earlier (Section 11B, Rule 24B).

### Q: What is the time for putting an application in order for grant?
**A:** 12 months from the date of First Examination Report (Section 21, Rule 24B(4)).

### Q: What is the time limit for filing a post-grant opposition?
**A:** Within 12 months from the date of publication of grant of patent (Section 25(2)).

### Q: What are the grounds for pre-grant opposition?
**A:** Grounds under Section 25(1): wrongful obtaining, prior publication, prior claiming, prior public knowledge/use, obviousness, not an invention, insufficient description, false information, convention application not within time, incorrect biological material source, anticipation by traditional knowledge.

### Q: What are the grounds for post-grant opposition?
**A:** Grounds under Section 25(2): same as pre-grant plus any other ground.

### Q: What is the procedure for amendment of patent application?
**A:** File Form 13 with fee; amendment allowed only by way of disclaimer, correction, or explanation; for incorporation of actual fact; must not describe new matter or extend scope of claims.

### Q: What is the role of Opposition Board?
**A:** Constituted by Controller for post-grant opposition; examines notice and documents; submits report with reasons on each ground within 3 months.

### Q: What is the time limit for filing a review petition?
**A:** Within the time limits prescribed in Rule 130.

### Q: What are the grounds for review?
**A:** Discovery of new and important matter or evidence; mistake or error apparent on face of record; any other sufficient reason.

### Q: What is the significance of IPC in patent examination?
**A:** IPC provides language-independent symbols for classification of patents according to technology; used for publication and search.

### Q: What are the exceptions to anticipation?
**A:** Sections 29-34: previous publication, previous communication to Government, public display, public working, use and publication after provisional specification.
