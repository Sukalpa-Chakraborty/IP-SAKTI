# The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001

## 1. Document overview

The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 (Act No. 53 of 2001) establishes an effective system for the protection of plant varieties, the rights of farmers and plant breeders, and the encouragement of the development of new varieties. It gives effect to India's TRIPS obligation (Article 27(3)(b)) to protect plant varieties by patents or an effective *sui generis* system — India chose this sui generis system.

| Item | Position |
|---|---|
| Enactment | Act No. 53 of 2001 (in force from dates notified by the Central Government — verify commencement) |
| Subordinate legislation | Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Rules, 2003 (as amended) and Authority regulations |
| Authority | The **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Authority** (body corporate, established under section 3; headquartered at **New Delhi**; website plantauthority.gov.in) |
| Registry | The **Plant Varieties Registry** (section 12) with the **National Register of Plant Varieties** (section 13); Registrar-General is ex-officio member-secretary of the Authority |
| Appellate forum | Chapter VIII creates the Plant Varieties Protection Appellate Tribunal (section 54) — note that appellate functions were transferred to the **High Courts** following the Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021; verify the current forum |

**Why it matters for Ayurveda:** the source plants of Ayurvedic medicines are increasingly cultivated. New or improved cultivars of medicinal plants (ashwagandha, tulsi, sarpagandha, stevia, aloe, neem, etc.) can be protected under this Act — while farmers' entitlements to save, reuse and share seed remain statutorily protected.

## 2. Core definitions (Section 2)

| Term | Statutory meaning (abridged — read the section for exact text) |
|---|---|
| **Variety (s.2(za))** | A plant grouping, except micro-organism, within a single botanical taxon of the lowest known rank, which can be (i) defined by the expression of the characteristics resulting from a given genotype; (ii) distinguished from any other plant grouping by at least one of those characteristics; and (iii) considered as a unit with regard to its suitability for being propagated, remaining unchanged after such propagation. Includes propagating material, extant variety, transgenic variety, farmers' variety and essentially derived variety |
| **Extant variety (s.2(j))** | A variety available in India which is (i) notified under section 5 of the Seeds Act, 1966; or (ii) a **farmers' variety**; or (iii) a variety about which there is common knowledge; or (iv) any other variety in the **public domain** |
| **Farmer (s.2(k))** | A person who cultivates crops himself, or supervises cultivation through another, or conserves/preserves wild species or traditional varieties (severally or jointly) or adds value through selection and identification of useful properties |
| **Farmers' variety (s.2(l))** | A variety (i) traditionally cultivated and evolved by farmers in their fields; or (ii) a wild relative or land race of a variety about which the farmers possess common knowledge |
| **Breeder (s.2(c))** | A person/group of persons, a farmer/group of farmers, or an institution which has bred, evolved or developed a variety |
| **Essentially derived variety (s.2(i))** | A variety predominantly derived from an initial variety while retaining its essential characteristics, clearly distinguishable, and conforming (except for derivation differences) to the initial variety |
| **Benefit sharing (s.2(b))** | Such proportion of the benefit accruing to a breeder (or from an agent/licensee) for which a claimant is entitled, **as determined by the Authority under section 26** |
| **Propagating material / seed (ss.2(r), 2(x))** | Plant/part capable of regeneration; seed = living embryo or propagule capable of regeneration true to type |
| **Gene Fund (s.2(m))** | The National Gene Fund constituted under section 45 |

## 3. The Authority and the Registry

- **Section 3 — Establishment of the Authority:** a body corporate with perpetual succession; headed by a Chairperson; composition includes ex-officio members (Agriculture Commissioner, ICAR Crop Sciences, Joint Secretary (Seeds), Horticulture Commissioner, Director NBPGR, representatives of Biotechnology, Environment, Law, plus nominated members from farmers' organisations, tribal organisations, the seed industry, an agricultural university, a women's agricultural organisation and State Governments); the **Registrar-General is the ex-officio member-secretary**.
- **Section 8 — General functions:** promote development of new varieties and protect farmers' and breeders' rights; register **extant varieties**; characterise and document registered varieties; **document, index and catalogue farmers' varieties**; ensure availability of seed (including compulsory licensing where the breeder fails to make seed available); collect statistics including contributions of persons in evolving varieties; maintain the Register.
- **Section 11 — Powers of the Authority/Registrar:** powers of a civil court for evidence, witnesses, documents and costs (executable as a civil court decree).
- **Section 12/13 — Plant Varieties Registry and National Register:** entries record breeders' names/addresses, rights, denomination particulars, seed/propagating material and salient features.

## 4. Registrability — Sections 14 to 15 (NDUS)

### 4.1 Who may apply (Section 14)

An application may be made for a variety that is (a) of genera/species specified by the Central Government under section 29(2); or (b) an **extant variety**; or (c) a **farmers' variety**.

### 4.2 Who is eligible (Section 16)

- the person claiming to be the **breeder**, or the breeder's **successor** or **assignee**;
- any **farmer, group of farmers or community of farmers** claiming to be the breeder;
- a person **authorised** in the prescribed manner by any of the above;
- any **university or publicly funded agricultural institution** claiming to be the breeder.

(Individuals or jointly.)

### 4.3 The tests — Section 15 (the heart of the Act)

| Criterion | Meaning (Section 15(3)) |
|---|---|
| **Novelty** | At the filing date, the propagating or harvested material has **not been sold or otherwise disposed of** by or with the consent of the breeder/successor for exploitation: **(i) in India earlier than one year; (ii) outside India — earlier than six years for trees or vines, or four years in any other case.** A trial that has not been sold does not affect novelty; and material becoming common knowledge other than through such sale does not destroy novelty (as the provisos provide) |
| **Distinctiveness** | Clearly distinguishable by **at least one essential characteristic** from any other variety whose existence is a matter of common knowledge in any country at the filing date (an application leading to a granted right in a convention country is deemed to render the variety common knowledge from its filing date) |
| **Uniformity** | Subject to the variation to be expected from the particular features of its propagation, sufficiently uniform in its essential characteristics |
| **Stability** | Its essential characteristics remain **unchanged after repeated propagation** (or at the end of each cycle for a particular cycle of propagation) |

**Extant varieties (Section 15(2)):** registration is available within a specified period if the variety conforms to **distinctiveness, uniformity and stability as specified by the regulations** — the novelty bar does not apply in the same way, reflecting the Act's aim to bring existing/traditional varieties into the register.

### 4.4 Denomination (Sections 17 and 15(4))

- Every variety must have a **single, distinct denomination** (a "compulsory variety denomination").
- Refused if it does not identify the variety, consists solely of figures, is liable to mislead or cause confusion about characteristics/value/identity or the breeder's identity, is not different from a denomination of a variety of the same or closely related species already registered, is likely to deceive the public, likely to hurt religious sentiments, is barred under the Emblems and Names Act, 1950, or is **comprised solely or partly of a geographical name** (unless the Registrar is satisfied it is honest use).
- **Section 17(4):** notwithstanding the Trade Marks Act, 1999, a variety denomination **shall not be registered as a trade mark** — denomination and trade mark systems stay separate.

### 4.5 Application content (Section 18) and testing (Section 19)

- Form as specified by regulations; the **denomination**; an **affidavit that the variety contains no gene/gene sequence involving terminator technology**; **complete passport data of parental lines** with the **geographical location in India from which the genetic material was taken** and information on the contribution of any farmer, village community, institution or organisation; a statement bringing out NDUS characteristics; fees; and a **declaration that the genetic/parental material was lawfully acquired**.
- **Farmers' variety applications** are simplified: the proviso to section 18(1) disapplies clauses (b) to (i) (the applicant uses the prescribed simpler form).
- **Section 19 — Test:** the applicant must supply seed for testing against the prescribed standards; tests are conducted in the prescribed manner (with fee).

### 4.6 Examination, advertisement and opposition (Sections 20-22)

1. Registrar may accept, conditionally accept, require amendment, or reject (with a reasonable opportunity of being heard).
2. Accepted applications are **advertised** (with the variety's specification/photographs/drawings).
3. **Opposition: within three months of advertisement**, on payment of fee; grounds include entitlement to breeder's right, non-registrability, public interest, or **adverse effect on the environment** (section 21(3)).
4. Applicant must file a **counter-statement within two months** of receipt of the opposition copy (failure = application deemed abandoned); evidence, hearing and reasoned decision follow (sections 21(4)-(9), 22).

### 4.7 Essentially derived varieties (Section 23)

EDVs are examined by the Authority; registration only where the EDV is derived from the initial variety and satisfies section 15 (with the appropriate substitutions); the EDV breeder's rights under section 28 apply subject to agreed terms with the initial-variety breeder.

## 5. Duration, effect and fee (Section 24, 28, 35)

- **Section 24(2) — Certificate:** issued after acceptance and either expiry of the opposition period without opposition, or rejection of opposition.
- **Section 24(6) — Term (total validity):**
  - **Trees and vines: 18 years** from the date of registration;
  - **Extant variety: 15 years** from the date of its notification under section 5 of the Seeds Act, 1966;
  - **Other cases: 15 years** from the date of registration.
  - The certificate is initially valid for **nine years (trees and vines) or six years (other crops)** and may be reviewed and renewed for the remaining period on the prescribed fee — i.e. renewal is required to reach the full term.
- **Section 28 — Registration confers the exclusive right** to produce, sell, market, distribute, import, export, etc., the registered variety (read the section for the exact bundle of rights and the researcher/farmer exceptions).
- **Section 35 — Annual fee/royalty:** payment of the annual fee is required; **default leads to forfeiture of registration** (diary management is critical).
- **Section 30 — Researcher's rights:** a researcher may use a registered variety for experimentation and research (including breeding other varieties) — the statutory research exemption (read the section's conditions).

## 6. Farmers' rights (Chapter VI) — Sections 39 to 44

**Section 39 — Farmers' Right (verbatim structure):**

1. **Section 39(1)(i):** a farmer who has bred or developed a **new variety** is entitled to registration and protection **in like manner as a breeder**.
2. **Section 39(1)(ii):** a **farmers' variety** is entitled to registration if the application contains the declaration specified in section 18(1)(h) (lawful acquisition of genetic material).
3. **Section 39(1)(iii):** a farmer engaged in **conservation of genetic resources of land races and wild relatives** of economic plants, and their improvement through selection and preservation, is entitled to **recognition and reward from the Gene Fund** — provided the material has been used as donors of genes in registrable varieties.
4. **Section 39(1)(iv):** a farmer is **deemed entitled to save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share or sell his farm produce — including seed — of a variety protected under this Act**, in the same manner as before the Act came into force.
   - **Proviso:** the farmer **shall not be entitled to sell branded seed** of a protected variety — "branded seed" means seed put in a package/container and labelled in a manner indicating it is of a protected variety (Explanation to section 39).
5. **Section 39(2) — Performance warranty/compensation:** where propagating material sold to a farmer/farmer group fails to give the **expected performance disclosed by the breeder** under given conditions, the farmer may claim compensation before the Authority, which hears both sides and can direct the breeder to pay.

**Section 40 — Disclosure of contribution:** every application must disclose **the use of genetic material conserved by any tribal or rural families** in the breeding/development of the variety; **wilful and knowing concealment** can lead to rejection of the application.

**Section 41 — Rights of communities:** persons/organisations may file, on behalf of a village or local community, a claim attributable to the community's contribution to the evolution of a variety; a notified centre verifies; the Authority, after hearing the breeder, may grant **compensation** (recoverable as an arrear of land revenue) which must be **deposited in the Gene Fund** by the breeder.

**Section 42 — Protection of innocent infringement:** a farmer who, at the time of infringement, was **not aware of the existence of the right** does not infringe; courts must not grant relief or take cognizance against such a farmer.

**Section 43 — Authorisation of farmers' variety:** where an essentially derived variety is derived from a **farmers' variety**, the authorisation of the EDV breeder requires the **consent of the farmers/group/community who contributed** to its preservation or development.

**Section 44 — Exemption from fees:** a farmer, group of farmers or village community **is not liable to pay any fee** in any proceeding before the Authority, Registrar, Tribunal or High Court (including inspection/copy fees).

**Section 45 — National Gene Fund:** credited with benefit-sharing deposits (section 26), annual fee/royalty (section 35), community compensation (section 41) and contributions; applied for the purposes prescribed (including recognition/reward under section 39(1)(iii) and benefit sharing).

## 7. Benefit sharing (Section 26)

1. On registration, the Authority publishes the certificate contents and **invites benefit-sharing claims**.
2. Claims may be filed by Indian citizens or entities established in India, within the prescribed period and with fee.
3. The breeder may **oppose** the claim; the Authority hears both sides.
4. The Authority's order states the **amount of benefit sharing**, taking into account: **(a) the extent and nature of the use of the claimant's genetic material in developing the variety; and (b) the commercial utility and market demand of the variety.**
5. The amount is deposited by the breeder into the **National Gene Fund** (section 45).

**Note on section numbering:** benefit sharing is in **section 26** ("Determination of Benefit Sharing by Authority") with the fund in section 45 — not section 44 (which is the fee-exemption provision) and not section 35. Secondary sources frequently mis-cite these.

## 8. Exclusions, compulsory licence and infringement

- **Section 29 — Exclusion of certain varieties**, and the power of the Central Government to **specify the genera and species registrable** (section 29(2)) — check the current notification before filing (registrable species lists are notified for crops).
- **Section 31 — Convention country applications:** special provisions for applicants from convention countries (reciprocity — section 32).
- **Chapter VII — Compulsory licence (sections 47-53):** the Authority may order a compulsory licence where the reasonable requirements of the public are not satisfied (section 48 sets out when public requirements are deemed not met), with provisions on terms, duration, revocation and modification.
- **Chapter X — Infringement, offences, penalties:**
  - **Section 64 — Infringement** (use of the registered variety/denomination contrary to the exclusive right);
  - **Section 65 — Suit** for infringement;
  - **Section 66 — Relief in suits** (injunction, damages, etc.);
  - **Sections 68-73 — Offences:** prohibition on applying the denomination of a registered variety, meaning of falsely applying, **penalty for applying a false denomination**, penalty for selling varieties to which a false denomination is applied, penalty for **falsely representing a variety as registered**, and enhanced penalty for subsequent offences;
  - **Section 74 — No offence in certain cases** and section 75 (exemption of persons employed in the ordinary course of business);
  - **Section 77 — Offences by companies.**
  - **Penalty-figures caution:** this summary deliberately omits imprisonment/fine amounts — read sections 70-73 in the official text.
- **Section 81:** registered agents and registered licensees may institute suits.

## 9. ITPGRFA and international interface

1. **ITPGRFA (International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture):** the Treaty establishes a **Multilateral System** of facilitated access for Annex I (food and agriculture) crop genetic resources under a **Standard Material Transfer Agreement (SMTA)**, with benefit-sharing where a recipient commercialises a product incorporating the material. India is a Contracting Party (verify India's ratification/accession date and current obligations).
2. **Nagoya Protocol / Biological Diversity Act:** access to biological resources of India and associated knowledge for R&D or commercialisation is separately governed by the biodiversity legislation (prior approval/intimation, benefit sharing) — the PPV&FR registration of a variety built on such resources does not by itself discharge those obligations.
3. **TRIPS Article 27(3)(b):** this Act is India's sui generis response; plant varieties are excluded from patents in India by section 3(j) of the Patents Act (see the section 3(j)/3(p) corpus document).
4. **UPOV:** India is **not** a member of UPOV 1991; the PPV&FR Act is deliberately *not* UPOV-conformant in respect of farmers' rights (UPOV compliance issues are a standing debate). Do not describe Indian law as UPOV-style protection.
5. **Seeds Act, 1966 and Seeds (Control) Order:** varietal notification/registration under the Seeds Act interacts with extant-variety registration (section 2(j)(i) and section 24(6)(ii)).

## 10. Relevance to Ayurveda and medicinal plant cultivars

| Scenario | Route under this Act |
|---|---|
| Company develops a high-withanolide ashwagandha clone | **New variety** — NDUS (section 15(1)); novelty windows 1 yr India / 4 yrs abroad (6 for trees/vines) |
| Grower registers a traditional landrace used for Ayurvedic drugs | **Farmers' variety / extant variety** — simpler application (proviso to section 18(1)); DUS as per regulations |
| Breeders used germplasm conserved by a tribal community | **Disclosure duty (section 40)**; community claim and compensation (section 41); benefit sharing (section 26) |
| Company wants exclusive seed sales | Registration + exclusive right (section 28) — but remember the **farmers' save/reuse/sell right (section 39(1)(iv))** and the branded-seed limitation |
| Public institution improved a farmers' variety | **Essentially derived variety** (section 23) and consent requirements for farmers' varieties (section 43) |
| Breeder fails to supply seed | **Compulsory licence** (section 47) and Authority's function under section 8(2)(e) |
| Grower unaware of right | **Innocent infringement protection** (section 42) |

**Boundary notes:**

- Plant variety rights protect the **variety**, not the **use** of the plant in medicine (traditional use knowledge is not protected here) and not the **formulation**.
- A cultivar name cannot double as a trade mark (section 17(4)) — brand separately.
- A registered variety does not authorise use of biodiversity of India — check the Biological Diversity legislation separately.

## 11. What an Ayurvedic product owner must check

- [ ] Is the plant grouping a **variety** in the statutory sense (s.2(za)) and is the species among those notified under section 29(2)?
- [ ] Which route: **new variety** (NDUS + novelty) vs **extant/farmers' variety** (simpler application, DUS per regulations)?
- [ ] Has there been any **sale** of propagating material? (1 year India / 4 years abroad / 6 years abroad for trees and vines — section 15(3)(a).)
- [ ] Does the application contain the **passport data, geographical origin of the material, contributors' details** and the **lawful acquisition** declaration (section 18)?
- [ ] Has the contribution of **tribal/rural families** been disclosed (section 40)? Failure on purpose can sink the application.
- [ ] Is the **denomination** compliant (section 15(4), section 17) — not solely geographical, not confusing, distinct from existing denominations, not registered as a trade mark?
- [ ] Has the **benefit-sharing claim window** been monitored (section 26) and funds deposited into the **Gene Fund** where applicable (section 45)?
- [ ] Are **annual fees** diarised (section 35 — default forfeits registration), with renewal to reach the full term (section 24(6): 15 years / 18 years for trees and vines)?
- [ ] Have **opposition deadlines** been tracked (three months from advertisement — section 21(2); counter-statement two months)?
- [ ] Has the **research exemption, farmers' rights and compulsory licence** exposure been priced into the seed business model?
- [ ] Have the **biodiversity access** obligations (Biodiversity Act) been cleared separately for the source material?
