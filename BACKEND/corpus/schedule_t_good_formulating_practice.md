# Schedule T: Good Formulating Practice of Indian Systems of Medicine

## Document Metadata
* **Chapter**: 15[cite: 2]
* **Title**: Schedule T - Good Formulating Practice of Indian Systems of Medicine[cite: 2]
* **Authors**: Devender Sharma, Shweta Parihar[cite: 2]
* **Target Systems**: Indian Systems of Medicine (Ayurveda, Siddha, and Unani)[cite: 2]

## Infrastructure and Facility Space Requirements
* **Quality Assurance**: Quality assurance is an integral part of all systems of medicine, requiring complete compliance[cite: 2].
* **Plant Layout**: Formulating plants must maintain sufficient, dedicated space for:
  * Formulating process areas[cite: 2].
  * Quality control sections[cite: 2].
  * Finished goods storage[cite: 2].
  * Receiving and storing raw materials[cite: 2].
  * Storing rejected goods/medicines[cite: 2].
  * Container cleansing areas with proper water supply[cite: 2].
* **Process Space & Layout**: Formulating and quality control areas must be spacious enough to enable the orderly arrangement of equipment and materials[cite: 2].

## Storage and Equipment Specifications
* **Storage Areas**: Adequate space must be allocated for the proper storage of:
  * Raw materials[cite: 2]
  * Packing materials[cite: 2]
  * Finished products[cite: 2]
* **Machinery & Equipment**: The size and nature of machinery/equipment must directly correspond to the scale of operation and product specifications[cite: 2].

## Documentation and Record-Keeping
* **Batch Manufacturing Records (BMR)**: Manufacturers are required to maintain a Batch Manufacturing Record for every formulation process[cite: 2].
* **Sales and Distribution Records**: Detailed records for the sale and delivery of every batch of Ayurveda, Siddha, and Unani medicines must be managed[cite: 2].

## Quality Control Regulations
* **Testing Facilities**: Every licensee must establish quality regulation facilities either:
  * Within their own premises (in-house laboratory)[cite: 2].
  * By utilizing an authorized government testing laboratory[cite: 2].
