# Study Methodology and Strategy for Demand and Supply of Medicinal Plants in India

## 1. Document Overview

This is Chapter 2 of a report on the demand and supply of medicinal plants in India. It details the study methodology and strategy employed to assess the complex herbal sector, which includes over 8,600 licensed domestic herbal units, thousands of cottage-level unregulated units, millions of folk healers and households, and a complex trade web. The chapter outlines the identification of key issues, study strategy, data sources, sampling designs for different user categories (industry, households, exports), assessment of supply (wild, cultivation, imports), study of raw drug markets, botanical correlation, wastage estimation, policy analysis, highlighting red-listed species, and synthesis of data.

The study was commissioned by the National Medicinal Plant Board (NMPB) and carried out by the Indian Council of Forestry Research & Education (ICFRE) in partnership with the Foundation for Revitalization of Local Health Traditions (FRLHT).

## 3. Introduction

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-intro-001 -->

India's herbal sector comprises 8,610 licensed herbal units, thousands of cottage-level unregulated herbal units, millions of folk healers, and household-level users of thousands of herbal raw drugs, all connected through a complex trade web. Assessing demand and supply in this diverse arena is challenging. This study employs a comprehensive sampling technique and technical rigour to understand the dynamics of demand and supply within the prevailing legal and administrative environment.

## 4. Identification of Key Issues

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-key-issues-001 -->

An Inception Workshop was held at Dehradun on 08 May 2015 to identify key issues and develop strategy. Key issues identified:

- Diversity of herbal raw drugs used by local communities, registered ISM practitioners, and domestic herbal industry, along with estimated annual quantum.
- Diversity and annual quantum of medicinal plant species in foreign trade (export and import).
- Sources of supply: wild harvested, cultivated, and imported.
- Existing and potential trade/marketing of medicinal plant resources for optimizing benefits to local communities.
- Legal and administrative environment related to harvest, trade, and use.

## 5. Study Strategy

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-study-strategy-001 -->

The herbal sector was categorized into two operational arenas:
- **Consumption (demand)** – use by industry, practitioners, households, and exports.
- **Supply** – wild, cultivation, and imports.

Due to data deficiencies, annual demand was assumed equal to annual consumption by various user categories. Supply from all sources was taken as equal to demand. The estimated demand was integrated with supply sources to test validity and assess gaps and pressure on natural resources.

Data sources included:
- Previous reports (Ved & Goraya 2008; EXIM Bank 2003; CERPA 2002; Planning Commission Task Force 2000).
- Department of AYUSH website for licensed herbal units.
- DGCIS Foreign Trade Statistics (2005-06 onwards).
- Annual statements from domestic herbal industry under Section 157A of Drugs and Cosmetics Act.
- State Forest Department records (weak).
- Ethno-medicinal literature for botanical correlation.

## 6. Computation of Annual Demand

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-demand-001 -->

Total estimated annual demand of herbal raw drugs = total estimated annual consumption by:
- Domestic herbal industry (Rui)
- Household level users (Rut)
- Exports (Rue)
- Resource wastage during handling (RHw)

Formula: \( f_nD \simeq \sum Rui_j + \sum Rut_j + \sum Rue_k + (\sum RHw) \)

Quantities converted to equivalent dry weight:
- Fresh fruits: 5:1 (fresh:dry)
- Extracts: 1:8 (extract:dry weight of raw drug)
- Exports: no conversion to maintain compatibility with DGCIS data.

### 6.1 Estimation of Consumption by Domestic Herbal Industry

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-industry-001 -->

Assumptions:
- Annual consumption of raw drugs by volume is a fair indicator of annual turnover.
- Resource use largely independent of ISM streams.
- Only limited entities traded in large quantities (>100 MT/year).
- All licensed small and very small units taken as operational to offset unlicensed cottage units.

Ministry of AYUSH recognizes 8,610 licensed manufacturing units (2011). Industry categorized by annual turnover: Large (>₹50 crore), Medium (₹5-50 crore), Small (₹1-5 crore), Very Small (<₹1 crore).

### 6.2 Estimation of Consumption by Households and Folk Practitioners

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-household-001 -->

Multistage sampling: 13 states, 6 eco-geographical regions, 39 districts (3 per state), 195 villages (5 per district), 15 households per village (total 2,925 planned). Due to elections and insurgency, data collected from 2,450 households across 15 states. Folk healers identified using a priori information. Pre-tested semi-structured questionnaires used. Plants identified via local floras, experts, and ethno-medicinal literature.

### 6.3 Computation of Exports

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-exports-001 -->

DGCIS data for 2005-06 to 2014-15 analyzed. ITC (HS) codes deciphered for species-level export. Validated through consultations with exporters, regulating agencies, and data management agencies.

## 7. Assessment of Supply of Medicinal Plants

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-supply-001 -->

Supplies assumed equal to demand. Data collated from various sources.

### 7.1 Estimation of Supplies from Wild Resources

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-wild-001 -->

Wild resources include forests and habitats outside forests (farm lands, road/rail sides, canal banks, ponds, wastelands). Major supply from forests. State Forest Departments, MFP Federations, Corporations, Cooperatives approached. Data generally weak. Field visits documented wild harvest and trade chain. Local gatherers/agents provided qualitative and quantitative information.

### 7.2 Estimation of Supplies from Cultivation

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-cultivation-001 -->

No national species-wise data. Information inferred from resource use side. For species sourced only from cultivation (e.g., isabgol, mentha, senna), use figures give production estimate. Strategy:
- Record source (wild/cultivated) during market survey.
- Record buy-back arrangements during industry visits.
- Review literature and ICFRE/FRLHT databases.
- Field visits to cultivation sites; discussions with farmers.

### 7.3 Computation of Imports

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-imports-001 -->

DGCIS data for 2005-06 to 2014-15 analyzed. ITC (HS) codes deciphered for species-level import. Validated through consultations with importers, regulating agencies, and data management agencies.

## 8. Study of Raw Drug Markets

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-markets-001 -->

More than 40 herbal raw drug mandis of different types visited. Information generated on:
- Diversity of botanical entities in trade (trade names, prices).
- Species in significant trade (>100 MT/year) with supply chain/trade web.
- Red-listed species of high conservation concern.

Data also from retail shops. Used to compile Comprehensive Inventory of Medicinal Plants in Commercial Trade.

## 9. Establishing Botanical Correlation of Herbal Raw Drugs

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-botanical-001 -->

Biggest challenge: identity of raw drugs in trade. List of controversial groups (shankhapushapi, daruharidra, jivanti, rasna, gokshura, vidari, vidanga, etc.) prepared. Samples procured from mandis for confirmation. Samples helped confirm botanical identity of some controversial drugs (jivanti, gurhal, ativisa, etc.). Controversial drugs enlisted for detailed investigation.

## 10. Assessment of Raw Drug Wastage

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-wastage-001 -->

Wastage from collection to use due to insect/fungal attacks, poor post-harvest handling, poor packaging during transit. Percentage unknown, not factored in previous studies. This study estimated wastage along supply chain for selected herbs and computed average wastage percentage.

## 11. Analysis of Policies and Regulatory Regimes

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-policy-001 -->

Main policy and regulatory issues analyzed:
- ITC (HS) Code for foreign trade classification.
- Transit Rules for wild harvested and cultivated material.
- MFP Federations, Corporations, Cooperatives and trade.
- Devolving rights over MFPs to local communities.
- Implications of Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

Literature reviewed and expert consultations held for recommendations.

## 12. Highlighting Red-Listed Medicinal Plants

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-redlist-001 -->

Reports on threat status using IUCN Red-List Categories and Criteria examined. Lists compared with high-trade species. Threatened species flagged to guide conservation and ensure sustainable supplies.

## 13. Synthesis of Data and Compilation of Study Report

<!-- chunk_id: herbal-ch2-synthesis-001 -->

Data collated, cleaned, computed, and analyzed per study arena. Qualitative information grouped and correlated with quantitative data. Prioritization for data collation:
- Volume of annual trade (>100 MT/year).
- High monetary value.
- Species of conservation concern.

Draft report presented at National Consultation on 23 March 2016 at Dehradun. Modified based on stakeholder comments. Highlights presented to NMPB committee chaired by Secretary AYUSH on 01 September 2016. Comments incorporated to refine report. Recommendations made based on inferences.

## 15. RAG Q&A Extraction

### Q: What is the main objective of this chapter?
**A:** To describe the study methodology and strategy used to assess the demand and supply of medicinal plants in India, including sampling designs, data sources, and analytical approaches.

### Q: How was the domestic herbal industry stratified?
**A:** Into four size-wise strata based on annual turnover: Large (>₹50 crore), Medium (₹5-50 crore), Small (₹1-5 crore), Very Small (<₹1 crore). Sampling was proportional to size.

### Q: How many households were surveyed for consumption data?
**A:** Data was collected from 2,450 households across 15 states, using a multistage sampling design.

### Q: How was export and import data analyzed?
**A:** Using DGCIS data for 2005-06 to 2014-15, deciphering ITC (HS) codes for species-level analysis, and validating through consultations with exporters/importers and regulating agencies.

### Q: What is the equation for estimated annual demand?
**A:** \( f_nD \simeq \sum Rui_j + \sum Rut_j + \sum Rue_k + (\sum RHw) \), where Rui = industry consumption, Rut = household consumption, Rue = exports, RHw = resource wastage.

### Q: How were fresh fruits and extracts converted to dry weight?
**A:** Fresh fruits: factor of 5:1 (fresh:dry); extracts: factor of 1:8 (extract:dry weight of raw drug). Exports not converted to maintain DGCIS compatibility.

### Q: What was the strategy for assessing supply from cultivation?
**A:** Record source during market survey, record buy-back arrangements during industry visits, review literature and databases, and conduct field visits to cultivation sites.

### Q: What are the key policy and regulatory issues analyzed?
**A:** ITC (HS) Code, Transit Rules, MFP Federations/Corporations/Cooperatives, devolving rights over MFPs to local communities, and implications of Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

### Q: How were red-listed medicinal plants highlighted?
**A:** By comparing IUCN Red-List reports with high-trade species lists, and flagging threatened species for conservation action.

### Q: What was the sample size for the domestic herbal industry survey?
**A:** Planned sample size was 396 units, stratified proportionally: 25 large, 80 medium, 100 small, 191 very small.
