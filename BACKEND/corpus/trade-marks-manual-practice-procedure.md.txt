# Manual of Trade Marks Practice & Procedure

## 1. Document Overview

This is a draft manual published by the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs & Trade Marks, Government of India, Ministry of Commerce & Industries. It explains the practice and procedure followed by the Trade Marks Registry under the **Trade Marks Act, 1999** and the **Trade Marks Rules, 2002**.

The manual aims to bring uniformity and consistency in trade marks administration. It is a guide; if any statement conflicts with the Act or Rules, the Act/Rules prevail. It references Indian case law and is expected to be updated periodically.

## 3. Chapter I: Filing of Documents in Trade Marks Registry

### Section A: General Filing Requirements

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch1-secA-001 -->

- A document may be filed by a person or duly authorised agent at the appropriate office.
- Appropriate offices: Head Office (Mumbai) and branch offices (Ahmedabad, Chennai, Delhi, Kolkata).
- Certain documents under Rule 8(2) may be filed only at Head Office unless Registrar directs otherwise.
- Relevant provisions: Section 18; Rules 4, 8, 15.

#### Requirements as to Content

Applications, notices and requests must generally include:
- Trade mark number(s)
- Name and address for service
- Name of proprietor if different from applicant
- Details and grounds of action
- Appropriate class(es)
- Proprietor code or agent code, if allotted
- Signature(s) and name of signatory
- Date and place of filing

For online filing, digital signature is treated as signing.

#### Who Can Sign?

- Applicant or authorised agent.
- Legal practitioner, registered trade mark agent, or person in sole and regular employment of principal.
- Partnership firm: at least one partner signing on behalf of firm.
- Body corporate: director, secretary, or other principal officer; capacity must be stated.

#### Address for Service

- Person with no principal place of business in India must supply an address for service in India.
- Indian applicants may also mention address for service.
- Change of address for service: Form TM-16 or TM-50 with fee.
- Correspondence sent to first addressee where multiple addresses given.

#### Fees

- As per First Schedule, Trade Marks Rules 2002.
- Payment by cash, bank draft, cheque drawn on scheduled bank, or online payment through ipindia.nic.in.

#### Non-Compliance

- Fee-bearing documents: if appropriate fee not paid, deemed not filed.
- Dishonoured cheque / non-realised demand draft = fee not paid.
- Non-fee-bearing documents: not accepted if unsigned or missing application/opposition/rectification/RU/CC number.

#### Filing Process

1. Received through FRONT OFFICE module of TMS.
2. Documents addressed to Registrar received by office superintendent or assistant/clerk.
3. R&E section opens post; fee-bearing documents sent to cash counter; acknowledgement generated for non-fee documents.
4. Stamp with date and signature affixed.
5. Data entry in EDP module.
6. Device marks scanned in original colours and uploaded.
7. R&E section digitises incoming papers/documents.
8. Post-registration requests with multiple marks: scanned copies uploaded against every registered trademark covered.

### Section B: Filing of Trade Mark Application

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch1-secB-001 -->

#### Who Can File?

- A person claiming to be proprietor of the trade mark in relation to goods and/or services.
- “Person” includes: natural person, body incorporate, partnership firm, HUF, association of persons (collective marks), joint proprietor, trust, society, government authority/undertaking.

#### Jointly Owned Trademark

- Two or more persons who use or propose to use a trademark independently may apply as joint proprietors.
- Circumstances under Section 24(2).

#### Trade Mark May Be

- Already in use; or
- Proposed/intended to be used; or
- Intended to be assigned to a company about to be formed and registered under Companies Act, 1956.

#### Details Required from Applicant

- Individual: full name; business/trading name is additional, not substitute.
- Joint owners: full name of each joint owner.
- Partnership firm: names of all partners.
- Companies: name under which incorporated; abbreviations acceptable.
- Government departments: “Government of India/State of … represented by …”
- Statutory organisations/public sector undertakings: reference to statute; e.g., Life Insurance Corporation of India (established under … Act).
- Conventional application: home country address plus address for service in India.
- Statement of use: date since when mark used, unless proposed to be used; cannot be changed subsequently. Rule 27.
- Clear specification of goods/services; not unrealistically broad. Rule 25(15).

#### Form of Application

- Prescribed forms in Schedule II, Trade Marks Rules 2002.
- Section 18; Rules 12(1), 13(1), 14(2).

#### Information Required

- Name of proprietor(s)
- Valid email ID
- Principal place of business
- Address for service
- Type of mark: word, label, device, etc.
- Convention applications: single priority date; separate applications for different priority dates.

#### Representation of Trademark

- Size: generally no larger than 8 cm x 8 cm.
- Must show all features clearly, be reproducible, and preserve features over time.
- Series marks: endorsements required where colours differ.
- Shape marks: must state if shape trade mark; perspective drawing; concise description; up to 5 further views if needed; photographs acceptable if clear originals.
- Combination of colours: claim as colour trade mark; exact description as per International Classification System of Colours; graphical representation; concise description.
- Non-English/Hindi words or non-Roman/Devnagari characters: English/Hindi translation and/or transliteration required. Rule 33.
- Endorsement format: “The applicant has provided that the translation of the (nationality/language) words appearing in the trademark is …”

#### Specification of Goods/Services

- Single application may cover one or more classes.
- Goods/services grouped by class number and presented in ascending numerical order.
- Use terms from published Classification, available at TMR or www.ipindia.nic.in.
- “All goods”, “all services”, “all other goods”, “all other services” prohibited. Rule 25(15).
- Unrealistically broad specifications may require affidavit or amendment.
- Multi-class applications: if goods/services fall in more than one class, applicant may delete items or add classes with divisional fee and class fee.

#### Signature and Date

- Application must be signed and dated.
- Signatories: applicant or authorised person under Section 145: legal practitioner, trade marks agent, person in sole and regular employment.
- If signed on behalf, name and designation of signatory must be given.
- Trustee applications: signed by managing trustee or authorised person.
- Unauthorised signatory: applicant asked to have authorised person sign.
- Multiple applicants: each individual must sign unless agent signs for all.
- Name of each signatory written legibly below signature.
- If undated, no request for date; filing date is fee stamp date.

#### Fees

- As per Schedule I.
- Specification exceeding 500 characters: excess character fee in Form TM-61. Rule 11(3) & (4), Rule 25(16).

#### Consequences of Non-Compliance

- Registrar notifies deficiencies; one month to rectify.
- If not rectified, application may be treated as abandoned. Rule 36.

#### Data Entry and Vienna Codification

- Data entry in EDP module.
- Device marks scanned in original colours and uploaded.
- Vienna Codification Section codifies figurative elements under Vienna Agreement.

## 4. Chapter II: Examination of Applications

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch2-001 -->

### Introduction and Scope

- After data entry and digitisation, application allotted to examiner.
- Examiner examines under Trade Marks Act & Rules.
- Allotment automatic through TMS in order of filing date/time.
- Expedited examination: Form TM-63, approved by Receipt & EDP Section.
- Examiner typically handles 20 applications.
- Examiner checks:
  - Whether application filed as prescribed.
  - Whether trademark can be refused on relative/absolute grounds.
  - Whether restrictions/conditions/limitations required.
- Examiner searches earlier identical/similar marks and prepares consolidated examination report.
- If unable to examine due to formality requirement, may ask applicant to comply and defer examination.

### Key Examination Points

1. **Identification of Nature of Application**
   - Forms: TM-1, TM-2, TM-51, TM-52, TM-3, TM-64, TM-4, TM-65, TM-66, TM-67, TM-68, TM-69, TM-8, TM-37.
   - If wrong form, examiner examines as per nature and requires correction via TM-16.

2. **Appropriate Name of Applicant**
   - Individual: full name; e.g., “Ram Kumar Verma, an Indian National trading as Verma Trading Company”.
   - Company: e.g., “M/s Singh Industries Limited, a company registered in India under Indian Companies Act 1956”.
   - Firm: names of all partners; guardian for minor partner.
   - Trust: managing trustee/chairman representing trust.
   - Society: secretary or authorised person.
   - Government: “Government of India/Government of (state) represented by (name of department) through its Secretary Mr. xxx”.
   - Government undertaking: reference to statute.
   - Joint applicants: names of all joint proprietors.

3. **Power of Attorney**
   - Form TM-48 duly stamped required if application filed by person other than applicant.
   - Agent can be legal practitioner, registered trade mark agent, or constituted attorney.
   - Power of Attorney in favour of firm without individual agent name not accepted. Section 145, Rule 21.

4. **Principal Place of Business**
   - Must be mentioned.
   - If no principal place in India, address for service in India required. Section 18(3), 143; Rules 17 & 18.

5. **Transliteration and Translation**
   - Non-English/Hindi marks: transliteration into Roman script and translation into English required. Rule 33.
   - Endorsement on application and additional representations.
   - If not provided, examination deferred and deficiency communicated.

6. **Consent for Names/Representations**
   - Consent of person or legal representative required if trademark contains name or representation of that person.

7. **Collective Marks**
   - Draft regulations under Section 63, Rule 128, in triplicate with Form TM-49.
   - Contents: name/addresses of association, object, members, membership conditions, authorised users, control, sanctions, appeal procedure.

8. **Certification Marks**
   - Draft regulations under Section 74, Rule 135, on Form TM-49.
   - Contents: description, nature of business, infrastructure, competence, financial arrangement, non-discrimination undertaking, characteristics indicated, monitoring.

9. **Priority Claims**
   - Convention applications: priority document/certificate from Registry/competent authority required.
   - Section 154; Rule 26.
   - If missing, objection raised.

10. **Classification of Goods and Services**
    - NICE Classification current edition.
    - Section 8; Rule 22(2).
    - If wrong class, examiner suggests appropriate class and requires correction via TM-16.
    - Wide/vague specifications objected under Rule 25(15). Examples: “machines” in Class 7, “electric/electronic apparatus” in Class 9, “miscellaneous services”.

11. **Relative Grounds for Refusal**
    - Section 11(1): identity/similarity with earlier mark and identity/similarity of goods/services causing likelihood of confusion.
    - Earlier trade mark includes registered mark or prior pending application.
    - Identical mark: reproduces all elements without modification, or differences insignificant.
    - Similarity: “deceptively similar”.
    - Likelihood of confusion: probable, not necessary actual confusion.
    - Factors: similarities/differences, distinctiveness of earlier mark, degree of similarity of goods/services.
    - Pianotist case rules: judge by look, sound, goods, customer, surrounding circumstances.
    - General rules: compare marks as whole; no letter-by-letter comparison; average intelligence and imperfect recollection; overall structural, visual, phonetic similarity and similarity of idea.
    - Medicinal products: exacting judicial scrutiny.
    - Composite marks: if prominent/distinctive portion identical, earlier mark may conflict. Example: CROCIN PLUS vs CROCIN FORTE.
    - Office action wording for Section 11(1) objection.

12. **Absolute Grounds for Refusal**
    - Section 9(1)(a): devoid of distinctive character.
    - Section 9(1)(b): exclusively descriptive of kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, value, geographical origin, time of production, or other characteristics.
    - Section 9(1)(c): customary in current language or bona fide established trade practices.
    - Acquired distinctiveness: evidence of use by affidavit; factors: market share, intensity/geographic spread/duration, investment in promotion, proportion of relevant class identifying goods, statements from chambers/associations.
    - Distinctiveness in various types:
      - Letters/numbers: single/two letters generally devoid; numbers designating quantity, quality, date, size, etc. objectionable.
      - Common names/surnames: prima facie not capable; Section 35 protects bona fide use.
      - Misspellings of descriptive words: still objectionable.
      - Foreign languages: if not known to significant public, not descriptive; if local language descriptive, objection.
      - Domain names: registerable if otherwise registrable; “.com”, “co.in” non-distinctive.
      - Slogans: registrable if distinctive or acquired distinctiveness; plain descriptive slogans objectionable.
      - Combination of colours: colour trademark; single colour usually objectionable; weighty evidence needed.
      - Sound marks: must be clearly stated; graphically represented by musical notes; prima facie no sound marks accepted without evidence of factual distinctiveness.
    - Section 9(2): prohibits marks that deceive/confuse, hurt religious susceptibilities, scandalous/obscene, or prohibited under Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950.
    - Geographical origin deception: e.g., PIAZZA D’ITALIA for clothing, SWISSTEX for watches.
    - Olympic symbols: not to be registered except under authority of Olympic Committee.
    - Religious susceptibilities: names/pictures of Gods/Goddesses generally not per se offensive, but use on certain goods may offend.
    - Scandalous/obscene matter: objective assessment; outrage among identifiable section.
    - Prohibited names/emblems: UNO, Ashok Chakra, British Royal Arms, Lord Buddha, Shree Sai Baba, Sri Ramkrishna, etc.
    - Red Cross/Geneva Cross: not accepted; green crosses on white background generic for pharmacy; white crosses on green backgrounds generic for first aid.
    - Section 9(3): shape of goods not registrable if exclusively results from nature of goods, necessary to obtain technical result, or gives substantial value.
    - Section 13: prohibition of registration of names of chemical elements or international non-proprietary names.

13. **Draft Communication of Objections**
    - Standard wording for objections under Sections 9(1)(a), 9(1)(b), 9(1)(c), 9(2), 9(3), 13, and Central Government instructions under Section 23(1).

14. **Examination of Series Marks**
    - Section 15: series marks substantially resembling in material particulars, differing in non-distinctive matter.
    - If not eligible, objection raised and applicant required to delete or divide via TM-53.

15. **Conditions of Acceptance / Limitation of Use**
    - Section 18(4): Registrar may accept absolutely or subject to amendments, modifications, conditions or limitations.
    - Examiner proposes conditions in examination report.
    - Examples: variation condition, blank space condition, joint use condition, association condition, limitation as to area/mode of use, colour limitation, disclaimer wording.

16. **Communication of Examination Report**
    - Consolidated report through system: formality objections, Section 9/11 objections, proposals for conditions/limitations.
    - Transmitted to Supervising Examiner for approval.
    - Released on website www.ipindia.nic.in.
    - Sent to applicant/agent by email and post.

## 5. Chapter III: Post Examination Disposal

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch3-001 -->

### Flow

1. Examination report sent.
2. Application objected.
3. Applicant replies to examination report.
4. PARM officials consider reply.
5. If not accepted, show cause hearing.
6. If accepted/ABA, publication in Trade Marks Journal.
7. No opposition within 4 months: registration.
8. Registration certificate issued.

### Consideration of Applicant’s Reply

- Reply can be submitted by scanned colour PDF to parm.tmr@nic.in, online with Level III digital signature, or at office.
- Affidavits/supporting documents may be attached.
- Formality objections: applicant complies or explains.
- Classification objection: amendment or explanation.
- Non-distinctiveness: establish capability or acquired distinctiveness with affidavit.
- Relative grounds: distinguish marks/goods/services, or consent/no objection from cited mark proprietor.
- PARM officer considers reply; may accept, publish, impose conditions/limitations, or move for hearing.
- Advertisement Before Acceptance (ABA) invoked in exceptional circumstances, strong evidence of bona fide commercial adoption and use.
- TM-16 amendment requests disposed first.

### Abandonment if No Reply

- If no reply within one month from receipt of examination report by post, notice under Section 132 generated.
- If still no response, order treating application as abandoned generated.
- Rule 38(4), 38(5); Section 132.

### Show Cause Hearing

- If objections cannot be waived, matter set for hearing through TMS.
- Hearing scheduled at appropriate office.
- Cause list prepared monthly, displayed on website/notice board.
- Hearing notice sent approx. 15 days before hearing.
- Hearing Officer hears submissions, peruses documents, disposes application.
- TM-16 amendments disposed first.

### Publication in Trade Marks Journal

- Accepted applications advertised in journal.
- Advertisement contains mark, application date, priority claim, applicant details, address for service, class/specification, statement of use, appropriate office.
- Journal published every Monday on www.ipindia.nic.in.
- Contents: public notices, accepted applications, re-advertisements, corrigenda, registered/renewed/removed marks, post-registration changes, INNs, other information.

### Withdrawal of Acceptance

- Registrar may withdraw acceptance if accepted in error or should have been subject to different conditions/limitations.
- Officer in charge of EPR/PRAS puts note to CGPDTM.
- After approval, matter moved to Show Cause Hearing Section.
- Notice under Rule 42 to applicant.
- Applicant may withdraw, agree, amend, or request hearing.
- If no response, acceptance deemed withdrawn; application proceeds as if not accepted.
- Section 19, Rule 42.

### Registration of Trademarks

- After 4 months from advertisement, if no opposition or opposition disposed in favour of applicant, proceeds for registration.
- Applications taken in order of seniority of advertisement.
- Officer ensures data entry correct, advertisement correct, no pending amendment, no withdrawal of acceptance, no pending opposition, no prohibition, no formality requirement left.
- Entry made in Register of Trade Marks under Section 6.
- Section 23: registration date is date of making application.
- Certificate issued with seal of Trade Marks Registry.
- Rule 59: entry specifies filing date, actual registration date, goods/services, class, particulars under Section 6(1).

## 6. Chapter IV: Tribunal Section — Opposition & Rectification

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch4-001 -->

### Section A: Opposition

- After advertisement, any person may oppose within 4 months. Section 21.
- Opposition filed before Registrar only; not directly to Court/IPAB.
- Form TM-5; fee Rs. 2,500.
- Grounds: Sections 9, 11, 13, 14, 18(1).
- Notice of opposition contents: impugned application no., goods/services, applicant name; earlier mark/right details; opposing party details; verification.
- Applicant files counter statement in Form TM-6 within 2 months; fee Rs. 1,000.
- If no counter statement, application deemed abandoned.
- Formality check by Cash Section; entry in Tribunal module of TMAS.
- Service of notice on applicant; service of counter statement on opponent.
- Evidence by affidavits; Rule 116.
- Opponent files evidence within 2 months; extension up to 1 month via TM-56.
- If no evidence, opposition deemed abandoned.
- Applicant files evidence within 2 months; extension up to 1 month.
- Opponent may file evidence in reply within 1 month.
- Further evidence with leave.
- Digitisation of record.
- Hearing: notice at least 1 month after first notice; Form TM-7 to notify intention to appear.
- Adjournment: Form TM-56 with fee Rs. 500.
- Non-appearance: applicant application may be abandoned; opponent opposition may be dismissed.
- Correction/amendment: TM-16, TM-53.
- Decision communicated to parties; updated in TMAS.

### Section B: Rectification

- Any person may apply for removal/cancellation/variation/rectification.
- Grounds:
  - Section 47: non-use within 5 years.
  - Section 57(1)&(2): person aggrieved; contravention/failure to observe conditions, absence/omission, entry without sufficient cause, wrongly remaining, error/defect.
  - Section 68: collective mark removal.
  - Section 77: certification mark cancellation/variation.
  - Section 57(4): Tribunal suo motu.
  - Section 58: correction by registered proprietor.
  - Section 59: alteration of registered trade mark.
  - Section 60: change of classification.
- Application in Form TM-26 or TM-43, in triplicate, with statement.
- Registered proprietor files counter statement in Form TM-6 within 2 months.
- Rules 50–57 apply mutatis mutandis.
- Hearing and disposal as in opposition.
- Digitisation.
- Tribunal suo motu rectification under Section 57(4): notice to proprietor and registered users; hearing.

### Section C: Review

- Section 127(c): Registrar may review own decision.
- Form TM-57 within 1 month, extendable by 1 month.
- Grounds: discovery of new and important matter/evidence; mistake/error apparent on face of record; any other sufficient reason.
- Fee Rs. 2,000.
- Service on other person if decision concerns them.
- Hearing and decision by same officer generally; Section 4 allows transfer.
- Digitisation.

## 7. Chapter V: Pre-Registration Amendment

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch5-001 -->

- Correction of data entry errors or amendment before registration.
- Form TM-16 with fee and supporting documents.
- No substantial alteration in trademark, proprietor details, specification (except deletion), statement of use.
- Permitted: change in proprietorship via valid assignment/transmission, address change, deletion/confinement of goods/services, limitation in area of sale, division of multi-class application.
- PRAS section disposes requests; updates system.
- Correction of data after advertisement: corrigendum for clerical errors; re-advertisement for major errors.
- Correction in registration certificate: minor errors corrected and certificate reissued; major errors initiate Section 57(4) proceedings.
- Division of applications: Form TM-53; divided application retains original filing date; new serial number.

## 8. Chapter VI: Renewal, Assignment/Transmission, Registered User and Post-Registration Changes

<!-- chunk_id: tmm-ch6-001 -->

### Renewal

- Registration valid for 10 years from date of registration. Section 23(1), 25(1).
- Prior notice on Form O-3 not less than 1 month and not more than 3 months before expiration. Section 25(3), Rule 64(1).
- Application for renewal: Form TM-12, not more than 6 months before expiration. Rule 63(1).
- Who can apply: registered proprietor or duly authorised agent.
- Fee: Rs. 5,000 per class; series Rs. 5,000 per mark; multi-class Rs. 5,000 per class; collective/certification Rs. 10,000.
- Renewal after expiration: within 6 months with surcharge Rs. 3,000 on Form TM-10.
- Restoration: Form TM-13 with Rs. 5,000, renewal fee and surcharge, after 6 months but within 1 year. Section 25(4), Rule 66.
- If no renewal up to 1 year, mark removed from date of expiration.
- Renewal processed through Renewal module; intimation generated; published in journal.

### Assignment & Transmission

- Assignment/transmission passes proprietorship.
- Registered trademark assignable with or without goodwill.
- Unregistered trademark may also be assigned/transmitted with or without goodwill.
- Restrictions on creating multiple exclusive rights; Registrar may approve if not contrary to public interest.
- Assignment without goodwill: assignor must obtain Registrar’s directions and advertise.
- Certification trademark assignment requires Registrar’s consent.
- Associated trademarks assigned together.
- Disputed validity: Registrar may refuse registration until court determines.
- Forms: TM-24 (application to register title), TM-23 (joint request), TM-17/TM-19 (multiple exclusive rights), TM-20 (directions), TM-21 (extension), TM-62 (certification mark consent).
- Fees vary; see First Schedule.
- Documents: original/certified copy of instrument of title; affidavit Form TM-18 if required.
- Recordal process: scan/upload, data entry in POST REGISTRATION module, scrutiny, examination, disposal, publication.

### Registered Users

- Permitted use defined in Section 2(1)(r).
- Application jointly by registered proprietor and proposed user on Form TM-28.
- Documents: agreement in writing, authenticated copies, affidavit of registered proprietor.
- Variation: Form TM-29; cancellation: TM-30 or TM-31.
- Fees: TM-28 Rs. 5,000 first mark, Rs. 3,000 additional; TM-29 Rs. 5,000 first, Rs. 2,500 additional; TM-30 Rs. 2,500 first, Rs. 500 additional; TM-31 Rs. 5,000 first, Rs. 2,000 additional.
- Recordal process: scan/upload, data entry in REGISTERED USER module, scrutiny, examination, publication.

### Post-Registration Amendments

- Correction/amendment of name/description: Form TM-33.
- Address of proprietor: Form TM-34.
- Address for service: Form TM-50.
- Cancellation of registered trademark: Form TM-35.
- Strike out goods/services: Form TM-36.
- Adaptation after amendment in Fourth Schedule: Form TM-40.
- Alteration in registered trademark: Form TM-38.
- Fees as per First Schedule.
- Recordal process: scan/upload, data entry in PR Entry of POST REGISTRATION module, scrutiny, examination, publication.

## 9. RAG Q&A Extraction

### Q: What is the objective of the Trade Marks Manual?
**A:** To bring uniformity and consistency in practice and procedure of the Trade Marks Registry, and to serve as a guide for officers and users.

### Q: What is the time limit for filing a notice of opposition?
**A:** Within 4 months from the date of advertisement or re-advertisement in the Trade Marks Journal. Section 21(1).

### Q: What is the fee for filing a notice of opposition?
**A:** Rs. 2,500, First Schedule entry No. 12, Trade Marks Rules 2002.

### Q: What is the term of registration and renewal period?
**A:** 10 years from date of registration, renewable from time to time.

### Q: What form is used for renewal?
**A:** Form TM-12.

### Q: What is the effect of non-payment of renewal fee?
**A:** Mark becomes liable to be removed; restoration possible via Form TM-13 within 1 year with fees and surcharge.

### Q: What is the difference between opposition and rectification?
**A:** Opposition challenges registration before grant after advertisement; rectification challenges a registered trademark after registration.

### Q: What is “permitted use”?
**A:** Use by a registered user or by a person other than registered proprietor/registered user with consent in a written agreement, in the course of trade, for registered goods/services, complying with conditions/limitations.
